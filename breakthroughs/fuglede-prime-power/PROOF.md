# Prime-power cardinalities in one-dimensional Fuglede

Research note, 2026-11-26. Not peer reviewed. The unrestricted one-dimensional Fuglede conjecture remains open. The results below settle prime-power **cardinalities** in finite cyclic groups of arbitrary ambient order, give a common complement for families sharing one spectrum, and classify all normalized spectra of each finite prime-power integer tile. They imply a spectral-to-tiling result on the real line under a restriction on the spectrum's period. Cardinality one is trivial; below, prime-power size means p^k with k≥1. They also prove a deletion theorem with no cardinality restriction other than coprimality to the prime being deleted.

Sources and provenance. The base field comparison and p-adic block argument for one prime coordinate are due to Hu Tan and Ying Zhang, Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.4. The high-digit induction below extends their argument to arbitrary powers. The two-prime reduction uses the exact integer convolution identity in Malikiosis’s proof of Theorem 6.1. The pure p-group homogeneity characterization is due to [Fan, Fan and Shi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04837), and the integer tiling criterion is due to Newman (see [Hu et al., §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22849v1#S2)). The proof of finite rationality uses the positive-kernel argument of Fu and Song, with a shorter proof of kernel positivity. The tiling classification and gcd invariants are due to Hu, Wang, Wang and Zhu. [Tan–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02218v1) · [Malikiosis](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2022.14) · [Fu–Song](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19858v1) · [Hu–Wang–Wang–Zhu](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22849v1).

The standalone exact test script [`replay.js`](replay.js) is included. It compares two independent finite enumerations against the counting formula for ten test sets and checks grouped Gram conditions and all digit-removal stages for tensor character examples. The general proof does not depend on search.

## 1. Odd prime-power deletion

Let p be an odd prime, (p,nm)=1, a≥1. Let W be an m×m matrix of n-th roots of unity. Fix spatial and frequency labels r_i,c_j in Z/(p^a), and suppose F_ij=W_ij ζ_(p^a)^(r_i c_j) is Hadamard, so FF*=mI. Then W is Hadamard. For a spectral pair in H×Z/(p^a) with H exponent n, both projections to H and its dual are injective and form a spectral pair.

We prove by induction that truncating all c_j to their least nonnegative residues modulo p^(a-k) preserves the full Hadamard matrix identity, for k=0,…,a. Spatial labels r_i remain in Z/(p^a), and the twist throughout uses ζ_(p^a). At a step k<a, write current column labels in a fixed parent class as c=u+t h, with h=p^(a-k-1), 0≤u<h, 0≤t<p. This is the collection of sibling columns that will be collapsed onto label u. Let W_t be its child submatrices of W. Define Q_t=W_t W_t*. The Q_t lie in R=Z[ζ_n], are Hermitian, and satisfy Q_t²=mQ_t because the child columns have the same phase factor along rows and are orthogonal with norm √m. Empty groups are allowed.

Consider two distinct rows having different labels modulo p^(k+1). If v=v_p(r_i−r_i′)<k, their orthogonality gives a polynomial of degree <p^(a−k) evaluated at a primitive p^(a−v)-th root, with coefficients (W_j W_j*)_(i,i′) for the current full label groups. Its degree is smaller than the cyclotomic degree, so the coefficients vanish individually. If v=k, the minimum polynomial over Q(ζ_n) is the sum of X^(t h) over t=0,…,p−1, because p is coprime to n. Divisibility implies the coefficients agree for all t within each parent u. Therefore the Q_t in this fixed parent have a common off-diagonal block part B when rows are blocked by r mod p^(k+1). Write Q_t=D_t+B.

After stripping the common parent row phase ζ_(p^a)^(r_i u), the actual child columns in this parent are T_t W_t with diagonal T_t, entries ζ_(p^(k+1))^(r_i t). Their combined Gram is S=Σ T_t Q_t T_t*. It is block diagonal on the row blocks. Across different blocks with v<k every summand is zero; when v=k the common coefficient times the complete nontrivial p-root sum cancels. On each diagonal block T_t is scalar, so S=Σ D_t. In particular, S belongs to M_m(R), although it was defined from p-primary phases.

The columns in the parent are orthogonal. Hence S(T_t W_t)=m T_t W_t. Since S commutes with each T_t, this gives S Q_t=Q_t S=mQ_t. Taking off-diagonal blocks yields S B=B S=mB. Sum Q_t²=mQ_t over children and compare off-diagonal blocks to get

    p(B²)_off = m(p−2) B.

The coefficient m(p−2) is invertible modulo p and every power of p. Starting with entries in R, if B lies in p^j R entrywise then the equation implies B lies in p^(j+1) R entrywise. The intersection of these submodules is zero because R is free over Z. Thus B=0.

It follows that the Gram of the unphased child columns is ΣQ_t=S. Reintroducing the common parent row phase therefore preserves the parent's Gram and all columns together still satisfy FF*=mI after truncation. The last stage is W. Duplicate projected points or spectrum labels would give identical rows or columns of W in the character-matrix setting, so they cannot occur.

The proof also classifies these phase twists for W already Hadamard. For each t=1,…,a and residue u modulo p^t, the W-column submatrix on c≡u mod p^t must have a Gram matrix block diagonal by row label r mod p^(a-t+1). These are exactly the block conditions B=0 established at each stage. Conversely, for a row pair of valuation v<a, group the twisted sum over c mod p^(a-v) and apply that condition. Equal spatial labels use orthogonality in W. This gives sufficiency. The result does not cover p=2 in general, since m(p−2) vanishes.

If the projected spatial set tiles the smaller group, the original spatial set tiles by the full inverse image of any complement under the quotient map. Therefore any smallest-order cyclic spectral non-tile must have every odd prime divisor of its group order divide its cardinality. This also holds for any unused odd prime cyclic factor of a finite abelian group. The theorem leaves repeated powers of primes that divide the cardinality untouched.

## 2. Prime-power cardinality in arbitrary cyclic groups

For a spectral pair of size m=p^k with k≥1 in any finite cyclic group of order N, both p-primary projections are injective and remain a spectral pair. The projected sets tile the p-primary group. All compatible spatial sets with one fixed spectrum have the same p-primary digit complement, which lifts to a common tiling complement in the original group.

Use Section 1 to remove every odd prime-power coordinate except p. For p=2 this leaves only a p-power. For odd p only p-power and 2-power coordinates can remain, and the following two-prime argument removes the unused factor. A remaining group of order a power of a different prime cannot support m=p^k by the same digit count or by the two-prime argument with a trivial p-factor.

Let a spectral pair in C_(p^a)×C_(q^b) have q not dividing m. Projection of either member onto C_(p^a) is injective, since a collision would give an orthogonality sum of m pure q-power roots whose polynomial coefficient comparison forces q|m. Let L be the set of levels ℓ=a−v_p(λ−λ′) realized by distinct projected spectrum members. Partition the spectrum by its p-adic digits starting at the lowest digit. A branch at depth r can occur only if a−r belongs to L, so m≤p^|L|.

Let A′(X) be the nonnegative mask polynomial of the spatial projection. For every ℓ∈L, orthogonality gives a vanishing sum at a character whose p-component is primitive of order p^ℓ. The q-component has pure q-power order, possibly 1. Reduce the coefficient comparison modulo q and send the q-root to 1. This shows Φ_(p^ℓ)(X) divides A′(X) over F_q. More explicitly, for a nontrivial q component, the two primitive roots generate a tensor product with minimal polynomials Φ_(p^ℓ) and Φ_(q^t), because the cyclotomic degrees multiply. In the integer quotient by these two polynomials, reduction Y↦1 is well-defined modulo q because Φ_(q^t)(1)=q. For trivial q-component the relation already holds over Z.

Multiply A′(X) by the product D(X)=Π_(ℓ∉L) Φ_(p^ℓ)(X), with 1≤ℓ≤a. All coefficients are nonnegative. Modulo q the full product is divisible by 1+X+⋯+X^(p^a−1) because these cyclotomic factors are pairwise coprime in F_q. Reduction modulo X^(p^a)−1 makes all coefficients congruent modulo q to the same nonzero residue: their total is m p^(a−|L|), nonzero modulo q. Thus their nonnegative total is at least p^a. The branching bound gives the opposite inequality. Every reduced coefficient is exactly 1. This proves the common tiling by the digit set with mask D(X). Evaluation at each primitive p^ℓ root, ℓ∈L, also shows actual Fourier zeros of A′, as the multiplier D is nonzero there. Thus the projected spectrum really is a spectrum. The exact convolution identity already occurs in the proof of Malikiosis’s Theorem 6.1. The projection and common-complement statements follow directly from that identity; this two-prime step is used here as prior work.

For a pure p-power ambient group, a spectrum difference with p-order p^ℓ forces uniform masses in the p children of every occupied prefix of length ℓ−1. This is cyclotomic coefficient comparison. The earlier bound m≤p^|L| applied on the spectrum side and these forced full spatial branches give m=p^|L|. No spatial levels other than L can branch, and in exactly those levels every occupied parent has all p children. The same reciprocal conclusion applies to the spectrum. The complement is the digit set on the other levels and depends only on L. Distinct points of it and of A′ have disjoint nonzero difference valuations, so their addition is bijective by cardinality.

Conversely any tile of prime-power cardinality in a finite cyclic group projects as an injective tile to the p-primary subgroup, via repeatedly applying the Tijdeman finite dilation lemma to multipliers from the coprime factor N/p^a, and restricting the resulting tiling to that subgroup. For the dilation, write the tiling convolution as C(X)D(X)=J modulo X^N−1, with J=1+X+⋯+X^(N−1). For a prime q not dividing |C|, Frobenius gives C(X^q)D(X)≡|C|^(q−1)J≡J modulo q. The nonnegative coefficients sum to N, so each is exactly one. Iterate over the primes of the coprime factor, restrict to the p-primary subgroup, and undo the unit multiplier there. The cyclic p-power tile is spectral by the classical p-digit criterion, which supplies a spectrum using only p-primary characters for the original tile. This proves equivalence in the stated cardinalities. [Finite dilation lemma in Tan–Zhang §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02218v1#S3).

## 3. All normalized spectra of a prime-power integer tile

Let A⊂Z have cardinality p^k with k≥1. Assume it is spectral or tiles Z. If spectral, normalized spectrum rationality yields a finite cyclic realization and Section 2 gives a tiling of Z by a periodic lift. If a tile, Newman's criterion gives the fully branching p-adic description, and hence a pure p-power spectrum. See Section 4 for a concise rationality proof.

Use the same invariants as Hu–Wang–Wang–Zhu. Let

    ν_1<⋯<ν_k,   {ν_i−1} = {v_p(a−a′): distinct a,a′∈A}.
    Δ_i = gcd{|a−a′|/p^(ν_i−1) : a≠a′, a≡a′ mod p^(ν_i−1)}.
    a_*=ν_k,   P=p^a_*.

The Δ_i are coprime to p, and Δ_i divides Δ_(i+1). A normalized spectrum contains the frequency 0 in R/Z. Every such spectrum has the following exact form:

1. Its p-primary projection is a p-homogeneous spectrum in (1/P)Z/Z whose active p-adic numerator depths are D={a_*−ν_i : 1≤i≤k}. Conversely any normalized support Λ_p of this form is an allowed p-primary projection. This follows from Section 2 and p-tree duality. If the original rational spectrum has least common denominator p^a d, then the maximum p-order of a difference is p^a (it contains 0), while the spatial branch levels force that maximum to be p^ν_k. Thus a=a_*.

2. To each leaf λ∈Λ_p assign a prime-to-p phase θ(λ), with θ(0)=0. For two leaves whose p-primary difference has order p^ν_i, the exact requirement is that the order of θ(λ)−θ(λ′) divides Δ_i. Then the combined frequencies are λ+θ(λ). This describes all permitted assignments.

Sufficiency in 2 follows by grouping the spectral orthogonality sum at level ν_i. Below any occupied parent prefix of depth ν_i−1, all A points are congruent modulo every d|Δ_i. The p-branch has p children of equal size, so the sum of the p-root phases across its children is zero even with the common extra phase.

For necessity, let M_i be the lcm of prime-to-p orders among frequency differences whose p-order is ≤p^ν_i. It is enough to take differences of exact p-order p^ν_i. Indeed, for a smaller-order pair in one child of that spectral branch, choose any leaf in a different child of the same parent. The smaller-order difference is the difference of two exact-level comparisons. Both exist because the p-spectrum is fully branching there. The M_i form a divisibility chain.

At level i=k, each occupied spatial child of depth ν_k consists of one point. For a spectrum difference of exact p-order p^ν_k and prime-to-p order d, cyclotomic coefficient comparison over Q(ζ_d) forces those points to have equal characters in each sibling group. Thus all such d divide Δ_k, and M_k divides Δ_k.

Descend by induction. Suppose M_(i+1) divides Δ_(i+1). In each spatial child prefix of depth ν_i all A points agree modulo p^(ν_(i+1)−1), since intervening depths are nonbranching. They are therefore congruent modulo Δ_(i+1), and in particular modulo M_i. At a spectral difference of exact p-order p^ν_i and prime-to-p order d|M_i, each child's weighted root sum is its common cardinality p^(k−i) times a single d-root. The p-cyclotomic comparison forces these root sums, and thus their residues mod d, to agree across siblings. This says d divides Δ_i. Taking the lcm over the exact-level comparisons proves M_i|Δ_i. This completes the classification.

### Counts, denominators, and tiling equivalence

Let b_d be the number of active depths in D strictly below d. There are p^(Σ_(d∉D,0≤d<a_*)(p^b_d−1)) normalized p-primary supports. At a nonbranching depth the p^b_d occupied prefixes each have p choices, except the zero prefix is forced to choose zero because the support must contain 0. All active depths have every child.

For each such support, contract unary edges and choose one distinguished child and its representative leaf at each branch. Each parent's representative is recursively inherited from its distinguished child; choose the distinguished path from the root to end at leaf 0. At a branch associated with ν_i, the increments from the distinguished child's representative to the other p−1 child representatives can be chosen independently in the cyclic subgroup of R/Z of order Δ_i. All descendant increments have orders dividing Δ_i by the gcd chain. The increments are exactly a reparameterization of the allowed leaf assignments. There are p^(k−i) branch nodes at that level. Consequently the exact number of normalized spectra is

    p^( Σ_(d∉D, 0≤d<a_*) (p^b_d−1) ) · Π_(i=1)^k Δ_i^((p−1)p^(k−i)).

The possible least common spectrum denominators are exactly P d with d|Δ_k. Necessity is proven above. For each such d one can put an order-d increment at a single root child and set all other increments to zero. The p denominator always equals P. This matches the possible fundamental tiling periods in Hu–Wang–Wang–Zhu Theorem 1.1. The family of spectra recovers the ν_i from any p-primary spectrum's difference orders and each Δ_i as the largest possible prime-to-p order at exact p-level ν_i. This value is attained by putting a generator at one branch there and zero elsewhere. Thus two prime-power integer tiles have the same spectra if and only if they have the same tiling complements, by the independent tiling classification in their Theorem 1.3.

Example A={0,3,10,13} has exactly five normalized spectra with least denominators 4 or 20. Two different 8-point tiles with invariants ν=(1,2,3), Δ=(1,1,3) have exactly the same three normalized spectra even though their mask zero sets differ at order 6. The saved verifier enumerates all cliques containing 0 via exact cyclotomic equations and separately generates all spectra by the trees. Equality is checked as sets, including plausible extra denominators chosen in several experiments. The 1296-spectrum case is a five-point progression of step 6.

## 4. The finite rationality bridge and a real-line case

Fu and Song prove finite normalized rationality and real-line rationality by modulus rigidity. The following check gives a shorter version of the needed finite argument. If integer-exponent inverse orthogonality holds at nonzero complex nodes w_j, invert the square Vandermonde-like matrix to get the reciprocal row relations Σ w_j^(a−b)=mδ_(a,b). Scale the minimum modulus to 1, put t_j=log|w_j|, T=max t_j, and u_j=w_j/|w_j|. For a≠b subtract the complex conjugate of the reversed row relation. It yields Σ sinh(t_j(a−b)) u_j^(a−b)=0.

If T>0, the kernel sinh(tx)/sinh(Tx) is positive definite for 0≤t≤T, with value t/T at 0. For 0<t<T, the standard Euler product for sinh writes it as t/T times the product of factors r+(1−r)/(1+(Tx/(πj))²) with r=(t/T)². The constant and reciprocal quadratic are positive definite by the Laplace Fourier integral. Products and limits preserve positive semidefiniteness on every finite test matrix. The cases t=0,T give the zero and constant-one kernels. Take the entrywise product of these kernel matrices on differences a−b with the rank-one Gram matrix for (u_j^a)_a, and sum over j. The sum is positive semidefinite, is diagonal by the subtracted row relation, and has every diagonal equal to Σ t_j/T<m, since at least one t_j is zero. Any max-modulus j contributes a rank-one term with eigenvalue m. This is a contradiction. Thus all |w_j| agree. For normalized spectral nodes originally on the circle, translate the spatial integer set to minimum zero so that the mask is monic. The nontrivial nodes are algebraic integers. Apply the same argument after every Galois automorphism of the normal closure, retaining the node 1. All conjugates have modulus 1, and Kronecker gives roots of unity. [Fu–Song, Sections 4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19858v1#S4).

For a bounded measurable spectral Ω⊂R of measure 1 whose spectrum has a period m=p^k, translate that spectrum to include 0. Write Λ=S+mZ. By the standard spectral fiberization, almost every x∈[0,1/m) has an integer fiber A_x={n : x+n/m∈Ω} of size m and common normalized finite spectrum S/m. Finite rationality applies to every such common frequency set. Use a common denominator and Section 2 to project all fibers to one p-primary group. They have a common digit tiling complement determined solely by the spectrum. Lifting it to Z gives T_0 with A_x⊕T_0=Z almost everywhere. Therefore Ω tiles R by T_0/m, by translating along each fiber. The measure-one normalization is essential to the statement about the integer period. Equivalently, without fixing the measure, if the spectrum is a union of a prime-power number of cosets of h⁻¹Z, the tiling uses a subset of hZ. In the terminology of [Bell, §2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00307v1), the finite result proves the common-complement statement (U_k) and the lift proves (F_k) for every prime-power fiber cardinality k. Bell establishes these fiber lemmas for k≤7, including earlier work of Dutkay–Haussermann for k≤5. There is no claim of general one-dimensional Fuglede. [Spectral fiberization, Fu–Song Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19858v1#S3) · [Dutkay–Lai reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.0814).
