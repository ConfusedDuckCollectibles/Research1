# Twice-prime cardinalities in one-dimensional Fuglede

An AI-assisted research note, 2026-11-26. Not externally peer reviewed; no priority claim. The previous [prime-power project](../) proves deletion of unused odd prime-power coordinates and handles prime-power cardinalities. These new arguments handle |A|=2q, q any odd prime, in every finite cyclic group. They also handle |A|=pq in every odd cyclic group, for distinct odd primes p,q. The universal complement upgrades the 2q result to bounded real-line spectral sets of measure one with a spectral period 2q. A full proof of one-dimensional Fuglede remains open.

Main literature comparisons. The positive decomposition into p- and q-cycles is Lam–Leung, Theorem 3.3. The outline below includes a direct two-prime proof. Malikiosis's paper studies arbitrary cardinalities in two-prime-power groups, including primitive counterexamples and cycle decompositions. The cardinality pq makes that decomposition pure and also allows an independent direct argument for the complementary zero levels. Finite tile-to-spectral for two prime divisors is a standard consequence of Coven–Meyerowitz conditions. The setting of arbitrary cyclic order at size 2q differs from the known results for fixed ambient-order families. [Bell](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00307v1) already proves the same matched zeros, common omitted-digit complement and real-line result at fiber size six (Theorems 10.13, 10.14 and 10.16). The contribution here is the unbounded twice-prime family (including the core at two arbitrary prime powers), not the size-six result or the fiberization argument. [Tan–Zhang’s periodic-tiling paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26576v1) concerns periodic complements when tiling in Z^d is already known; it does not assert the spectral-to-tile implication here. [Lam–Leung](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9511209) · [Malikiosis, Sections 3 and 5](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2022.14) · [Tan–Zhang prime-coordinate result](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02218v1#S2).

## 1. The two-prime-power core

Let G=C_(p^a)×C_(q^b), p and q distinct primes, a,b≥1. Let A,B be a spectral pair of cardinality pq. An order pair (ℓ,t) refers to a character of exact orders p^ℓ and q^t in those factors; order exponent zero means the trivial character in that coordinate. An integer mask vanishes at one such character if and only if it vanishes at every character of that order pair, by Galois and the Chinese remainder theorem.

For A, there is at most one positive pure p order p^r at which its Fourier sum vanishes. Indeed each cyclotomic divisor Φ_(p^ℓ) contributes p to A(1), and v_p(pq)=1. The same holds at q, with level s. At any vanishing mixed order, the pq terms form a disjoint multiset union of p-cycles and q-cycles. If there are x p-cycles and y q-cycles, px+qy=pq implies q|x and p|y, so precisely one of the two types occurs. A pure p-cycle decomposition at mixed order (ℓ,t) forces the pure p^ℓ zero as well, since the q-primary character is constant on each p-cycle. It also forces every mixed order (ℓ,t′) with t′≤t, since each projected p-cycle survives further reduction of the q-coordinate. The q case is the exact analogue.

For completeness, this nonnegative decomposition follows directly in two prime powers. Arrange the counts of the phases at a character of order pair (ℓ,t) by low exponent prefixes modulo p^(ℓ−1) and q^(t−1). Each prefix block has p highest p-digits and q highest q-digits. By coprimality, the rectangular tensor basis of the two root fields consists of the monomials with the last high p-digit and last high q-digit omitted. Reducing those last digits using their pure cyclotomic sums shows that a block count matrix c_ij satisfies c_ij−c_(p−1,j)−c_(i,q−1)+c_(p−1,q−1)=0 for all i<p−1 and j<q−1. Equivalently it is u_i+v_j. Subtract min_i u_i from the u values and add it to the v values. Both resulting vectors are nonnegative integers because each new v equals a count in a minimum row. This writes every count block as a nonnegative sum of full rows and full columns. They are the stated prime cycles.

Orthogonality implies that for every distinct pair in B the p-order of its difference must be p^r or its q-order must be q^s, referring only to pure zero levels that exist. If one such level is absent, the points of B are injectively labelled by one distinguished digit in the other coordinate, giving at most p or q points. This is impossible. Thus both pure levels exist, and the pair condition holds with these r,s.

For λ∈B let (i,j) be its digits at the spatial depths a−r and b−s in the two respective primary coordinates. A difference of order p^r requires unequal i, and one of order q^s requires unequal j. Hence two B points cannot have the same (i,j). The pq points use the entire p×q grid.

In any column j, points of B have the same q-distinguished digit and therefore all their mutual p-order differences are p^r. Their lower p-prefix is constant down that column. Write it u_j. Similarly the lower q-prefix v_i depends only on row i. It is impossible for both prefix maps to be nonconstant: choose j,j′ with different u and i,i′ with different v, then the diagonal grid pair has both orders larger than (r,s), violating the pair condition. A diagonal pair always exists because p,q≥2. Its order pair has both coordinates at least (r,s) and at least one equal. If both equal, A has the mixed zero (r,s). If one is larger, its other-coordinate prime cycle purity implies the same mixed zero by further reduction, as above. Thus A has exactly the three required zeros p^r, q^s, p^r q^s, possibly along with other mixed zeros.

The mirror pure levels of B are a−r+1 and b−s+1. For example, each column contributes all p distinguished p-digits above its fixed lower p-prefix. Thus the projection of B to C_(p^(a−r+1)) is balanced on the p children of each occupied prefix, giving that pure p zero. It is unique by v_p(|B|)=1; the q statement is symmetric. Consequently the two pure levels of A are determined by B alone, whether or not A was known to tile.

A common tiling complement is the product of the p-axis digit set omitting digit r−1 and the q-axis digit set omitting digit s−1. The p-axis set's Fourier transform vanishes at every positive p-order other than p^r. This follows by summing the p-digit at position ℓ−1 for each character of order p^ℓ unless that digit is omitted. The q-axis is analogous. At every nontrivial character either the complement's Fourier value is zero or the order pair is among A's three zeros. Its size is |G|/(pq), so convolution is identically 1. This proves tiling and universality for every set A having the given B as a spectrum.

Conversely, a tile of size pq in G has the two-prime Coven–Meyerowitz (T1) and (T2) conditions. They give a pure p zero p^r, pure q zero q^s and their mixed product. One can see spectrality directly by taking the product of frequency digits with spatial steps p^(a−r), q^(b−s). This has pq characters and all nontrivial differences have order pairs among the three zeros. This direction uses the established two-prime CM theorem. [Malikiosis, Theorem 3.5 and introduction](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2022.14).

## 2. Arbitrary cyclic periods and real-line periods

For size 2q, delete every unused odd prime-power factor by the previously proved high-digit result. It preserves spectrality and injectivity of both projections. The retained cardinality 2q requires both relevant prime factors to be present, since spectral cardinality in a single primary factor is a prime power. Apply Section 1. The common complement pulls back by taking its full inverse image under the quotient. Injectivity gives exactly one lift of each spatial point, and the complement's full fibers supply exactly the lift needed for each sum. If there is one spectrum B for many spatial sets, its reduced spectrum is fixed. The mirror levels and hence the same complement apply to every set.

For the converse, suppose A tiles an arbitrary finite cyclic group and has size 2q. Every unused prime is prime to |A|. Apply Tijdeman finite dilation by the product of those entire primary factors. The resulting A is contained, injectively, in the primary product of orders powers of 2 and q. It tiles that subgroup by restricting the original complement of the dilated set to the subgroup. The dilated set is an invertible scaling of the natural projection, because its scalar is a unit in the retained cyclic factor. Use Section 1 and lift the quotient spectrum. The same reasoning gives both directions and a common complement for arbitrary odd N at size pq (both primes odd). For odd pq in an even ambient group, tiling still implies spectrality by dilating out the unused binary factor as well. The unaddressed general implication in that case is spectral-to-tiling, and the general common-complement statement. The case of trivial cardinality and prime powers is covered separately in the previous project.

For Ω⊂R bounded with measure 1 and a spectrum of period 2q, normalize the spectrum to contain 0. Periodic fiberization gives almost-everywhere integer fibers of size 2q with one common finite spectrum. These finite normalized frequencies are rational by Fu–Song, with the shorter kernel proof reproduced in the previous project. A common denominator gives a single finite cyclic group and hence one tiling complement for every fiber. Pull back to Z and divide translations by 2q. This gives one real-line tiling, not separate incompatible tilings of the fibers. Without measure-one normalization, for a set of measure L and a spectral period ρ the invariant hypothesis is Lρ=2q; the translation set lies in (1/ρ)Z. The result complements the prime-power periods. [Fu–Song, Sections 3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19858v1) · [Dutkay–Jorgensen universal tiling formulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.0666).

## 3. Exact number of normalized spectra in the two-prime core

Every spectrum of A is classified by the p×q digit grid in Section 1, together with its two lower-prefix maps and higher digits. From the three matched zeros define
- L_p = max of 0 and all ℓ≠r with a zero at order (ℓ,s).
- L_q = max of 0 and all t≠s with a zero at order (r,t).

By pure cycles and downward closure, the allowed p orders paired with q^s are exactly the initial segment [0,L_p] plus the distinguished level r; the q analogue holds. The case L_p=0 includes the pure q^s zero and similarly for L_q. These are finite order exponents in the given group.

Normalize B to contain 0, so u_0=v_0=0 and the higher tags at the cell (0,0) are zero. If both prefix maps are constant, the p-coordinate of B at cell (i,j) is p^(a−r)i+p^(a−r+1)z_ij for z_ij mod p^(r−1). Within any fixed i, different j have q-order q^s and p-order at most r−1. Their p higher tags must agree modulo p^(r−1−min(L_p,r−1)). In each nonzero row the first higher tag is arbitrary, and in row zero it is fixed at zero. Each other tag has p^min(L_p,r−1) choices. The q higher tags obey the transposed condition. The number of constant-prefix spectra is
     
    F = p^((p−1)(r−1)+p(q−1) min(L_p,r−1))
        q^((q−1)(s−1)+q(p−1) min(L_q,s−1)).

If the p lower map varies, the q map must be identically zero. Now L_p>r, and the p lower prefixes u_j are exactly arbitrary multiples of p^(a−L_p) modulo p^(a−r), with u_0=0, at least one nonzero. This ensures that distinct prefixes have p-order ≤p^L_p, while coincident prefixes and equal p-digit can have any higher tag. Since L_p>r, higher p tags were already unrestricted in F. The q higher tags still obey precisely their fixed-column constraints, and pairs differing in both distinguished digits obey the matched or downward root conditions automatically. There are p^((q−1)(L_p−r))−1 extra choices of nonconstant prefix map. The symmetric statement holds if q lower map varies.

Therefore the exact number of spectra containing 0 is

    F · (p^((q−1) max(L_p−r,0)) + q^((p−1) max(L_q−s,0)) − 1).

This also gives a finite formula for all spectra using only p,q-primary phases of an integer tile of size pq. Compute r,s,L_p,L_q from its finite mask polynomial. Choose ambient exponents max(r,L_p),max(s,L_q) because the prefix-map proof bounds each possible exponent this way. At most one primary exponent can exceed its matched r or s in any one spectrum, because at least one prefix map is constant. Extra unused prime phases are outside this counting formula. For 2q in arbitrary finite ambient groups they may instead be studied through the previous prime-power twist conditions.

## 4. What can be done in even ambient order for odd pq

Let 2^c be the remaining unused primary factor after deleting unused odd factors. If the spectrum B has a cohort of more than max(p,q) points sharing its exact full 2-coordinate, any A having spectrum B tiles. Projection A onto the p,q product is injective since a spatial collision would require a vanishing sum of an odd number of pure 2-power roots. For differences of pairs of frequencies inside the cohort, the 2-phase disappears, so every positive vanishing sum has only the p and q prime factors. The same single-digit argument forces both pure levels r,s for A. The cohort is injectively labelled by the two distinguished digits. More than max(p,q) distinct grid cells imply a pair differing in both digits: otherwise all cells lie in a common row or column. Then the same downward argument gives the mixed zero. Thus Section 1's product digit complement tiles A's projection, and its full inverse image tiles A. In particular, this applies uniformly to individual spectral sets whenever 2^c<min(p,q).

Here is a sufficient criterion for a common complement in the even case. Write p<q and let L be the number of occupied 2-cohorts of the fixed B. If L<p and q>L p, the pure levels are uniquely determined by the pair constraints inside the cohorts. To prove uniqueness, any two candidate pairs with one coordinate in common and the other different would force that one order for every pair in every cohort. A cohort then has size at most p or q, contradicting the large cohort. If both coordinates differ, every pair in each cohort must have one of two exact crossed order pairs (r,s′) or (r′,s). If one type is high in p and low in q, any non-monochromatic triple is impossible: the largest order in each primary coordinate must occur at least twice in a triple by its ultrametric inequality. Thus each cohort is monochromatic and has size at most p. Otherwise the high type is high in both. The lower p-order classes are inside one high-order p-branch, with at most p classes and at most p points per class. Each cohort then has size at most p², contradicting pq>L p². This yields a common digit complement whenever the two stated inequalities are met. The proof leaves the general odd semiprime case with large unused 2-primary factors open.

The replay includes a synthetic 15-point cohort pattern for p=3,q=5,L=2 with two possible level pairs under only the within-cohort pair constraint. It is not a counterexample to any Fuglede statement. It confirms that the uniqueness argument cannot ignore the q>L p inequality without additional spectral input.
