# Equality and strictness in the signed support-fiber certificate

This proof uses the [complete normalized PSD certificate](proof.md) to classify all maximum intersecting families for a common alphabet size k≥2. The cell weights are arbitrary and all retained cell weights are positive; in the original unweighted conjecture every weight is one. Let M be the largest original point-star weight.

## Reduction to the balanced completion
A maximum intersecting family containing a singleton is the whole original star at that singleton: pairwise intersection puts the whole family inside that star, whose weight is at most M, and positive weights force equality of families. Otherwise the family uses no singleton, so its weight and indicator do not change on adding singleton mass or supports to balance the original family. Extend the indicator by zero on added cells. Both completed and original caps are M. Let f_U=sqrt(w_S) on a face U of support S and let y_U=f_U 1_A(U). For the completed normalized certificate P=H−ff^T/M, any intersecting maximizing A has y^T P y=0, since H has identity entries on the diagonal and zero on distinct intersecting pairs and A has weight M. So Py=0. Working on a positive cell, divide face coordinates by its constant f_U to recover its indicator.

## Kernel information
For J=empty the block of P is I−Π: H adds W=zz^T/M−Π and ff^T/M becomes zz^T/M in normalized constant cell coordinates. Its kernel consists of the support incidence columns V_i=(z_S 1_{i∈S})_S. For a singleton J={i}, P is I−W_i/(k−1); its kernel is precisely the z_i direction on cell indices times the mean-zero label space. Indeed z_i is the eigenvector for the positive eigenvalue k−1 and all vectors perpendicular to it have nonpositive quadratic form. Thus the indicator's constant and first-order parts are sums of functions of the individual groups, shared across all containing support cells. To check normalized scaling, a product basis vector of mean-zero functions at J and normalized constants in S∖J, multiplied by z_S, equals f_U times the same product on J with factor k^(|J|/2) independent of S.

If k≥3, all |J|≥2 sector blocks of P are positive definite: even |J|≥2 use W_J≥−I and divisor (k−1)^|J|>1; odd |J|≥3 use λ_max(W_J)≤k−1< (k−1)^|J|. Therefore the indicator is an additive function of label choices on every cell, with each group function shared globally. An additive function into {0,1} has at most one nonconstant summand on a cell: varying two attains a spread of at least two, since each nonzero difference with others fixed is an integer. A cell with no active group must have indicator zero, because indicator one would include two disjoint complete label assignments in that nonempty support. If two groups were active globally, choose cells for each; a cell cannot contain both, and so neither active group is shared. Choose a selected label assignment in each, then make every common inactive group take different labels, obtaining a disjoint pair. Thus there is just one active group. In cells containing it the accepted label set is nonempty and proper. It can contain at most one label, since otherwise choosing two such labels and differing on all other groups gives a disjoint pair within a single cell. It is the same single label throughout: cells are additive with a shared coordinate function and both values 0 and 1 must occur, which fixes the cell constant. This proves strictness for k≥3.

## Binary case: decomposition into identical-incidence classes
Now k=2. Partitions into binary labels use coordinates x_i in {−1,1}. Define g_S(x)=1_A(S,x)−1_A(S,−x). The two faces are disjoint, so g_S∈{−1,0,1} and 1_A(S,x)=1 if and only if g_S(x)=1. Only odd Fourier sectors contribute to g. For any odd J, a nonzero kernel requires an eigenvector of W_J with eigenvalue 1. For each i∈J, extend this vector by zero from supports containing J to those containing i. It attains the top Rayleigh quotient 1 of W_i and thus is a multiple of its unique top vector z_i, with every coordinate strictly positive. It follows all supports containing i contain J, for every i∈J. Equivalently J lies in one class C of groups occurring in exactly the same set of completed positive support cells. The same argument shows any contributing coefficient is shared across all cells containing C, after undoing f_U (the factors explained above depend only on J). Hence there are globally defined odd functions g_C of the labels in each such class, and on every support S:
g_S(x)=sum_{C subset S} g_C(x_C).
Every class either lies wholly in the support or is disjoint from it. An active class means g_C≠0. Fix a containing support and vary labels of just that class: differences in g_C are integers. Oddness implies that every value of g_C is either an integer, or every value is a nonintegral half-integer. Since all class labels vary independently and each odd summand attains its positive maximum, the sum of the active class amplitudes in every support is at most 1. Hence each active integer class has amplitude exactly 1 and is the sole active class in every containing support. Each active nonintegral half-integer class has amplitude 1/2, takes only ±1/2 equally often, and always occurs with exactly one other half-integer class: a single such term gives a noninteger g, and three exceed amplitude 1.

If two supports have disjoint nonempty sets of active classes, pick assignments giving +1 for both, which exist for either integer or paired half-integer types. All groups common to both supports are in classes inactive in both, so choose differing labels on every common group. The resulting two selected faces would be disjoint, impossible. Consequently if any integer class is active it is the only active class; otherwise all are half-integer and their active pairs form a graph whose edges are pairwise intersecting. Give each edge the total completed cell weight over supports with its active pair. Every active class has weighted degree 2M because its group has per-label star weight M, and the whole cell sums over its two labels. A simple graph of pairwise-intersecting edges either has a common vertex or consists of a triangle (if no common vertex, take edges ab, ac and an edge not containing a; it is bc and no other edge is compatible). With positive equal weighted degree, a common-vertex graph has only one edge; thus the total number t of active half classes is at most three. Each active support selects one quarter of its faces, because its two half signs are independent and both must be positive. Total active-cell weight is tM by weighted degree counting, so selection has weight tM/4<M. Therefore the half-integer case is impossible.

With exactly one active integer class C, every containing support selects orientations with g_C=+1, and others select none. Whole cell weights through C sum to 2M, so weight M forces g_C=±1 everywhere (oddness then gives exactly half positive). In other words all maximizers here choose one orientation from each antipodal pair of binary labelings on a single incidence class. This class contains only centers with original maximum star weight: if any group in C had a smaller original weight, singleton completion gives that group a singleton cell in which no other member of a class of size>1 occurs; a size-one class yields a usual star and its weight in the original is M. For a class of size at least two all its support incidences are therefore identical already in the original. An orientation on one or two binary groups necessarily selects a single fixed label in one coordinate: for two groups there are two selected assignments, and noncomplementary distinct assignments agree in exactly one coordinate. If there are at least three such identical-incidence groups at maximum star weight, taking the majority of three specified binary labels provides a nonstar optimum: any two accepted assignments agree on a positive label somewhere among the three, and exactly half of the assignments are accepted; none of those three labels is fixed, nor is any other coordinate fixed. This is precisely the Borg strictness exception for unit weights. Intersecting families containing singletons, treated first, are also stars.

The classification makes no claim for arbitrary deleted assignments inside a cell or for general spectral Chvátal H. We work with nonempty supports as in Borg’s original convention. Any zero-weight cells can be dropped for the weighted classification on positive vertices.

Prior context: [Borg 2007, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2](https://staff.um.edu.mt/peter.borg/publications/v14i1r41.pdf#page=4) already proved the antipodal description when the optimum saturates each of a prescribed group of binary cells, including cells whose supports are known to all contain a fixed base element. The argument above derives that support structure for arbitrary optimal families, rather than assuming saturation or a common base element. The two-label majority construction is the classical exception; the new assertion is necessity in arbitrary complete signed systems.
