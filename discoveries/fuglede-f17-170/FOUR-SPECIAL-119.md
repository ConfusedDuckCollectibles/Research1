# No Boolean 119-point shadow with exactly four special directions

Here a special direction is the projective normal of a parallel line family whose populations are not uniformly seven. The result is a direct exact replay of a narrow consequence of Qihang Wang, Four Special Directions over Prime Fields, Table 3 (https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20495v2#S7), with no reliance on the classification. The applications to size 170 in F17³ are in the [main proof](PROOF.md).

Choose linear coordinates so the four normals are represented by L=(x,y,y−x,y−rx), r∈F17∖{0,1}. If V is the set's indicator and each g_i is its line count profile, the usual Radon incidence equality is sum_i g_i(L_i)=21+17V. Each profile has values 0,…,17 and total 119. Modulo 17 each g_i is represented by polynomial degree at most 16; for a homogeneous degree j≥3 the four forms L_i^j are independent. Indeed the three coefficients involving positive powers of y and x give the rank of the finite-slope columns 0,1,r by normalized binomial coefficients and Vandermonde, after which the x^j column is also independent (use coefficients x^k y^(j−k) for k=0,1,2, which all have y exponent≥1 for j≥3). Thus degree ≤2. If no quadratic exists then no nonconstant affine profile can have mass 119, since its least residues sum to 136. Some nonuniform constant then has zero residue (nonzero counts are uniquely determined), making the indicator a union of full lines and leaving only one special normal. Hence there is a quadratic; comparing x²,xy,y² forces leading tuple lambda times (r(1−r),r−1,−r,1), all nonzero. The two linear identities cut out a kernel of dimension two. The two coordinate translations add precisely the rank-two family (2a_i L_i(v))_i in that kernel, as follows from the quadratic identity. Thus translate to simultaneous centered quadratics.

For each leading class (scale the argument so the lead is 1 for square, 3 for nonsquare), test constants c=0,…,16 in ax²+c. The only lifts of least residues are at roots where count may be 17 rather than 0. Exact integer enumeration of the 17 values gives the table below, showing c followed by the sum of squared deviations from 7 divided by 34. A star denotes that either one of two root locations may be lifted, giving the same variance.

Square: (1,15), (2,14)*, (3,12), (4,11)*, (5,9), (9,9).
Nonsquare: (7,14), (11,14), (12,13)*, (13,11), (14,10)*, (15,8).

Different normal families' centered profiles are orthogonal over the uniform planar grid because two distinct linear forms are independent uniform coordinates. Squaring the Radon identity centered around its mean gives sum of the four integer deviation totals =17*7*10=1190, i.e. 35 normalized units: the left across the planar grid is 17 times the four deviation totals, and the right is 17 squared times sum of (V−7/17)^2=17²*(70). The product of the four leads is lambda^4*r²*(r−1)², so the nonsquare multiplicity is even. With zero nonsquares, minimum 4*9=36. With two, minimum 2*9+2*8=34, but no single profile can add one unit. With four the minimum is 32; the only way to add three units is one nonsquare with 11 and three with 8. Their constants sum to 13+3*15=58≡7 mod 17, whereas Radon requires 21≡4. Contradiction.

The executable verifier enumerates all relative r and lambda, admissible zero lifts, and independently tests these two filters and Boolean realizability. Run `node verify-four-special.cjs 7` for the exclusion, and use arguments `8` or `9` for positive controls. There are eight labeled centered profiles per nonzero lead for mass119, 96 variance quadruples and zero constants. n=8 and n=9 each have 11 profiles per lead, 7296 variance tuples and 24 Boolean configurations, in line with the known class (different triples of parameters can be affine-equivalent).
