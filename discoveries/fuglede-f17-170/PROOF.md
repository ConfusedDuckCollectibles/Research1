# Excluding spectral sets of size 170 in F_17^3

Proof and exact replay, 26 November 2026. **Theorem: there is no spectral pair of cardinality 170 in F_17^3.** Together with the earlier 204 exclusion and the standard prime-field/arithmetic ledger, the remaining intermediate sizes are 34, 68, 102, 136, and 153. This is not a full F17 theorem, is not yet externally reviewed, and makes no priority claim.

## 1. Standard geometry and the 4-fold blocking cutoff

Let p=17,m=10 and N=pm=170. Treat a spectral pair (E,A) as subsets of V and V*, respectively, paired by evaluation. Write D(E) and D(A) for directions of nonzero differences and U(E),U(A) for uniform normal directions (all parallel planes have m points). The square character matrix is orthogonal and its transpose is orthogonal as well. Thus D(A) is contained in U(E), and \(D(E)\subseteq U(A)\). Fourier zero at a nonzero character is equivalent to uniformity by the rational minimal polynomial Phi_p of a primitive pth root; in particular the nonzero Fourier zero set is closed under scaling by nonzero field elements.

The standard line/plane argument is [Fallon–Mayeli–Villano](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.02936) and [Ferguson–Iosevich Lemma 8.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1). The sum of occupancies of the p+1 planes through an affine line with a points is mp+pa. A full uniform pencil would require pa=m, impossible; and a plane with >p points determines every tangent direction by pigeonholing into p parallel affine lines, wrongly producing a full uniform pencil of the partner. Thus plane cap p. Given any affine line direction d, the partner has >p points, two of which have the same value on d; their difference is a uniform normal of a plane through that line. The pencil occupancy inequality is mp+pa≤m+p², giving line cap seven.

Suppose the projective nonuniform set S_E of E is 4-fold blocking (every projective line contains at least four). Take an inclusion-minimal 4-fold blocking B in S_E, b=|B|. The primary [Bishnoi–Mattheus–Schillewaert Theorem 1.1](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v25i4p66), with n=17,t=4, gives b≤(17 sqrt(233)+59)/2<160, hence b≤159. Exact strictness is 17²*233=67337<261²=68121. Use [Malikiosis §6 Eq. (6.2)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43670-023-00078-2#Sec6): the radial h on V* equals b−4p at zero, one on nonzero vectors of directions in B, zero elsewhere. Its Fourier transform is p(|B∩y^perp|−4) for y≠0 and p(b−4) at zero, and is nonnegative. Because every different pair in A has difference direction outside B, Fourier inversion gives

    |A| h(0) = sum_{a,a' in A} h(a−a') >= |A|² hhat(0)/p³,
    |A| <= p² (b−4p)/(b−4) <= 289*91/155 = 26299/155 <170.

The quotient increases with b>4. This rules out a 4-fold blocker; it does not apply the unconditional Malikiosis Theorem 1.4 at 170. Interchanging E,A gives the same conclusion for the other side. Each now has a pencil with at least 15 uniform normals.

## 2. Both-side layering and perpendicular linkage

For any affine line in the annihilator of a pencil of fifteen uniform normals, its a points satisfy 170+17a≤15*10+3*17=201, hence a≤1. Projection along the annihilator is injective; its Boolean planar shadow Q has size 170. The complement Q' has mass 119=7p and the same uniform normals, so at most three are exceptional.

For later use, a Boolean planar set of size 119 with at most three exceptional normals must be the union of seven parallel lines. Make a uniform normal vertical and let L≤3 be the number of exceptional finite slopes s_i. The usual integer Radon identity, as in [Ferguson–Iosevich Lemma 8.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1), is

    sum_i g_i(y−s_i x)=7(L−1)+17*1_Q'(x,y),

where g_i(t) are the line populations of the exceptional directions. The sum counts all points once and the reference point 18 times, and then drops the uniform contributions. Reduce modulo 17 and represent g_i by degree≤16 polynomials. The positive homogeneous coefficients imply sum_i c_(i,j)*s_i^h=0 for 0≤h≤j; Vandermonde kills all positive degrees j≥L−1, so degree≤max(0,L−2)≤1. A nonconstant affine function has least residues summing to 136, too many for mass119. A constant nonzero residue gives equal actual counts in [0,17] and hence uniformity. Therefore an exceptional profile has residue zero and counts only 0 or17, displaying seven full lines. There is at least one exceptional direction, since all-normal uniformity would make the indicator constant 7/17 by the full Radon identity. Consequently Q is ten full parallel lines and E is contained in ten parallel affine planes with exactly 17 points per plane, using injectivity and plane cap (or the pullback directly). Similarly A has ten full layers.

A second finite gate gives a sharper tangent-direction count than the general [Rédei–Megyesi theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00013-024-01979-x): every 17-point planar set with line cap seven over F17 has at least 14 directions. If five directions were missing, choose five; normalize three of them by PGL to 0,1,infinity. The four possible orbit representatives of 105 normalized masks are in the verifier. Vertical missing permits a unique graph over the whole field. Normalize its height at 0 to zero, and enumerate exact line-cap-seven graphs compatible with each mask. The four recursion counts are 17,670 / 16,126 / 15,309 / 14,710, with no complete graphs; the separately coded search had different traversal counts, likewise zero. The included four-missing positive controls show this tangent-direction statement is sharp within its stated scope.

Choose any layer normal n_A in V for A. Because n_A is not uniform on A, it is not a direction in D(E); projection of E along n_A is injective. In the resulting quotient plane the dual is the tangent direction plane of A's layers. One full A layer supplies at least 14 directions which are uniform normals on the E projection. Thus its Boolean complement Q' of size119 has at most four exceptional normals. The exactly-four case is impossible: [Wang 2609.20495v2, Table 3 and complements](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20495v2#S7) gives at p=17 only sizes 136 and153, not119 or170. A standalone exact recheck of the specific 119-point obstruction is in [the shadow lemma](FOUR-SPECIAL-119.md) and `verify-four-special.cjs`; there is no need to assume the full Wang classification beyond this single corroborating scope. By the at-most-three proof above, Q' is seven parallel lines, so the E shadow is ten full parallel lines. Pulling this back gives a layer normal n_E of E in the annihilator of n_A, i.e. n_E(n_A)=0. Because n_E is nonuniform for E, A projects injectively along n_E. This works if an original pair of layer normals was already perpendicular or not, and assumes neither uniqueness nor an ordinary Euclidean rotation.

Take a basis whose second vector is n_A, first vector independent of it in ker n_E, and third vector evaluated to one by n_E, and its paired dual basis. The two injectivities give

    E={(x,f_s(x),s): x in F_17, s in S},
    A={(u,t,g_t(u)): u in F_17, t in T},   |S|=|T|=10.

Each graph has line cap seven. An affine function would put its 17 points on a line, impossible.

## 3. Cross charts have at most nine finite slopes

Let zeta be a primitive 17th root and define H_s(r)=sum_x zeta^(r*x+f_s(x)). Call r active when some H_s(r) is nonzero. The 17-point direction lemma on any single E layer gives ≥14 active r: in graph coordinates all determined slopes are finite, and slope −r is missing exactly when f_s(x)+r*x is a permutation, equivalently H_s(r)=0 by Phi_17.

If (r,1,w) is proportional to any cross-layer difference in A, the spectral condition makes sum_s H_s(r)*zeta^(w*s)=0. For an active r this is the Fourier transform on F_17 of a nonzero complex vector supported on at most ten positions. [Tao's prime cyclic uncertainty theorem / Chebotarev](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0308286) bounds its number of zeros by nine. Thus at each active r there is a fixed (possibly padded) palette of nine finite slopes containing every such cross-layer w. For any x, the particular ten points of A with u=x+r*t are identified with the graph (t,g_t(x+r*t)) for t in T. It has line cap seven and all its pair slopes belong to this same r-palette, regardless of x.

## 4. Exact atlas and a fixed pair force a contradiction

The certificate is for a given abscissa set T (size ten) and allowed palette of nine finite slopes, with all heights normalized by subtracting height at one fixed abscissa of T. Affine transformations (t,y)->(a*t+b,a*y) preserve slopes and reduce all 19,448 T to 75 classes. Independently, (t,y)->(t,c*y+d*t) with c nonzero induces any affine slope permutation without moving abscissae; the 24,310 complements of the finite palettes reduce to 95 classes. Thus the Cartesian product suffices. Normalize the first height to zero. The DFS assigns a height to one abscissa at a time, excluding exactly forbidden slope pairs and any violation of the specified line cap (an eighth point on a nonvertical line in these runs). Empty future height domain is the only other pruning; the smallest-domain choice changes order, not coverage. A separate exact fibre check validates every emitted full vector.

The complete ten-domain atlas has 632,898 recursion nodes. There are nine nonempty cells on nine distinct abscissa classes, and ten height vectors (eight cells are singletons, one contains exactly two). The two-height cell is on T=[0,1,2,3,4,5,8,10,12,13], normalized forbidden finite palette [0,1,2,3,4,5,7,15]. Its normalized heights, in that T order, are

    [0,8,1,7,13,6,3,15,5,16],
    [0,14,9,8,7,2,3,1,4,15].

They have common height differences on pairs (0,8) and (12,13). Every singleton cell has a fixed difference on every pair. In particular the property needed is stronger than having a fixed pair in each separate cell: for any given T with any feasible palette, one pair is fixed across all feasible normalized palette cells for that T. Here there happens to be only one such palette class per admissible T. The verifier also checks this common-pair property directly.

For a hypothetical pair, choose one abscissa normalization once for T, the same for every active r. Normalize each r-palette independently by a height-only affine transformation (plus linear shear), not moving the already normalized abscissae. Consequently it is the same pair t,t' of original labels for every active r. For each r separately its difference g_t(x+r*t)−g_t'(x+r*t') is constant in x. Applying an inverse affine height normalization preserves constancy; the linear shear contributes a constant for the fixed t,t'. Comparing two distinct active r gives, after writing y=x+r*t, that g_t'(z+d)−g_t'(z) is constant in z, with nonzero d equal to (r_1−r_2)*(t'−t) up to sign. Iterating translation by d generates the whole prime field; hence g_t' is affine. This contradicts line cap seven and excludes size170.

As an optional distinct endgame check, the atlas also gives at most two normalized rows and affine rank at most one on every cell. Thus each shifted positive-degree coefficient matrix has rank at most two after restoring the common coordinate. Nonconstant degree≤8 has image at least three; any pair of graph functions of degree≤8 would yield constant shifted differences at all active r and the same derivative contradiction. Hence at least nine of the ten degree-reduced polynomials have degree≥9. At most two distinct positive degrees can occur by the rank bound, so at least five share a degree d. Choose any three of them and leading coefficient rows d,d−1,d−2. The three-by-three determinant in r has degree≤3, with degree-three coefficient the product of the three leading coefficients and the nonzero binomials binom(d,0..2), times the Vandermonde in their distinct t's. It could not vanish at all ≥14 active r as required. This optional argument does not use the special fixed pairs or distinct abscissa fitting observed by the atlas.
