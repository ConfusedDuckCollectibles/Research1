# Independent audit: three-snapshot entropy-production floor

**Disposition.** The bound is correct, strict for every finite dissipative generator in its stated class, exact for the relaxed latent-product problem, and asymptotically sharp as a universal multiplicative inequality. It improves Petkov’s exact CP-deletion floor on these three readings (not on richer information). The closest earlier ingredients are more substantial than a parallel methodology: Skinner–Dunkel already formulate the identical two-marginal variational problem and give its balanced \(s=1/2\) formula; Petkov already gives the precise scalar affinity function and its strict convexity. A carefully attributed technical note is warranted for the full equal-marginal closed form, proof over all product mixtures, explicit same-input quantitative improvement and limiting construction. Not a new general thermodynamic uncertainty relation, priority claim, experiment, device, or population-to-sample inference procedure.

## Conditioning and normalization

Fix a finite stationary Markov jump process with row generator \(Q\), stationary weights \(\pi_i>0\) on the support, reciprocal allowed rates, and ordinary all-even reversal. The reverse generator has \(q^*_{ij}=\pi_jq_{ji}/\pi_i\) and the same exit rate by stationarity. At microscopic midpoint \(X_0=i\), future and outward-read past are independent, with respective generators \(Q,Q^*\). Apply the same endpoint event to both half-paths: whether the visible endpoint differs from \(h(i)\). The observed conditional two-endpoint block is a mixture of Bernoulli products over the posterior weight \(\pi_i/p_z\), with \(z=h(i)\); the two endpoints need not be conditionally independent given \(z\) alone.

Stationarity makes the row and column marginals of the two-time binary table agree. With two categories this forces its two off-diagonal entries to be equal, hence both adjacent endpoint-event probabilities are the same conditional mean \(s=s_z\). This does not assume detailed balance, or even time symmetry of the full visible process. The joint probability \(r=r_z\) of both endpoint events is distinct from the probability of changing twice or continuously observing uninterrupted residence. Even numbers of intervening changes are permitted.

For either direction of conditional path KL over the half-window \(t\), the holding factors cancel. Averaging over \(\pi\) yields \(t\sigma\) in each direction, since the reversed stationary flows transpose. Here
\[
 \sigma=\sum_{i<j}(\pi_iq_{ij}-\pi_jq_{ji})
 \log\frac{\pi_iq_{ij}}{\pi_jq_{ji}}
 =\sum_{i\ne j}\pi_iq_{ij}\log\frac{\pi_iq_{ij}}{\pi_jq_{ji}}.
\]
Either displayed sum counts each undirected edge once in the net-current convention. Applying binary endpoint data processing in both directions gives \(t\sigma\ge\sum_i\pi_i J_{\mathrm{Bern}}(u_i,v_i)/2\); the full record spans \(2t\), not \(t\). Physical units multiply by \(k_B\); a thermodynamic interpretation of the transition process requires the appropriate local detailed-balance resolution. One-way support with positive stationary mass instead yields infinite path entropy production; that case does not create a finite nontrivial comparison.

**Strict finite-rate result.** If \(\sigma>0\), some directed cycle has a nonzero sum of stationary log affinities \(a_{ij}=\log(q_{ij}/q^*_{ij})\). Otherwise the antisymmetric field \(a\) is a gradient on each component (define a potential by a path sum), and stationarity makes its flow-weighted sum zero. Start at a vertex of a nonzero-affinity cycle. Both the no-jump path and paths making exactly that one cycle have positive mass under both half-path laws over any \(t>0\). All end at the starting microscopic state, hence have identical observed endpoint bit; their likelihood ratios are respectively 1 and \(\exp(\sum a_{ij})\ne1\). The likelihood ratio is therefore not constant on an endpoint fiber. Equality in binary data processing fails: the conditional-on-fiber KL in the chain rule is strictly positive. This initial vertex has positive stationary mass, so \(\sigma>\sum_z p_z B(s_z,r_z)/t\) if \(\sigma>0\). At detailed balance the bound and \(\sigma\) both vanish. Petkov Proposition G.1 proves a related strictness statement for his weaker CP floor; the endpoint-fiber argument here directly proves strictness of the refined floor.

## Exact Bernoulli-product cost

Write the latent table entries as \(a=(1-u)(1-v)\), \(r_i=uv\), \(b=u(1-v)\), \(c=v(1-u)\) and \(w=b+c\). The product constraint gives \(bc=ar_i\). The Jeffreys cost \(J/2=(b-c)\log(b/c)/2\) equals \(w\phi(2\sqrt{ar_i}/w)\), where
\[
 \phi(x)=y\operatorname{atanh}y,\qquad y=\sqrt{1-x^2}.
\]
For \(0<x<1\),
\[
 \phi'(x)=-x\operatorname{atanh}(y)/y-1/x,\quad
 \phi''(x)=\{y/(1-y^2)-\operatorname{atanh}(y)\}/y^3>0.
\]
The reason for strict convexity is the positive integral gap between \(y/(1-y^2)\) and \(\int_0^y (1-v^2)^{-1}\,dv\). This exact function and curvature are credited to [Petkov Appendix F, (98)–(99)](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00658v1), which treats the probability-pair affinity Jensen inequality. Here the weighted Jensen step retains the weight of discordant entries separately, rather than mixing affinity with the two agreed outcomes.

Write \(W=\mathbb E w=2(s-r)\) and \(A=\mathbb E a=1-2s+r\). Weight by \(w/W\) to apply Jensen and bound \(\mathbb E\sqrt{ar_i}\le\sqrt{Ar}\) by Cauchy–Schwarz. The lower cost is \(W\phi(\min(1,2\sqrt{Ar}/W))\). When \(W=0\) all constituent \(w=0\), so the optimal cost is zero. The determinant identity \((s-r)^2-Ar=s^2-r\) gives the closed form. For \(g=s^2-r>0\), the two equiprobable pairs \( (u,v)=(s+\sqrt g,s-\sqrt g)\) and \((v,u)\) realize both inequalities with equality; the roots lie in \([0,1]\) since \(r\ge0,A\ge0\). For \(g\le0\) and \(s>0\), set \(u=v=r/s\) with probability \(s^2/r\), zero otherwise. The case \(s=0\) is all zero. The boundary \(g>0\) and \(Ar=0\) necessarily has infinite cost; clipping one of the zero entries would alter the stated variational problem. Strictly positive finite-lag probabilities in active irreducible components avoid that endpoint whenever the physical bound is positive. No branch infers equilibrium from a zero floor.

## Same-data CP comparison, including all matched common classifiers

For full half information encode each of the two half-records by its ordered pair \((Z(0),Z(\pm t))\), equivalently \((Z(0),\mathbf 1_{Z(\pm t)\ne Z(0)})\). The symmetrized observed matrix on four categories is block diagonal, with \(z\)-block \(p_z\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&s-r\\s-r&r\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). For nonnegative \(2\times2\) symmetric matrices, complete positivity is equivalent to positive semidefiniteness (e.g. nonnegative Cholesky columns with the zero-case separated). Any CP minorant has offdiagonal at most the geometric mean of the available diagonals; keep both diagonals and lower the offdiagonal to exactly that cap when needed. Zero cross-block entries force every admissible minorant to share the block structure. Thus Petkov’s exact global deletion deficit is \(\sum_z p_z d_z\), with \(d_z=[2(s-r-\sqrt{Ar})]_+\). His floor is \(2F(\sum_z p_zd_z)/t\) for \(F(v)=\sqrt v\operatorname{atanh}\sqrt v\), not \(F/t\) or \(4F/t\).

For a violating block \(x=2\sqrt{Ar}/W<1\), then \(d=W(1-x)\), \(B=WF(1-x^2)\), and \(W(1+x)\le1\). Equality in the last inequality occurs exactly when \(A=r\), i.e. \(s=1/2\). Put \(W_*=1/(1+x)\), \(d_*=(1-x)/(1+x)\). Since \(\sqrt{1-x^2}=2\sqrt{d_*}/(1+d_*)\), the double-angle identity gives \(W_*F(1-x^2)=2F(d_*)\). Convexity with \(F(0)=0\) shows
\[
 2F(d)\le \frac{W}{W_*}\,2F(d_*)=B.
\]
The inequality is strict for a finite positive-defect block with \(s\ne1/2\), since \(F(v)=\sum_{k\ge1}v^k/(2k-1)\) is strictly convex. A second Jensen inequality gives \(\sum p_z B\ge \sum p_z\,2F(d_z)\ge 2F(\sum p_zd_z)\). The proposed bound is positive on exactly the same feasible tables as the full four-category CP test, but quantifies the cost more strongly. It also dominates the same-triplet Pinsker floor, from \(J/2\ge2(u-v)^2\) and the relaxed two-atom optimizer.

The comparison extends to **every Petkov Theorem 4.1 classifier using precisely this triplet and this same two-half decomposition**, provided the same rule is applied on each outward-read half and auxiliary randomness, if any, is independent between halves and of the process conditional on each category. A common finite rule is a row-stochastic matrix \(K\) from the full half-category to the final output. It sends the symmetrized observed matrix to \(K^T S K\). For every feasible CP minorant \(0\le G\le S\), \(K^TGK\) is CP, is dominated entrywise by \(K^TSK\), and retains the same total mass because \(K\mathbf1=\mathbf1\). Thus its deletion deficit cannot increase. This is Petkov Eq. (18) and Appendix H, not a new contraction theorem. When there is strict improvement over the full classifier there is strict improvement over all these garblings as well. This does not rank waiting times, unobserved/no-jump events, longer motifs, more times, other partitions of a longer record, mixed/past-dependent classifiers, or independent non-CP lower bounds. Do not conflate common classification of deterministic labels with coupled detector noise. For any proposed extension with randomized sensing, stationarity and identical endpoint and midpoint readout channels (or explicit symmetrization) must be justified anew.

## Sharp limiting example and independent results

For four-cycle with labels 0011, alternate edge intensities \(H,k,H,k\) and bias every clockwise edge by \(+j\), every counterclockwise edge by \(-j\). Uniform stationary law follows from each row and column sum being zero. The label-preserving state permutation \(i\mapsto1-i\bmod4\) maps \(Q\) to \(Q^T\); this proves reversibility of the entire stationary observed process, not just its three readings. It is not a declaration of physical odd-variable reversal. The half-turn maps each visible block to the other and preserves the generator. In each block the latent pairs \((u,v),(v,u)\) occur with equal weights, hence \(s=(u+v)/2,r=uv\) and the product-cost step is attained exactly. The actual entropy rate is \(j\log[(k+j)/(k-j)]+j\log[(H+j)/(H-j)]\).

Uniformly when \((H+k)t\to0\), a direct semigroup norm bound gives
\[
 (u/t,v/t)=(k+j,k-j)+O((H+k)^2t).
\]
Indeed \(\|Q\|_\infty=2(H+k)\), and the remainder of \(e^{tQ}\) after its linear term is bounded by \(O(\|tQ\|_\infty^2)\). Take \(k=1,j=1/2,t=H^{-3}\). Then the nonlinear bound tends to the finite positive crossing term \(j\log[(k+j)/(k-j)]\), while the hidden-edge term vanishes. The ratio tends to 1 despite strictness for each positive \(t,H\). A positive fixed lag is achieved by rescaling all rates, not by bounding traffic or fixing nondegenerate observed probabilities. No data-by-data exact CTMC optimum is established. Separately, with fixed reciprocal \(k,j,H\) and \(t\downarrow0\), the same-input CP floor tends to \(4(k-\sqrt{k^2-j^2})\), whereas the new floor tends to \(j\log[(k+j)/(k-j)]\). Choosing \(j\uparrow k\) and then sufficiently small finite \(t\) at each \(j\) gives arbitrarily large ratio over CP. This uses distinct limits, not a uniform small-lag expansion up to a vanishing backward rate.

Independent [independent.py](independent.py) integrates the signed matrix series for \(e^{tQ}\) by exact fractions with induced-\(\ell_\infty\) tail, not the producer’s uniformization. It computes \(Q^*\) separately, tests weighted stationarity, and evaluates the conditional triplets and each dimensionless entropy and lower-bound expression with rational square-root and logarithm tails. The rare finite case certified \(3.45679138911<B/t<3.45679138913\), while the CP floor lies in \((2.73865902408,2.73865902410)\), true rate about \(3.66142952745\). The moderate case agrees independently. Additional checks allow unequal visible masses \((2/3,1/3)\) with the singleton block exactly zero, an independent driven factor with positive true EP and zero binary Markov-readout witness, the reversible equilibrium, and rational latent optimizer moments. Exact tested ratios for \(H=10,100,1000\) on the sharp family are approximately \(0.896143944806,0.990759912084,0.999088376451\). See [replay.log](replay.log); run `python3 independent.py`.

## Close prior and what may be claimed

- **Skinner–Dunkel [PNAS 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2024300118), [author full supplement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.08765), Eqs. (A18)–(A20):** after normalization, the identical latent moments and objective appear explicitly: prescribed \(\sum\lambda,\sum\lambda u,\sum\lambda v,\sum\lambda uv\) and \(\sum\lambda J(u,v)\). Our objective has the factor \(1/2\) required by half-path averaging. Their Eqs. (A28)–(A29) already give the exact same balanced \(s=1/2\) functional form via a symmetric two-state ansatz (and numerical general comparison there). In their notation \(p=n_{ABC}=1-2r\), \(\sqrt{2p-1}=2\sqrt g\); their local cost is \(4B(1/2,r)\) because the fixed flow mass is 2 and our cost is half Jeffreys. Their “Coarse graining in time” deductions (A39)–(A50) explicitly reduce finite sampling to a sampled-chain entropy floor; it is incorrect to say all uses of their method require continuously resolved transitions.
- In fact their local two-step framework applies to the same mathematical relaxation: augment the sampled chain \(P=e^{tQ}\) with a reversible alternating tick parity, using \(P_{\rm aug}((i,a),(j,1-a))=P_{ij}\), and Poissonize it at rate \(1/t\). This is an auxiliary process, not a claim that the original holding times were observed. Its stationary weights \(\pi_i/2\) give transition flow \(\pi_iP_{ij}/(2t)\) per parity. Its entropy equals the sampled chain’s entropy per unit time, which is bounded by the original path entropy. Both sampled “stay” and “change” now correspond to observed jump categories in the enlarged four-label system, without using added data. Each midpoint macrostate has two opposite-parity destinations. Aggregate the incoming/outgoing fluxes from either destination macrostate at a hidden midpoint into \(F_i(v_i,1-v_i)\) and \(F_i(u_i,1-u_i)\). The consecutive category counts are the associated weighted outer products and the incident entropy cost is \(\sum_i F_i J\) after log-sum. Summing the per-midpoint lower bounds with factor \(1/2\), because every edge is incident to two endpoint macrostate blocks, yields the same finite-lag result once the general equal-marginal local minimum is evaluated. Thus do not claim a new variational principle or the first use of two-step finite-time observation. The full \(s\ne1/2\) closed form and its Jensen proof were not found in those prior formulas.
- **Petkov [author circulation, Theorem 4.1 and Appendix F/H/J.5](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00658v1):** earlier path-middle framework, identical scalar affinity inequality, CP deletion with broader motif scope, garbling theorem, exact strictness for CP, and homogeneous ring with the same label-preserving reversal reflection. His J.5 comparator uses the resolved no-switch event, not these raw snapshots. The Hexagon ID should not be used as an arXiv ID.
- **Dobrynin–Stroujkova [Nov. 18, 2026, S5.3 and S8.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24898):** earlier midpoint/Pinsker floor for uninterrupted visible persistence, a finite-lag spectral sign test and related rate asymptotics. Their Section S5.2 already gives the same crossing contribution under the symmetric short-time model; no infinitesimal-coefficient novelty is claimed.
- **Anonymous [alternating-renewal notes](https://evidencepress.org/releases/hidden-dissipation-floor/), [exact-signal note](https://www.evidencepress.org/releases/hidden-architecture-exact-signal/):** respectively uninterrupted-residence mutual information and an entrance/short-dwell affinity formula. These are very close goals but do not provide the finite \(B(s,r)\) envelope on three snapshots. [Raux–Maier–Seifert](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03619v2) bounds ordered-correlation asymmetry, which vanishes on the reversible control. [Cao–Xiao–Wang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28874) uses binary divergence of explicitly time-reversed tasks, a prior data-processing tactic not an optimizer of the mirrored two-moment table. [Ehrich](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08803) addresses compatible-process optimizations using richer masked observations. No general priority conclusion follows from this focused search.
