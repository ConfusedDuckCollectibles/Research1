# When stellar velocities cannot see an unresolved dark-matter core

This proof constructs two idealized spherical galaxies with the same pattern of starlight. One has a dark-matter density cusp; the other has a smooth central core. Outside the projected core radius, they give exactly the same distribution of all three stellar velocity components. This holds even for perfect velocity measurements there, not just equal averages or dispersions.

The proof also gives a sampling bound. In one explicit design with stars drawn throughout the projected half-light aperture, a core extending to 5% of that radius needs **at least 5,566 stars** to reach 80% detection power at 5% false-positive rate. That is a lower bound for those models and that design, not a sample-size recommendation for any real galaxy. Targeting the center changes it.

A separate annular contrast uses both proper-motion components to distinguish a *resolved* cusp from a core with independently bounded smoothness in the same spherical steady framework. Neither result determines what dark matter is or what happens in an observed dwarf.
