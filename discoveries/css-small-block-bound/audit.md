# Independent audit: finite CSS size bound with small-block exceptions

**Disposition.** The stated theorem, logical distances, and eight controls are correct. A narrow technical note is reasonable if it presents the result as an elementary combination of known binary/CSS ideas, emphasizes the exception term and the intrinsic projected rank test below, and does not claim a new qLDPC, geometry, or distance benchmark. The broad pair contraction and complementary-support identity are not new. A novelty claim stronger than "this specific finite certificate was not located in the sources checked" is unsupported.

## 1. Block quotient and the two distance metrics

Let $U=\mathrm{row}(H_X)$, $V=\mathrm{row}(H_Z)\subseteq U^\perp$. The intrinsic partition has a one-line characterization: two coordinates share a block iff $e_i+U=e_j+U$ in the quotient $\mathbb F_2^n/U$. Thus the graph computed from intrinsic pair membership already has complete components, and all singleton-pinned coordinates share the zero signature. A span basis for $U^\perp$ gives the same signatures using its columns.

Pairs along paths span the even-weight subspace of a block. A singleton pin upgrades it to every vector there, and every vector of $V$ or $U^\perp$ is zero on such a block. Otherwise vectors in $U^\perp$ are constant on that block. Parity projection on X vectors and whole-block expansion on Z vectors are adjoint for the binary dot product. The kernel of X parity projection on the survivors is already inside $U$; this proves the logical X quotient $\ker B/\mathrm{row}(A)$, while adjointness proves the Z quotient $\ker A/\mathrm{row}(B)$. For an X quotient representative the physical minimal lift is one position per active block: odd parity requires one and intrablock pairs attain one without touching other blocks. For a Z quotient representative expansion forces the entire block, hence weight $\sum_i w_i z_i$. These are different metrics and should not be interchanged.

If $\mathrm{row}(B)$ contains the unit vector $e_i$, the corresponding pure, whole-block physical Z check belongs to the original stabilizer span. Every row of $A$ and every X centralizer parity is zero at $i$. The local factor consisting of its X-even checks and all-block Z has rank $w_i$ on $w_i$ qubits, is encoding-zero, and separates: restricted cross-checks can subtract their even local X parts or whole local Z part before removal. This preserves both minimum logical weights and rank dimension. Simultaneous removal of all such columns introduces no new X supports (all their $A$ entries were already zero) and no hidden new Z-fixed unit (lift and separately subtract the deleted fixed units). The same argument justifies removing pinned X blocks. No geometry or actual gate schedule was used. Physical check support/column degree cannot grow under restriction to the surviving physical positions; **compressed** columns can have different degrees, so do not treat that as a bound on the contraction's column degree.

The residual $A$ span has no word of weight 1 or 2: use the original pure X stabilizer whose parity image is such a word and clean its even parts by within-block pairs. A unit would have been pinned; a pair would have joined the two purported blocks. This conclusion uses the original stabilizer span, not the weight of visibly listed rows.

## 2. Complementary support, graph bound, and edge cases

Let $S,L$ partition the surviving blocks by their sizes being below or at least $D$, respectively. Rank-nullity gives
$$
\ell=|S|-\mathrm{rank}(A|_S)-\mathrm{rank}(B)+\mathrm{rank}(B|_L).
$$
Indeed the dimension of $\mathrm{row}(B)\cap V_S$ is the kernel dimension of projection to $L$ on the row space. Applying the complementary calculation to X gives $|L|-\mathrm{rank}(B|_L)-\mathrm{rank}(A)+\mathrm{rank}(A|_S)=k-\ell$. Nothing presumes that cross-checks stay in either region. If $n_S<D$, all physical Z supports there are too small to be logical.

If projected Z constraints on $L$ are generated by singletons and pairs, the pure shortened kernel on $L$ contains exactly the strings constant on each graph component and zero on a component touched by a singleton. Its disjoint indicators span. Their images span the logical quotient of dimension $k-\ell$, so select that many independent nonzero images. Each of their physical minimal X lifts costs its number of blocks, at least $d_X$. The physical sizes of distinct indicators add without overlap and each block costs at least $D$. Consequently $n_L\ge Dd_X(k-\ell)$ and $n\ge n_0+n_S+Dd_X(k-\ell)$. The same argument shows the exact restricted X minimum is the smallest unpinned indicator not in the *full* $\mathrm{row}(A)$, or infinity when none. Indeed a nontrivial sum must include at least one nontrivial indicator, and weight adds on disjoint components.

For the rank relaxation every surviving small column of $A$ must be nonzero: otherwise its weighted unit Z gives a nontrivial physical logical of weight less than $D$, because its unit is not in $\mathrm{row}(B)$. Choose $r_S=\mathrm{rank}(A|_S)$ independent rows *from the given projected presentation*. They cover $S$ and each covers at most $W_X$ small blocks. This gives $|S|\le W_Xr_S$ and then $\ell\le |S|-r_S\le (W_X-1)r_S$. If $W_Z<2D$ all large constraints are units at most and the same exact-minimum result rules out all large-supported X logicals when $d_X\ge2$; hence $\ell=k$. When $A=0$, the no-zero-small-column argument makes $S$ empty. Empty sections, redundant rows, and $W_X=0$ or $W_Z=0$ fit these statements (use 0 as maximum for an empty matrix). The hypothesis $k>0$ is important because global distances would otherwise have no nontrivial representatives. A positive real $D$ also makes the algebra correct, but taking integer $D$ is natural for Hamming distance.

For the point $W_Z=3D$, take three equal repetition blocks, with X-even intrablock constraints and one Z row across all three. After contraction $A=0$, $B=\langle111\rangle$. Nonzero even X strings have min weight 2, while a Z unit is outside $\langle111\rangle$ and costs $D$. This uniformly gives $[[3D,2,(2,D)]]$ for integer $D\ge1$, contradicting a non-strict claim $n\ge4D$ (e.g. $[[9,2,(2,3)]]$). This is not a counterexample when the actual weight-two-projection condition holds.

## 3. Presentation-free projected rank test

Let $K=\mathrm{row}(B|_L)\subseteq\mathbb F_2^{L}$. The main conclusion and restricted-minimum formula hold whenever
$$
\mathrm{span}\{z\in K:\mathrm{wt}(z)\le2\}=K.
$$
Enumerate the $|L|+\binom{|L|}{2}$ nonzero candidates, test membership in $K$ by rank, and then test their rank against $\dim K$. This condition is equivalent to the shortened kernel $K^\perp$ possessing a basis of disjoint nonempty support indicators. Forward: pairs impose equality and units pin components, exactly as above. Reverse: for a disjoint basis $1_{C_j}$, $K$ consists of vectors of even parity on each $C_j$ with arbitrary entries on unused coordinates, so the pairs within each $C_j$ and the units on unused coordinates generate $K$. The graph formulation is thus intrinsically testable on the projected row span. This is a **sufficient condition for the stated bound**, not a necessary condition for the numerical inequality itself. Taking products of displayed checks in this rank test is an algebraic operation, not a claim about achievable physical measurement weights.

Concrete control: four width-six blocks, all pair X stabilizers within blocks and outer $A=0$. Use projected Z rows $1111,0110,0011$, displaying weight 4 in the projection and $W_Z=24\ge3D$ at $D=6$. The same span is generated by the adjacent pairs $1100,0110,0011$ (xor the first displayed row with the last), and the shortened X kernel is the single all-ones vector. Thus the test certifies $n\ge24$ for $[[24,1,(4,6)]]$, exactly saturated. The strict $[[9,2]]$ control above fails even this intrinsic test since its projected span contains no nonzero weight-at-most-two word.

## 4. Exact verification and minor packaging fix

`audit.py` was written independently from the producer. Large distances are certified on the physical matrices by binary meet-in-the-middle: the two halves must match check syndromes but have different signatures against a basis of the opposite stabilizer's orthogonal complement. For any fixed syndrome in the first half, the two best distinct signatures suffice to minimize against an arbitrary forbidden signature from the second half. Small-region logical dimensions for the large examples are also found by solving the physical restricted kernel and taking its image modulo the original Z stabilizer row span. All eight control presentations, plus the basis-obscured $[[24,1,(4,6)]]$, match as applicable. The coupled $[[32,1,(3,12)]]$ works because adding a diagonal X logical forces a nontrivial commuting Z to carry both former Z classes, while a weight-three X logical survives; the direct exact distance check agrees.

An independent exhaustive search enumerated every positive-dimension CSS pair of row spaces through $n=6$, namely 98,780 pairs, and every integer $1\le D\le d_Z$ for those pairs, 133,013 target cases. Of those, 80,806 satisfy the projected-row condition in the tested minimum-weight-first basis presentation. Intrinsic quotient, both weights, rank-complement formula, rank relaxation, the restricted exact minimum and all applicable bounds passed. This is a stress test, not an empirical proof or a sweep of all generating presentations.

The replay reports both the physical bound and the bound after removing encoding-zero factors. The 21-qubit product-factor control distinguishes these two numbers: 21 and 18.

## 5. Primary comparison and novelty judgment

- [Haah–Preskill, Lemma 2 Eq. (3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.3529) already give exactly the oriented CSS complementary-support dimension relation, even for subsystem variants. The $\ell$ rank formula is its elementary rank computation here, not a new cleaning lemma. [Yoshida–Chuang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.0085) also classify complementary logical support for stabilizer codes.
- [Sabo et al., §III.A Lemma 1](https://journals.aps.org/prxquantum/pdf/10.1103/PRXQuantum.5.040302) use this same orientation in their **copying** construction: within-copy weight-two X rows, duplicated Z across each block, $\tilde d_X=d_X$ and $\tilde d_Z=q_Xd_Z$ for uniform width. Their separate thickening step in §III.C is a different orientation/construction. Intrinsic recognition of unequal blocks recovers that structure in reverse, but the act of adding repetition or scaling one weight is not new.
- [Wang–Liu–Li–Kubica–Gu v1 §III.3, Theorem III.15 proof](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15446v1#S3.SS3) already group qubits into components of weight-two X/Z **gauge** checks and use the same whole-or-absent mechanism for commuting bare logicals. Their Theorem III.14 concerns CSS subsystem gauge weight 2, not this stabilizer row-budget and exceptional-cut theorem. Their III.9 concerns a different surface-like weight-four subclass with both sector degree conditions and a symmetric $k d^2$ bound. [Bravyi's generalized Bacon–Shor construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.1029) is antecedent to the gauge interpretation, not this one-sided stabilizer certificate.
- The elementary "a minimum logical support cannot split into two nonempty zero-syndrome pieces" is also explicitly used by [Cheng–Guo–Pan, Lemma 5.1, certifying distances](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08247v1#S5). It does not give the exceptional-dimension bound or the rank certificate. [Sarvepalli–Klappenecker–Rötteler](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Asymmetric-quantum-codes-constructions-bounds-and-performance.pdf) give asymmetric constructions and general coding bounds, not this projected-weight criterion; [Yuan–Baspin–Williamson](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04883) concern weight reduction by layer-code constructions.

I did not locate the specific $n_L\ge Dd_X(k-\ell)$ statement, including uncorrectable small blocks accounted for by $\ell$, or the accompanying projected rank certificate in these sources. This is a useful formalized shortcut in a restricted class; it follows naturally by combining familiar pieces and should be positioned accordingly. It is not a priority finding or a claim about the practical noise-bias performance of the demonstrated matrices.
