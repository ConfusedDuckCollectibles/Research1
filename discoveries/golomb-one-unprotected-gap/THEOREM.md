# Exact chamber minimum when one gap lacks a protected prefix

## Scope and prior work

The largest-gap Singer theorem is prior work: Esther Notik posed it in [arXiv:2611.22656v1, §7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22656), but the [TrureTuring release e5692c67d463](https://www.trureturing.org/research/notik-singer-chamber/) and its [Lean blueprint](https://the-omega-institute.github.io/trureturing-mdbook/Blueprint/D5/S3/Geometry/FiniteGeometry/NotikSingerChamber.html) already use the complete prefix and coordinate-wise domination argument. The sister maximal-cut claims for Bose and Ruzsa have already been refuted in the [primary TrureTuring dossier](https://www.trureturing.org/research/notik-bose-ruzsa-chambers/). Notik §7 also reports exact LP verdicts for all other cuts up to order 47; Proposition 2 already shows a unique nonintegral minimum for one nonmaximal cut. The result below is a solver-free formula for every size with one unprotected gap, including a cyclic perfect difference set cut at its **second-largest circular gap**. It does not settle global real-vs-integer Golomb optima, larger nonmaximal-cut classes, or the Eberhard–Manners Sidon extension conjectures. No matching analytical formula was located in the named prior; no priority claim.

## Theorem

Let A=(a_0,...,a_n), 0=a_0<...<a_n=L, be an integer Golomb ruler. Label a distance by its ordered mark pair, and let its real order chamber require the same labelled order and all positive distances and successive differences in that order to be at least one. Suppose there is an adjacent gap M, at index k, such that every other retained adjacent gap g is **protected**: A has each positive distance 1,...,g. Split the marks into {a_0,...,a_k} and {a_(k+1),...,a_n}. A crossing distance uses opposite blocks; an internal distance uses the same block. Include 0 as a virtual internal distance independent of the marks. Define

    b = min(c − i − 1 : c a crossing distance, i an internal distance, i<c).

Then the exact real chamber minimum is L−b. It is attained by keeping the left block unchanged and translating the entire right block left by the integer b. In particular the original ruler is shortest if and only if some occupied consecutive distances d,d+1 have d internal (possibly virtual 0), d+1 crossing. It is enough to inspect consecutive positions in the full sorted distance list with initial 0: between any lower internal distance and any higher crossing distance there is an adjacent internal-to-crossing transition, whose distance increment is no larger. This gives a one-pass scan after sorting the n(n+1)/2 distances.

### Proof

For each protected nominal gap g, its labelled pair is g-th in the original order, because exactly 1,...,g−1 are smaller. In every real chamber point its gap is at least g, by summing the minimum-spacing inequalities. Thus every interval of marks lying in a single block has real length at least its original integer length; the same holds for any union of protected gaps.

In the block translation, internal distances are unchanged, and every crossing distance decreases by b. Comparisons between two internal or two crossing distances are unchanged, while ordering gaps can only get larger when the lower distance is crossing and the higher internal. When i is lower internal and c upper crossing, their new separation is c−i−b≥1, including i=0. Hence the translated ruler lies in the chamber, as do all order and unit separations. Its length is L−b.

Choose a minimizing pair (i,c). In any real chamber point the interval for c has length at least the interval for i plus one, hence at least i+1=c−b. All gaps outside the c interval are protected because c crosses gap M. Their sum is at least L−c. Adding proves L'≥L−b. This also supplies an explicit finite nonnegative dual by telescoping the initial distance prefix for each protected gap and summing along the named labelled-distance order; no solver is required. ∎

### Cyclic specialization

In a cyclic perfect difference set modulo v, cut at a circular gap of length G and normalize the first retained mark to zero. Its length is v−G. Every residue 1≤r<G has an ordinary positive representation: a wrapped representative would require the underlying ordinary positive difference v−r>v−G. This yields the whole protected distance prefix. Circular gap lengths are distinct, because each is realized by an ordered modular pair and each nonzero residue has only one such pair. Thus at a second-largest cut there is exactly one retained gap above G; the theorem applies to all prime powers, all units, and even any cyclic perfect difference set. When cutting at the largest gap, all gaps are protected and the known theorem applies instead.

There is also a direct comparison between the two chambers of the same cyclic set. For a second-largest cut at G retaining the largest gap M, the shortest crossing distance is M, and the internal distance G−1 exists by the prefix (use virtual 0 if G=1). It follows that b≤M−G, so every real point of the second-cut chamber has length ≥v−M. In other words, even after optimizing its marks, the second-cut chamber cannot beat the original largest-cut ruler of that same cyclic set.

If M≥2 and no bridge exists, b≥1, so one full integer unit of shortening always works; one can often do more (for [0,4,5] mod 7 cut at 2, b=2 gives [0,2,3]). A nonmaximal-cut example at finite Singer order 17 is included in check.js (mod 307, second-largest cut 32, retained largest gap 91, b=1, length 275→274). This is not claimed as a new finite obstruction: Notik's all-cut computation already covered order 17. The classification is new relative to that computation.

## Uniqueness certificate, not assumed universally

If the distinguished gap is also protected, all adjacent gaps have coordinatewise lower bounds and the original ruler is uniquely shortest. Otherwise let A* be the compressed integer optimum from the theorem. In a same-length chamber point write h for the perturbations of A*'s adjacent gaps. The protected adjacent gaps are unchanged by compression and remain bounded below in the original labelled chamber. Hence h_j≥0 for every gap j≠k other than the single possibly unprotected gap; equal total length gives h_k=−sum_(j≠k) h_j.

A labelled distance with interval I then has perturbation sum_(j in I) h_j if I is internal, and −sum_(j outside I) h_j if it is crossing (the latter outside sum has no index k). For each comparison tight at A*, subtract the lower expression from the upper expression and require a nonnegative result. Start with no forced zeros. Whenever all positive coordinates of a row are already forced to zero, all its negative coordinates are forced to zero too, by h_j≥0. Iterate. Any bridge at A* initially forces the protected gaps inside its lower internal interval and outside its upper crossing interval to zero. Moreover if a protected gap is forced to zero, every protected gap shorter in nominal length is forced zero by the complete-prefix chain. Full zero closure proves uniqueness. Failure of closure is not a tie or a contradiction; a universal uniqueness proof for second cuts is not included. For general nonmaximal cuts Notik's report of equal-length alternatives cannot be ignored.

The exact replay generated one trace-zero Singer set for every prime-power order ≤49 (all linear functionals of this form differ by a multiplicative translate, and all unit multipliers were scanned), detected duplicates only by the complete ordered normalized ruler, then verified the formula and zero closure. Of 3398 distinct second-largest cuts, 30 shortened and all 3398 formula optima were unique by zero closure, including the 30 shortened cases. These are supplemental controls, not an extrapolation. The known maximal-cut result, which has complete prefix through every retained gap, is not claimed by this note.

## Arbitrary-cut limit

For any integer Golomb chamber, give each tight consecutive distance comparison its gap vector, upper interval minus lower interval, and include virtual 0 before the least distance. If some adjacent gap occurs in no positive support of these tight rows, its right block can be shifted left by 1. Tight rows do not deteriorate, non-tight rows have integral slack at least 1, and the distance of that gap itself remains at least one (a length-one gap would appear in a positive first row). Thus any chamber-minimal integer ruler has all gaps singly blocked.

The converse genuinely fails when several gaps are unprotected, even within a cyclic perfect difference set. For [0,1,11,19,26,28] modulo 31 cut at 3, every gap is individually blocked but the unique chamber optimum is [0,1,8,13,17,19], of length 19 instead of 28. Direct replay verifies the entire labelled order. With the consecutive rows indexed by their nominal upper differences 1,2,9,10,15, positive weights (7,3,2,4,3) sum them to the objective, proving length at least 19; their determinant is −1, proving uniqueness. Here 19 even beats the largest-cut ruler of this same set (modulus 31 minus largest circular gap 10 gives 21), illustrating why the two-cut comparison does not extend to arbitrary cuts. The earlier [TrureTuring Bose example](https://www.trureturing.org/research/notik-bose-ruzsa-chambers/) also exhibits simultaneous shortening when individual moves are blocked. This control illustrates the restriction inside the cyclic perfect difference set class; it is not a claim that simultaneous-only shortening is new. A conditional arbitrary-cut certificate is available: take any nonnegative combination of distance-order rows having coefficient exactly one on every unprotected gap and coefficient at most one on the protected gaps; fill the remaining coefficients with their prefix bounds. This is only a sufficient certificate, not proof that the protected gaps may be frozen for every multisided optimum.
