# Sources and independent photon calibration

## Field context and novelty boundary

- [Rackham, Apai and Giampapa (2018), *The Transit Light Source Effect*](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/aaa08c), for the chord/disk mismatch and spot/facula contamination. The present work does not rediscover the ordinary product degeneracy.
- [Morris, Agol, Hebb and Hawley (2018), *Robust Transiting Exoplanet Radii in the Presence of Starspots from Ingress and Egress Durations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04886), followed by [their Spitzer TRAPPIST-1 analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.02808). This is close prior work on duration-based geometric exoplanet measurements and already distinguishes depth from occultor radius. The present ceiling does not hold for their full ingress/egress fitting data and does not make generic contact timing new.
- [Grant and Wakeford, *Transmission strings*](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stac3632) treats chromatic finite occulting footprints in transit light curves, including terminator asymmetry. We do not claim novelty for area intersection or for using shape as a measurement. [Agol et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3847/psj/abd022) anchors the narrow system scale with its Table 5 and combined Tables 2, 6–7; the [NASA-hosted full text](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210000129/downloads/Agol_2021_Planet._Sci._J._2_1_Refining.Trappist1.pdf) is independently available.
- [Allen et al. 2026, JWST TRAPPIST-1 e/b Program](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ae28cb) already cross-calibrates near-consecutive transits with the b/e spectrum ratio, under similarity assumptions and a flat b spectrum. [The STScI metadata](https://www.stsci.edu/jwst-program-info/program/?program=9256) identify such paired observations; [TrExoLiSTS](https://www.stsci.edu/~nnikolov/TrExoLiSTS/JWST/trexolists.html) lists multiple archived e and b/e exposures, but this is not proof of clean coverage of both 22-s windows. [Espinoza et al.'s frozen source release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16125662) identifies the archive snapshot of four DREAMS e transits. No timing/covariance or retained-window analysis of those native transit light curves is performed here.
- [*Leveraging Impact Parameter to Mitigate the Transit Light Source Effect* (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20450) already models relative calibration across chords in TRAPPIST-1. [*Detecting atmospheres in the presence of stellar contamination* (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37722) uses multiple science epochs and 17 b transit observations as a stellar/false-positive control, including injection/recovery and a 10-transit scale. Neither is the restricted exterior-contact experiment here. [2026 pixel-resolved contamination models](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202556358) already show geometrical/limb dependencies, and [spot-only line spectroscopy](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202557886) examines rotation and spectral effects outside the static-baseline null.
- [Davoudi et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ad5c6c) use absolutely calibrated JWST NIRISS/SOSS and NIRSpec/PRISM spectra; Table 1 and the [MAST data DOI](https://doi.org/10.17909/ws34-yb06) identify program 1981 (2023-12-11), not the e sample used as benchmark here. We checked this metadata, not the calibrated pixels. None of the retrieved disk-integrated flux products identifies the independently bounded monochromatic **local** limb brightness assumed in the benchmark.

## Native AllWISE retrieval and bound

The calibration is independent of JWST transit retrievals and requires no stellar-continuum model. [IRSA AllWISE TAP](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/TAP/) table allwise_p3as_psd returned one source in the query:

    SELECT TOP 8 designation,ra,dec,w2mpro,w2sigmpro,w2snr,cc_flags,ph_qual,nb,ext_flg
    FROM allwise_p3as_psd
    WHERE CONTAINS(POINT('ICRS',ra,dec),CIRCLE('ICRS',346.6265,-5.0435,0.006))=1

Return CSV SHA-256 6a98146f7c226d20de6aa8c639459d60ab8f4ea811da97372c49490e4cc7a470. The row is J230630.02−050234.1, W2 \(9.807\pm0.020\), W2 S/N 54.6, artifact flags 0000, photometry quality AAAU, 1 component, extended flag 0. [SIMBAD's cross-identification](https://simbad.idia.ac.za/simbad/sim-id?Ident=TRAPPIST-1) independently maps this exact WISEA designation to TRAPPIST-1. Adopt the conservative quiet-state W2 magnitude floor \(m\ge9.70\), explicitly an input and not a certified all-epoch bound. The official [WISE photometric calibration supplement §IV.4.h, Table 1](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/WISE/docs/release/All-Sky/expsup/sec4_4h.html) gives the flat-\(F_\nu\)-equivalent zero magnitude; 200 Jy is a conservative upper bound. We use its *equivalent band response* definition, not an assumption the star has flat \(F_\nu\) or a color correction evaluated for some stellar template.

The native [WISE W2 relative response per photon](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/WISE/docs/release/All-Sky/expsup/figures/RSR-W2.txt) has SHA-256 b0a86a7e86b07713cecfe9c853292d56f19da6c385de45f6265b676cc1330f2a. The 548 rows span 2.53–8.00 μm; the third column gives parts-per-thousand uncertainty, interpreted multiplicatively as described on [Wright's instrument passband page](https://www.astro.ucla.edu/~wright/WISE/passbands.html). With piecewise-linear interpolation, treating response as zero outside the documented grid and using up to four times the quoted row uncertainties as a pointwise envelope rather than a statistical claim, \(\inf_{[4.10,4.50]}R>0.45\) and \(\int R\,d\lambda/\lambda<0.185\). The upper result is certified with upper rectangles \(R_{\max,i}(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_i)/\lambda_i\) (no quadrature estimate), and the lower result follows from checking the interpolation knots. Errors/unknown unlisted tails outside those declared bounds are not covered.

With the calibration convention, nonnegative source \(F_\nu\), and magnitudes, the in-band source photon rate collected with throughput ≤1 is bounded by
\[
 \frac{A_{\mathrm{tel}}}{h_{\mathrm{P}}}\int_{4.10}^{4.50} F_\nu(\lambda)\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}
 \le \frac{A_{\mathrm{tel}}}{h_{\mathrm{P}}}\,
            \frac{F_{\nu,0}\,10^{-0.4m}}{0.45}
             \int R(\lambda)\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}.
\]
Here \(h_{\mathrm P}\) is Planck's constant; use \(1\ {\rm Jy}=10^{-26}\ {\rm W\,m^{-2}\,Hz^{-1}}\). Because the published response is per photon, the Jacobian is \(d\lambda/\lambda\). The conservative magnitude cap implies \(10^{-0.4m}<1/7500\), checked exactly by raising to the 25th power. [STScI's telescope specification](https://jwst-docs.stsci.edu/jwst-observatory-hardware/jwst-telescope) gives clear area 25.4 m²; use a slightly larger 26 m² and 100% throughput. The quiet-source cap is under 4.302 million incident photons/s. For the benchmark allow twice this value in each actual observing window. This last factor is an explicitly imposed cross-epoch envelope, not an inference about every epoch from noncontemporaneous photometry. A sufficiently bright flare both violates the static-known-baseline contract and might exceed the bound.

No raw third-party data are redistributed here. The exact replay can operate offline from the conservative calibration constants; the optional native replay refetches and checks both sources and certifies their use in those constants. No target atmospheric fit has been run.
