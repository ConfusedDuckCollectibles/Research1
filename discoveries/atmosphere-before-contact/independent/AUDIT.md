# Independent audit: compact atmospheric absorption outside opaque contact

**Conclusion.** The published-input, conditional two-window no-go calculation checks. In the stated e-like benchmark with an oracle baseline, true opaque core contacts and ideal time/mark-resolved photon counts, ten perfect visits cannot simultaneously attain size 5% and power 80%; the conservative information gate actually excludes power 59.1% and 17 or fewer such visits. This is necessary, never sufficient. Independently calibrated local brightness and per-epoch photon and feature constraints are inputs, not established by WISE. A separate analytic refinement in this audit gives a stronger 20-visit requirement using the same conditional inputs, suitable only as an appendix. Neither conclusion describes target data or full-transit retrievals.

## Finite geometry and sharpness

Put the emitting stellar disk at unit radius, opaque core radius \(p>0\) and projected center separation \(d=1+p+s\), with \(s\ge0\) outside opaque contacts. The fraction of the absorber's ring at radius \(r\) inside the star is \(f(d,r)=\theta/\pi\), with \(\cos\theta=(d^2+r^2-1)/(2dr)\) in the intersection regime \(|d-r|<1<d+r\), else zero. The opaque core contributes zero throughout these windows for every fixed projected stellar map and any limb profile; the exact point of contact has zero area.

On \(p\le r\le p+H\), if \(p+H<\sqrt{(1+p)^2-1}\) then \(f(d,r)\) is nondecreasing for all such \(s\). Indeed when covered, the derivative of the arccos argument is \((r^2-d^2+1)/(2dr^2)<0\); otherwise the ring fraction is zero prior to its initial touch. This bound is comfortably met by all \(p\in[0.068,1]\), \(H\le0.01\), since it simplifies to \(2p(1-H)>H^2\). For \(a(r)\in[0,1]\) of prescribed uniform-disk-equivalent area \(\Delta=\int 2r a(r)\,dr\), replace it by opaque outer shell \(\sqrt{(p+H)^2-\Delta}\le r\le p+H\); this maximizes the geometrical weighted overlap simultaneously for all \(s\). Direct proof subtracts the ring fraction at the cutoff under the difference integral of equal masses; both remaining factors have the same sign on both sides. It requires the feasible area \(\Delta\le (p+H)^2-p^2\), automatic for admissible \(a\). If thinness does not hold then a far-enough shell can have *decreasing* covered fraction and this outermost rearrangement cannot be assumed. For an arbitrary fixed heterogeneous map, a radial brightness average around the planet need not itself be monotone; the rigorously optimized object is the geometry-only brightness-cap envelope, not necessarily a known map's exact optimal opacity.

The author's curved upper bound, independently derived, needs no thinness assumption for its valid ring regime:
\[
 \tan(\theta/2)=\sqrt{\frac{1-(d-r)^2}{(d+r)^2-1}}
 \le\sqrt{\frac{2(r-(d-1))}{4p(1+p)}} .
\]
Use \(w=r-(d-1)>0\), \(1-(d-r)^2=w(2-w)\le2w\), \(d+r\ge1+2p\), and \(\arctan x\le x\). Therefore for \(r=p+z\), \(f\le\sqrt{2(z-s)_+}/[\pi\sqrt{p(1+p)}]\) for every radius. The signal fraction \(m_\lambda(s)\le L\int 2r a_\lambda(r)f(d,r)\,dr\) because \(L\) bounds the true monochromatic local intensity relative to the actual disk mean. Exact disk-intersection difference can also be used directly for the outer-shell envelope under thinness.

For \(0\le H<1\), a single allowed static map can actually saturate the L factor simultaneously on every exterior ray if \(L[1-(1-H)^2]\le1\), as in the benchmark. Every such stellar ray has stellar radius at least \(d-(p+H)\ge1-H\); this whole rim occupies area fraction \(b=1-(1-H)^2\). Set brightness \(L\) times the mean on the rim and \((1-Lb)/(1-b)\) times the mean elsewhere. The map has mean one in these units, is nonnegative and satisfies the cap for \(L\ge1\). It need not resemble a real photosphere. This proves sharpness of the exact geometrical envelope over the declared maps in that parameter range. With fixed \(p\) as \(H\to0\) and an outer shell of thickness \(o(H)\) (equivalently \(\Delta(H)=o(pH)\)), at \(s/H=x<1\) the normalized angular law tends to \(\sqrt{2(1-x)/(p(1+p))}/\pi\) after dividing by \(\sqrt H\). Dominated convergence with the curved bound proves the exact leading multiplier of \(\Phi L^2\Delta(H)^2 H^2/(\pi^2 p(1+p)u)\) for the two-window KL under fixed-rate, fixed-speed and this limb-map control (or \(L=1\) uniform). A fixed positive \(\Delta\) cannot be used in this limit. This is a sharp mathematical scale, not an actual atmospheric radial profile assumption.

## Directed information and exact gate

The ideal data are independent marked Poisson arrivals across visits conditional on these fixed models. Intensity \(\phi_\lambda(t)\) under null versus \(\phi_\lambda(t)(1-m_\lambda(t))\) under alternative yields the directed relative entropy \(D(P_1\Vert P_0)=\int\phi_\lambda[(1-m_\lambda)\log(1-m_\lambda)+m_\lambda]\,dt\,d\lambda\); see [Leskelä 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00294). The bracket is bounded by \(m_\lambda^2/[2(1-\epsilon)]\) when \(0\le m_\lambda\le\epsilon<1\) by twice integrating curvature \(1/(1-m_\lambda)\). The signal envelope gives
\[
 m_\lambda^2(s)\le\frac{2L^2\Delta_\lambda^2}{\pi^2p(1+p)}(H-s)_+ .
\]
Require \(\int\phi_\lambda(t)\,d\lambda\le\Phi\) and \(\int\phi_\lambda(t)\Delta_\lambda^2\,d\lambda\le\Phi\Delta_{\rm rms}^2\) at each time, with true or ideal generic marks as appropriate. A cap on every eligible feature channel suffices; an unweighted average or a band-summed 100 ppm depth (or observed interior contaminated depth) does not. In particular narrow high-depth lines can have a small weighted mean but large quadratic signal; one cannot grant the experiment finer marks than the stated RMS is valid for. With a bounded, monotone radial speed at each window, \(dt\le ds/u\). Each window contributes the triangular shape \(H^2/(2u)\) after integrating \((H-s)_+\), and their factor of two is included. Thus the per-visit KL is at most
\[
 \frac{\Phi L^2\Delta_{\rm rms}^2 H^2}{\pi^2p(1+p)u(1-\epsilon)}.
\]
At size at most \(\alpha\), power at least \(\beta>\alpha\) needs \(D(P_1\Vert P_0)\ge d_{\rm Bern}(\beta\Vert\alpha)\), by processing to the binary decision (adding independent randomization changes nothing) and monotonicity in both arguments. This is the **alternative-to-null** direction, giving 1.906442054… at size 0.05, power 0.8. Unmodeled shared nuisance or experiment-dependent censoring cannot be assumed to give additive evidence. Finite integration and fixed nonnegative throughput, noise and detector processing can only reduce information if modeled as a hypothesis-independent postprocessing of the stated source photons. The main benchmark gives more information than using any specific instrument count stream, and observed equivalent depth is not the assumed geometric quantity.

## Optional 20-visit appendix: finite shell area plus weighted RMS

This is separate from the conservative 18-visit scope and uses the same physical/data inequalities. Let \(R=p+H\), \(D=\Delta_{\rm rms}\), and define
\[
 G_s(d)=\int_0^d\sqrt{(\sqrt{R^2-x}-p-s)_+}\,dx ,
\]
where \(x=R^2-r^2\) measures area inward. Extend \(G_s\) constantly past the feasible full area \(R^2-p^2\). The curved geometric kernel is maximized by this same outer area allocation regardless of exact circle-fraction monotonicity. For every \(s\), \(G_s(0)=0\) and \(G_s\) is concave nondecreasing because its integrand is. The function \(y\mapsto G_s(\sqrt y)^2\) is concave nondecreasing: its derivative at regular points is \((G_s(d)/d)G_s'(d)\) with \(d=\sqrt y\), a product of nonnegative nonincreasing factors. Continuity handles \(y=0\) and saturation. Jensen over wavelength weights \(\phi_\lambda(t)/\Phi\), with leftover mass placed at \(y=0\), bounds their encoded sum by \(G_s(D)^2\) at each \(t\). This works even if the actual weights change with \(s,t\).

Enlarge support cap to \(H_{\max}=0.01\) before the bound if needed; this only enlarges feasible profiles and ensures \(D=10^{-4}\) fits uniformly at \(p\ge0.068\). Minkowski in \(s\ge0\), using \(\|\sqrt{(z-s)_+}\|_2=z/\sqrt 2\), gives \(\int G_s(D)^2 ds\le M^2/2\) where
\[
 M=\int_0^D(\sqrt{R^2-x}-p)\,dx
 \le D\left(H-\frac D{4R}\right);
\]
indeed \(R-\sqrt{R^2-x}=x/(R+\sqrt{R^2-x})\ge x/(2R)\). Thus replace \(H^2\) by \([H-D/(4(p+H))]^2\) in the KL formula. With fixed enlarged \(H,D\), its ratio to \(p(1+p)\) decreases for \(p\ge0.068\): compare the relative derivative of the square root of the numerator to \(1/(2p)\), which reduces to \(D(3p+H)<4H(p+H)^2\), implied by \(D\le pH\). Maximize at \(p=0.068\).

The uniform geometric \(\epsilon=.0013\) is valid in the same benchmark even if individual channels exceed \(D\):
\[
 m_\lambda\le L(2pH+H^2)\sqrt{2H/[p(1+p)]}/\pi .
\]
The RHS increases in \(H\) and has increasing \((2p+H)/\sqrt{p(1+p)}\) for \(p>H/[2(1-H)]\), so \(p\le1,H=.01,L=2\) give \(2(.0201)(.1)/\pi<.0013\). No channelwise RMS assumption was added. The independent rational replay verifies \(D(P_1^{(19)}\Vert P_0^{(19)})<1.903<1.90644\), and ten-visit KL \(<1.0011<d_{\rm Bern}(.556\Vert .05)\). Thus 20 or more ideal visits are necessary for the original power target under these same frozen assumptions. This is still an upper information bound, not an achievable visit count. The pointwise efficiency of the outer shell was proved, not the equality of the Minkowski bound at finite equivalent thickness.

 \int_{4.10}^{4.50} F_\nu\,d\lambda/(h_{\mathrm P}\lambda)

\]

**Nearest primary methods:** [Mandel–Agol 2002](https://faculty.washington.edu/agol/MandelAgol2002.pdf) already use exact circular overlap and limb effects; [Burrows et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/719/1/341) explicitly integrate \(1-\exp(-\tau)\) over each partially illuminated annulus using orbit-dependent intercept angles from just before ingress to just after egress. The incremental contribution is an analytically optimized bounded-opacity envelope combined with an optimistic decision-theoretic ceiling; no claim to first atmospheric use of exterior timestamps, actual instrument non-detectability, or general priority.
