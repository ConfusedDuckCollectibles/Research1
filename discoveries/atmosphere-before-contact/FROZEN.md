# Freeze: geometric-zero spectral test, before outcome or native-flux lookup

Question: at a known opaque-core ephemeris, can observing only the intervals before first or after fourth opaque contact distinguish a compact planetary atmospheric absorption band from a static arbitrarily heterogeneous stellar surface? An opaque core outside the stellar disk blocks zero flux, regardless of the surface map. The alternative response depends on that map. Test only these intervals, not all transit-shape information; confer Morris et al.'s existing ingress-duration method.

Controls: full-disk spectrum/baseline and contacts are given by an independent oracle; no flares, unmodeled stellar time variability, atmospheric chromospheric limb or planet light. Finite opaque circular core, concentric nonnegative radial excess absorber bounded by unit opacity, radius extension H, and calibrated stellar surface-brightness upper bound L times disk mean. Photon arrivals are ideal Poisson with perfect temporal tagging, no background/read noise/losses, known coefficient template and windows. Actual instrument and detrending cannot improve on this experiment under those noises being independent of the hypotheses.

Benchmark fixed by round conservative model parameters: TRAPPIST-1 e regime, 4.1–4.5 micrometres, R_p/R_star >= 0.068, H/R_star <= 0.01, absolute radial contact speed >= 0.00045 R_star/s for the windows, L <= 2 monochromatically. Absorptive uniform-disk-equivalent feature depth at most 100 ppm in absolute value per resolved channel, or ≤100 ppm photon-weighted RMS in the independently specified usable spectral experiment, not 100 ppm unweighted band mean while retaining unresolved narrow-line information. Comparison: 10 perfectly captured e transits. Decision requirement: a randomized or deterministic test at size 0.05 and power 0.8, with fixed band/window, no look-elsewhere. No candidate atmospheric in-transit outcome will be read/fitted. Use published independent orbit and source-band flux provenance if obtainable; otherwise conditional calculation with an explicitly unverified flux cap. Do not claim a verified upper bound on real JWST throughput or that these are today's last ten native exposures.

Order of work: prove optimum (given total area, maximize atmospheric overlap over radial admissible profiles); derive a simple analytic information ceiling and a scale law; then inspect independent photon-rate inputs only. Stop if not practically informative or physical assumptions unsupported.
