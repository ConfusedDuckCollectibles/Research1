# Independent audit: two-slot syndrome-only echo cadence

**Disposition:** correct as an ideal odd repetition-block theorem. The contribution is the exact midpoint-component selection rule for the entire joint outcome law and the tight two-slot repetition costs. This is neither the first syndrome-only coherence diagnosis nor a claim that missing measurements improve error correction, that arbitrary coherence is detectable, or that a device has this noise.

## Independent derivation

Products of edge checks span all even supports for the final connected graph, and supports even on each component for the middle graph: pair the selected endpoints within a component by paths. \(X_D\) has zero midpoint syndrome iff the indicator of \(D\) is constant on every component, by taking the difference across each measured edge. Redundant edges give redundant labels.

Conjugation by a physical \(Z\) on each selected qubit sends \(X\mapsto-X\). For one qubit \(Z\mapsto Z\cos(2\theta)+iXZ\sin(2\theta)\) in the Heisenberg rotation. Expanding final \(Z_A\) through slot 2 selects \(D\subseteq A\). Midpoint dephasing deletes terms of nonzero midpoint syndrome, and inserting the midpoint observed character multiplies survivors by \(Z_B\). Pull \(i^{|D|}X_D Z_{A\triangle B}\) through slot 1. Its slot-1 subset \(E\subseteq C=A\triangle B\) leaves net \(X_{D\triangle E}\). Encoding forces this net flip to be empty or all vertices. The latter is impossible for odd \(n\): it requires \(D\setminus C=[n]\setminus C\) of odd size, whereas \(D\subseteq A\) implies \(D\setminus C=D\cap B\), even by the component restriction on \(D,B\). Thus \(D=E\subseteq A\setminus B\) and phase \((-1)^{|D|}\), giving the author's formula (1). Fourier inversion on consistent syndrome alphabets gives the probability law. A fixed initial sector gives the same formulas when the records are relabeled by the corresponding known initial syndrome.

Every proper nonempty union \(D\) is in a suitable even \(A\) (add one vertex if odd), and already appears with \(B=\varnothing\) generically. In small-angle scaling it has order \(\epsilon^{2|D|}\) with nonzero coefficients on \(D\) in both slots. Connected midpoint permits no nonempty \(D\). Randomizing one representative sign per component cancels all nonempty modes simultaneously. In both cases the empty term factors into the independent Pauli-twirl increments of the two slots. Recording and deleting an edge label leaves the physical projection and cannot restore interference.

For repeated full extraction the error Kraus map between given complete syndrome sectors is \(aI+bX_L\) in fixed logical bases. The two supporting strings differ by all \(n\) qubits and have opposite parity, so \(\bar ab\) is imaginary under the real single-axis angles. Thus \(K^\dagger K=(|a|^2+|b|^2)I\) and complete syndrome increments conditionally match the twirl even on the evolving coherent logical state. No physical recovery is needed, although an appropriate Pauli recovery is allowed. Fixed classical angle histories or record-conditioned slot choices work if the comparison uses the same choices. This does not cover coherent quantum environment memory, faulty physical measurements, or arbitrary axes.

The stochastic null need not be an independent trajectory law: conjugating any Pauli by a Pauli changes only phase. Conversely every echo-independent law on the two actual consistent syndrome records is realized by an \(X\)-trajectory law: choose a first flip configuration with the selected middle label and a second bringing the total to the chosen final label. Hence setting invariance is the observational null, not a conclusion of classicality when a test does not reject.

## Statistical checks and exact single-site condition

For fixed \(n,G_{\rm mid}\) and two slots, every true one-shot distribution differs from the matched Pauli law by \(O(\epsilon^{2r})\) in total variation, uniformly across echoes. Coupling successive experiments conditional on equal histories gives \(O(N\epsilon^{2r})\) total variation even for adaptive design. Fixed nonzero power-minus-size requires \(\Omega(\epsilon^{-2r})\).

For matched slot angles, the hit at final class \(D_0\) or \(D_0^c\), with \(D_0\) a smallest component, is rare. Use \(\lvert0^n\rangle\) and the state-independence already proved. No echo and final physical error \(D_0\): there are \(2^r\) lowest-order paths, paired by swapping which slot flips each site in \(D_0\). These are exactly the leading paths in each such midpoint class and have equal amplitudes, giving the coefficient \(2^{r+1}\prod_{j\in D_0}\alpha_j^2\); other paths need at least two extra flips. Echo on one site cancels the pairs exactly for final physical error \(D_0\), even with arbitrary matched spectators, by toggling that component of the first-slot flip set while preserving the others. The complementary final string needs at least \(n-r\ge r+1\) flips, hence probability \(O(\epsilon^{2(n-r)})\). If no spectator rotates, no echo gives the exact probability \(2^{r+1}\prod_{j\in D_0}\sin^2(\theta_j)\cos^2(\theta_j)\) and one echo gives exact zero.

Given the past and a present hit under the matched invariant null, the fresh randomized label is fair. Do **not** additionally condition on eventual total hits when the null is history-dependent. A nonnegative capital doubles on no-echo hits, vanishes on echoed hits, and freezes at the \(k\)-th hit. It has constant mean and the rejection event has capital \(2^k\) at the cap, so size is \(\le 2^{-k}\). For the independently repeated coherent alternative, \(N=\lceil C\epsilon^{-2r}\rceil\) gives arbitrarily high fixed probability of \(k\) hits for large fixed \(C\), and echoed labels form a vanishing fraction of hits. No uniformity over arbitrarily small coefficients is promised.

Fixed classical readout independent of settings preserves the null. Full support gives a fixed positive probability floor on the finite observed alphabet. The chi-square bound yields \(O(\epsilon^{4r})\) per-shot KL; adaptive chain rule and Pinsker imply \(\Omega(\epsilon^{-4r})\). Injectivity on the true alphabet, or merely on the leading contrast vector, preserves an order-\(\epsilon^{2r}\) contrast, allowing the matching upper bound by bounded statistics. Full support alone does not imply attainability; unknown calibration can break a preselected statistic without invalidating the null. This is classical corruption of reported digits, not a false physical projection.

With just qubit \(j\) rotating, sign response occurs iff it is isolated at midpoint and the sine product is nonzero. A final edge containing it has no-echo minus echo click gap \(\sin(2\theta_{1j})\sin(2\theta_{2j})\); relabel a known nonzero baseline. For matched slots this is \(4p(1-p)\). In the many-qubit version the same edge with a nonisolated other endpoint additionally has the two cosine factors at that endpoint. For an open-chain local check set, isolating a fixed interior (endpoint) site requires omitting both (one) incident edge checks, not necessarily a minimum count of arbitrary replacement operations.

The author's one-bit refinement with symmetric readout \(0\le q\le q_{\max}<1/2\), including \(q\) tending to zero, also holds. Let \(\Delta=(1-2q)\sin^2(2\theta)\) for matched rotations. The two click rates \(q+\Delta,q\) can be compared to the admissible common null \(m=q+\Delta/2\), with one-shot KL at most \(\Delta^2/[4m(1-m)]\). The coherent clicks have label bias \(\Delta/(4m)\) and hit rate \(m\). Collect fixed \(k\) of order \((m/\Delta)^2\) hits with a cap of order \(k/m\), and use a fair-binomial tail on those labels. The same stopped-capital argument applies to any specified length-\(k\) label sequence, so size holds under a history-dependent null by summing the success sequences. This gives \(\Theta((q+\theta^2)/\theta^4)\) for the **one-bit observation only** as \(\theta\ne0\) tends to zero. At \(\theta=0\) or \(q=1/2\), the two settings coincide.

## Exact replay and sources

Run python check.py. It is computational-basis state-vector propagation, not the author's path pairing. It explicitly applies physical two-sided \(Z\), ideal middle projection and final Born probabilities using Gaussian-integer rotations and rational normalization. It checks 1,088 instruments and 116,960 characters over all midpoint subgraphs and echo settings of a three-vertex triangle and a five-vertex cycle, including redundant outcomes, unequal/negative rotations, and real/imaginary logical superpositions. Separate controls check rational \(r=1,2,3,4\) contrasts, classical-readout attenuation/erasure, and even four-qubit echo response under fully measured midpoint. REPLAY.txt records exact outputs. These are controls, not replacements for the proof.

- [Combes et al., In-situ characterization of quantum devices with error correction, §§ II–III and V](https://arxiv.org/html/1405.5656) is closest: any encoded state in the three-bit repetition block, syndrome-only data, passive degeneracy and active discrimination of coherent/stochastic effects via counter-rotations and duration variation, plus distinct controlled model selection. Their added calibrated \(X\) rotation changes the likelihood or varying duration distinguishes a specified time law. The audited problem uses physically matched \(Z\)-conjugation, graph-dependent omission of midpoint projection, and arbitrary setting-invariant Pauli trajectories.
- [Greenbaum–Dutton, Modeling coherent errors in quantum error correction, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.03908) gives odd repetition-code branch rotations and syndrome probabilities. [Estimating and decoding coherent errors of QEC experiments with detector error models, §III](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23797) notes no enhancement in its fully extracted data-only repetition model and coherent effects for other circuit/noise models. Full complete-round blindness under the current ideal assumptions is not the main novelty.
- [Omkar–Srikanth–Banerjee, Characterization of quantum dynamics using quantum error correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0964) uses preprocessed stabilizer measurements for characterization. [Orsucci–Tiersch–Briegel, Estimation of coherent error sources from stabilizer measurements](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.93.042303) and [erratum](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.99.059901) show graph-dependent identification via graph-state correlators, not omission of physical midpoint checks.
- [Debroy et al., Stabilizer Slicing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01040) and [Wallman–Emerson, Noise tailoring via randomized compiling](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.94.052325) ground coherent cancellation and physical frame randomization. [Identifying the Sign of Coherent Over-Rotations with Logarithmically Many Pauli Settings](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21663) has exact active/passive identifiability with added final product-Pauli settings, a different measurement access model and goal.

No matching prior for the combined component selection rule, invariant-null testing exponents, and readout crossover found in this primary-source pass. Present as a restricted ideal-model null separator, not an exhaustive priority determination or an experimental design that bypasses control-induced confounding.
