# A sharp check-cadence limit for an echo test using only syndromes

## 1. Model and result

Take an odd-length repetition block of `n>=3` data qubits, encoding the span of `|0^n>, |1^n>` and using ideal nearest-neighbor `ZZ` checks. This is one error axis, not a distance-`n` quantum memory against arbitrary noise. The commuting checks can more generally be edges of any connected graph on the same qubits. Start in any encoded state or in any fixed known initial complete-syndrome sector (relabel the checks in the latter case). The two matched physical noise slots have local coherent rotations
`U_l = prod_j exp(-i theta_lj X_j)`, `l=1,2`. In between, **physically measure** only the checks on a fixed spanning subgraph `G_mid`, with all vertices and any subset of edges. There is no adaptation or recovery between these two slots. Read all checks at the end; do not read the final logical qubit. The midpoint and final syndromes are the entire recorded outcome. If the complete graph has redundant checks, only independent or consistent labels are used.

A randomized classical setting `z_j in {+1,-1}` conjugates the second slot with a *physical* Pauli `Z_j` twice when `z_j=-1`. The ideal net control is the identity, but this really changes the sign of the intervening `X_j` Hamiltonian. It is not a logging change of Pauli frame. The null consists of **any distribution of classical Pauli trajectories**, allowing spatial and temporal correlations and unknown rates, with the same law in different randomized echo settings. The null may depend on the choice of measured checks and on past data independent of the present echo setting. Within a randomized contrast the timing, measurement and recovery context must be matched. A setting-dependent Pauli channel or control-induced detuning is not this null.

Let `A` be the support of any product of final checks, so it ranges over even subsets. Let `B` be the support of any product of the measured midpoint checks, so it ranges over sets having even intersection with each connected component of `G_mid`. A product of outcome bits with these supports is called the `(B,A)` joint character. Let `C=A xor B`, `s_lj=sin(2 theta_lj)`, `c_lj=cos(2 theta_lj)`. The **complete joint law** of the two true syndrome records is characterized by the following expectation of every joint character:
```
sum_{D a union of components of G_mid, D subset A minus B}
     (-1)^|D| prod_{j in D}(z_j s_1j s_2j)
                  prod_{j in C minus D} c_1j
                  prod_{j in A minus D} c_2j.                   (1)
```
For any phase distribution chosen independently of the echo, the formula can be averaged over that distribution. Complete duplicate outputs are immaterial. Pauli mixtures in this formula need not be restricted to `X`: any trajectory of Paulis shifts the recorded stabilizer signs by its anticommutation labels. Hence conjugating any Pauli by another Pauli changes only an unobserved scalar and yields **the same full joint law** for all echo settings.

Three consequences of (1):

1. If midpoint checks still connect the whole graph, **no echo choice** changes the law. In this case it agrees exactly with the local Pauli twirl in both time slots. Fully measuring a check and hiding its digit does not count as omitting it. For ideal full extraction after *every* slot, passivity extends to arbitrary numbers of rounds, as checked in §2.
2. For general `G_mid`, every Fourier harmonic of the echo choice is a union of entire components, never the whole odd block. At uniform small angle scale `theta_lj=epsilon alpha_lj`, an eligible harmonic `D` first occurs at order `epsilon^(2|D|)` if the participating angles in both slots are nonzero. Averaging independent echo signs on just one qubit per component kills every nonzero harmonic and gives exactly the complete syndrome law of the matched independent two-slot Pauli twirl: this is not just matching each isolated check marginal.
3. If only qubit `j` rotates, an echo detects it with syndrome data **if and only if it is isolated** in the midpoint graph, assuming `sin(2 theta_1j) sin(2 theta_2j) != 0`. On the ordinary open nearest-neighbor chain this requires withholding exactly its two incident measurements if it is interior, or its one incident measurement if it is an endpoint. This count is for the original local edges, not a count of constraints under arbitrary nonlocal replacement measurements. A final edge parity containing `j` gives exact click contrast `sin(2 theta_1j) sin(2 theta_2j)` between no echo and flipping its sign. For equal angles the contrast is `4p(1-p)`, where `p=sin(theta)^2`. If other qubits rotate too and `j` is isolated while its neighbor `k` is not, the same contrast is multiplied by `cos(2 theta_1k) cos(2 theta_2k)`. It does not require all (n) checks or qubits to become unobserved.

## 2. Proof of complete law

Write `Z_A=prod_{j in A} Z_j` and similarly for `X_D`. For the second slot, (U_2(z)^* Z_A U_2(z)) expands into terms `i^|D| X_D Z_A prod_D(z_j s_2j) prod_(A minus D)c_2j`, indexed by `D subset A`. This follows from the single-qubit identity `U^* Z U = cos(2 theta) Z + sin(2 theta) Y` and `Y=iXZ`. Let `P_m` denote the projector onto a consistent midpoint syndrome. For the joint character, insertion of the midpoint projectors deletes precisely the terms for which `X_D` anticommutes with any measured edge. That means the indicator of `D` must be constant along each measured edge, i.e. `D` is a union of components. The midpoint character multiplies the surviving terms by `Z_B`, since this product acts by its observed sign on `P_m`. It commutes with every surviving `X_D`. Thus the operator left to pull through slot 1 is proportional to `i^|D| X_D Z_C`.

The slot-1 expansion is indexed by `E subset C` and produces strings proportional to `i^(|D|+|E|) X_(D xor E) Z_C`. All `Z_C` are stabilizers of the initial encoded sector. The string can have nonzero expectation in any encoded input only when `D xor E` is empty or all vertices. The latter is impossible: if `E` is the complement of `D` and `E subset C`, then every qubit outside `C` is in `D`, so `D minus C` equals the complement of `C` and has odd cardinality. But `D subset A` gives `D minus C = D intersect B`, an even number since `X_D` commutes with every measured midpoint check. Thus only `E=D` survives; it must lie in `A intersect C = A minus B`. Its sign is `i^(2|D|)=(-1)^|D|`. This proves (1); inversion over the two binary check outcome groups proves it for the entire record. A possible proper union `D` has an even superset `A` (itself when even, or one extra vertex if odd), thus every such harmonic really occurs at generic nonzero small angles, already with `B` empty. Exact zero can occur at angle nodes or by sign/drift averaging.

For full extraction after every slot, each syndrome increment is reached by exactly two complementary physical-`X` subsets of the odd block. After a Pauli recovery returning to the code, the conditional coherent Kraus operator has the form `a I+b X_L` with `a*b` purely imaginary, since the two weights have opposite parity. Its squared norm as an operator is `(|a|²+|b|²)I`, the Pauli-twirl probability, independent of the evolving logical state. Iterating this argument proves equality for any number of ideal full rounds with appropriate Pauli recovery, including preassigned or syndrome-conditioned slot angles as long as the same stochastic comparison uses the matched slot rates. A pre-drawn classical history of rotation angles can also be averaged. This is not a claim about arbitrary quantum environment memory, measurement faults, or other logical observables.

For the matched independent Pauli twirl, an (X_j) is independently present in each slot with probability `p_lj=sin(theta_lj)^2`. An error in slot 1 contributes to both midpoint and final parity; the joint character then has exactly the `D=empty` value, `prod_(C)c_1j prod_(A)c_2j`. A uniform sign on one representative per midpoint component independently makes all the nonempty terms vanish, as claimed.

## 3. Sharp information scaling with two slots

Assume here fixed finite `n`, a fixed disconnected midpoint graph, independent repetitions, and coherent `theta_1j=theta_2j=epsilon alpha_j` with all `alpha_j` on one smallest component nonzero and fixed finite. There is no requirement that different components have the same angle. Let `r` be the smallest component size. Since the block is odd, `r <= (n-1)/2`.

**Lower bound.** Equation (1) bounds every one-shot coherent-vs-matched-Pauli difference of joint moment by `O(epsilon^(2r))`, uniformly in the echo vector. Inversion on the fixed finite record alphabet bounds total variation by the same order. Even if the experimenter chooses the echo vector adaptively across shots (with this graph and budget), conditional coupling or a hybrid argument bounds total variation of the entire experiment by `O(N epsilon^(2r))`. For fixed target size `alpha_test` and power `1-beta_test>alpha_test`, any test therefore needs `Omega(epsilon^(-2r))` independent repetitions against a null class that includes the matched Pauli. This statement does not grant adaptive designs a change of midpoint graph within one shot or more than two noise slots.

**Attainment.** Let `D_0` be a size-`r` component. Randomly choose with equal probability no echo or one echo on a fixed qubit of `D_0`. Count the *final* syndrome reached by the bit-flip set `D_0` (or its complement, which has the same full syndrome); do not require any particular midpoint syndrome. The probability of this hit is
```
no echo:     2^(r+1) epsilon^(2r) prod_(j in D_0) alpha_j²
                                             + O(epsilon^(2r+2)),
with echo:   O(epsilon^(2(n-r))).                              (2)
```
To see this, for a total physical error exactly `D_0`, its minimum-weight paths assign a subset of its qubits to slot 1 and the rest to slot 2. There are `2^r` such paths of equal amplitude to leading order. The midpoint measurements distinguish them except for paired complementary splits inside this connected component. There are `2^(r-1)` pairs, each giving squared amplitude four times that of a path with no echo, hence the coefficient. With echo the pair cancels not just to leading order but exactly for a final physical error `D_0`: change which slot each of its sites flipped in, keep the other site paths unchanged, and obtain opposite echo sign and otherwise identical amplitude at matched angles. These paired paths have the same midpoint syndrome. The complementary final error has minimum number of flips `n-r` and parity opposite to the paths for the chosen `D_0`, so the two never interfere in a probability, for an arbitrary encoded state (or use the state independence of (1) and work with `|0^n>`). This gives (2).

Under an echo-independent Pauli null, echo labels of hits are fair randomized labels; even fully correlated trajectories within a shot cannot counterfeit the difference. With i.i.d. shots under the coherent alternative, the rate of hits is of order `epsilon^(2r)` and their conditional no-echo probability tends to one. E.g. take a predeclared cap `N`, and reject only if at least `k` hits occur and the first `k` all have the no-echo label. With independent uniform random labels and matched context, the size is at most `2^(-k)` (independent of the unknown hit probability), and with a sufficiently large fixed multiplier in `N=ceil(C epsilon^(-2r))` the power has any desired fixed value below one at sufficiently small angle. For the stated null, including history dependence, this size bound does not condition on the *future* hit count. On each included hit the label is conditionally fair given the past and current hit, so a capital that doubles on no-echo hits and goes to zero on echo hits, frozen after the k-th hit, has conditional mean unchanged. Rejection entails capital \(2^k\) at the cap. Combined with the lower bound this proves `Theta(epsilon^(-2r))`. The measured circuit can keep one midpoint connected component of size 1 at any chosen site without giving up checks elsewhere, yielding the distance-independent exponent 2; the fully checked case, by contrast, is exactly blind under this model regardless of repetitions. “Distance-independent” refers to the local exponent and, for the single-site parity formulas, the local signal coefficient, not to a uniform-in-`n` remainder for arbitrary global observables.

## 4. Readout and calibration: what survives

Fixed sign-independent *classical* errors applied after ideal projection preserve the entire randomized-null equality. They only contract total variation. If the readout is a fixed full-support channel on the joint outcomes (e.g. independent binary misclassification, constant rate strictly between 0 and 1 on each independent recorded check), there's an extra sharp penalty. Each recorded atom then has a positive lower probability uniformly in `epsilon`, at fixed `n`. Differences in probabilities remain `O(epsilon^(2r))`, hence KL divergence to the matched Pauli is `O(epsilon^(4r))` by the elementary chi-square inequality. The adaptive chain rule and Pinsker give `Omega(epsilon^(-4r))` shots. If the channel is fixed and invertible (independent bit inversions with rates different from 1/2 are one example), the nonzero leading difference of the observed distribution retains order `epsilon^(2r)` for the settings in §3. A bounded prechosen event or linear contrast and independent samples then achieve `O(epsilon^(-4r))` (e.g. invert known readout to recover that event, then use Hoeffding). Constants can grow fast with graph size, poor conditioning, or tiny readout probabilities. Unknown calibration does not invalidate the echo-symmetric null itself, but a prechosen contrast can lose all power under unknown/singular readout; this does not estimate readout. The result models reported-bit corruption only, not faulty physical projection or syndrome feedback.

**A local readout crossover.** If only the isolated qubit rotates, the two slots are equal, and the observation contract is one final parity bit, there is also a sharp interpolation as the symmetric misclassification rate `q` varies. The click rates of the two settings are exactly `q+Delta` and `q`, where `Delta=(1-2q) sin(2 theta)^2`. For `q<=q_max<1/2` and small `theta`, the sharp one-bit shot order is `Theta((q+Delta)/Delta^2)=Theta((q+theta^2)/theta^4)`. Take the common null click rate `q+Delta/2` to get a per-shot KL upper bound of order `Delta^2/(q+Delta)` from chi-square, for either setting. This common final-edge error rate is permitted by the unrestricted Pauli fit when the readout is fixed: before readout take parity-flip probability `sin(2 theta)^2/2`. For the upper bound, interleave settings uniformly and test imbalance among click labels: under the independent coherent alternative the hit rate is `q+Delta/2` and the conditional no-echo probability is `1/2+Delta/[4(q+Delta/2)]`. Testing a fixed number of hits with fair labels and allowing a fixed cap gives the matching order by binomial concentration. For a history-dependent sign-invariant null, the chance of collecting the required hits by the cap *and* seeing any specified fixed-length label sequence is at most its fair-coin probability, by the same stopped-label argument above. This crossover is for one bit only, not a bound on every way of using the whole record.

In the local single-site model, a symmetric final-check readout flip probability `q` attenuates the click probability gap by exactly `1-2q`. Any certified upper bound `delta` on sign-dependent nuisance difference for the predeclared event under a Pauli-only model has to be added to the test threshold. A fixed nonzero `delta` therefore swamps an arbitrarily small quadratic signal; randomized assignment alone does not rule out an echo pulse changing gate errors, actual durations, measurement, leakage or correlation laws. The test detects an echo-responsive component relative to a matched invariant stochastic model, **not** all coherence, absolute rotation sign, spatially misassigned coherent source or generic decoherence. It certifies neither that any existing hardware exhibits the effect nor that skipping checks during intended protected operation is advantageous. Interleaved rotations must be physical, with matched timing/context; a virtual bookkeeping toggle that does not change the actual physical evolution does not test (1).
