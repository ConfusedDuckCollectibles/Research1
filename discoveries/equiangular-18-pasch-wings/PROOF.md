# An exact extension bound for the Pasch wings of 57 equiangular lines

**Theorem.** Form the three disjoint seven-line wings described below, in
any of the configurations of Munemasa–Szöllősi–Yoshino, arXiv:2606.10421,
Theorem 6.1. Every real equiangular line set in dimension 18 containing an
isometric copy of their union has at most **57 lines**. This is sharp.
The theorem makes no assumptions on the other lines or on the integral
lattice generated by them.

This is a scoped, computer-assisted exact proof, not the unrestricted
answer for dimension 18. AI-assisted; not peer reviewed. No priority claim.

## Baseline and distinction

[Greaves and Syatriadi, *Real equiangular lines in dimension 18 and the
Jacobi identity for complementary subgraphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04267),
Theorem 1.1, prove the global upper bound 59. The lower bound 57 comes from
[Greaves, Syatriadi, and Yatsyna](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04330).
Thus the established global interval used here is 57 to 59. The 2025
[Lin–Munemasa–Taniguchi–Yoshino construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06377)
uses a different lattice.

The nearest result is [Munemasa, Szöllősi, and Yoshino, *Sets of
equiangular lines in dimension 18 constructed from A₅³⊕A₁⁴*](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10421v2).
Their Theorem 6.1 proves maximum 57 **in their lattice** and gives a Latin
square (36 lines) plus three Pasch wings (21 lines). Their Theorem 7.5
rules out extending such a **full 57-line set** in the same dimension.
Their proof already uses only the 21 wings and a specified four-cell
Latin-square rectangle as a sufficient lattice anchor (Eq. 7.4 and
Lemma 7.1). Our theorem retains just the 21 wing lines and removes the
four core anchors: all 36 other lines may be replaced by any candidate
vectors in real space. We do not use a fixed lattice for the extra
vectors, nor require any Latin square. The bounds
have no implication for a potential 58-line set not containing these
wings, or for an extension in dimension 19.

## Exact coordinates for the wing family

Use the orthogonal sum of three coordinate-sum-zero subspaces of R⁶
and four coordinate-sum-zero subspaces of R², of total dimension 19.
Let \(a=(1/2,-1/2)\), and let \(\rho\) be \(2a\) in the last R²
block and zero elsewhere. For a triple \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,6\}\),
write \(p_I=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)-\mathbf1_I\). Number the R⁶ blocks and
the first three R² blocks by residues \(i=0,1,2\) modulo 3.

A wing vector \(x(i,I,\epsilon)\) has \(p_I\) in R⁶ block \(i\),
zero in the other two R⁶ blocks, and has components \(0,\epsilon a,a\)
in R² blocks \(i,i+1,i+2\), followed by \(a\) in the last R² block.
Its geometric line is spanned by \(x-\rho/2\) in \(\rho^\perp\).

Here are the two seven-entry patterns from the source Proposition 5.2
(entries are \((I,\epsilon)\)). Both have 135, 146, 236, 245 with sign
minus. The three sign-plus triples are 123, 345, 156 in the first
pattern, or 126, 234, 456 in the second. Each wing may independently
use either pattern or reverse all its signs. Arbitrary permutations
of the six entries' ground set do not affect the claim, since the
three coordinate blocks may be permuted internally and independently.

Any geometric extension keeps angle arccos(1/5), since the existing
lines have that angle. Exactly, vectors \(x,y\) of squared norm 3
with \(\langle x,\rho\rangle=\langle y,\rho\rangle=1\) satisfy
\[
 \|x-\rho/2\|^2=5/2,\qquad
 \langle x-\rho/2,y-\rho/2\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle-1/2.
\]
Hence unoriented equiangular lines at our angle correspond exactly to
such vectors with pairwise inner products 0 or 1, up to replacing any
\(x\) by \(\rho-x\) (the other orientation of the same line).

## Four templates exhaust the wing family

There are four named choices per wing. They are indexed in the code
as 0,1 for the first pattern with all signs reversed/unchanged, and
2,3 for the second. This gives 64 labelled triples. Simultaneously
permute the three R⁶ blocks and the corresponding first three R²
blocks. Independently flip the signs of those first three R² blocks.
These are ambient isometries fixing \(\rho\). Normalize each vector
so that the last of its two nonzero cyclic R² components is \(+a\):
if it was \(-a\), replace \(x\) by \(\rho-x\), complementing \(I\)
and negating both of those R² components. The exact orbit routine
then identifies each individual wing modulo permutations of its six
coordinates. The 64 labelled cases reduce to representatives
000, 001, 002, 003 with multiplicities 8, 8, 24, 24, respectively.
No converse classification of ambient isometries is needed.

## Finite enumeration of all ambient extra lines

For each representative, the exact rank test finds a basis \(B\)
consisting of \(\rho\) and 18 of the 21 wings. Write \(G=B^TB\),
using orthonormal coordinates for this identity. An extra affine
vector \(x\) has \(z=B^Tx\) with first coordinate 1 and each remaining
coordinate 0 or 1. It is therefore uniquely
\[
                       x=B G^{-1}z.
\]
This exhausts all possible extra lines in dimension 18. The exact
rank-19 basis test includes \(\rho\), so the wing directions span
all of \(\rho^\perp\). No rationality assumption is imposed on the
input geometry: the basis identity implies rational coordinates
for every candidate in the chosen coordinate system.

The helper enumerates all \(2^{18}\) tuples \(z\) on each representative.
It tests \(\langle x,x\rangle=3\), then all 21 inner products with the
wings, and keeps exactly one orientation of each remaining line.
Neither a wing line itself nor its reversed lift passes, since its
inner product with its wing vector is 3 or −2. The coordinate
denominators in the chosen reduced representation divide 36 for these
cases. All reconstruction and tests use exact fractions or integers.

## Exact clique bound

Construct two graphs on the candidate lines: the **integrality graph**
joins pairs of integral affine inner product, and the
**compatibility graph** joins pairs of inner product 0 or 1.
Compatibility implies integrality. In the integrality graph, group
vertices with equal **closed** neighborhoods. A group consists of
pairwise adjacent vertices, and its adjacency with any other group
is uniform. Therefore every compatible set lies within the union of
the groups in some maximal clique in the integrality quotient.
The replay enumerates every such maximal clique via
Bron–Kerbosch. This separates the arithmetic cases before any
harder geometric independence check.

| Wing representative | Labelled cases | Candidate lines outside the wings | Sizes of expanded maximal branches |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 000 | 8 | 864 | 4 × 216 |
| 001 | 8 | 1,053 | 4 × 216, 36 × 93 |
| 002 | 24 | 864 | 4 × 216 |
| 003 | 24 | 1,053 | 4 × 216, 36 × 93 |

In every 216-vertex branch the replay partitions the compatibility
graph into 36 explicitly checked independent groups by projection to two
of the R⁶ blocks modulo suitable signs. It checks directly that no
edge lies inside a group. A clique has at most one vertex per group.
For each of the 72 branches of size 93, the complement of its
compatibility graph has exactly six connected components of order 8 and
three of order 15. The replay checks this and computes the independence
number of every component by the exact recurrence
\[
\alpha(H)=\max\{\alpha(H-v),\;1+\alpha(H-N[v])\},\qquad
\alpha(\varnothing)=0.
\]
Each component has independence number 4. Thus the complement's
independence number, equal to the compatibility clique number, is
exactly \((6+3)\cdot4=36\). No solver or numerical tolerance is used.

Thus at most 36 new lines can accompany the 21 wing lines. Sharpness
is also directly checked: the three chosen representative wings
and the 36 core vectors from the cyclic Latin square give 57,
verifying every norm and pairwise inner product as exact integers.

## Replay

Run `python3 replay.py` in this directory with Python 3.10+ and a
GCC- or Clang-compatible C++20 compiler. There are no auxiliary data
files or third-party Python libraries. The default compiler is
`c++`; pass `--cxx g++` or `--cxx clang++` if necessary.
Do not use Python's `-O` or compile with `NDEBUG`.
A representative run took about four seconds.

Reduced integer coordinates drop the sixth coordinate of each R⁶
block and the second coordinate of each R² block; each omitted
coordinate is the negative sum of the retained coordinates. The
C++ helper uses Gray-code updates and a guarded 128-bit integer
dot product. The checked input-coordinate bound makes this
arithmetic exact even before the norm filter; no floating-point
operations enter the enumeration.
