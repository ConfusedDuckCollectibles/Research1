# Checked literature and limits of priority

- [Hill, *Elastic properties of reinforced solids: Some theoretical principles*, JMPS (1963)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-5096(63)90036-X): the exact, geometry-independent composite response at equal constituent shear is old. See also [Milton, *The Theory of Composites*, §5.3](https://www.math.utah.edu/~milton/TheoryCompositesNOPRINT.pdf). These are the starting point, not the new claim.
- [Bogdan, *On fourteen problems in the theory of composites*, arXiv:2611.29402v1, 22 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.29402v1): Lemma 57 explicitly gives the very same baseline hydro strain field and its invariance to changes on a core in which its deviatoric part vanishes. Lemma 58 and Proposition 59 give bulk endpoints and coated/matrix-laminate constructions. Supplement S8.2–S8.6 treats joint finite/weak-contrast bulk–shear information, including expanding both bulk and shear contrasts towards zero and angular constraints for specified sequential grains; S8.12 Eq. (22) is a much closer finite positive-phase benchmark. Its static exact endpoint rules applied to the synthetic phases here give the isotropic high-bulk edge shear cap 387/322, and the low-bulk edge shear floor 61/50 (its Eq. (15) gives the matrix-laminate values, Eq. (22) plus the zero-core-corrector condition bounds them). Those results are not superseded by the finite example in this note. We do not claim whether the static optimum at the interior benchmark bulk 1.30 lies at or below the displayed finite cap. The distinction investigated here is an exact scalar/tensor differential locus along one contrast path with fixed unequal bulks and arbitrary anisotropy.
- The confocal construction and all-direction geometry are classic. [Grabovsky–Kohn, *Microstructures minimizing the energy of a two phase elastic composite in two space dimensions. I: The confocal ellipse construction*, JMPS (1995)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-5096(95)00016-C) uses coated confocal ellipses in planar elasticity. [Milton, *The Theory of Composites*, §§7.8–7.11, especially Eq. (7.55) and discussion after it](https://www.math.utah.edu/~milton/TheoryCompositesNOPRINT.pdf) gives exactly the shape matrix M=(D_core−f D_outer)/(1−f), credits Tartar's *Estimations fines des coefficients homogénéisés* (1985, pp. 168–187) with its entire positive-semidefinite trace-one range at fixed fraction in 3D, and discusses cylindrical limits. [Benveniste–Milton, JMPS (2003), Eq. (4.12)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5096(03)00074-7) uses this matrix for exact neutral inclusion solutions. [Liu, *Effective conductivities and elastic moduli of composites…*, Theorem 1 in the 2009 author version](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~ll502/papers/Liu2009_Ein.pdf) has the related prescribed constant Hessian and phase fraction in periodic E-inclusions. The direct finite-integral and simplex argument are supplied for a self-contained application to the stiffness derivatives, not a priority claim for the geometry or shape-map surjectivity.
- [Valmalette, Bellis, Hochard and Lahellec, *Relating local field fluctuations in composites with the sensitivity of their effective response to constitutive parameters*, IJSS (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijsolstr.2025.113580) explicitly connects first and second effective derivatives to local fields (Eqs. (26), (28)). Variational differentiation and its broad identification use are not the contribution here. [Torquato, *Effective stiffness tensor of composite media—I. Exact series expansions*, JMPS (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5096(97)00019-7) includes anisotropic exact correlation expansions; [Milton, PRL (1981)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.46.542) and [Berryman–Milton, *Microgeometry of random composites and porous media* (1988)](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/21/1/013) develop phase-correlation parameters and property correlations. The possibility of recovering special cases by differentiating those general formulas is not excluded.
- [Cherkaev–Gibiansky, *Coupled estimates for the bulk and shear moduli of a two-dimensional isotropic elastic composite*, JMPS (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-5096(93)90006-2) provides classical coupled estimates for fixed phase tensors, including translation machinery applicable to anisotropic tensors, and [Gibiansky–Torquato, *Link between the conductivity and elastic moduli of composite materials*, PRL (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.71.2927) bounds planar elastic moduli using another effective property of the same geometry. Neither is an original motivation to treat moduli as independent; both are relevant prior art. The exact three-observable shear-contrast derivative identity and its simultaneous full attainable set were not located in this bounded screen. This is a scoped priority assessment, not an exhaustive claim.

No external source text, code, or data are bundled. The examples are mathematically specified elastic models, not empirical fits or measured material performance.
