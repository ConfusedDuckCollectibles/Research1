# Frozen objective and benchmark

Use ordinary static, perfectly bonded linear elastic periodic cell homogenization in dimension d>=2. Both phases isotropic and strictly positive. The very same architecture has a common phase shear at baseline; only phase 1 shear is then increased, with phase fractions and both bulks fixed. Define displacement-controlled hydrostatic energy modulus, orientation-independent average stiffness across an orthonormal deviatoric load basis, and the hydro/shear coupling vector. Target an exact first/second differential relation and a sharp realizability interval under zero first-order coupling, not a new static G-closure.

Freeze a two-dimensional test case: half of phase 1 K=.1, mu=1 and half of phase 2 K=10, mu=1, then phase 1 shear increased by t=.5 while bulks are unchanged. Hypothesized simultaneous goals B(t)>=1.30 and averaged S(t)>=1.214, disprove with no symmetry requirement if possible. Need explicit continuum positive-phase architectures attaining each goal individually and a finite bound (not only asymptotic differentiation). Use coated disk controls for attainability, and a layered anisotropic control for normalization/cross-coupling. Do not claim any observed material has these parameters, no costs/experiments/communication/public writes.

Before novelty claims compare Hill/common-shear shift, Cherkaev–Gibiansky coupled static bounds and coated assemblages, strong-contrast stiffness literature, 2025 Valmalette et al. general derivative/sensitivity framework, and Bogdan’s Nov 2026 fourteen-problem preprint. A new differential exact identity and sharp attainable derivative tradeoff could be useful even if some static thresholds can independently exclude a particular numerical pair.
