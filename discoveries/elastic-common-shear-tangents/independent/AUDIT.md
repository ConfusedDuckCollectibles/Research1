# Independent audit: elasticity near common constituent shear

**Result checked.** At a common positive phase shear, fixed positive (not necessarily close) phase bulks, and fixed phase geometry, the volumetric slope and mixed slope determine the second derivative of the rotationally averaged deviatoric stiffness. The full projected derivative locus follows from a sharp two-moment geometry statement. The finite planar exclusion offered as an example is correct but strictly preempted by Bogdan's bounds on the same phases. Confocal geometry, its depolarization integral and its fixed-fraction shape surjectivity in 3D are classical. Only the corrected joint elasticity derivative theorem is a candidate contribution.

## Setup and independent derivative proof

Take any dimension d≥2 and any periodic indicator χ of mean f∈(0,1). Write g=1−f. The moduli are K(x)=K1 χ+K2(1−χ) and shear μ(x)=μ+tχ; K1,K2,μ>0 and t>−μ. Use the energy integrand K(tr E)^2+2μ(x)|dev E|², minimized over mean-zero periodic compatible strain fluctuations. The periodic cell has normalized volume one. Physical loading remains geometrically linear; t is a material parameter, not finite strain.

Let H=I/d, and let E_j be any Frobenius-orthonormal basis of symmetric traceless tensors (size n=d(d+1)/2−1). The hydrostatic stiffness scalar B is H:C*:H, the mixed vector X is dev(C*:H), and S=(Σ_j E_j:C*:E_j)/(2n). For anisotropic effective tensors B is a prescribed-dilation stiffness, not a pressure-defined compliance modulus; S is a deviatoric mean, not the entire shear block. Both coincide with the usual scalars in the isotropic case. Put
- a=2μ(d−1)/d, w_i=(K_i+a)^−1, h=f w_1+g w_2, Δw=w_1−w_2, β=Δw/h;
- Q=(D²Δ⁻¹−I/d)(χ−f), J=⟨χ|Q|²⟩, R=⟨χQ⟩ and T=(d−1)fg/d.

Hill's base field for load H is H+D²Δ⁻¹(w/h−1) with trace w/h and local deviator βQ. The divergence of its stress is zero because (K+a)w/h is constant. Its mean stress is (h^−1−a)I. The corrector for every fixed traceless E_j is zero at t=0, since its stress is 2μE_j. Differentiating the minimized quadratic energy gives B′=2β²J, X′=2βR, S′=f. Opposite shears are not substituted and the geometry is fixed in the differentiation.

For a load E_j, the t-linear corrector response is driven by functional F_j(u)=2⟨χ E_j:e(u)⟩. The coefficient of t² in the minimum is the negative squared dual norm of F_j for the base energy, by completing the square (the sign of the actual first corrector is immaterial here). For the Helmholtz split u=∇Δ⁻¹s+u_T with s=div u mean-zero and div u_T=0, the energy bilinear form separates:
`a_0(u,u)=⟨(K+a)s²⟩+μ⟨|∇u_T|²⟩.`
The cross part vanishes by Fourier orthogonality and the constancy of μ. The longitudinal forcing is 2⟨(E_j:Q)s⟩. With q=E_j:Q its squared dual norm is **4[⟨wq²⟩−⟨wq⟩²/h]**. The subtraction enforces ⟨s⟩=0; omit it only if R=0, Δw=0, or the mixed statistic in that loading is zero. Summing over j gives the terms 4w_2T+4ΔwJ−4(Δw)²|R|²/h. For a nonzero Fourier direction k/|k|=e, the transverse forcing squared sums 4/μ times |χhat|² times Σ_j|(I-eeᵀ)E_je|²; the latter equals (d−1)/2 (choose e as a coordinate direction and the (d−1) off-diagonal normalized modes). Parseval gives ⟨|Q|²⟩=T and the transverse term 2(d−1)fg/μ. Dividing consistent with S=(1/(2n))Σ W and second derivative twice the coefficient yields
`−n S″ = 2(d−1)fg/μ + 4w_2 T + 4 Δw J − 4(Δw)² |R|²/h.`
Equivalently the last term is −h|X′|², and for K1≠K2 the J term is (2Δw/β²)B′. Do not divide by β in the equal-bulk case: B and X are then constant (pure dilation is an exact uniform-strain solution for every t), while the shear curvature formula remains meaningful with Δw=0.

The independent exact code solves the layer-normal displacement minimization as a quadratic in phase-constant displacement gradients, not via the Helmholtz computation. Its 36 rational layer controls in d=2,3 check the Hill base, all first derivatives, the curvature, both signs of finite t, and detect the wrong answer obtained by dropping the mean-zero correction. One anisotropic control (K1,K2,μ,f)=(1/10,10,1,1/2) gives (B(0),B′,B″/2)=(1,81/242,−405/14641) and −S″=141/484.

## Exact projected derivative locus

Fourier gives `R=fg(M−I/d)` where M is a convex combination of unit rank-one direction projections with weights |χhat(k)|²/(fg). Thus M≽0 and tr M=1. Jensen on the two phases, using ⟨Q⟩=0, gives precisely the necessary bounds
`|R|²/f ≤ J ≤ T−|R|²/g.`
They are consistent for the entire stated M simplex because tr M²≤1. No assertion is made for all derivatives of the *individual* deviatoric matrix entries.

### Confocal construction at the lower boundary

For any core volume fraction f and strictly positive target depolarization eigenvalues, choose normalized reciprocal squared core axes p_i>0, Σp_i=1, and a_i²=1/p_i. Outside axes have squared lengths a_i²+s, where s uniquely solves ∏(1+s p_i)=f^−2. Write c=∏a_i, D(v)=√∏(a_i²+v), and H(x,v)=1−Σx_i²/(a_i²+v). Set λ=0 in the core, and for points in the shell let λ∈(0,s) be the unique root H(x,λ)=0. Define an extended-by-zero potential using the **finite** integral
`ψ(x) = −(c/4) ∫_λ^s H(x,v)/D(v) dv`
inside the outer ellipsoid. This direct expression is valid in d=2 without normalization of a divergent far-field log integral. Where λ>0 the moving endpoint contributes zero to the first derivative. Thus
`∂_iψ = (c x_i/2)∫_λ^s[(a_i²+v)D(v)]^−1 dv.`
The gradient pastes continuously at both boundaries and is zero outside. In the inner ellipsoid its Hessian is diagonal with entries (1−f)F_i(p), where
`F_i(p) = [2(1−f)]^−1 ∫_0^s p_i/(1+v p_i) ∏_j(1+v p_j)^−1/2 dv.`
Its trace is 1−f by differentiating D(v)^−1. In the shell the trace from integrating the diagonal expression is c[D(λ)^−1−D(s)^−1]; the moving endpoint subtracts c/D(λ) because implicit differentiation gives Σ_i [x_i/(a_i²+λ)] ∂_i λ=2. Thus Δψ=−f there. The C¹ pasting, smooth ellipsoid boundaries and bounded a.e. Hessian for each fixed shape give compact H² and distributional source exactly χ_core−f within its outer ellipsoid, zero outside.

The map F extends continuously to the closed p-simplex: s is bounded by f^−2−1 using ∏(1+s p_i)≥1+s; continuity follows by subsequence and uniqueness of the strictly increasing root. It is face preserving with F_i(p)>0 iff p_i>0 and sums to one. To see surjectivity without a separate degree theorem, for relative-interior target y in a face apply Brouwer to the map on that face sending p to the normalization of the coordinatewise positive parts of p_i+y_i−F_i(p); the denominator is positive since the unclipped sum is one. At its fixed point all face coordinates are positive (a zero coordinate would be mapped to a positive one because y_i>0). Thus none is clipped, so the denominator is one and F(p)=y. Apply separately to each face. **This is the classical confocal depolarization tensor with Tartar's known 3D onto property**, not a claim of priority, independent of the elementary proof used here.

### Exact periodic realization and boundaries

For any fixed finite shape and any oriented core fraction, the translates and homothetic copies supply a Vitali cover of an open cell region. Take disjoint outer ellipsoids covering it a.e. In each, place its compact potential from above scaled as r²ψ((x−x0)/r) so its Hessian rescales without amplitude. The potentials are supported disjointly; each contributes an H² norm squared bounded by a fixed-shape constant times its volume (radii uniformly bounded). Hence the sum converges in periodic H²; after subtracting a constant it is exactly Δ⁻¹(χ−f). No incorrect patching of global shear fields is needed. In every core Q=(1−f)(M−I/d), giving the lower Jensen boundary. Macroscopic anisotropy is allowed.

For the upper boundary start with a χ′ core of fraction g and complement the phases globally. Q changes to −Q, but R is unchanged because ⟨Q⟩=0, and J changes to T−J. To reach any intermediate J at the same R use any two equal-fraction regions of the desired mixing volumes (an open partition up to null sets suffices) and paste the two kinds of compact ellipsoid potentials. The resulting global geometry has exact f, exact target R and mixed J; local potentials are supported within their outer ellipsoids even for the complemented type. This is not an arithmetic mixing rule for the full finite-contrast elasticity tensor.

When M has rank r<d, do the construction in its support, extend independently of the null directions on a compatible product torus, and subtract the full d-dimensional trace in Q; the core Hessian is (1−f)M with zero null coordinates. Rank one can be a simple periodic layer. Any period lattice chosen to fit the geometry is allowed. On a *fixed* lattice singular M with an irrational direction need not be attained exactly; approximate by positive definite M (exact full-rank realizers on any lattice), taking matching points in their continuous intervals. Exact arbitrary anisotropic realizers do not imply isotropic finite-t tensors, even at R=0. If desired in d=2 with R=0, a triangular torus and C6-invariant coated-disk partition forces actual elasticity isotropy for all t. Arbitrary coated-ball packings do not automatically have that shear property in d≥3.

An independent exact planar shape control uses inner axes (5,1), outer (7,5), common square shift 24, f=1/7; known elementary interior ellipse factors (b/(a+b),a/(a+b)) give M=(1/8,7/8) and J interval [81/2744,309/5488]. The code checks the aspect inversion for both f and 1−f algebraically and the implicit shell endpoint contraction in dimensions 2,3,4. This checks formulas, not a numerical simulation of a microstructure.

## Finite contrast and explicit priority check

For a base minimizer, let v be a compatible fluctuation beyond it, with reference energy norm ||v||_0, and let L(v)=√(2χ)dev v. Writing z=√(2χ)dev E_base, completing the square gives
`min_v [||v||_0²+t||z+Lv||²] = t⟨z,(I+t LL*)⁻¹ z⟩.`
The nonnegative operator LL* is ≤1/μ because ||Lv||²≤||v||_0²/μ. Positive t therefore puts the bulk secant (B(t)−B(0))/t in `[B′/(1+t/μ),B′]`. Expanding the same identity for E_j shows `(μ+ft−S(t))/t² ∈ [(-S″/2)/(1+t/μ),−S″/2]`. The inequalities reverse the factors when −μ<t<0. These are rigorous uniform bounds with the geometry fixed as parameters vary.

For f=1/2, μ=1, K1=1/10, K2=10, t=1/2, choose phase 1 as the coating to get exact B=343/262. The radial boundary matching itself uses Ax in the disk core and (B+C|x|^−d)x in the shell; constant hydrostatic traction on the exterior permits patching independently across all disks (and balls). The opposite coating gets B=1 and J=0. In any geometry with B≥343/262 the finite bulk bound forces J≥121/1048>T/2; use |R|²≤(T−J)/2 to bound −S″/2≥28461/126808. This yields S≤307533/253616 while the opposite coating gives S≥107/88, a positive gap 841/253616. These exact numbers are independently replayed with 32 rational radial tests; true isotropic planar witnesses can use the C6 packing above.

**Not a new finite-contrast separation.** [Bogdan, arXiv:2611.29402v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.29402v1), Lemma 58 / Prop. 59 / Supplement S8.12 Eq. (22), gives a stronger same-phase inequality by Jensen at the upper bulk edge. His edge spectral measure has mean (1−f)[(k+m)^−1+m^−1]/2, where (k,m) is the matrix. For softening the core (negative δ), convexity of (1+δv)^−1 bounds M above by the isotropic matrix-laminate value; for stiffening it bounds M below. In the example these mean-node values are H(1,3/2;3/62)=387/322 at the high bulk and H(1,3/2;5/6)=61/50 at the low bulk, with H the half-fraction shifted harmonic mean. The gap is 73/4025 > 841/253616. [Cherkaev–Gibiansky (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-5096(93)90006-2) already established nonrectangular coupled bulk/shear regions in planar finite contrast. Label the particular finite calculation a redundant illustrative check, not a first or improved static no-go.

- [Berryman–Milton (1988)](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/21/1/013), Eqs. (18)–(19), bounds isotropic 3D bulk by Hill shifted harmonic means using arithmetic / harmonic geometric-weighted shear averages. These squeeze to the same first derivative at equal shears. Thus even the scalar bulk slope is a classical consequence, in the isotropic 3D scope. Their shear bounds Eqs. (20)–(23) involve distinct comparison values at arbitrary unequal bulks, not in general an exact curvature, and do not give the arbitrary-anisotropy mixed-slope correction. [Valmalette et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijsolstr.2025.113580) gives general first/second sensitivity via cell problems, not this elimination.
