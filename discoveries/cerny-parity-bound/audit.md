# Independent audit: parity-distance bound for excluded quasi-Eulerian configurations

**Result checked.** The bound with leading upper coefficient $5/4$ and its exact parity-distance criterion are correct for the three stated binary configurations. The additional automaton-specific mass-profile bound in `bound.py` is correct on the same domain. Neither result proves the Černý conjecture throughout these configurations or more generally. The proof is noncomputational; the independent finite program is a falsification/control replay.

## Primary-source scope and prior comparison

The relevant source is [Dave Goel's November 18 version on hexagonmath](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00635v1), not the unrelated arXiv identifier with the same number. Lemma 5.7 makes uniform letter probabilities admissible for a binary degree-two witness and gives exactly two arcs from $E$ into the exceptional triple. Table 2 and Theorem 5.9 omit exactly $(s,x,x,s;0,1)$, $(s,s,x,x;0,1)$, and $(s,x,x,s;1,1)$; the final pair gives the numbers of boundary arcs ending at $x,y$, respectively. Hence the first two cases have boundary targets $y,s$ and class $\{1/2,1/2\}$; the last has $x,y$ and class $\{1/2,3/2\}$. These are three configurations in two stationary-vector classes. Goel's theorem applies to the **automaton** if **any** witness has an included configuration. Merely exhibiting an excluded witness alone does not show its reset bound was previously open. Goel §6 explicitly separates this issue and acknowledges the known aligned-set costs; it specifies that the unresolved automaton range starts at $n=13$ by [Kisielewicz–Kowalski–Szykuła's exhaustive binary verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.04025).

Do not compare $5/4$ solely against Berlinkov's $4$ for the entire strongly connected degree-two family. [Berlinkov's Corollary 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.3402) specializes here with normalized denominator $2n-2$ or $2n$, giving $2n^2+O(n)$; the denominator is exact because $w(y)=1$. More tightly, Goel Lemma 5.10 plus the generic $n-1$ per odd mass gives the directly derivable comparator $E+(n-1)L$, where $E,L$ are as below, with leading coefficient $7/4$. This is an inferred comparator, not a formula printed as such in Goel. Goel's highest-cost multiplicity cap from Lemma 2.4 could further save linear terms, not that leading coefficient. The new work improves the odd-stage costs and how many odd masses can be slow. A targeted review of Goel's §6, [Volkov's survey list](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15655v4) and searches of the recent quasi-Eulerian literature located no identical parity-cover criterion or matching leading bound in these three configurations, but does not establish comprehensive priority.

## Proof audit

1. **Weights and stationarity.** The $2(n-3)$ arcs entering targets outside the triple all start in $E$. All other $2(n-2)-2(n-3)=2$ arcs from $E$ terminate in the triple. Assign weight 2 outside $x,y$, and respectively $(1,1)$ or $(3,1)$ at $x,y$. The aggregate incoming mass at the triple is $(2,2,4)$ in cases A/B and $(6,2,4)$ in C. Elsewhere the incoming mass is 4. Parallel labeled arcs and loops are counted with multiplicity. Hence the row vector is fixed by the averaged transition matrix.

2. **Shortest change is shortest gain.** For a proper nonempty $K$, the averages of $wM_u\mathbf1_K$ over all words of length $d$ equal $w\mathbf1_K$, by stationarity. Thus any change at the first interesting length forces an increase at that same length. For existence, $T_i=\operatorname{span}\{wM_u-w:|u|\le i\}$ satisfies $T_{i+1}=T_1+\sum_c T_iM_c$, by writing $wM_{uc}-w=(wM_u-w)M_c+(wM_c-w)$. Equality once implies forever, and the ambient zero-sum space has dimension $n-1$. If the stable span annihilated $\mathbf1_K$, a reset followed by a path into $K$ would contradict it. Prepending each chosen gain word to the previous word keeps the inverse-image process exact. Integral mass increases strictly from the selected singleton, so every proper mass can be charged at most once; no nesting of visited sets is assumed.

3. **Even mass.** If shorter than $h$ no word changes mass, comparing preimages for $u,au$ identifies $c(s.u)=c(y.u)$ for $|u|\le h-2$, since $x.a=s$, $y.a=x$. The original parity says $c(x.u)+c(y.u)=W(K)\pmod2$ through length $h-1$. Thus if $m(K)\ge2$ and mass is even, all three seeds lie on one side. The first exit from an aligned $K$ can only be from seed $s$, because a first letter from $x,y$ would reduce the exit time from some seed by one. The successive joint reachable balls strictly grow until exit (otherwise a nonempty subset is closed), so $f\le |K|-2$. At exit $u$, the masses for $u,au$ have differing parity, giving $m(K)\le f+1$. For an aligned triple outside $K$ use its complement, with identical $m$. At even mass $2j$ A/B have $|K|=j+1$ or $j$ in the two aligned cases. C has $|K|=j$ in either. If not aligned the cost is 1. The resulting maxima are $\max(j,n-1-j)$ on $1\le j\le n-2$ for A/B and $\max(j-1,n-j-1)$ on $2\le j\le n-1$ for C. These are at least 1 for $n\ge4$.

4. **Odd mass, prescribed colors.** For $|u|\le h-2$ the indicators at $y.u,s.u$ agree and at $x.u,y.u$ are opposite. Induct on the first letter of $u$: from $s.cv$ transfer to $y.cv$ using the identity at the entire word, then to $x.v$ if $c=a$ or case B's $b$, otherwise to $s.v$. This establishes exactly the parity cover (possibly complemented according to $c(s)$). If the cover never collided, strong connectivity would imply a well-defined global labeling with both colors from the seeds. Acting by a word would permute the two colors, which forbids synchronization. Hence $\tau$ is finite, at least 1, and an odd stage has $m(K)\le\min(n-1,\tau+1)$. It is essential that the definition of collision permit paths of different lengths.

5. **Indegree and the layer count.** For $3\le h\le D$ the joint ball of radius $h-3$ has at least $h$ states. Colors in the next ball are unambiguous. A/B have a single missing incoming slot at $y$ and one extra at $s$, both color zero, so either color in the next ball has capacity twice its size. In A each earlier state contributes one arc into each color (one flip and one preserve); in B each earlier state contributes two to the opposite color, and inclusion of the earlier same-color ball adds the other inequality. Either way each later color size is at least $\lceil h/2\rceil$. In C the deviations are $-1$ at $y$, $+1$ at $x$, so the color sizes are at least $\lceil(h+1)/2\rceil$, $\lceil(h-1)/2\rceil$. When included in $K$ or $Q\setminus K$, either color then supplies mass at least $2\lfloor h/2\rfloor+1$ in C and $2\lceil h/2\rceil-1$ in A/B. Count odd integers respecting both those bounds and the actual start mass (2 or 3): the printed functions $G(h)$ are valid, $h=1,2$ naturally use all $L$ odd levels. The only arc-color statement needed is for sources in the radius $h-3$ ball, with both endpoints then certified at depth at most $h-2$. Do not assert it for a source known only at depth $h-2$ just because an endpoint happened to be reachable earlier.

## Finite cutoff and the sharper instance profile

The sum $E$ of even maxima is $3n^2/4+O(n)$ in both kinds. For $D=\min(n-1,\tau+1)\ge2$, the exact odd sums check as
$$
 O_{AB}(D)=nD-\lceil D^2/2\rceil-2,\qquad
 O_C(D)=nD-\lfloor D^2/2\rfloor.
$$
Beyond $h=2$ the clipping is inactive through $h=n-1$; the two initial entries change the uncorrected A/B sum by $-2$ and the corrections cancel in C. Thus the exact criterion $E+O(D)\le(n-1)^2$ is monotone in $D$. Replayed largest sufficient A/B,C $\tau$ at $n=13,20,30,100,1000$ are respectively $(3,4),(5,6),(8,9),(29,29),(292,293)$. The leading bound for $D\sim \alpha n$ is $3/4+\alpha-\alpha^2/2$; it reaches 1 at $\alpha=1-1/\sqrt2$ in $[0,1]$, so a fixed positive margin suffices asymptotically. At $D=n-1$ the leading coefficient is $5/4$. These criteria are sufficient, not necessary. The arithmetic helper should either validate $n\ge4,\tau\ge1$ or explicitly distinguish hypothetical arguments from stated scope.

The added function `profile(T,kind)` narrows the full list of mass levels separately at every height $h$. For an even mass in a slow stage, the same induction on the first letter makes all ordinary projections of the radius $h-2$ triple ball have the **same** indicator: use $y.cv$ and its first successor (one of the three seeds), all agreeing at suffix $v$. So either that whole ball or none belongs to $K$. For an odd mass use exactly one of the two color classes, and none if the ball already collides. All as-yet unknown vertices have weight 2. The resulting mass candidates at each height are exactly the announced necessary forms $j$ or $W(R)+j$ when even, $W(R^0)+j$ or $W(R^1)+j$ when odd, with $j\in\{0,2,\ldots,2(n-|R|)\}$. Intersection with prior layers is safe because $m\ge h$ implies every earlier constraint. Counting the remaining possible levels proves the profile bound. It is no larger than the closed formula: the uncolored ball contains at least $h+1$ states for $h\ge2$, yielding the original even bounds, and the odd colors yield the original $G$ and collision caps.

## Reproduction and controls

- The independent checker uses two layer bit masks for parity reachability, ordinary forward subset BFS for exact reset length, and a separate preimage BFS from every proper subset to find shortest mass change and gain. It checks every qualifying even stage and the cardinality of every odd mass layer, not just one greedy trajectory. It also verifies the extra profile by checking every shortest stage against its permitted mass layers.
- For each case at $n=4,5,6$, permute the multiset of prescribed free destinations over the labeled arcs at $E$, without importing the author's code. This enumerates 12, 180, 5,040 completions per case, 15,696 total. Among 13,182 strongly connected completions, 12,754 synchronize and 428 do not; every one of those 428 has no parity collision. This does not assume or prove a converse to the parity-obstruction claim. Some $n=4$ witnesses overlap an already solved Goel type, as the log records.
- Separate verified examples at $n=13,17$ in each case pass the exact criterion, have no alternative Goel degree-two witness among the solved configurations, no degree-one witness, and no one-exception positive stationary vector even with arbitrary letter probabilities. For the last check, for each potential exceptional state $r$ there is a target $q\ne r$ whose exactly two arrivals use the same label and neither starts at $r$; its stationarity forces $p=1/2$, where the computed stationary vector is uniquely scaled and exceptional at two states. Both letters have at least two cycles and ranks at most $n-2$, ruling out the single-letter one-cluster condition and also permutation-letter and binary complete reachability (no word can first reach rank $n-1$). The latter two overlaps are distinct from the exact exclusions in Goel. [Zhu's one-cluster preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19675) is cited only for its stated scope, not as an independently reviewed result. The witness checks are not a classification of all possible prior solved classes.
- A late $n=13$ case from the candidate author's separate construction is an independent negative control for **sufficiency of the test on the whole class**: $\tau=9$, exact reset length 56, but the refined profile is 164, still above the Černý target 144. The control does not challenge the theorem or conjecture.

This supports a narrowly described research result on three previously excluded configurations and a recognizable subfamily, not a global or full degree-two resolution.
