# Three unit-distance contacts with the Moser lattice

Imagine a map of points generated by four fixed moves, each usable any integer number of times. This map, the Moser lattice, is useful for building configurations with many pairs of points exactly one unit apart.

**Main result.** A point not on this map can sit exactly one unit from at most three points on it. Three is possible. Whenever it happens, the neighbors form an equilateral triangle of side √3, and the center belongs to one of four explicitly described translates of the map. This holds for arbitrary real-plane centers, not just centers from an arithmetic search grid.

The proof bounds a finite search using two real coordinate embeddings and then evaluates every candidate in exact arithmetic. The checks agree on 204 possible relative anchor positions and 12 normalized exterior centers. Translation gives the four full families.

**What this does not settle.** It does not determine the maximum number of unit-distance pairs among 25 arbitrary planar points. Under the separately published computational bounds for smaller point sets, a hypothetical 25-point set with 73 unit-distance pairs and at least 20 points in one Moser copy is confined to a specific index-three enlargement of that copy, up to isometry. Sets with no such core are unconstrained by that reduction.

**A separate 25-point result.** If 20 of the points already achieve the 20-point maximum of 54 unit pairs, then the full set has at most 72. A construction reaches 72. This rules out a 73-pair configuration containing any such subset, without assuming a lattice embedding. It uses published smaller-size bounds that have not been externally refereed.
