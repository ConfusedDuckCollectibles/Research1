# Independent near-Moser 25/73 reduction audit

**Pass, conditional as stated.** Taking the externally unrefereed October computational bounds \(u(22)\le60, u(23)\le64, u(24)\le68\), any 25-point 73-edge set with at least 20 points in an isometric Moser copy lies entirely in that copy or in one of its two explicit index-three extensions. The two extensions are isometric, so the near-Moser case gives a single ambient group up to Euclidean isometry; it does not exclude that group or prove every 73-edge set has a 20-point Moser core.

## Graph, propagation, and modular checks

Normalize the fixed Moser copy to \(\mathcal M\). Deleting a vertex shows minimum full degree at least 5; every outside point has at most three \(\mathcal M\)-contacts by the independent contact audit, so its outside degree is at least 2. One or two outside points are impossible, and three give at most 12 edges touching the triple, leaving 61 on 22 points. For \(k=4,5\), all labelled outside graphs are generated directly by bit masks; rejecting any K4 or K2,3 subgraph is necessary (at most three mutually equidistant planar points and at most two common unit neighbors for distinct points). For each outside point with inside-contact count \(t_i\), the exact necessary range is \(5-d_F(i)\le t_i\le3\), intersected with nonnegative integers. Deleting any nonempty subset \(T\) leaves exactly \(73-\sum_{i\in T}(d_F(i)+t_i)+e_F(T)\) edges, including non-induced outside four-cycles. The cap values \(54,57,60,64,68\) for orders 20–24 are used only on induced deletions.

Initialize membership in \(\frac13\mathcal M\) at every \(t_i=3\), by the exact four cosets. On any triangle one remaining vertex is obtained from two known vertices by a rotation through \(\pm60^\circ\) about one vertex; multiplication by \(\eta\) and by \(\eta^{-1}=\bar\eta=1-\eta\) both preserve the module. On a four-cycle, both points not used as opposite centers are the two intersections of the equal unit circles. Their midpoint is the midpoint of the centers, so the opposite-sum identity holds; no drawing simplicity assumption is needed.

The independent exact profile census identifies 9 candidates on four outside vertices, with 21 graph/contact profiles passing deletions, all fully propagated. On five outside vertices 147 candidate graphs yield 762 profiles; the \((e(F),\text{unassigned})\) counts are \((5,0):12,(6,0):210,(7,0):480,(7,3):60\). Canonicalizing all label permutations shows the 60 residual profiles are one gem orbit: universal center attached to a path P4, rim tips with \(t=3\), all other counts 2. The only orbit with \(t_i\le1\) has a gem whose center has \(t=1\) and all four rim points have \(t=3\). This separate orbit is already propagated, but its center needs an extra residue argument below; no zero-contact case occurs. The replay uses actual labelled adjacency matrices and safely identifies all four-vertex subsets of minimum induced degree at least 2 as containing a 4-cycle (K4 already forbidden).

In a gem the unit path on the hub's unit circle is forced to step by the same sign of \(60^\circ\) on each of three steps; a sign reversal repeats a vertex. The tips are antipodal. Their half-difference is a unit in \(\frac16\mathcal M\), and lies in \(\frac13\mathcal M\) by the following exact arithmetic. In a general unit of \(\frac1{2m}\mathcal M\), for integer \(m>0\), vanishing irrational norm component gives \(ad=bc\). Represent the two integer rows as \(u(s,t),v(s,t)\) with primitive common pair \((s,t)\). They satisfy
\[
(s^2+st+t^2)(3u^2+5uv+3v^2)=12m^2 .
\]
For primitive \((s,t)\) the first factor is odd, and the second is even only when both \(u,v\) are even, by testing the four pairs modulo 2. This proves the claimed denominator removal. Hence the gem center, the half-sum of the tips, lies in \(\frac13\mathcal M\), and rotations give the two interior rim points too.

For exact unit directions in \(\frac13\mathcal M\), the right side of the same factored equation is 27. A primitive first factor has 3-adic valuation at most 1: modulo 3 its only divisibility occurs when \(s=t\bmod3\), and then \(s=t+3j\) gives \(s^2+st+t^2=3(t^2+3tj+3j^2)\) with \(t\not\equiv0\bmod3\). Thus it is 1 or 3 and the second factor is divisible by 3, forcing \(uv=0\bmod3\). This already proves any possible unit residue has at least one zero row block, without enumeration. Independently enumerating the two primitive norms and their two-variable second factors gives 42 units and reproduces the complete 5×5 count matrix. In particular precisely four nonzero classes have more than one available unit to the original \(\mathcal M\), and they are \(0011,0022,1100,2200\) with three each. For any fixed off point the number of possible contacts to the infinite \(\mathcal M\) equals the size of its unit-difference residue class (anchor subtraction is bijective), so two actual contacts force a special class.

For the one-contact gem hub, the half-sum of the tip residues cannot be a nonzero residue in both row blocks, because that has no unit directions to \(\mathcal M\). Opposite tip residues within one type would give the zero class, disallowed for an off point. The remaining case puts the hub in the same special type. All \(k\le5\) outside graphs of minimum degree at least 2 are connected (two components would each require at least three vertices), and no cross-type unit edge exists by the modular argument. This finishes the claimed classification. Each extended ambient group is degree-24 as a unit graph (18+3+3 directions); not all 25 subsets have been enumerated.

## Isometry and an analytic optional diamond check

Let \(A=\mathbb Z[\eta]\). The reflection \(S(z)=\omega\overline z\) exchanges \(A\) and \(\omega A\), preserves \(\mathcal M\), and has \(S^2=1\). Since \(S((1+\eta)/3)=\omega(2-\eta)/3\equiv-\omega(1+\eta)/3\bmod\mathcal M\), it exchanges the two index-three groups. Their isometry does not depend on a point set choosing one of them.

In a seeded outside diamond, with shared-edge vertex \(B\) and rim tips \(A,D\) in the special classes, their adjacency to \(B\) makes both tips the same type as \(B\). Their vectors from \(B\) differ by multiplication by \(\eta^{\pm2}\), since the two equilateral tips are on opposite sides of a unit edge. Multiplication by \(\eta\) acts as \(-1\) in either order-three quotient (indeed \(\eta(1+\eta)/3+(1+\eta)/3=\eta\)), so \(\eta^2\) acts as identity. Both tips have equal class \(a\), and the other shared-edge vertex has class \(2a-b\) by the opposite-sum identity. For \(a,b\in\{\pm1\}\) this is \(b\) or zero. This independently proves the qualitative optional refinement; the theorem does not rely on the optional 72/12 indexed-sample census.
