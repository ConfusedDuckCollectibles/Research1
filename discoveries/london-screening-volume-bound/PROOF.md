# Sharp three-axis equilibrium London screening at fixed active volume

## Observable and assumptions

Let a specimen lie in a known ball B_R in vacuum. Use the local, linear, vortex-free, static London model with unchanged normal-state permeability μ0 throughout. Exclude independent magnetic backgrounds, finite-frequency normal screening, fluxoid/trapped-field sectors, transport currents and hysteresis. An applied uniform induction B0 e is prescribed at infinity (B0>0, |e|=1). Let q(x)=λ(x)^(-2) denote a measurable scalar stiffness and vanish outside B_R. Fix an independently enforced upper cap Q=λ_min^(-2)>0 on q everywhere. For a support budget |{q>0}|≤ f |B_R| the weaker integrated constraint ∫q ≤ Q f |B_R| follows.

Let S_e(q)=−μ0(e·m)/(2π B0 R³), where m=(1/2)∫ x×j(x) dx is the induced SI dipole. S=1 for a perfectly excluding sphere (χ_ext=−3/2 when M is normalized to the ball and H0=B0/μ0). Let Sbar=(S_e1+S_e2+S_e3)/3 for any orthonormal basis. Because the response is linear in the uniform applied field, Sbar is independent of basis and is the normalized trace, not a single-axis reading.

**Theorem.** For fixed R,Q and f∈[0,1], the unique maximizer of Sbar over measurable 0≤q≤Q with ∫q≤Q f |B_R| is q*=Q 1_{a<|x|<R}, a=R(1−f)^(1/3), up to null sets. This in particular maximizes over arbitrary active supports of volume ≤f |B_R|. For symmetric tensor q with 0≼q≼ QI and a support-volume bound, scalar domination by Q on the support gives the same upper bound, attained by the scalar shell (no tensor uniqueness or trace-weighted budget claimed).

## Variational proof

For fixed e let A0=(B0/2)e×x. Write the total potential A=A0+a_ind with a_ind in divergence-free homogeneous Sobolev space D^(1,2)(R³) (completion in ∥∇a_ind∥₂, represented in L⁶). Minimize
\[
 E_q(e)=\inf_{a_{ind},\phi}\frac{1}{2\mu_0}\left(\int_{\mathbb R^3}|\nabla\times a_{ind}|^2+\int_{B_R}q|A_0+a_{ind}-\nabla\phi|^2\right).
\]
This is the response-energy functional with fixed external applied field, using the induced field for its field-energy term. The vortex-free scalar phase potential φ can be taken in H¹(B_R); where q vanishes, eliminate potential closure issues by minimizing over g in the closed L²(B_R) subspace generated by q^(1/2)∇φ, with phase term ∥q^(1/2)(A0+a_ind)−g∥². For each a_ind the g minimizer is an orthogonal projection. The reduced quadratic has a unique a_ind minimizer by coercivity of the curl norm on the divergence-free space, the Sobolev inequality, and a bounded-ball restriction into L². These choices describe the zero-winding linear response class. Put W=q^(1/2)(A0+a_ind)−g at the minimizer, j=−q^(1/2)W/μ0. Since W is orthogonal to weighted gradients, the extended compactly supported j is divergence free. Variation in a_ind gives Ampère's law in weak form; variation in the direction a_ind itself gives ∥curl a_ind∥²=−∫q^(1/2)W·a_ind. Using W⊥g yields
\[
 E_q(e)=\frac{1}{2\mu_0}\int q^{1/2}W\cdot A_0=-\tfrac12 B_0 e\cdot m
        =\frac{\pi B_0^2R^3}{\mu_0}S_e(q).
\]
This also shows positivity, and linearity of the minimizing response in e gives the trace relation. For tensor support domination the phase formulation before closure immediately yields monotonicity of the minimized energy under the pointwise Loewner order.

For radial q*, the solution has φ=0, with full A*=(B0/2)(u(r)/r)(e×xhat). This tangential, divergence-free field is orthogonal to weighted gradients; in its annuli imposing Ampère's law gives u''=(2/r²+q*)u, matching u and u' at interfaces. The regular interior starts u~C r². In the exterior u=r²−S R³/r (the decaying term reproduces the SI dipole). The coefficient of r² fixes C>0. Indeed, start w=u/r² from w(0)=1; (r⁴w')'=r⁴q*w shows w>0 and w'≥0 globally and w approaches a finite strictly positive exterior limit. Divide by that limit. Consequently u/r=rw is strictly increasing on r>0. The same solution applies to each e_i and ∑_i |A*^(i)|²=2(B0 u/(2r))²=:Wrad(r), strictly increasing. Substitute all three shell minimizers with zero gradient as trials for arbitrary q:
\[
 \sum_i(E_q(e_i)-E_{q_*}(e_i))\le (2\mu_0)^{-1}\int_{B_R}(q-q_*)W_{rad}.
\]
For 0<f<1, subtract Wrad(a) inside the integral; the residual integrand is nonpositive both in the interior (q−q*≥0, Wrad−Wrad(a)<0) and in the shell (q−q*≤0, Wrad−Wrad(a)>0). The removed term is nonpositive by the budget. Equality therefore requires q=q* a.e. For f=0 the only competitor is zero; for f=1 apply the trial directly, as Wrad>0 a.e. The competitor may be nonspherical, disconnected or only measurable. If one requires continuous q and 0<f<1, the value is a supremum: smooth radial tapers inside the extremal shell maintain the budget and converge in radial L¹ through uniformly bounded coefficients away from r=0; continuous dependence of the radial ODE from the inner a>0 gives the limit.

## Finite calibration and equal-exterior counterexample

Use dimensionless x=R√Q and s=a√Q. In the shell set X=r√Q; the interior normalization u(s)=s², u'(s)=2s is convenient before final external rescaling. The two fundamental solutions are v_±(X)=exp(±(X−s))(1∓1/X), and the solution is c_+v_++c_-v_-, c_±=(s²±3s+3)/2. At s=0 take the regular limit (c_+=c_-=3/2, with overall scale immaterial). Matching to the exterior gives S=(xu'(x)−2u(x))/(xu'(x)+u(x)), hence
\[
 S_*(f;x)=1+3/x^2-(3/x)(1+z)/(1-z),\quad
 z=\frac{s^2-3s+3}{s^2+3s+3}e^{-2(x-s)} .
\]
The regular solid sphere has z0=e^(−2x), equivalently coth(x) in place of (1+z)/(1−z). The f=0 formula gives zero. The normalized field in the center of a hollow shell is 3x/[3s cosh(x−s)+(s²+3)sinh(x−s)], matching the independent inside-shell formula in Prat-Camps et al. Eq. (15); their general susceptibility treatment is not this arbitrary-layout constrained optimum.

For x=100 and s=98 the active fraction is 7351/125000 = 0.058808, yet S>0.969 by rational bounds (replay.cjs). The solid response at Q is strictly greater than this shell response by the strict trial inequality from the f=1 proof. The solid response is continuous and strictly increasing with q>0, by its radial formula/ODE or trial at the stronger solid versus a smaller one (A≠0 a.e.). It tends to 0 as q→0 by the a_ind=0, φ=0 trial. Thus a unique constant q'∈(0,Q) gives a uniform solid ball with the **exact same exterior vector magnetic field** as the 5.8808% shell at every point r>R, for any uniform applied-field magnitude/direction within the linear model. The radial l=1 solution is a pure exterior dipole, not only a far-field fit. Both are vortex-free equilibria, and the spherical shell is simply connected. This pair tests volume identifiability only; both have nonzero superconducting stiffness. Neither represents a measured LaH10 sample.

A certified lower response Sbar_L rules out all scalar q (or the tensor-support extension) with support of volume ≤f0 |B_R| when Sbar_L>S_*(f0;x). The response-only threshold is sharp because reducing the stiffness continuously in the extremal shell spans [0,S_*]. If the half-volume criterion refers to actual known target-phase volume V_t (not the surrounding ball), take f0=V_t/(2|B_R|), and establish that measured stiffness is attributable to that phase. Positive semidefiniteness gives the conservative Sbar ≥ S_single/3; do not otherwise identify a one-axis NV map with the three-excitation trace. For x=100 the ideal saturated full-minus-half-volume gap obeys 0<S_full−S_*(1/2;100)<3.5×10^(−19). This shows how small the gap can be for large independently calibrated R/λ_min; it is not a measured λ ratio or noise floor for LaH10. Calibrated nonuniform excitation (higher multipoles), interior magnetic access or independently calibrated phase-specific thermodynamics are outside the uniform-field alias. The enclosing-ball cutoff is sufficient, not optimal for a known nonspherical target shape; do not use the vacuum kernel for a DAC without controlling the walls and background.
