# A polynomial descent theorem for the Erdős–Straus equation

An AI-assisted research note. The proof is elementary algebra. The accompanying symbolic replay checks selected identities and examples; it is not the proof. Not peer reviewed; no priority claim.

## Theorem

Let R = Q[X₁,…,Xₖ], and let P ∈ R be nonconstant and irreducible. Suppose that the only units of R/(P) are the nonzero rational constants. If nonzero A, B, C ∈ R satisfy the identity

    4/P = 1/A + 1/B + 1/C

in the fraction field of R, then either **A, B and C all belong to Q[P]**, or, after permutation, **B = −A and C = P/4**.

In particular, every such identity that can produce positive denominators at even one input depends on the parameters only through P. There are no exceptional prime values in this algebraic statement. The hypotheses hold when the highest-degree homogeneous part of P is irreducible over Q, for example when P = X² + Y².

This is an obstruction to formal polynomial identities for a fixed target P, not to arbitrary rational-function evaluations, factorization-based searches, or a new choice of P whose quotient has nonconstant units. It does not settle the Erdős–Straus conjecture or supply a new prime residue class.

## Proof

Write a bar for reduction modulo P, and d = deg P, where degree means total degree. If a polynomial reduces to a nonzero rational constant, then it is constant, has degree at least d, or, at degree exactly d, is affine in P. Indeed, the polynomial differs from its constant residue by a multiple of P. If a product reduces to a nonzero constant, each factor is a unit modulo P and hence reduces to a nonzero rational constant. We will also use Q(P) ∩ R = Q[P]: if h(P)/k(P) lies in R for coprime h,k ∈ Q[T], substitution in a Bézout identity shows that k(P) divides 1 in R, so k is constant.

For the sufficient condition on P, the initial ideal of (P) in the total-degree filtration is generated by its leading homogeneous part P_top: the leading homogeneous part of each PQ is P_top Q_top. Thus the associated graded ring of R/(P) is R/(P_top), a domain if P_top is irreducible. Degrees in the quotient filtration then add on products of nonzero elements. A unit and its inverse both have degree zero, so they are constants. Irreducibility of P also follows, since any factorization of P would factor P_top.

Clearing the original denominators gives 4ABC = P(AB+BC+CA); irreducibility forces P to divide at least one of A, B, C. The following cases count divisibility in the polynomial ring, not at specialized integer values.

### Exactly one denominator is divisible by P

Write C = P R₀ and P ∤ AB. The cleared equation is

    (4R₀−1)AB = P R₀(A+B).

Hence W := 4R₀ = 1+PU with U ∈ R. If U=0, then C=P/4 and A+B=0, the stated cancellation. Otherwise

    4UAB = W(A+B).

Write A=D a, B=D b with gcd(a,b)=1. The sum a+b is nonzero. Since gcd(ab,a+b)=1, the equation gives ab | W; set W=abe. It then gives

    4UD = e(a+b),    U | (a+b),

the latter because gcd(U,e)=1 follows from W=1+PU. Each of a,b,e has a nonzero constant residue modulo P.

If U is constant, W has degree d. All its nonconstant factors have degree at least d, hence each of a,b,e is either constant or affine in P. Then D and all three denominators belong to Q[P].

If U is nonconstant, its degree is deg a + deg b + deg e − d. Since it divides the nonzero sum a+b, we obtain

    min(deg a,deg b) + deg e ≤ d.

If both a,b are nonconstant, interchange them so deg a ≤ deg b. Then deg a=d and e is constant. If exactly one is constant, say a, and e is nonconstant, then deg e=d. In either situation a,e are in Q[P], and deg U=deg b, so a+b=λU for some λ ∈ Q nonzero. Substitution into W=abe=1+PU shows

    b(P−λae) = −Pa−λ.

The coefficient of b is nonzero because its residue is −λ ā ē, so b ∈ Q(P)∩R = Q[P], and then so are U,D,A,B,C.

If both a,b are constant their nonzero sum cannot be divisible by nonconstant U. The only remaining case is a,e constant and b nonconstant, up to interchange. Then ae(a+b)=a²e+1+PU, so U divides the constant a²e+1. That constant is zero, giving 4UD=PU/a and therefore D=P/(4a), contrary to P ∤ A.

### Exactly two denominators are divisible by P

Write B=P R₁, C=P S₁ with P ∤ A. The cleared equation reads

    A(4R₁S₁−R₁−S₁) = P R₁S₁.

Modulo P we obtain (4R̄₁−1)(4S̄₁−1)=1. Thus R̄₁ and S̄₁ are rational constants, and if one is zero then both are zero.

**Both residues zero.** Write B=P²D a, C=P²D b with gcd(a,b)=1. Clearing the two reciprocals after subtracting 1/A gives

    A(a+b) = P D ab(4A−P).

The sum a+b is nonzero, and gcd(a+b,ab)=1, so ab divides A. Write A=abE. Since P ∤ E, we have a+b=PF for some F, and then EF=D(4A−P). As gcd(E,4A−P)=gcd(E,P)=1, we have D=EH for some H and

    a+b = P H(4A−P).

In particular, deg(a+b) ≤ max(deg a,deg b) ≤ deg A. If deg A>d, the right-hand side has degree greater than deg A; if deg A<d it has degree at least 2d. Thus deg A=d. Degree comparison now forces both H and 4A−P to be constant and max(deg a,deg b)=d. Since deg A=deg a+deg b+deg E, exactly one of a,b has degree d and the other and E are constant. As A is affine in P, the nonconstant factor is affine in P too, and A,B,C ∈ Q[P].

**Both residues nonzero.** Write R₁=D r, S₁=D s with gcd(r,s)=1. All of D,r,s have nonzero constant residues. With H=4Drs−r−s, the equation is AH=PDrs. Reducing modulo any irreducible factor of r or s gives gcd(H,rs)=1. Consequently H | PD. Also P | H because P ∤ A. Thus H=PG with G | D, and AG=Drs.

If D is constant, G is constant and deg H=d. With both r,s nonconstant the term 4Drs would force deg H≥2d; with both constant H would be constant. Therefore, up to interchange, r=a is constant and H=(4Da−1)s−a=PG, where the coefficient of s cannot vanish since H has degree d. So s is affine in P, giving A,B,C ∈ Q[P].

If D is nonconstant, the product 4Drs has strictly higher degree than each of r,s. From H=PG and G|D, we get deg r+deg s≤d. If r=a,s=b are constant, then a+b cannot vanish modulo P because 4Dab is nonzero there. From 4abD−(a+b)=PG and G | D, it follows that G divides the nonzero constant a+b. Thus G is constant and D is affine in P. Otherwise exactly one of r,s is nonconstant and has degree d; say r=a and s=b+cP with a,b,c nonzero rational constants. Then deg G=deg D and G=λD, with λ nonzero rational. The equation is

    D(4ab+(4ac−λ)P) = a+b+cP.

Its right-hand side has degree d. Since D is nonconstant, degree comparison gives 4ac=λ, and D is affine in P. In both cases A,B,C ∈ Q[P].

### All three denominators are divisible by P

Write A=PU, B=PV, C=PW. Then 4=1/U+1/V+1/W. If all three quotients were nonconstant, the degree of 4UVW would exceed that of each of UV,UW,VW. Take U constant, up to permutation. If 4−1/U=0, then V=−W, yielding the stated cancellation. Otherwise (4−1/U)VW=V+W again forces at least one of V,W to be constant by degree comparison, and then the other is constant as well. This completes the proof.

The same formal proof replaces 4 by any nonzero scalar over any field, with the same hypothesis that all quotient units are nonzero scalars. The arithmetic corollary below concerns the rational-coefficient equation with numerator 4.

## Consequence for Gaussian norm templates

For P=X²+Y², every fixed template that works at a positive prime value has A=f(P), B=g(P), C=h(P) for rational-coefficient polynomials f,g,h. Their integrality, where achieved, is constant on the corresponding classes modulo a cleared coefficient denominator L. If such a triple is positive for unbounded values, its leading coefficients are positive and it is eventually positive throughout those classes. In particular it gives no new infinite prime support beyond ordinary one-variable polynomial congruences. For primitive classes, the essentially complete list of those is given by [Elsholtz–Tao, Proposition 1.9 and §10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.1010).

A finite repertoire leaves infinitely many primes uncovered **inside each** of the six residual classes 1, 121, 169, 289, 361, 529 modulo 840. To see this, discard cancellation triples. Choose a common modulus M divisible by 840 and all cleared coefficient denominators, and also by the primes dividing the nonzero cleared constant terms of the reduced denominators. In any one of the six classes, choose a square root s modulo 840 and lift it to a unit modulo M (at each added prime choose a nonzero residue). Dirichlet gives infinitely many primes p≡s² mod M. They are 1 mod 4 and hence represented by X²+Y².

If a collapsed triple works at unbounded primes in that progression, it gives positive integral denominators at all sufficiently large integers n≡s² mod M, in particular at odd squares n=(s+jM)². At such n, a denominator with zero constant term is divisible by n, since n is coprime to the cleared coefficient denominator. A denominator with nonzero constant term is coprime to n, because its cleared numerator is congruent to its nonzero cleared constant term modulo n. At least one denominator polynomial has zero constant term by the cleared identity, and not all three do, since 3/n<4/n. This would give a Type I or II solution at an odd square, contrary to the classical square-class obstruction; see [Elsholtz–Tao, Proposition 1.6 and the discussion before Proposition 1.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.1010). So every triple works at most finitely often on that progression, and a fixed finite repertoire leaves all sufficiently large primes there unhandled. The modulus and its coefficient factors are only part of this corollary, not an exception clause in the theorem.

This consequence uses the classical obstruction after the new descent step. It is not a set of counterexamples to Erdős–Straus, and does not obstruct an infinite adaptive repertoire or guarantee represented primes in any given class for arbitrary target P.

## Why the unit condition matters

For P=4uv−u−v, the reciprocals with denominators uv, Pu, Pv give 4/P. The irreducible polynomial P defines a quotient isomorphic to Q[t,t⁻¹], with t=4u−1 and t⁻¹=4v−1 since (4u−1)(4v−1)=4P+1. There uv has image (t+2+t⁻¹)/16, which is not constant; hence this is a genuinely multivariate, noncancelling identity outside the theorem. At (u,v)=(3,92), it gives the prime 1009 and denominators (276,3027,92828). In fact u=3 leaves p=11v−3, and CRT and Dirichlet give infinitely many positive prime evaluations in each residual class, with the extra condition p≡−3 mod 11. This is an existing Type II parametrization ([Elsholtz–Tao, Proposition 2.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.1010)), not a new coverage result.

The closest published-form multivariable formulas we checked are [Ghermoul, arXiv:2508.07383, equations (18)–(21) and (26)–(29)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07383). Three target polynomials there are reducible: (4x−1)(4yz−1), (8y−3)(4x−3), and (2x−1)². The remaining target is P₂=4zF−(4x−1), where F=4xy−x−y. It is irreducible (primitive and linear in z), but for b=(4y−1)z−1 one has 4Fb−1=(4y−1)P₂, giving nonconstant quotient units, as deg F=deg b=2<deg P₂=3. Its denominators zF, P₂Fb, zFb are exactly the Elsholtz–Tao Remark 2.5 formula with (c,e,f)=(z,4y−1,4x−1); its coverage conjecture is not assumed here.

Other related work concerns different questions: [Bradford–Ionascu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4025) consider unit fractions in rings of integers of quadratic fields; [Bright–Loughran](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02526) prove arithmetic obstructions on prescribed integer fibers; [Dahan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24035) studies fixed-depth divisor search and a quadratic-form construction of a fixed-shift blind set; [Epstein](https://doi.org/10.1080/00029890.2024.2379749) treats alternating rational-function decompositions; and [Houze, Corollary D](https://aixiv.online/pdf/2611.00365) discusses the classical finite-repertoire obstruction for univariate output polynomials. None of these statements, nor a theorem about a single variable n, directly forces polynomial denominators in a fixed multivariate parametrization to lie in Q[P]. No matching general statement was located in these scope checks.
