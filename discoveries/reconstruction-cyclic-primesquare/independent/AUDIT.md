# Independent ordinary vertex-deck reconstruction audit

## Result

The proof is correct for connected simple circulants of order \(p^2\), and for the stated prime-power no-full-fibre companion. Absolute reconstruction against arbitrary simple graphs, not merely within the circulant class, follows from the deficient completion. This is a scoped theorem about deleting **one edge** and using the resulting graph's ordinary **vertex** deck. It is not general reconstruction, edge-deck reconstruction, or the prime-square classification of all vertex-transitive hosts.

**Precision edit:** The \(2\)-power clause must count repeated exponents in a frequency sum. At \(t=n/2\), spectrum class \(M=\{\pm1\}\) yields \(\zeta^t+\zeta^{-t}=2\zeta^{n/2}=-2\), not the unweighted sum over the singleton step orbit. This does not change the theorem. The zero fibre has coefficient zero at index 0, so equality of evaluations forces both coefficients to agree there. The other potential full fibre from the pair \(\{\pm n/4\}\) cannot be a step orbit in this no-full-fibre theorem (symmetry of \(S\) would force both members). The rest of the fibres each hold at most one atom of the signed step pair, and the fibre coefficient test gives equality of the pairs. If discussing non-edge steps as well, only the quarter-step pair gives a full nonzero fibre and evaluation zero.

## Proof checks and independent derivations

1. **Absolute same-parent reduction.** For \(G=F-e\), the ordinary deck determines size by \(\sum_H|E(H)|/(n-2)\) and therefore the deleted degree on each card. A card deleting a deficient vertex \(u\) has no degree \(r-2\), because the other deficient vertex \(v\) is not adjacent to it. Any same-deck \(G'\) has degree list \((r^{n-2},(r-1)^2)\), and its matched deficient deletion forces its two deficient vertices nonadjacent as well. In both graphs the regular completion by joining these two vertices exists, and is a unique \(r\)-regular extension of the common deficient-deletion card: add a vertex adjacent to exactly the \(r\) degree-\((r-1)\) card vertices. Hence \(G'\) is an edge deletion of the same \(F\), with no hypothesis on its original construction as a Cayley graph.

   If \(e\) is triangular in \(F\), take a common neighbour of the deficient pair in \(G\). Its card, identified as deleting a degree-\(r\) vertex, has two degree-\((r-2)\) vertices. Both must be the deficient pair and both need the missing neighbour in any reconstruction. Every remaining degree-\((r-1)\) card vertex arose from degree \(r\), and all degree-\(r\) card vertices need no attachment. Thus the graph is forced. The two degree deficiencies and the known removed degree prevent accidentally attaching a surviving deficient but untouched vertex. This leaves pairs of triangle-free edges.

2. **Derivative equals resolvent.** Characteristic-polynomial derivatives are sums of card polynomials by multilinearity. A same-deck pair therefore has characteristic polynomials differing by a constant, not necessarily already known equal. With common diagonal resolvent \(R_{uu}=\rho\), the rank-two update \(e_u e_v^\top+e_v e_u^\top\) on \(zI-A_F\) gives the determinant factor \((1+R_{uv})^2-\rho^2\). Thus \((R_e-R_f)(2+R_e+R_f)=C/P_F\), with the second factor tending to 2. The rational function \(R_e-R_f\) decays as \(O(z^{-n})\) and has denominator degree at most the number of distinct host eigenvalues \(d<n\) in the circulant. Hence it vanishes. This is not a claim of universal polynomial reconstruction; the usual constant ambiguity has been explicitly eliminated.

   Optional modest strengthening of this intermediate step: for *any* simple walk-regular common host, if \(d=n\) then that same bound implies \(R_e-R_f=c/P_F\). Substitution gives \(c(2+R_e+R_f)=C\); for actual edges \(R_e+R_f=2z^{-2}+O(z^{-3})\), so again \(c=0\). This does not imply general walk-regular reconstruction, because endpoint orbit separation remains necessary.

3. **Arithmetic.** For \(n=p^a\), \(d=n/p\), the rational kernel of evaluation at a primitive \(n\)-th root is exactly the coefficient vectors constant on fibres mod \(d\); divide by \(\Phi_n(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}X^{jd}\). At \(a=1\), all subset comparisons omit coefficient index 0, so equality is ordinary equality, giving the prime-order result. The connected host has Perron eigenvalue simple; in Fourier notation a nonzero frequency with a nontrivial phase on a unit step has eigenvalue strictly below \(r\).

   In the no-full-fibre companion, the primitive stabilizer equals the actual multiplier group \(M\): otherwise a nonzero fibre difference forces one whole fibre against an empty one. Connectedness supplies a unit step; its partial fibre is not preserved by \(1+d\), which translates the fibre transitively, forbidding the primitive eigenvalue from the lower conductor field \(\mathbb Q(\zeta^p)\). The full frequency class is therefore \(M\), not a mixture with nonunits. For odd \(p\), avoidance of \(1+d\) avoids the unique order-\(p\) subgroup in the unit group, giving injection mod \(p\). Its orbits have no repetitions for any nonzero step; away from the zero fibre, distinct elements of \(Mt\) are in distinct fibres mod \(d\) because \(v_p(t)\le a-2\) means an equality mod \(d\) forces equal multipliers mod \(p\). If \(d\mid t\), the whole multiset is in the zero fibre but excludes 0; that fibre comparison still works. For \(p=2,a\ge3\), \(M\) contains \(-1\) and avoids the involution of \(\langle5\rangle\), so \(M=\{\pm1\}\); use the explicit multiplicity qualification above. For \(a=2,p=2\), connected symmetry forces both odd residues, so the companion is vacuous; \(C_4,K_4\) directly prove the main theorem at four vertices.

4. **Prime square with a full fibre.** Such a fibre is nonzero and makes all nonzero \(p\)-divisible steps triangular: translating within a full fibre preserves membership. A candidate pair left for spectral comparison thus has unit steps. If some nonzero fibre is partial, the primitive stabilizer \(M\) preserves all matched partial patterns. Since \(1+p\) cycles each nonzero fibre, \(M\) injects mod \(p\) and forces separate conductors. Primitive coefficient comparison yields \(Mt=Ms\), hence \(m=s/t\bmod p^2\) lies in \(M\). The order-\(p\) eigenvalues compare the fibre masses \(w_r\). Their equality stabilizer \(H\) preserves these masses exactly: in the degree-\(p\) coefficient test the constant difference must be zero because the coefficient at 0 agrees. Since these eigenvalues neither meet primitive weights nor the top weight \(r\), the \(\lambda_p\)-residue also implies \(H\bar t=H\bar s\), so \(\bar m\in H\). Combining \(m\in M\) and \(\bar m\in H\) preserves full/empty fibres as well as the partial patterns. It would be false simply to identify the primitive stabilizer with all actual multipliers in this mixed case.

   If no nonzero fibre is partial, the host is a lexicographic join pattern over a connected prime quotient with identical internal fibres. If the internal graph has an edge, every inter-fibre edge has a common neighbour in one endpoint's fibre, and every internal edge has a common neighbour in a full neighbouring fibre; the triangle argument handles all. Otherwise uniformly filled nonzero fibres give the quotient blow-up with empty inner fibres. Nontrivial quotient eigenvalues are nonzero by the prime coefficient test and differ from degree; primitives are zero. The quotient residue identifies a multiplier preserving the quotient step set, and \(s/t\) lifts it because every nonzero fibre is either full or empty. No mixing of conductor-weight classes remains in either branch.

## Exact controls

The sibling independent checker uses integer polynomial remainders in the cyclotomic field, not the primary walk-column calculation. It checks all connected symmetric circulant patterns of orders 9 and 25 on triangle-free pairs (14 and 4,092 hosts); exact equal residues for 30 pairs at 25 all have a real preserving multiplier. The separate no-full-fibre sweep checks every edge pair for 48 hosts at order 16 (the half-step occurs in 24, the quarter-step in none) and 4,788 hosts at order 27. The totals have direct counts: \(16\) has two independent bundles of opposite unit fibres with choices \(3^2-1\), one even conflict with 3 choices, and the singleton half-step optional; \(27\) has three unit symmetry-paired fibre choices each with 7 partial/empty subsets, excluding all empty, one more nonunit pair with 7 choices, and the zero-fibre pair optional, giving \(2\cdot7(7^3-1)\). These controls test arithmetic and labels, not exhaustive arbitrary-graph deck isomorphism. An exact fraction-free determinant example independently verifies the rank-two update sign.

At order 289, cyclotomic class calculation directly obtains primitive class \([1,38,251,288]\) and lower class \([17,272]\). All spectral residues for steps 17 and 68 match, those for the triangle-free unit steps 1 and 38 differ, and the respective third walk numbers are 469 and 177. The two nonunit deletions are nonisomorphic independently of the author's iterative colour labels: define a profile by degree, number of deficient neighbours, and counts of neighbours having respectively 0, 1, or 2 deficient neighbours. The number of profile \((42,0,42,0,0)\) vertices with exactly two neighbours of profile \((42,0,41,1,0)\) is 30 versus 32. The card removing neighbour 6 from deletion 17 has exactly the two deficient card vertices \(0,17\) at degree 40, and all submaximal-degree vertices are exactly the attachments. This is a genuine limitation of the spectral argument alone near the theorem, not a pair of nonisomorphic graphs with the same ordinary vertex deck. ## Primary-source comparison

- [Kocay 1984, §§1,4 and Theorem 4.17](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/640C6BB6DDA6677E4C7694098111FA94/S1446788700022291a.pdf/partial-automorphisms-and-the-reconstruction-conjecture.pdf) explicitly uses the classical common \(r\)-regular ambient graph of two-deficient same-deck competitors (pp. 318–319), refers out for the cubic case, and specializes further to quartic cases. The common parent, degree deductions and marked-card simplicity are not novelty.
- [Beezer 1987, Orbit polynomial graphs of prime order](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0012365X87900239) provides the prime-order adjacency-constant orbital mechanism. The primary publisher abstract and subsequent [Fiol's Quotient-polynomial graphs](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2015.09.053) were available; do not attribute a general prime-square result to it. [Muzychuk–Tinhofer's circulant recognition article](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v5i1r25) uses coherent configurations and Schur rings, not ordinary deck reconstruction or proof that every relevant \(p^2\) orbital indicator lies in the adjacency algebra.
- [Godsil–Sun–Zhang, arXiv:2302.03854, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.03854) gives the related characteristic-polynomial identity (their Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.2) and establishes cospectral edge deletions in 1-walk-regular hosts. It does not establish equality or separation of ordinary vertex decks. [Fan–Wang–Zhang, arXiv:2606.10404, Theorem 1.2 and Remark 2.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10404) prove orthogonal degree-similarity in 1-walk-regular graphs and explicitly distinguish this from a permutation/isomorphism. No ordinary deck theorem follows from those spectral results.
- [Dufresne et al., arXiv:2604.16567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16567) is a contemporary reconstruction setting reference, not an antecedent for these hosts. Distinguish [Myrvold–Ellingham–Hoffman](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190110304) on the **edge-deletion deck** of bidegreed graphs.

After a bounded primary search, no matching all-valency ordinary reconstruction theorem for edge deletions of connected prime-square circulants appeared. The plausible additive contribution is the combination of the derivative-only invariant with two-conductor separation precisely on the residual triangle-free case. The order-289 primary control illustrates why an all-edge adjacency-algebra argument is insufficient. Recommend scoped mathematical note with no unqualified priority or general reconstruction claim.
