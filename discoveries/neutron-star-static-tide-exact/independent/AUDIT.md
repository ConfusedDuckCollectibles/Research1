# Independent audit: exact fixed-exterior static Love collision

**Disposition.** Both positive-compactness constructions check. One cold, isotropic, spherical, nonrotating GR perfect-fluid model has a finite positive-pressure **energy-density** jump; a second has a smooth interior equilibrium barotrope. They have identical areal radius, gravitational mass and finite static electric \(l=2\) Love response, and identical hydrostatic profiles and metric from \(a=R/200\) outward. Both are causal on each realized bulk phase and strictly linearly radially stable under the modeled pressure response; the discontinuous star is stable for the standard rapid and slow conversion laws. Both are self-bound with the same nonzero surface density and same surface-to-vacuum jump. “Smooth” does not mean smooth across vacuum. This is a schematic one-configuration inverse nonuniqueness theorem, not a measured neutron-star inference, nuclear model, rotating or dynamical result.

## Hydrostatics, thermodynamics and the junctions

Set \(G=c=R=1\), \(B=\epsilon(R^-)>0\), \(\sigma=4\pi B/3\), \(x=r/a\), \(E=\epsilon/B=1+\nu_0(1-r^2)+f(x)\). Normalizing all bumps to \(H=3\int_0^1 f(x)x^2\,dx\) fixes \(m(a)\), not just the total mass. Since \(E\) is literally the same outside \(a\), \(m(r)\) is the same there. TOV with exterior condition \(p(R)=0\) thus uniquely gives the same \(p(r)\) outside. The asymptotically normalized lapse obtained from \(e^{2\nu(R)}=1-2M\) and \(\nu'=g=(m/r^2+4\pi rp)/(1-2m/r)\) is also the same outside, as is \(e^{-2\lambda}=1-2m/r\). TOV is integrated piecewise across a jump, with \(m,p,\nu,\lambda\) continuous. No thin shell appears. The exact common compactness is \(\sigma[1+(2/5)\nu_0+Ha^3]\).

Using \(E_{\max}\), \(P_{\max}\) for the chosen constants and \(\chi=1-2\sigma E_{\max}\), \(1-2m/r\ge\chi>0\). On the interval before a first inward crossing of \(p/B=P_{\max}\), \(0<\sigma r\le g\le G_*r\) with \(G_*=\sigma(E_{\max}+3P_{\max})/\chi\). The assumed upper \(G\) is larger than \(G_*\); then the first-crossing pressure is bounded by \((E_{\max}+P_{\max})G/2<P_{\max}\), contradiction. The exact bootstrap margins are \(13/800\) and \(47/45000\), respectively. A continuous pressure increases strictly inward, is finite including the centre, and satisfies the intended nonzero-interior-pressure phase condition. Since \(-E'\ge2\nu_0r\) in a bulk phase, \(0<dp/d\epsilon\le(E_{\max}+P_{\max})G/(2\nu_0)<1\). Near the centre \(f\) is constant, so the nonsingular representative uses \(r^2\) as a local variable: both pressure and density are smooth in \(r^2\), with finite strictly positive derivative ratio when inverting. In the smooth star this makes a smooth one-sided realized barotrope all the way to the centre and to the matter side of the boundary. No fixed lower bound on allowed sound speed is imposed across the proposed narrow smooth layer class.

Cold phase thermodynamics is admissible: choose a positive baryon density normalization in each phase using \(dn/n=d\epsilon/(\epsilon+p)\), independently rescale so \(\mu=(\epsilon+p)/n\) agrees at the common transition pressure. Then \(n_{\rm inner}>n_{\rm outer}\) at transition and \(d\mu/dp=1/n\) in each phase, giving the usual local branch ordering (higher-density phase preferred just above the coexistence pressure). The jump can be represented by the usual pressure-constant coexistence interval; strict causal sound bounds refer to the pure phases, not to that interval. No microscopic quantitative interpretation is assumed.

For the static quadrupole, the standard Riccati form is \(ry'=-y^2-Fy-r^2Q\) with regular value \(y(0)=2\). The sound term in \(Q\) is \(4\pi(\epsilon+p)/(D\,dp/d\epsilon)\) with \(D=1-2m/r\). Inserting \(p'=-(\epsilon+p)g\) converts it to the Stieltjes term \(dE/(I+P)\) in \(dy\), where \(I=m/(4\pi B r^3)\), \(P=p/B\). Thus a downward jump \(-h\) in \(E\) gives outward jump \(-h/(I+P)\), including the pressure term; at \(R\) the outward jump is \(-4\pi B R^3/M\). This agrees with [Takátsy–Kovács Eq. (11)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.01139). The derivative-free version \(y-E/(I+P)\) remains continuous at an internal jump. There is no discontinuity in \(H_{\rm met}\) itself when using the usual fluid perturbation junction; a discontinuity is in its radial derivative. The shear/stretch of the interface implicitly uses the equilibrium static tide, not an assertion about dynamical, out-of-equilibrium conversion.

## Exact matching at finite radius

The small-radius comparison is an analytic **reference**, not an incompressible physical model. In the normalized flat equation (with common \(P_a=P(a)\)), a step of excess height \(H/c^3\) at \(x=c\) drops \(y_0=2\) by
\[
J_c=(H/c^3)/(E_0/3+H/(3c^3)+P_a).
\]
Then \(T=(2-y_0)/(3+y_0)\) evolves as \(dT/dx=-5T/x\). In particular
\[
T(1)=\frac{Hc^2}{5(E_0/3+P_a)+(2/3)H/c^3}.
\]
At fixed \(H\) this is strictly increasing in \(c\). The larger-radius step also has smaller denominator, so the \(T\)-gap is at least \(H\Delta(c^2)\) divided by the given denominator ceiling. Since \(y=2-5T/(1+T)\) and the bounded reference has \(0\le T\le1/4\), the conversion has absolute derivative at least \(16/5\). Both uniform flat gaps exceed the rational lower witness \(3969/5000000\) over the full allowed interval of \(P_a\). Note the signs: increasing \(c\) lowers the final \(y_0\), so the jump reference is between the two smooth endpoints in the reversed \(y\) order.

In the finite-\(a\) equation, bounds on \(F-1\) and \(r^2Q_0+6\) give perturbing terms at most \(7a^2x^2\) and \(9a^2x^2\), respectively, while \(1\le y\le3\). In the equation for \(y-2\) the damping coefficient \(y+3\) is positive. Dividing by \(x\), integrating with the associated forward factor (at most one), and using total variation \(\le3(f_{\max}+\nu_0a^2)\) for the density term gives strict deviation \(<1\). At an internal atom the same estimate includes the atom at full weight, preventing exit from the claimed range there too. Difference of the two Riccati drift terms damps as \(1+y+y_0\); difference in denominator is bounded by 9 times the sum of \(|I-I_0|\), \(|P-P_a|\). These are bounded by \(\nu_0a^2/3\), \((E_{\max}+P_{\max})Ga^2/2\), respectively. The \(f\)-variation and remaining base variation then give \(<4a^2\) and \(<6a^2\) per profile for actual-versus-reference.

A mollified step with normalization height \(\hat h\) has \(|\hat h-H/c^3|\le3f_{\max}\eta/(c_--\eta)<\eta\). Throughout the transition, the averaged-density change from \((E_0+H/c^3)/3\) is bounded by the height difference divided by 3 plus \((f_{\max}/3)[1-((c-\eta)/(c+\eta))^3]\), also \(<\eta\). Jump integral comparison thus costs at most \((3+9f_{\max})\eta\). Both drifts over their compared portions of the layer cost \(<18\eta/(c_--\eta)\) in total, using the bound 6 for the absolute flat drift numerator. The resulting error is \(<40\eta\); the common later drift contracts. With \(a=1/200\) the worst relative comparison error is .00024 or .00034, strictly below .0007938. These estimates hold on the entire smooth interpolation, and are not numerical fits.

The parameter \(\lambda\mapsto y(a)\) is continuous. Besides standard ODE dependence on fixed-width smooth cutoffs away from zero, one can integrate from \(x=\delta>0\) with value 2 and pass to the limit uniformly in \(\lambda\): \(f\) is constant in a fixed initial interval, so the centre estimates give \(|y(\delta)-2|=O(a^2\delta^2)\) uniform in \(\lambda\), and the difference of solutions of the same Riccati equation is damped (\(y_1+y_2+F>0\) in the core). At the two smooth endpoints the rigorous signs are opposite relative to the actual sharp model, yielding a genuine interior IVT equality at fixed finite \(a\). From \(a\) to \(R\) the two tide equations and boundary correction are identical, so they give the same corrected \(y(R)\).

This equality is not at a singular Love-function pole. Outside \(a\), \(F\le1\) and \(r^2Q_0+6\le(3/4)r^2\) or \((3/2)r^2\), from \(I\ge1/3\) and the stated coefficient intervals. At \(y=1\), the outward drift for \(ry'\) is at least \(4-(6\nu_0+\mathrm{bound})r^2>0\). The bounded quadratic drift also prevents upper blowup. Hence \(y(R^-)\ge1\), and the common free surface gives \(y(R^+)\ge-2\) since \(M\ge\sigma\). For Schwarzschild exterior, the pure decaying mode \(H\sim r^{-3}(1+3M/r+\cdots)\) starts with \(y=-3-3M/r+\cdots<-3\). It cannot develop an inward zero: in the linear vacuum equation \(Q<0\), the integrating-factor multiple of \(H'\) is increasing wherever the normalized \(H>0\). A first inward zero would require positive derivative there and contradict negative derivative at large radius. The vacuum Riccati derivative at \(y=-3\) is strictly positive, so along backward integration that level cannot be crossed upward. Therefore \(y_{\rm dec}(R)<-3\), excluding a pure decaying match. Since \(M,R\) are also matched, so are the static Love coefficient and dimensionless deformability.

## Radial coercivity and interface sign

The standard radial equation with \(\zeta=r^2e^{-\nu}\Delta r\) is \((\Pi\zeta')'+(Q+\omega^2 W)\zeta=0\). The audited coefficients and free-surface \(\Delta p(R)=0\) agree with [Galaxies 11(2),60, equations (13)–(17)](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4434/11/2/60), with \(\Delta p=-e^\nu(\epsilon+p)(dp/d\epsilon)\zeta'/r^2\). Therefore \(S=\Pi\zeta'=-e^{\lambda+2\nu}\Delta p\). [Pereira–Flores–Lugones, equations (28),(31)–(32)](https://arxiv.org/html/1706.09371) use both actual and relative displacements; their actual-displacement equation gives \([\Delta r]=\Delta p[1/p']\) with outer-minus-inner brackets. Here \([1/p']<0\) for a downward jump, yielding \([\zeta]=C_jS\) with \(C_j=-r^2e^{-\lambda-3\nu}[1/p']>0\). Integrating by parts therefore adds the **positive** energy atom \([\zeta]^2/C_j\) (not its negative), and free variation of the two interface traces recovers continuous \(S\) and the preceding jump law. Slow conversion instead restricts to continuous \(\zeta\). No rapid-conversion jump is imposed on the vacuum surface.

Let \(D_{\rm en}=\int\Pi|\zeta'|^2+\sum [\zeta]^2/C_j\), and the inverse measure have densities \(\Pi^{-1}\) and atoms \(C_j\). Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz from the regular centre gives \(|\zeta(r)|^2\le D_{\rm en}\int_{0}^{r}dJ\). In the bulk,
\[
dJ=r^2e^{-\lambda-3\nu}(-d\epsilon)/(g(\epsilon+p)^2).
\]
At an interface the same expression integrated over the downward enthalpy range gives the atom, since \(g,p\) are continuous and \(-[1/p']=(1/g)\int_{\epsilon_{\rm out}}^{\epsilon_{\rm in}}(\epsilon+p)^{-2}d\epsilon\). The baseline Fubini estimate \(\int Q_+|\zeta|^2\le(54002/54675)D_{\rm en}\) follows by dropping the nonpositive pressure term in \(Q\), using \(g(s)\ge\sigma s,\ g(r)\le Gr\), and bounding the metric ratio by \((25/18)\). Integration \(\int_s^1dr/r^2=(1-s)/s\) leaves weight \((1-s)(-dE)\) with total \(\le\nu_0/3+f_{\max}\). The sharper upper-compactness bound uses monotone \(m(r)/r^3\) (average of a decreasing density) and pressure: \(g(r)/g(s)\le r/(\chi s)\) for \(r\ge s\). At \(r>s\), even when \(s\) is an atom, \((\epsilon(r)+p(r))/(\epsilon_{\rm drop}(s)+p(s))^2\le1/B\). Thus the geometric integrand is at most \(s(4+G)/(\chi r^2)\) before the metric factor, with metric bounded by \(5/3\). This gives \(2679/2720<1\). Both computations keep the positive jump cost throughout.

The same \(dJ\) inequality with \(W\) in place of \(Q_+\) bounds the mode norm by \(D_{\rm en}\) times a finite constant (near the centre \(J(r)=O(r^3)\), \(W=O(r^{-2})\)). One uniform explicit sufficient bound is \(\chi^{-5/2}e^{3G/2}(\nu_0/3+f_{\max})/\sigma\): the metric factor is \(e^{3\lambda(r)-\lambda(s)+\nu(r)-3\nu(s)}\le\chi^{-5/2}e^{3G/2}\), and the same enthalpy ratio and \(s^2/g(s)\le s/\sigma\) give it by Fubini. Thus the Rayleigh numerator has a strictly positive lower bound relative to this norm. The finite-energy centre, bulk pieces, standard free surface and positive atom define the associated closed quadratic form; the slow interface restriction is a subdomain. This is strict stability for the stipulated linear radial problem alone, not proof for finite-rate conversion, shear, heat transport or nonradial modes.
