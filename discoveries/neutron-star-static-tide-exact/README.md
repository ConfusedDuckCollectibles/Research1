# Two different star cores with the same static measurements

Could perfect measurements of a star’s mass, radius and distortion under a companion’s gravity tell us whether its core contains an abrupt change in density? Not in every model. The proof constructs two idealized, causal, radially stable stars with identical values for all three measurements, and identical structure outside the inner **0.5% of the radius**. One model has an internal density jump of at least **3%**; the other is smooth wherever pressure is positive.

The equality holds at finite radius, not just in the limit of an infinitesimal core. Both examples are nonrotating self-bound fluid models, and the transition is tiny in spatial extent. This does not show that a large core could evade observation, or that an observed neutron star has a concealed transition.
