# Sharp certification from unlinked compatibility and expiry margins

Abstract research, not medical advice, blood-group compatibility mapping, actual allocations, storage/handling guidance, or an operating policy. All examples are synthetic unit-token problems.

## Model

There are N integer tokens already present at time zero. Each has a scalar nested compatibility strength p and an inclusive last usable time e. No replenishment, arrivals, transport, indivisible bundles, or stochastic future demand. A finite, fully specified list D of unit requests q=(p_q,t_q) must be served in its specified slot t_q (no early fulfillment), and is compatible iff p≥p_q and e≥t_q. Each token and request can be used at most once. Exactly the separate marginal lists of strengths and times are known; their same-token linkage is unrestricted and hidden. All inventory completions below mean integer joint count tables, or equivalently all bijections of the two separately ranked lists. After observing each hypothetical completion, an *offline* matching is allowed. Guaranteed does not mean blind, online, or adaptive to unknown future requests. Real compatibility with incomparable classes and extra requirements is not a single ordered axis.

Let r_q=#tokens with p≥p_q, s_q=#tokens with e≥t_q, and g_q=(r_q+s_q−N)_+. Each request has at least g_q compatible tokens in every completion just by inclusion–exclusion. Use E_z={q:g_q>z} and W_z=D\E_z for z=0,…,N.

## Theorem 1: cutoff formula and explicit adverse completion

The exact worst-case maximum total service over stock completions is
    V = min_{z=0,…,N} [z + |E_z|].
More generally, let M be any fixed matroid on the requests (its independent subsets are the allowed fulfillment sets), with rank r_M. Then
    V_M = min_{z=0,…,N} [z + r_M(E_z)].
For any single matroid there is a fixed set of V_M request IDs that can be served in every completion, possibly by different token matchings. One need not know X to select these IDs. This is not a general claim about arbitrary nonmatroid constraints, non-unit weights, or supposedly universal choices of tokens.

Lower bound: form a virtual inventory of N ordered slots where q can use only slots 1,…,g_q. Any subset feasible there can be assigned in every actual completion: sort its g from small to large. Virtual feasibility imposes g_(i)≥i. Assign actual neighboring tokens greedily in that order; fewer than i have been consumed and ≥g_(i) are compatible. For any list I of requests, the virtual maximum is min_z[z+#(I∩E_z)]. One proof discards d=max_z[#(I\E_z)−z]_+ smallest-g elements; all remaining demand prefix counts at cutoff z are ≤z, so the g_(i)≥i criterion holds. Conversely the first z virtual slots alone are available to I\E_z, so at least d deletions are necessary. Now scan E_z in descending g with usual matroid independence greedy and retain I, a nested-prefix basis: after each full equal-g block it is maximal independent in that prefix, hence |I∩E_z|=r_M(E_z). A maximum virtual subset of I attains the formula in every actual completion. This proof only uses matroid augmentation/basis cardinality.

Upper bound: rank the two supply attributes in descending order, breaking ties arbitrarily (position sets for thresholds are still prefixes). At any cutoff z, pair ranks 1…z within themselves and use the reversed remaining tail:
    pi_z(i)=i for i≤z, and pi_z(i)=N+z+1−i for i>z.
Any tail token has rank sum N+z+1. A q with g_q≤z satisfies r_q+s_q≤N+z, so neither coordinate-rank threshold can be met simultaneously by a tail token. Thus every W_z request together has at most z actual neighbors; at most r_M(E_z) other requests are permitted by M. This completion supplies the exact upper witness at whichever z minimizes the formula. It is an explicit integer rank construction, not optimization over latent tables. Evaluate only 0 and observed g values, since between such values the rank term is constant and z grows. For the unconstrained case, sorting g suffices.

Fixed-graph precedent: [Aigner–Dowling 1971, Theorem 6](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1971-0286689-5) already gives the exact fixed-graph rank cut min over U⊆D of r_M(D\\U)+|neighbors_X(U)| as the special case of a free stock matroid. The distinct step here is to remove X entirely using the unlinked reports, identify a common matchable demand subset, and prove a sharp adverse completion menu. It is not a new fixed-graph matching theorem.

Interpretation: collapsing independent-looking capacity indicators into a linked capacity under a common cutoff is not valid. This theorem characterizes the infimum over all linked completions with specified separate margins. It does not presume independence or claim the constructed one is an observed scenario.

## Corollary: exactly guaranteed protected mix

For disjoint request classes D_j with integer service floors k_j (0≤k_j≤|D_j|), simultaneous fulfillment in every completion is equivalent to
    for all z: sum_j max(0, k_j − |D_j ∩ E_z|) ≤ z.
Choose the k_j largest-g IDs per class. Their counts in virtual weak prefixes are exactly the left side. If the inequalities pass, their virtual matching and hence actual adaptive matchings exist in every completion. If they fail, the same cutoff's explicit pairing leaves only z units to meet the forced weak part of all floors *together*. Alternatively use the partition matroid with caps k_j and target Σ k_j. Unrestricted requests can be left in an extra zero-floor class for this test. Joint satisfaction of these floors plus the worst-case optimal total V is possible with a fixed selection: extend their virtual matching to a virtual maximum by augmentation while keeping the protected request IDs included. These are floors on matched demand, not still-unused stock held in reserve at intermediate times.

## Best attainable total and exact integer range

The maximum over completions can be computed with a three-layer network on strength-list tokens → request nodes (capacity one via node splitting) → usable-through-list tokens; each arc tests only its corresponding axis. A flow gives distinct strength and expiry labels for every served request: tie those two lists of used labels together and pair remaining labels arbitrarily to fill the margins, then assign requests. Conversely, any completion and matching induce such a flow. This establishes the unrestricted best total B. It does not assert the same simple flow handles extra matroid/quotas.

For unrestricted total, and even for maximum cardinality under a fixed hereditary constraint on the served request set, possible optimal values between the minimum and maximum have no integer holes. Any two completions are connected by transpositions of two expiry labels. The two affected units, in either direction, change between (p_lo,e_lo),(p_hi,e_hi) and (p_lo,e_hi),(p_hi,e_lo). If both affected units served before a swap, one request can still be served: on the way to the crossed pair, either crossed unit can meet the old weak unit's request; on the way to the aligned pair, the strong unit can meet either old request. If at most one was served, lose at most one. Keep all other assignments. Heredity permits dropping a matched ID. Thus the maximum changes by ≤1 per transposition, yielding all intermediate values.

## Frozen synthetic material example (2 compatibility tiers, 3 epochs, four request cells)

N=20; strong and base capability margins each 10; inclusive expiry counts at times 1,2,3 are (9,2,9). Requests are 9 strong at time 1, 1 strong at time 2, 1 base at time 2, and 9 base at time 3. Their guaranteed g scores in the same order are 10,1,11,9. In a good completion the two rows (strong; base) of expiry counts are (9,1,0); (0,1,9); all 20 can be served. A worst completion has rows (0,1,9); (9,1,0). All strong requests and all base time-3 requests must use the same ten strong stock units in this latter completion; the sole base time-2 request has one other compatible unit, so exactly 11 are possible. Formula and direct enumeration confirm the exact identified set {11,…,20}, a 45-point width in the service fraction despite both single-axis reports individually accommodating every request.

Protect pool A (all ten strong requests) and pool B (nine base requests at time 3). The exact guarantee is k_A≤10, k_B≤9, k_A+k_B≤10. Both 7-count floors are individually guaranteed (indeed A alone can receive all 10 and B alone all 9); together they exceed the worst completion's common capacity by four. The good completion serves both floors with all 20. These abstract labels do not map to groups of patients. Larger illustration, N distinct tiers/expiry ordinals with one token at each rank on each side and N demand rank-threshold pairs r+s=N+1: a perfectly reversed completion serves all N, while the z=1 completion serves one. This shows that in an unrestricted many-tier setting there is no constant positive fractional guarantee from full separate serviceability alone.

## Exploratory extensions and controls

### Exact adverse menu, and why no universal coupling suffices

For fixed supplies, the same menu of N cutoff pairings z=0,…,N−1 handles every possible request schedule/matroid (N≥1). If a minimizing cutoff z<N exists, its pairing attains the minimum. If the unique minimizing cutoff is z=N, the minimum is N, so every completion already attains it and any menu member suffices. N=0 is trivial.

Worst-case necessity of N: take all supply ranks 1,…,N unique on both lists. For each z=0,…,N−1 let the request universe include N copies of each distinct threshold rectangle r+s=N+z (if z=0, N≥2; for N=1 needing one scenario is trivial). Focus on the sublist belonging to z. Its stock neighborhood is exactly the triangle i+j≤N+z: integer threshold pairs fill the triangle by increasing i,j as needed to reach the boundary. The N copies per rectangle suffice to assign each compatible token separately, so optimal service in this scope equals triangle occupancy. Its minimum is z: one boundary rectangle alone forces ≥z and pi_z attains ≤z. If a minimizer has exactly z triangle points, the sum of their row+col ranks is at least z(z+1); the remaining N−z pairs each have sum at least N+z+1. These lower bounds sum to the fixed total N(N+1), hence are tight. Weak points use precisely the first z ranks in both margins, and all remaining sums are exactly N+z+1. Any such minimizer has *all* N points in any higher-cutoff triangle z'>z. It cannot minimize another triangle at z'<N. The N minimizing families are pairwise disjoint: no single rearrangement or smaller menu suffices to certify all these scopes. For z=0 with N=2, the joint high-high requirement is starved by reversal; two broad requests in complementary axes are instead worst under positive alignment.

### Finite tier or wave counts limit the missing-linkage loss when demand equals stock

If |D|=N and each axis alone can serve every request, then V is at least the largest request group sharing any one strength threshold or any one time. Hence with at most K occupied demand strength tiers and T occupied waves, V≥max(ceil(N/K),ceil(N/T)). Proof for strength: order its request groups from strongest to weakest, with n_l in group l and cumulative P_l. Solo strength feasibility implies r_l≥P_l. Solo time feasibility supplies a distinct compatible time-rank j_q≤s_q for every request. At cutoff z, a weak request from group l or any weaker group has r_q≥P_l, hence j_q≤s_q≤N+z−r_q≤N+z−P_l. There are at least N−P_(l−1)−|E_z| such weak requests and only N+z−P_l such ranks. Thus z+|E_z|≥n_l for every cutoff. Exchange axes for the time statement. With K equal-sized batches of B units at each of K distinct ranks on each axis (N=KB), and K opposite rank-threshold pairs r+s=N+B, each repeated B times, every g is B; V=B and the match aligned to the demand pairs serves all N. Therefore the bound is sharp; a vanishing service fraction when every request was separately serviceable does require an increasing number of occupied strata. This is a bound on finite abstract readiness under the stated total and feasibility, not a policy recommendation.

### Both stock and demand linkage unreported, within disjoint pools

Assume even the two ordered attributes of requests are unlinked within each independent demand class j, though both integer marginal lists within each class are known. Total M_j per class fixed. For a given z its strong set E_z among hypothetical (p,t) is monotone *downward* in the difficulty of the requested p and t. Define L_j(z) as zero or the maximum over (p,t) in this region of
    (#class-j requests with required strength ≤p) + (#class-j requests with time ≤t) − M_j.
Then L_j(z) is the exact least possible number of easy requests in class j. Proof: in increasing ranks, the downset is the union of the lower rectangles [1,a]×[1,b] supplied by the easy region. Each rectangle forces at least (a+b−M_j)_+, giving the lower bound; put y=L_j(z), pair the first y ranks and reverse the remaining tails. Any rectangle in the region has a+b≤M_j+y, so cannot meet a residual pair. This includes a proof for all upper/lower Ferrers regions on tied rank lists, a finite-rank construction for a classical type of fixed-marginal overlap bound: see [Makarov 1982](https://doi.org/10.1137/1126086) and [Frank–Nelsen–Schweizer 1987 §6](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00569989) for monotone operations (the union of rectangles is the sublevel set of a minimum of coordinatewise maxima). The contribution here is propagating this to an exact guaranteed service and protection frontier on two unlinked sides, not a new Fréchet bound.

Worst total becomes min_z[z+Σ_j L_j(z)], and jointly guaranteed protected floors satisfy Σ_j(k_j−L_j(z))_+≤z for every z. Each fixed z optimizes each independently ambiguous class's coupling in the same direction, and the stock pi_z reaches the obstruction. Dispense with a preselected identity claim now that demand linkage is hidden. Assumes separate class couplings are free and no shared cross-class restrictions; does not replace timely observed demand.

### Two-axis boundary; scope

The naive formula using d marginal counts g=(Σaxis r−(d−1)N)_+ fails already with three axes. With two supply tokens, each axis having one strong and one base, require three requests: strong in axes 1&2, 1&3, and 2&3 respectively; third coordinate unconstrained in each. Each g=0, but one token must be strong in ≥2 axes by the pigeonhole principle. Some request is always compatible, and a stock where one token has two strong coordinates and the other one can serve exactly one. Hence the true worst total is 1 rather than 0. Treating several incomparabilities as freedom from couplings is not justified.

The model neither measures realized shortages nor prescribes handling or use. With all tokens at t=0 and examined through all expiry dates, no external flows, and no alternate disposition, unused terminal stock is N−matched by bookkeeping only; no claim about real waste or concurrent expiration counts. “Days of stock” is an inventory-to-demand statistic, not each unit's remaining lifetime. Issued units exclude unfilled requests and do not substitute for demand. Application would require same-site/time eligible starting stock by compatibility–expiry cell or both exact margins (for bounds), a complete pre-specified list of demand class–time slots incl. unfilled, revisions, transfers/receipts bounds, and validation of the simplified nested eligibility. No reviewed public aggregate holdout meets this complete scope. No deployment or outreach.
