# An exact two-use metrology advantage over the full parallel class

## Channel, resource and statement

Put $P=|1\rangle\langle1|$, $U_\theta=e^{i\theta P}$ and $\mathcal E_{\theta,s}=U_\theta\circ\mathcal A_s$, with the unitary used for its conjugation channel and
$$
 B_0=|0\rangle\langle0|+\sqrt{s}|1\rangle\langle1|,
 \qquad B_1=\sqrt{1-s}|0\rangle\langle1|.
$$
Thus $s$ is excited-state survival, $1-s$ is decay, and the phase convention differs from $e^{-i\theta Z/2}$ by a global phase. The damping and phase **channels** commute. There is one use of each of two independent channels $\mathcal E_{\theta,s_1}$, $\mathcal E_{\theta,s_2}$, with common unknown local phase and known possibly unequal survival; environments are fresh and inaccessible. The resource is exactly two uses, with the same allowance of noiseless ancillary registers in both families. Parallel permits arbitrary initial entanglement and arbitrary joint readout, but no action on either input using information from the other output. Fixed-order adaptive additionally permits phase-independent operations in between calls; the order is chosen before either call. Neither accesses an environment or indefinite order. The figure of merit is **unconditional output QFI** for local single-parameter estimation.

**Theorem.** For every pair $0<s_1,s_2\leq1$, other than $(1,1)$, at least one ordinary fixed order with just one parity measurement and a conditional reset strictly exceeds the maximum QFI over all parallel protocols, even with arbitrary noiseless ancillary dimensions. If one channel is noiseless and the other partially damped, the lossy-first order suffices. If either $s_i=0$, the two families' optima are the same as one use of the other channel; if $s_1=s_2=1$, both optima are 4. This result gives a strict lower certificate for the adaptive optimum, not its value or a claim about which order is always best.

**Priority.** [Liu, Hu, Yuan and Yang, PRL 130, 070803 (2023), Fig. 2 and surrounding text](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.130.070803) already state the all-interior equal-decay hierarchy based on their SDP curves; [Supplement E and I](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.09758) give exact optimization programs and numerical circuit synthesis. [Liu et al., Fully-Optimized Quantum Metrology (2024), §§5.1.1, 5.1.3](https://doi.org/10.1002/qute.202400094) give numerical benchmarks on the same equal-decay model, and for non-identical decay with $p_1=2p_2$ in Fig. 8, $p_i=1-s_i$. The point $p_1=p_2=0.4$ is not a new separation. [Demkowicz-Dobrzański et al., PRA 80, 013825 (2009), Eqs. (27), (41)–(42), (47), (55)–(57)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.0456) derive the equivalent passive one-arm-loss variance objective, its distinguishable time-bin formulation, and the exact equal-loss NOON threshold obtained by setting $N=2$; the parallel bound here is not offered as a novel optical bound. [Li, Tong and Bao, Conditional Reset-Enhanced Dissipative Quantum Metrology (2026), §Heralded recycling, Fig. 4 and Eq. (13)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31249v2) already demonstrate the general mechanism of recycling phase-independent heralded losses via conditional reset. Their setting is continuous-time frequency sensing with a three-level erasure qubit and a no-reset Ramsey comparator, not two black-box amplitude-damping calls versus all ancilla-assisted parallel protocols. The differentiating result here is a short **analytic** strictness proof, across the entire two-dimensional damping domain and boundaries, with an explicit syndrome-conditioned mechanism, independently of SDP precision. No proof of this full-domain statement or prior parity-reset construction was located in the checked works. No priority claim for a generic adaptive advantage, indefinite-order hierarchy, or implementation.

## 1. Exact parallel reduction (including arbitrary ancillas)

For a pure input $\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^2}|x\rangle|a_x\rangle$ let $p_x=\|a_x\|^2$. An arbitrary mixed input may first be purified into a noiseless ancilla, as discarding it cannot increase QFI. For a fixed damping-loss pattern $L\subseteq\{1,2\}$, an input bitstring $x$ contributes probability
$$
 w_L(x)=\mathbf1_{\{L\subseteq x\}}\prod_{i\in L}(1-s_i)
                          \prod_{j\in x\setminus L}s_j,
$$
using a bitstring also for its set of ones, with $K=|x|$. For fixed $L$, different surviving $x$ map to different sensor strings $x\setminus L$, so the vectors in this pure output sector are mutually orthogonal, regardless of ancillary overlaps. Phase is generated by $K-|L|$, so its conditional pure-state QFI is $4\operatorname{Var}(K\mid L)$. Sector probabilities are independent of phase. Giving an ideal sector label and then tracing it out bounds actual QFI from above by the probability-weighted sector QFI.

This upper bound is attained for **every** probability law without any environment access. Prepare $\sum_x\sqrt{p_x}|x\rangle_S|x\rangle_A$, using distinct noiseless computational tags. Basis vectors $|x\setminus L\rangle_S|x\rangle_A$ for different $(x,L)$ are orthogonal, and the phase-independent parities $S_i\oplus A_i$ identify losses without distinguishing terms within a sector. Thus the best parallel QFI is the maximum over simplex probabilities of
$$
 F(p)=4\sum_L \Pr(L)\operatorname{Var}(K\mid L)
     =4\sum_L\min_{\mu_L}\sum_x p_x w_L(x)(K-\mu_L)^2.
$$
This is concave and continuous, defining zero for a zero-weight term, so a maximum exists. Concavity is the infimum-over-means form. For $s_1=s_2$, averaging probabilities with their swap preserves optimality; the resulting probabilities are implemented as a single coherently tagged state, not a mixture.

In general write $p=(a,b,c,d)=(p_{00},p_{10},p_{01},p_{11})$, and let $m,h_1,h_2$ denote posterior means of $K$ for no loss, only loss 1, only loss 2. The double-loss variance is identically zero, including at zero weight. For any proposed means, the right side divided by 4 is at most $\sum_x p_x g_x\leq\max_x g_x$, where
$$
\begin{aligned}
 g_{00}&=m^2,\\
 g_{10}&=s_1(1-m)^2+(1-s_1)(1-h_1)^2,\\
 g_{01}&=s_2(1-m)^2+(1-s_2)(1-h_2)^2,\\
 g_{11}&=s_1s_2(2-m)^2+(1-s_1)s_2(2-h_1)^2+s_1(1-s_2)(2-h_2)^2.
\end{aligned}\tag{1}
$$
Actual posterior means saturate the first inequality. At positive relevant branch weights these scores equal the partial derivatives of $F/4$, since differentiating a term at its minimizing mean eliminates its mean derivative. Therefore at a maximizer, all active coordinates have equal scores and an inactive coordinate cannot have a higher score. A sector impossible for all inputs (e.g. loss of a noiseless channel) contributes identically zero.

For one use, excitation probability $y$ gives
$$
 F_{1,s}(y)=\frac{4s y(1-y)}{1-y+s y},\qquad
 y_{*,s}=\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{s}},\qquad
 F^*_{1,s}=\frac{4s}{(1+\sqrt{s})^2},\qquad
 F^*_{1,s}-F_{1,s}(y)=\frac{4s(y-y_{*,s})^2}{1-y+s y}.
$$
For $s>0$ this has a unique maximum, with valid noiseless limit $y_*=1/2$.

## 2. The strategy and its exact gain

Choose a maximizing parallel law and its tagged probe. Apply channel $i$ first to register $S_i$, leave other registers untouched, and measure **only** $S_i\oplus A_i$ (not individual bits). This is constant within each loss sector, so it does not destroy coherence within a sector. Record the outcome. If no loss, keep the entire state and make the second call as in the original parallel strategy. On a loss in the first call, $x_i$ was 1, the sensor/tag pair is a known tensor factor $|0\rangle|1\rangle$, and there is no relative phase from the first call. (If the phase were applied before loss it would be a global phase.) The remaining sensor/tag state in order $1\to2$ is exactly the tagged pure superposition with $y_2=d/(b+d)$; in order $2\to1$ it has $y_1=d/(c+d)$. A phase-independent control on these registers can replace this *known phase-independent* state with its one-use optimum before making the second call. This may be an ordinary conditional unitary within the two-level code. The parity and conditional control are ideal external operations; no access to jumps in the environment is used.

Both outcomes always get the second call, once. Loss flag probabilities are independent of phase and unchanged by this choice; the orthogonal flags are preserved. QFI is additive across flagged blocks weighted by probability, so the exact *unconditional* increments of these two protocols over their parallel starting law are
$$
 \Delta_{1\to2}=(1-s_1)(b+d)\,[F^*_{1,s_2}-F_{1,s_2}(y_2)],\qquad
 \Delta_{2\to1}=(1-s_2)(c+d)\,[F^*_{1,s_1}-F_{1,s_1}(y_1)] .\tag{2}
$$
If a branch has zero weight its contribution is zero. This uses no postselection and wastes none of the information from the first no-jump branch. The two sensor inputs, their two noiseless tags and a binary record suffice for either order. The phase information can be read out by ordinary local-in-parameter optimal measurements; no global phase-estimation performance is claimed.

## 3. Strictness for unequal interior survival

Suppose $0<s_1,s_2<1$. A single useful channel already gives positive parallel QFI, so the optimum cannot be $a=1$. If $d=0$, then at least one of $b,c$ is positive, and choosing the corresponding first call gives a nonzero first-loss probability with $y=0$, hence improvement. If $d>0$, both first-loss probabilities are positive. If $b=0$ (or $c=0$), the corresponding order has $y=1$, so also improves. Thus failure of both orders requires
$$
 b=t d>0,\qquad c=r d>0,\qquad r=\sqrt{s_1},\quad t=\sqrt{s_2},
$$
by the unique maxima of the one-call formula. Put $u=r/(1+r)$, $v=t/(1+t)$. These weights force $h_1=1+v$, $h_2=1+u$. Substitution in (1) cancels all single-loss contributions in the contrast, leaving
$$
 g_{11}-g_{10}-g_{01}+g_{00}
     =s_1s_2(2-m)^2-(s_1+s_2)(1-m)^2+m^2. \tag{3}
$$
If $a=0$, then $m>1$. The polynomial on the right has coefficients $(1-s_1)(1-s_2)$ on $m^2$ and $2(s_1+s_2-2s_1s_2)$ on $m$, so it is strictly increasing for $m\geq1$ and at 1 equals $1+s_1s_2>0$. But active-score equality and $g_{00}\leq g_{10}$ make the left side nonpositive.

If $a>0$, $g_{00}=g_{10}=g_{01}$ must hold. Define $H_i=s_i/(1-s_i)$ and $z=1-2m$. The equations are
$$
 m^2=v^2+H_1z=u^2+H_2z.
$$
For $r\ne t$, eliminating gives
$$
 z=\frac{v^2-u^2}{H_2-H_1}
   =\frac{(1-r)(1-t)(2rt+r+t)}{(1+r)(1+t)(r+t)},\qquad m=\frac{1-z}{2}.
$$
Direct reduction gives the strict residual
$$
 m^2-v^2-H_1 z
 =\frac{r^2t^2(1-r)(1-t)}{(1+r)(1+t)(r+t)^2}>0,
$$
a contradiction. Thus an eligible ordinary predetermined order must exceed parallel for **every** unequal interior pair.

## 4. The equal-survival diagonal and boundary

For $s_1=s_2=s\in(0,1)$, failure of both orders imposes the same relations with $r=t=\sqrt{s}$; one may choose a symmetric maximizing law so both orders have the same gain. The proof of the $a=0$ exclusion is unchanged. If $a>0$, all four scores equal. By (3) they give
$$
 0=[(1-s)m+2s]^2-2s,\qquad (1-s)m+2s=\sqrt{2s}.
$$
Also $g_{00}=g_{10}$ gives $m[(1-s)m+2s]=s+s(1-s)/(1+r)^2=2s/(1+r)$. Substituting $m=(\sqrt{2s}-2s)/(1-s)$ into this equation forces $1-\sqrt{2s}=(1-s)/(1+r)=1-r$, impossible for $r>0$. This completes the diagonal without taking an unequal-strength limit, which by itself would only prove a non-strict inequality.

Suppose $s_1=1$, $0<s_2<1$. Use the lossy channel 2 first; its jump probability is positive at an optimum. Indeed if $c=d=0$, that bit never excites and the optimum would be at most 1, while independent optimal one-use probes give $1+F_{1,s_2}^*>1$. No gain would require $c=d>0$ by $y_{*,1}=1/2$, so $h_2=3/2$. Active-score equality gives $g_{11}-g_{01}=s_2(3-2m)=0$, thus $m=3/2$; but then $g_{00}=9/4$ and $g_{01}=1/4$. This is incompatible with the condition $g_{00}\leq g_{01}$ at any optimum, active or not. Exchanging labels proves the other edge. If either $s_i=0$, that channel is a phase-independent reset, equivalent to free processing, and both strategy optima reduce to the one informative call. If $s_1=s_2=1$, dilate all controls to phase-independent unitaries and defer any measurements. The composite generator is the sum of two unitary conjugates of the one-use projector, hence has spectral width at most 2. Pure-state QFI is at most 4, also bounding reduced outputs; ordinary parallel GHZ attains 4.

## 5. Simple exact bounds and rational witnesses

For the GHZ-like law $p_{00}=z_0/(1+z_0),p_{11}=1/(1+z_0)$, $z_0=\sqrt{s_1s_2}$, the no-loss posterior is $m=2z_0/(1+z_0)$, with $h_1=h_2=2$. Equation (1) gives $g_{00}=g_{11}=4z_0^2/(1+z_0)^2$, and $g_{10},g_{01}$ do not exceed it exactly when
$$
 1+2z_0-3z_0^2-4z_0\min(s_1,s_2)\leq0.
$$
This proves $F_{\rm par}^*=16z_0^2/(1+z_0)^2$ throughout that region by matching the feasible state to the score upper bound. Else a feasible first-order transfer to a violating coordinate improves the GHZ law (for interior survival). In the equal case the region is $s\ge (1+2\sqrt2)/7$. The specified intervention adds exactly $(1-s_i)F^*_{1,s_j}/(1+z_0)$ when using $i$ first. Within this GHZ-based strategy it is better to use the noisier channel first, since $F^*_{1,s}$ increases in $s$; this does not compare globally optimal adaptive orders.

|---|---:|---:|
| $3/5,3/5$, first-jump replacement $y=9/16$ | $9/4$ (exact) | $2421/992$, relative gain $21/248$ (8.4677%) |
| $16/25,81/100$, noisier first, optimal reset $y=10/19$ | $5184/1849$ (exact) | $1996812/667489$, relative gain $387/5776$ (6.7001%) |

For the first line, the loss flag has weight $1/4$; the exact replacement QFI is $189/248$. The unequal line is not on the $p_1=2p_2$ ray of the numerical prior; its reverse order in the same construction adds $304/3483$, vs $2916/15523$ for noisier first. For the high-loss line use means $m=491/1000,\ h_1=h_2=743/500$ in the score certificate, and input $a=2/11,\ b=c=3/22,\ d=6/11$ in the parity-reset protocol. The witness does not require knowledge of the optimizer below the equal-strength GHZ threshold.

## 6. Replay and limits

Run python verify.py, using SymPy for exact polynomial identities and matrix SLD checks; no numerical optimizer, data or paid tool. See replay.txt and manifest.sha256. A numerical exploratory calculation only selected a convenient rational high-loss witness; none of the proof relies on it. The result does not establish a global finite-shot minimax precision bound, optima over all adaptive controls, hardware constraints/overheads, asymptotic gains, transverse phase sensing, correlated environments or indefinite order. [Kurdziałek et al., PRL 131, 090801 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08106) establish asymptotic equivalence of adaptive and parallel resources in the relevant limit; a finite two-call strict gap does not contradict that result.
