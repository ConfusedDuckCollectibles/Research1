# Independent audit: seven critical vertices in triangle-free boundary digraphs

**Disposition.** Correct as a scoped structural consequence of the September 2026 Seymour theorem. The seven-vertex threshold is computational only at the fixed support size six if the analytical size-five argument is used. [C4-replacement.md](C4-replacement.md) gives a shorter proof of the remaining four-vertex attachment obstruction, eliminating the Hladký–Král’–Norin dependency. Independent exact census and attachment checks agree. No claim about the unrestricted Caccetta–Häggkvist triangle conjecture, a new unrestricted density threshold, or optimality of seven.

## Weighted dual, degrees, and no-source condition

Write \(B_{ij}=\mathbf1_{i\to j}-\mathbf1_{\operatorname{dist}(i,j)=2}\). For an orientation all exact distance-two pairs transpose under arc reversal. The [September 23 OpenAI paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/A-proof-of-Seymours-second-neighborhood-conjecture-September-23-2026/paper.pdf), Corollary 5.1(ii), on the reversed digraph directly gives a probability vector \(p\ge0\) with \(p^TB\le0\) coordinatewise. The fetched PDF SHA-256 is 20be859799f83e3412dba6fd645a72a0d929cbe05ca546807c591f001f9e6e82. Corollary 5.1 explicitly allows zero entries.

Alternatively, the stated dual is elementary from weighted SNC: exact distance-two neighborhoods respect blow-up into positive integer independent clone classes, as both arcs and distance-two statuses between different classes project and lift and no length-two route returns to a class in an orientation. For positive real weights, a strict violation persists in a positive rational neighborhood; clear denominators to contradict integer blow-up. For nonnegative weights, if needed, add a positive rational uniform perturbation and pass to a fixed winning vertex on a subsequence. **Do not delete zero-weight vertices**: they can be intermediate vertices for positive distance-two targets. Strictly positive weights suffice for the producer's Gordan alternative at \(\mathbf1\): if all tight rows had positive variation, every slack row would remain positive under a sufficiently small perturbation, a contradiction. Separating zero from the convex hull of the tight row vectors proves the nonnegative zero linear combination.

In a triangle-free orientation, \(N_2(v)\subseteq U(v)\), where \(U(v)\) excludes \(v\). Therefore \(B\mathbf1\ge3r-n\) given \(\delta^+\ge r,\delta^-\ge r-1\). If \(r=\lceil n/3\rceil\) and \(n<3r\), uniform weights already rule out a counterexample. If \(n=3r\), then \(0\le p^TB\mathbf1\le0\), so every coordinate of \(p^TB\) is zero and the support \(S\) lies in the zero-row-sum locus. In each such row \(d^+=r,d^-=r-1\) and \(N_2=U\). The zero column balances are precisely \(p(N^-(j))=p(U(j))\) for **every** vertex \(j\). This distinguishes the real dual support from all degree-critical vertices: some may fail to saturate the two-neighborhood inequality and others may receive zero dual mass.

For a putative source \(s\) in \(D[S]\), positivity and the balance at \(s\) make \(s\) send to all other support vertices. Any predecessor \(z\) of \(s\) lies outside \(S\); no support vertex can send to \(z\) by triangle-freeness and the exclusion of opposite arcs. Balance at \(z\) therefore forces \(z\) to send to all of \(S\). This already fills \(r-1\) predecessor slots for every member of \(S\), incompatible with the additional \(s\)-arcs for \(|S|>1\). For \(|S|=1\), balance fails at any outneighbor of \(s\); \(r\ge1\). Thus \(D[S]\) has positive minimum indegree and a **directed** cycle. No saturation is needed. If one does saturate by adding triangle-free arcs, any critical vertex after saturation had the same degrees in the original and retains exactly its incident arcs. In a saturated orientation both missing directions of any nonedge are blocked by opposite two-paths, but that stronger statement is unnecessary here.

## Finite kernel and external attachment audits

A shortest directed cycle is induced: either orientation of a chord yields a shorter directed cycle. This makes support orders below four impossible. At order four there is only \(C_4\) with equal weights. At order five an induced \(C_5\) is incompatible with summing the balances, since each weight contributes once to incoming and twice to missing mass. If there is a \(C_4\) and an outsider \(x\), summing balances on the cycle forces equally many outgoing arcs and nonedges from \(x\) to the cycle. The outsider's positive indegree leaves counts \((0,0,4)\) or \((1,1,2)\) for outgoing, missing and incoming. The former fails its own balance. In the latter, an arc \(x\to c_i\) forces the sole missing cycle vertex to be \(c_{i+1}\). Balance at \(c_{i+3}\) already gives \(p(c_{i+2})=p(c_{i+1})\), whereas balance at \(x\) requires \(p(c_{i+2})+p(c_{i+3})=p(c_{i+1})\). This is impossible.

At order six the independent replay is not the original planted-cycle generator:

1. The C++ census adds a vertex \(v\) by choosing its successor mask \(R\subseteq[0,v)\) first. Its predecessor mask is any subset of the old vertices outside \(R\) and outside targets of arcs from \(R\). This excludes exactly the cycles \(v\to u\to w\to v\); inherited triples were already checked. Every labelled triangle-free orientation occurs once. Retain positive indegree on all vertices, then exact determinant zero for the principal balance matrix.
2. The Python check solves \(Mw=0,\ \sum w_i=1,\ w\ge0\), with exact rational elimination. If full affine column rank fails, enumerate all proper supports of simplex vertices. At a vertex the positive-support columns of the augmented matrix must be independent; otherwise perturb in either direction without affecting any equality. Conversely such an independent feasible solution is a vertex. Since the simplex slice is compact, a strictly positive vector exists precisely when the union of those vertex supports is full, including higher-nullity cases. This is not floating feasibility or a cofactor sign heuristic.

But internal indegrees of those paired vertices are respectively \(1<2\) and \(1<4\), so equal total indegree \(r-1\) forces the opposite strict outside-degree inequality. No need to assume any outsider can actually be realized jointly with the others.

The producer's older HKN-based four-cycle closure is also arithmetically valid. [Hladký–Král’–Norin v4, Theorem 1.2 and §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0908.2791v4) gives the requisite strict upper bound \(\delta^+<\frac{693}{2000}N\) on every nonempty triangle-free induced \(N\)-vertex orientation. The signed inequalities reduce to \(693q<79r+1228\), \(q(q-1)\ge3r-1\) and \(r-a<\frac{693}{2000}(r+q)\). Eliminating \(r\) yields \(79q^2-2158q+3763>0\); convexity and negative endpoint values at \(q=2,25\) imply \(q\ge26\). This gives \(r\ge213\), then \(q<r/8\), \(a>.6r\), and inconsistent lower \((63/64)r^2\) vs strict upper \((24/25)r^2\) on the same edge comparison. The minimum-indegree argument in the replacement makes \(q=0\) instantly, resolving the same case without integer thresholds or HKN.

## Prior scope and significance

- The actual OpenAI Corollary 6.1(i) already gives some vertex with \(n-1\ge 2d^+(v)+d^-(v)\). Both the nonzero-congruence case with one unit of indegree loss and existence of **one** critical saturated vertex at \(n=3r\) therefore follow from published-context material. Corollary 6.1(ii) proves the exact equal \(n/3\) in-/outdegree threshold. Corollary 5.1's weighted duality is attributed to [Tyler Seacrest](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.1883), so neither weighted SNC nor the dual LP alone is novel.
- HKN concerns a higher **unrestricted** min-outdegree ratio, and [Lichiardopol](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.3477) discusses the earlier both-degree threshold 0.343545 and strong-connectivity improvements. Neither is a comparator for the narrowly increased allowance at \(n=3r\) with a bounded number of indegree deficits. HKN's §5.2 mentions an unofficial 0.3388 computation; do not claim a current unrestricted record based on selected citations.
- [Grzesik–Volec 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12830) explicitly excludes the directed triangle from its complete semidegree theorem. The novel [Grzesik–Jaworska–Kuc November 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22909) concerns the same exact-cycle-length problem for large lengths, not length three. [Guo–Kang–Zwaneveld](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29626) studies Seymour-tight orientations and related constructions, not this induced critical-support obstruction.
- I did not locate the source-free dual support or its seven-vertex minimum in the primary sources checked. This is a narrow structural refinement: under \(\delta^-\ge r-1\), one may allow up to six deficient indegrees at \(n=3r\); one may also allow arbitrarily many if the critical vertices induce no directed cycle. Graphs outside these hypotheses and all unrestricted cases remain unaddressed. Seven is a proved necessary lower bound for this hypothetical balanced support, not an example attaining it in a counterexample, or a proof that no seven-vertex support extends.
