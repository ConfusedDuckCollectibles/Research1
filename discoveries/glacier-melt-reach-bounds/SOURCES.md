# Thwaites author-source provenance and data gate

Frozen scope: [Rignot et al. PNAS 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404766121), [Dryad deposit](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi%3A10.5061/dryad.3ffbg79rm), and [Poinelli et al. Nat Commun 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77976-1), including actual figure-source bytes. A new 5-cm vertical test would require coherent phase/unwrapping, exact native timestamps and tides, calibrated LOS/horizontal/covariance constraints, and independent absolute or fluid-composition observations for attribution. An eligible matched holdout was not verified. The mathematics is structural.

## 2024 measurements and archive

- [Original full PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt9q4236kr/qt9q4236kr_noSplash_ab29bcf636c2f04b1749b7356987c492.pdf), Fig. 2 and methods: March–June 2023 daily ICEYE X-band double-difference SAR measures a differential, not absolute vertical position or instantaneous grounding. The authors compare freely floating response to offshore CATS2008 + ERA5 IBE (~8 cm scatter), not a calibrated inland same-epoch tide and phase-error covariance. Fig. 2 states ~1.65 cm LOS per phase cycle and nominal look angles 12°, 18°, 34°. Three looks are not simultaneous (May 23 panels show ~05:45, ~07:21, ~23:27 UTC) and a shared calendar label does not enable simultaneous 3-vector projection. Equal-duration pairs remove steady horizontal displacement, not episodic acceleration. Reused input scenes induce correlations.
- Irregular upstream phase patches, sign/amplitude above one specific fixed-elastic-pivot prediction, and a first bull's-eye along a favorable modeled hydraulic path support the authors’ inference. The **first** inland patch is interpreted likely ocean-connected; the farther South Ridge patch is instead interpreted as subglacial water, using modeled potential barriers. Distinguish 2–6 km migrating ice grounding zone, the separate interval of irregular extreme reach (up to another ~6 km), and downstream floating flexure. Episodic movement detected across months/years is not continuous saltwater coverage or a measured melt rate. The paper does not infer from a scalar tide comparison alone.
- **Actual HTTPS registry**: [Dryad API deposit](https://datadryad.org/api/v2/datasets/doi%3A10.5061%2Fdryad.3ffbg79rm), version number 3, [version 284519 file list](https://datadryad.org/api/v2/versions/284519/files). Nine files with hashes. No direct saline composition is listed. ## 2026 model inference vs prescription

- [Full author-archival paper](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6vk4301z/qt6vk4301z.pdf), pp. 3–8 and Methods: historical model constraints favor joint combinations \(L\approx2–5\) km and peak basal melt 90–120 m/y within the tested ISSM/SSA family, conditional on priors, grid, masks, bed/friction and forcing. 105 simulations (15 melt rates 10–150 x 7 lengths 0–6 km) interpolated to 10,000 prior draws, not 10,000 independent simulations. Ice discharge derives from velocity and thickness, so measurement families are not proof of a diagonal joint covariance; their Eq. (5) provides scalar sigma per observable and no verified cross-family joint covariance was located here. No new fit.
- Prescribed controls: regularized Coulomb friction and inferred historical coefficients, corrected local bed under gravity-inversion floating ice up to +100 m to initialize grounding within stated uncertainty, fixed front/no moving calving, fixed mesh ~500 m near grounding, spin-up, constant historical-mean 1996–2023 surface forcing, time-constant base ocean forcing, fixed width in each scenario and a linear landward melt ramp from the flotation line. Depth ramp cap starts at 900 m, shallow melt vanishes at 300 m, eastern cap 10 m/y in baseline. No extra basal lubrication rule inside newly wet area; no resolved changing cavity-ocean feedback. The authors call fitted L an effective time-averaged scale, not a true measure of a fixed geometry (Discussion).

Second file Thwaites.mat is 111917357 bytes and described as initial-condition model object, not all projected trajectory fields; only metadata read. Pinned [ISSM version](https://zenodo.org/records/18166185) 2026.1, no rerun. Replaying the whole model would require selecting/replaying a grid and resolving setup; no claim it is forever unreproducible.

### Named model comparisons, not new forecasts

In Figure 4b's Source Data, the ISMIP6 mean at 2100 is 15.20 mm. The named 3 km and 5 km model curves are 19.43 mm and 30.15 mm, respectively (about 28% and 98% above that benchmark). The 3 km case is near the reported hindcast posterior peak; the 5 km case is a wider prescribed scenario consistent with the paper's interpretation of recent observations. These are conditional model outputs. No workbook or radar measurements are redistributed, no future event has been verified, and no revised sea-level estimate is made here.
