# Thwaites: what a matched-tide contrast can reject, and what an effective melt width misses

Scoped mathematical and data-methods note, 2026-11-26. No new satellite-field fit and no new forecast. Two separate questions: (A) can a same-patch interior differential lift of at least 5 cm be assigned to an inland hydraulic mechanism merely by matching modeled differential tide across repeats? (B) if the 2026 fixed-width melt ramp is a proxy for periodically varying **heat-delivering** reach, what is the identified cumulative distal melt once overall melt is held fixed? The answer to A is not without additional calibrated bounds, even if full science products become accessible; B has exact conditional sharp bounds.

## A. Matching the actual observable, not just a scalar tide difference

For a fixed, co-registered spatial patch and look geometry, the signed observable is the difference of two equal-duration displacement increments. For equally spaced three epochs it has coefficients \((1,-2,1)\), up to an overall sign. Four epochs for disjoint 1-day pairs have \((-1,1,1,-1)\) or its negative, in paired order. Convert from LOS to vertical only when horizontal residual is independently removed or bounded; constant horizontal velocity cancels under equal pair baselines and calibrated common LOS, but acceleration need not. A static vertical height and any LOS displacement affine in clock time are in each equal-baseline window's kernel; neither absence of fringes nor a comparison of several tracks is an absolute basal gap or full-state measurement. In the exact 1-solar-day repeat ideal the operator also annihilates any 1-day-periodic component (e.g. a pure S2 tide response), even a nonlinear periodic function; additional noncommensurate tidal components can mix under nonlinearities and actual timestamps deviate from the ideal. Sampling different phase each day, adding absolute elevation/vertical referencing, or measuring independent pressure/temperature/salinity are different observables.

Suppose a restricted null describes memoryless, time-invariant, potentially nonlinear vertical response \(z(t)=f(\eta(t))\) to a common scalar *absolute* local sea level \(\eta\) (ocean plus IBE at the relevant source), fixed spatial patch and matched geometry. This permits a kink/threshold from quasi-static moving contact. Set \(f\) absolutely continuous with \(m\le f'\le M\) almost everywhere in the observed tidal range; a signed interval allows a negative landward flexure response. Use gains only if independently certified; memory, evolving material/geometry, additional input fields, calibration errors and horizontal dynamics are not ascribed to seawater by rejecting this null. Let \(c_j\) be the net signed coefficients of a contrast of one or many DInSAR windows, coalesced at shared acquisition epochs first (so \(\sum c_j=0\)). Define the signed tide-height atom measure and cumulative mass
\[
\zeta=\sum_j c_j\delta_{\eta_j},\qquad G(s)=\zeta((-\infty,s]),\qquad D_\eta=\sum_j c_j\eta_j .
\]
Then its exact null attainable interval for the **vertical** contrast is
\[
\boxed{[(m+M)D_\eta/2-(M-m)\|G\|_1/2,\quad
        (m+M)D_\eta/2+(M-m)\|G\|_1/2].}
\]
By integration of \(f(\eta_j)\) from below all knots, the response is \(-\int f'G\) and \(D_\eta=-\int G\). Add/subtract the midpoint slope and choose endpoint slopes on the positive and negative intervals of \(G\); this attains both extremes on piecewise linear monotone curves if \(m\ge0\), and convex interpolation fills the interval. For exact \(D_\eta=0\) and a nontrivial slope interval \(m<M\), the null contrast vanishes for every allowed response if and only if \(\|G\|_1=0\), i.e. **the net signed absolute-tide measures agree**, not just their first moments. When \(m=M\), the known-affine slope means scalar matching suffices. Signed multisets may match without ordered temporal triplets within this memoryless model; for a history-dependent null this is insufficient. A positive Lipschitz bound is the classical one-dimensional Wasserstein-1 / integrated CDF duality applied to the two positive/negative signed-mass parts; don't claim the general theorem is new. If \(D_\eta=0\) and \( |f''|\le K\) instead, the corresponding sharp \(C^{1,1}\) envelope is \(K\|\sum_j c_j(\eta_j-s)_+\|_1\), by a second integration and independent choice of curvature signs (different function class; a moving contact may violate it).

Simple 3-epoch exact control (not real Thwaites tides): \(\eta_A=(-80,-50,0)\) cm and \(\eta_B=(-20,10,60)\) cm have **identical** +20 cm differential tide. A stationary hinge \(f(\eta)=\frac14(\eta-20)_+\) yields 0 cm and 10 cm vertical differences, respectively. On the same tides and the entire slope box [0,1/4], the exact possible A−B contrast is −20 to +20 cm (net \(\|G\|_1=160\) cm). Positive slopes switching between 0 and 1/4 on the computed intervals attain the endpoints; this last statement is in the specified response class, not a global contact mechanics solution. A freely floating affine \(f\) gives exactly identical contrasts and is therefore a useful negative control but not a bound for interior ice. Evolving geometry, modeled tide bias rather than true tide, rise/fall history and unresolved propagation can separately change the response.

Add uncertainty at the **native acquisition** level. For bounded tide errors \(|e_j|\le\epsilon_j\), worst extra tide mismatch is at most \(\max(|m|,|M|)\sum |c_j|\epsilon_j\), counting shared epochs once with their net weights; a common tide-origin shift leaves the sharp interval invariant. Air pressure corrections are the model's IBE offshore, not pressure under the ice. Interferogram noise from shared epochs propagates as \(D\Sigma D^\top\) plus additional processing noise when appropriate (not 73 IID slots); unwrapped phase offsets, atmosphere/scatter and tide-model errors need realistic separate budgets. A no-overlap forecast or good residual control on a floating pixel cannot calibrate inland \(m,M\) on its own. To attribute a beyond-bound residual to seawater instead of viscoelastic memory/internal water redistribution requires further independent direct pressure/temperature/salinity or calibrated mechanical bounds. Rignot et al. already distinguish their landward near-IGZ bullseye from a farther South Ridge feature they attribute to subglacial water, interpreted using modeled hydraulic potential rather than direct fluid composition, and give amplitude/sign arguments against a particular fixed elastic hinge, not against every moving/inhomogeneous/time-dependent response.

## B. Exact moments for distal melt under an instantaneous triangular parameterization

Freeze a one-dimensional transect with constant transverse unit width, fixed origin at flotation and a fixed independent inland position \(x=R\) (e.g., a schematic ridge). Ignore geometric evolution/feedback over the averaging interval. Let \(L(t)\in[0,b]\) be **thermally active** inland reach, \(A(t)\ge0\) an integrable local peak ocean-driven melt intensity (m/y, can covary arbitrarily with L). Assume the *instantaneous* melt is the nonnegative ramp
\[
q(x,t)=A(t)(1-x/L(t))_+ \quad (x>0),
\]
with zero inland melt when \(L=0\); \(A\) can still denote peak near the mouth on the ocean side at such times. This is one explicitly declared variable-width extension of the fixed parameterization in [Poinelli et al. 2026, Eq. (2)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77976-1), itself used in [Robel, Wilson & Seroussi 2022](https://doi.org/10.5194/tc-16-451-2022), not a resolved fluid/thermal law or a statement the authors estimated \(L=E[L(t)]\). Define \(A_0=\langle A\rangle>0\), \(\pi(B)=\langle A\,\mathbf1_{L\in B}\rangle/A_0\), \(\mu=E_\pi L\), \(s_2=E_\pi L^2\). Observed tide geometry would not by itself give any of these *melt-weighted* moments. Total inland melt per width is \(A_0\mu/2\) exactly; distal melt per width beyond \(R\) is \(A_0 E_\pi g_R(L)\), where
\[
g_R(l)=\begin{cases}0,&0\le l\le R,\\ (l-R)^2/(2l),&R<l\le b.\end{cases}
\]
For \(0<R<b\) this is strictly convex above \(R\) (\(g_R''(l)=R^2/l^3>0\)) and convex on all of \([0,b]\) (matching first derivatives at \(R\)). For fixed \(A_0\) and \(\mu\), exact sharp range by Jensen and the endpoint chord is
\[
\boxed{A_0g_R(\mu)\le{\cal M}_{>R}\le A_0\,(\mu/b)\,g_R(b).}
\]
The lower is attained at a fixed length; the upper at two states 0 and b. Therefore a single width that preserves peak input and total inland melt can entirely miss forcing beyond a fixed ridge even in this favorable quasistatic linear-shape model; for all \(R\) it yields the least distal area at that first-moment budget. This is **not** a pointwise comparison of melt rates, a glacier unpinning criterion, or a sign on the error of a centuries-scale forecast.

**Adding the second moment gives an exact threshold and two sharp endpoints in a useful regime.** Feasible \(s_2\) satisfy \(\mu^2\le s_2\le b\mu\). For \(0<\mu<b\), **every** feasible schedule delivers positive distal melt iff \(s_2>R\mu\): if all lengths \(\le R\), \(L^2\le RL\); if \(s_2\le R\mu\), mass \(\mu^2/s_2\) at \(\ell=s_2/\mu\) and the rest at 0 matches both moments and gives zero. At fixed positive gap of moments a nonzero lower bound follows from compactness or from Cauchy–Schwarz:
\[
E_\pi g_R(L)\ge (s_2-R\mu)_+^2/(2b s_2).
\]
Indeed \(s_2-R\mu\le E[L(L-R)_+]\le(2E[g_R]\,E[L^3])^{1/2}\). For \(2b/3\le R<b\), the exact lower bound improves this to
\[
\boxed{{\cal M}_{>R,\min}
     = A_0\,\frac{\mu}{2}(1-R\mu/s_2)_+^2.}
\]
Under the size-biased law \(d\nu=l\,d\pi/\mu\), one has \(E_\nu L=s_2/\mu\) and \(E_\pi g_R=\frac{\mu}{2}E_\nu (1-R/L)_+^2\), where the last function vanishes below \(R\), is \(C^1\) at \(R\), and has second derivative \(2R(3R-2L)/L^4\) above \(R\). It is convex under the condition and Jensen attains equality at constant nonzero size-biased length: the same \(\{0,\ell\}\) law, including boundary equality.

**The exact upper bound throughout the same high-ridge regime** (\(2b/3\le R<b\)) is as follows. The only cases requiring optimization have \(0<\mu<b\) and \(V=s_2-\mu^2>0\), since otherwise the length is determined. Write \(\ell=s_2/\mu\) and
\[
a(c)=\mu-\frac{V}{c-\mu}=\frac{c\mu-s_2}{c-\mu},
\quad a_{\rm tan}(c)=\frac{c(3R-c)}{c+R},\qquad c>\mu .
\]
If \(a(b)\le a_{\rm tan}(b)\), set \(c=b\). Otherwise let \(c\) be the unique point in \((\max(R,\ell),b)\) with \(a(c)=a_{\rm tan}(c)\). Equivalently this is the root in that interval of
\[
c^3-3Rc^2+(4R\mu-s_2)c-Rs_2=0 .
\]
Put \(a=a(c)\). Then
\[
\boxed{{\cal M}_{>R,\max}
        = A_0\,\frac{\mu-a}{c-a}\,g_R(c).}
\]
Indeed \(a(c)\) strictly increases and \(a_{\rm tan}(c)\) strictly decreases above \(R\). At \(\ell\ge R\) one has \(a(\ell)=0<a_{\rm tan}(\ell)\); if \(\ell<R\), one has \(a(R)\le\mu<R=a_{\rm tan}(R)\). Thus an interior crossing exists exactly when the endpoint criterion fails. In both cases \(0\le a<R\) (since \(a_{\rm tan}(c)=R-(c-R)^2/(c+R)<R\)) and \(c>\mu\). For \(l\le R\) the quadratic \(Q(l)=g_R(c)(l-a)^2/(c-a)^2\) majorizes zero. Above \(R\), the derivative of \(g_R(l)/(l-a)^2\) has the sign of \(2(R-a)l-(l-R)(l-a)\), a concave quadratic positive at \(R\). If \(c=b\), its value at \(b\) is nonnegative, and the ratio is increasing throughout. If \(c<b\), it vanishes at \(c\) and flips sign from positive to negative there, so its global maximum is at \(c\). Therefore \(Q\ge g_R\) on \([0,b]\), with equality at \(a,c\). Its expectation is fixed by the moments, and equality is achieved for mass \((\mu-a)/(c-a)\) at \(c\) and the rest at \(a\), since \(s_2=(a+c)\mu-ac\). The feasible lower and upper endpoints are filled in by mixtures of equal-moment schedules. This does not assert the formula for \(R<2b/3\). Example with \(b=6,\mu=3,R=4\) km, \(s_2=15\ \mathrm{km}^2\): exact lower \(3A_0/50\) and exact upper \(2A_0/15\) in length times melt units, from \(\{0,5\}\) weights \(\{2/5,3/5\}\) and \(\{1,6\}\) weights \(\{3/5,2/5\}\) respectively. For \(A_0=110\) m/y they are 6.6 and 14.667 m·km/y per unit width, same total 165 m·km/y. With only the first moment the same example has possible range 0 to \(A_0/6=18.333\) m·km/y. A separate exact interior optimum uses \(R=4,b=6,c=29/5,a=899/245\) km and equal weights on \(a,c\), with \(\mu=(a+c)/2,s_2=(a^2+c^2)/2\). The maximizing distal rate is \(81A_0/580\) km times melt intensity, strictly above the otherwise plausible endpoint-at-\(b\) construction at the same budgets. Here 6 km is a schematic assumed cap, not a bound attributed to the observations; the examples are not a fitted distance, actual peak inland temperature, duty cycle, or phase-coherent event sequence at Thwaites. Atomic schedules are attainable as occupation measures. Continuous geometric transitions can preserve the same weighted law if the model permits \(A=0\) while transitioning between reaches; otherwise discrete exposures can be limiting idealizations and physical speed or energy constraints might narrow the bounds.

The full mean melt field, if measured continuously and truly attributable to this ramp, gives \(\bar q''(dx)=A_0\,\pi(dx)/x\) on \(x>0\) including positive endpoint atoms, by distributional differentiation and extension to zero beyond \(b\). This recovers the peak-melt-weighted **positive** length distribution but not chronological order or an unweighted duty cycle; the mass at 0 requires separate \(A_0\). Differentiating noisy sparse measurements is not automatically stable. For an independently known nondecreasing width weight \(w(x)\ge0\) the analogue \(g_{R,w}(l)=\int_R^l w(x)(1-x/l)\,dx\) is convex: differentiating gives \(g_{R,w}''=w(l)/l-2l^{-3}\int_R^l xw(x)\,dx\ge0\) where smooth, and by monotone approximation more generally. Do not apply the unit-width endpoints to variable-width or moving-ridge coordinates (a localized width functional may not be convex). Full glacier trajectories couple basal friction, bathymetry, ocean physics and evolving grounding; this budget in a frozen coordinate is not a perturbation theorem for those trajectories.

## Narrow contribution and limiting evidence

The general moment, cumulative integration and Jensen machinery is classical. [Gadi et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023GL105869) models a genuinely tide-varying cavity, computes a time-average melt profile and finds modeled seawater reaches about 72% of cavity length in that Petermann setup. [2026 Yelmo intrusion parameterization](https://doi.org/10.5194/tc-20-5345-2026) uses height above flotation as a varying spatial proxy for intrusion length and notes this length is otherwise a tunable parameter. These make clear that variable cavity and melt supports were known. The conditional result here is the **exact same-budget distal range and second-melt-weighted-moment threshold** for the published triangular approximation, and a sharp observation-level tide-match criterion allowing arbitrary monotone nonlinearities. It does not refute the published observation or quantify the physics of a real melt excursion. See [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) for measurement provenance, distinctions and scope.
