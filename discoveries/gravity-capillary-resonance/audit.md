# Independent audit: finite exact-resonance sectors for mixed deep-water waves

**Disposition.** The result holds for the unbounded Fourier lattice at a fixed positive rational dimensionless capillarity and positive rational squared reciprocal aspect ratio. The contribution is the parameter-specific global finiteness and positive-action confinement, beyond fixed-mode locality or cutoff counts. The q-class partition, angle exchange, elliptic-curve strategy in a different water-wave geometry, and the quadratic-invariant mechanism each have identifiable antecedents. Recommend a narrowly attributed theory note, not a new general resonance method, uniform finite-size estimate, experiment, or PDE claim.

## 1. Scaling, fourth roots, and the exceptional prime

For periods \(L_x,L_y\), put \(\kappa_x=2\pi/L_x\). The physical horizontal wavenumber at \(k=(m,n)\) is \(\kappa_x r(k)\), where \(r(k)^2=m^2+(L_x/L_y)^2n^2\). With \(g>0\) and surface tension divided by density \(\gamma>0\), \(\Omega(k)=\sqrt{g\kappa_x}\,\omega(k)\), where \(\omega(k)=\sqrt{r(k)(1+\alpha r(k)^2)}\) and \(\alpha=\gamma\kappa_x^2/g\). This uses infinite vertical depth, a positive single surface-wave branch, and no current. It does not require either the dimensional \(\gamma/g\) or the aspect \(L_x/L_y\) itself to be rational; the rational quantities in the theorem are dimensionless \(\alpha=A/B\) and the squared aspect \(U/D\). The zero mode is excluded.

At fixed parameters let \(N=Dm^2+Un^2\) and \(F=N(BD+AN)^2\). A direct integer identity, useful for independent testing, is
\[
 \omega(k)^4=\frac{F(k)}{B^2D^3}.
\]
The original factorizations \(N=q\ell^2\) and \(\ell(BD+Aq\ell^2)=\rho j^2\) give \(F=q\rho^2j^4\). The fourth-free radicand determines the pair \((q,\rho)\): parity of an exponent gives its \(q\)-bit, and the next binary bit gives its \(\rho\)-bit. In particular overlap of the primes in \(q\) and \(\rho\) is allowed and produces exponent 3, not a degenerate case. On the positive real branch
\[
 \omega(k)=B^{-1/2}D^{-3/4}j(q\rho^2)^{1/4}.
\]
A positive definite rational Gram form works by clearing denominators in all coefficients of the quadratic form; finite angular multiplicity then follows from coercivity of that form.

The needed independence is [Besicovitch 1940, Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-15.1.3), restated on the first page of [Mordell 1953](https://msp.org/pjm/1953/3-3/pjm-v3-n3-p10-s.pdf), with one prime per variable and residual factors equal to 1. An independent Kummer check works as follows. In \(K=\mathbb Q(i)\), suppose \(\prod_{p\in S}p^{a_p}\) is a fourth power, for any finite set of positive rational primes and exponents \(0\le a_p<4\). For an odd prime, any valuation of \(K\) above it gives \(v_\mathfrak p(p)=1\), since every odd prime is unramified; every other rational prime has valuation zero at that place. Thus all odd exponents are zero. At \(2\), \(v_{1+i}(2)=2\), so only exponent 0 or 2 remains. Exponent 2 would mean \(4\) is a fourth power in \(K\). Its four roots are \(\pm\sqrt2,\pm i\sqrt2\), none in \(\mathbb Q(i)\). Equivalently \((1+i)^4=-4\), so it would require a fourth power equal to \(-1\) in \(K\). This is why the ramified valuation alone is insufficient and the sign matters.

The generated subgroup of \(K^\times/(K^\times)^4\) therefore has order \(4^{|S|}\). By the usual fourth-root Kummer degree formula (the base contains \(\mu_4\)), \(K(p^{1/4}:p\in S)\) has this degree. The \(4^{|S|}\) monomials \(\prod p^{a_p/4}\) span the field by reducing fourth powers and have this cardinality, so form a basis over \(K\), hence are independent over \(\mathbb Q\). This does not rely on a false blanket rule that distinct irrational numbers are linearly independent. Positive real radicals of all rational primes are the field embeddings appropriate to the frequencies.

Factoring out the common nonzero scale from a finite signed sum now gives exactly the signed integer-weight balance in **each** sector. Repetitions add multiple copies to the sum. No frequency cancellation depends on angular direction; momentum is the independent linear condition on the signed lattice vectors.

## 2. Integral points and unbounded-lattice locality

Fix \(q,\rho\), \(c=Aq\), and \(b=BD\). The exact equation is \(\rho j^2=c\ell^3+b\ell\). Set \(X=c\rho\ell\), \(Y=c\rho^2j\). Then
\[
 Y^2=c^2\rho^3(c\ell^3+b\ell)=X^3+CX,\qquad C=cb\rho^2>0.
\]
Conversely every positive integral point on this affine model with \(c\rho\mid X\) and \(c\rho^2\mid Y\) gives the displayed positive integer pair by division. No claim is made that every such pair has a lattice representation. There is one point at infinity in the smooth projective Weierstrass closure, and \(\Delta=-64C^3\ne0\) in characteristic zero. The affine curve is smooth of genus one, so the hypotheses of Siegel’s integral-point theorem hold. [Baker–Coates 1970](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematical-proceedings-of-the-cambridge-philosophical-society/article/abs/integer-points-on-curves-of-genus-1/2E2195CEC3D50EA56836A7C0945603B3) gives an effective algorithm in principle; it is not a uniform practical cutoff. For each allowed \(\ell\), the shell \(Dm^2+Un^2=q\ell^2\) has finitely many integer points. Thus a sector is finite globally, not merely at bounded Fourier radius.

Every resonance of degree three uses a single sector: a sector occurring just once cannot have zero weighted charge. Following overlapping momentum-conserving triads stays in that sector, so **all components of the exact triad graph** are finite. This conclusion is stronger than knowing each mode has finitely many direct partners; an infinite chain can be locally finite. It is not a claim that each triad graph component uses its entire sector. Nor is there a uniform bound on sector size, even at fixed square geometry and fixed rational \(\alpha\): \(m^2+n^2=5^e\) has \(4(e+1)\) modes, from the factorizations of \(5=(2+i)(2-i)\) in \(\mathbb Z[i]\) and the four units, and they lie in one sector. If one restoring term is removed, the corresponding curve becomes singular or the scaling is inapplicable; in both pure laws the square-axis modes \((j^2,0)\) give one infinite rational-frequency class (weights \(j\) or \(j^3\) respectively, after taking out a common scale).

## 3. Hamiltonian scope and higher-order checks

Use standard independent canonical complex coordinates \(a_k,\bar a_k\), with \(\bar a_k\) the complex conjugate on the physical real slice. For a sector \(S\), define \(I_S=\sum_{k\in S}j_k|a_k|^2\). This is a finite quadratic form, positive definite on that slice of modes. Its diagonal phase rotation gives each monomial the signed phase \(\exp(\pm i\theta\sum_{k\in S}\epsilon_k j_k)\), depending on Poisson convention. Every exactly frequency-resonant monomial is invariant, with or without imposing momentum. Thus \(\{I_S,H_{\rm res}\}=0\) termwise. For any finite collection of nonempty sectors these actions have disjoint support and are functionally independent where each such sector has at least one nonzero amplitude. The linear energy restricted to a sector is a positive constant multiple of \(I_S\); this is not a statement about separating all nonlinear energy terms.

A degree-four vertex containing two sectors uses one factor of each sign in each sector. Pair weights \(j\) coincide within sector, hence so do frequencies and radii, since \(r\mapsto\sqrt{r+\alpha r^3}\) is strictly increasing for \(r>0\). Direction changes are allowed, including the checked nontrivial angular quartet. Higher-order multisector edges can be common, and an exact Hamiltonian need not factor into uncoupled sector systems. In particular generic diagonal products \(|a_k|^2|a_l|^2\) already preclude using graph connectivity for all interaction orders.

For finitely many initially occupied sectors the union of active modes is finite. A frequency-balanced monomial that contains an omitted sector must have at least two factors from that sector, since a single one cannot cancel by itself. A derivative creating an outside-sector coordinate therefore still contains a vanishing factor. Derivatives along the active union retain only finitely many monomials of the given finite degree. This proves an invariant finite-coordinate subspace and a well-defined finite-dimensional Hamiltonian restriction, even for an unconstructed ambient formal sum. For a real autonomous polynomial on that subspace, the resulting solution exists globally: the positive conserved \(I_S\) bound each active coordinate, and a polynomial finite-dimensional ODE extends as long as its coordinates stay bounded. In an actual ambient well-posed exact polynomial flow uniqueness makes this embedded solution the solution from those data. For arbitrary unbounded coefficient arrays or unspecified infinite-dimensional realizations, only the termwise and restricted-solution claims are justified. No conclusion follows for a full nonlinear fluid PDE, for convergence of a normal form, for forced or dissipative dynamics, or for actual values of water-wave coupling coefficients.

## 4. Detuning: fixed parameters versus uniform variation

The fixed triad \((4,3)+(4,-3)=(8,0)\) at a square has signed mismatch \(2\sqrt{5(1+25\alpha)}-\sqrt{8(1+64\alpha)}\). The difference of the two positive squared terms is \(12(1-\alpha)\). At \(\alpha=1+1/M\) the mismatch is negative and asymptotic to \(-3/(\sqrt{130}M)\), entirely within rational positive parameters. Hence no positive lower mismatch can be uniform even for this fixed triad in a parameter interval containing 1.

**Optional elementary fixed-parameter scope clarification.** A very weak polynomial lower bound for any nonzero signed detuning is available when the parameters and order are fixed. For a list of \(d\ge1\) terms and \(M=\max(1,\max_i|m_i|,\max_i|n_i|)\), remove the scale \(\mu=B^{-1/2}D^{-3/4}\) for dimensionless \(\omega\), or \(\mu=\sqrt{g\kappa_x}B^{-1/2}D^{-3/4}\) for the physical \(\Omega\). The mismatch after division by \(\mu\) is the nonzero algebraic integer \(a=\sum_i\epsilon_i F(k_i)^{1/4}\), with positive real branches at the given embedding. The generated field has degree at most \(4^d\), without adjoining \(i\). Its other embeddings may choose complex fourth roots but preserve their moduli. Put
\[
 C_0=\big[(D+U)(BD+A(D+U))^2\big]^{1/4}.
\]
Then each conjugate has modulus at most \(dC_0M^{3/2}\) by the triangle inequality and the elementary bound on \(F(k_i)\). The norm is a nonzero rational integer, whence
\[
 |\text{detuning}|\ge \mu\,(dC_0M^{3/2})^{-(4^d-1)}.
\]
This standard algebraic norm estimate loses a very large exponent and its constants depend on the fixed reduced parameters. It does not contradict the varying-rational near-resonance example, nor imply a useful nonlinear estimate or normal-form convergence. No novelty is claimed for it.

## 5. Exact replay and literature boundary

The latter is a local-in-spectrum estimate (the presentation uses a radius spacing step); do not present that as an unbounded mixed-class theorem. Its general physical summary likewise does not establish the displayed mixed arithmetic on the full Fourier lattice.
