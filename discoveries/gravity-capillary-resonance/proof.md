# Finite exact-resonance sectors for deep gravity–capillary waves
Research note, 25 November 2026. An arithmetic obstruction for an ideal finite periodic horizontal basin, not a statement that physical waves do not exchange energy.

## The theorem

Consider the deep-water, zero-current mixed gravity–capillary dispersion for nonzero lattice modes k=(m,n) in a periodic rectangle. In reciprocal-length units along the first axis, set

    r(k)^2 = m^2 + (U/D)n^2,    U,D coprime positive integers,
    omega(k) = sqrt(r(k)(1 + alpha r(k)^2)),   alpha = A/B >0 rational in lowest terms.

For dimensional gravity g and capillary coefficient gamma=surface tension/density, alpha=(gamma/g)(2 pi/L_x)^2, U/D=(L_x/L_y)^2, and the common frequency unit is sqrt(g·2 pi/L_x). Infinite depth is essential to the displayed dispersion. The “finite” basin in this theorem is bounded through its two horizontal periods, not finite vertical depth. The zero mode is excluded.

Write the following unique positive integer decompositions:

    N(k) = D m^2 + U n^2 = q ell^2,                  q squarefree,
    ell(BD + A q ell^2) = rho j^2,                   rho squarefree.

The pair (q,rho) labels a *sector*. Every sector contains finitely many modes, with a finite description via integral points on one elliptic curve. Moreover, for **any finite signed list** of modes, repeated modes permitted,

    sum_i eps_i omega(k_i)=0
    if and only if for every (q,rho), sum_{i in (q,rho)} eps_i j_i=0.    (1)

Momentum conservation, when needed, is the separate condition sum_i eps_i k_i=0. Thus every connected component of exact momentum-conserving triads in the *unbounded* Fourier lattice is finite. Every formal exact-resonant polynomial Hamiltonian, of any finite degree on these modes, has a separate positive quadratic invariant for each finite sector. Consequently initial Fourier support in finitely many sectors cannot spread beyond the finite union of those sectors by this exact-resonant polynomial dynamics. This extends the kinematic obstruction beyond triads without claiming all-order normal-form convergence, control of the physical PDE, or any dynamical strength for a specific interaction coefficient.

## Proof

The two factorizations give exactly

    omega(k) = D^(-1/4) (BD)^(-1/2) · j · (q rho^2)^(1/4).
  
For each prime, its exponent in q rho^2 is either 0, 1, 2 or 3, and determines its membership in q and rho. Thus sectors correspond injectively to fourth-power-free radicals. For the finite set of primes in any proposed signed relation, the monomials in their positive fourth roots with exponents 0..3 are rationally linearly independent by [Besicovitch 1940, theorem as restated by Mordell 1953](https://msp.org/pjm/1953/3-3/pjm-v3-n3-p10-s.pdf). It applies to each prime with the other factor equal to 1. Cancel the common real prefactor and compare the coefficient of each independent radical to obtain (1). Three positive-frequency waves with a resonant sign pattern must all be in the same sector: otherwise one sector would occur alone and its signed integer coefficient could not vanish. Passing along triads through a shared mode therefore preserves the sector. At degree four, a resonance with two sectors requires one mode of each sign from each sector. Their integer weights and frequencies coincide pairwise, hence their radii coincide by strict monotonicity of the dispersion. This permits angular exchange; it does not force the exact-resonant Hamiltonian to factor into independent sectors.

For sector finiteness fix c=Aq>0 and b=BD>0. The radial index ell and weight j solve rho j^2=c ell^3 + b ell. Make the integer substitution

    X = c rho ell,       Y = c rho^2 j;
    E: Y^2 = X^3 + C X,       C=cb rho^2>0.

The discriminant is -64 C^3 !=0. The positive integer solutions (ell,j) are in bijection with positive integral points (X,Y) on E satisfying the divisibilities c rho | X, c rho^2 | Y; this follows in either direction by substitution. Siegel’s integral-point theorem gives a finite set (and [Baker–Coates' effective genus-one theorem](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100045904) gives an algorithm in principle, not a claim of practically small bounds). For any such ell there are only finitely many lattice solutions to D m^2+U n^2=q ell^2 by positive definiteness. This proves global finiteness of each sector independently of path length or Fourier cutoff. The same calculation would work for a positive-definite lattice Gram matrix with rational entries, after clearing denominators; no class is claimed for an irrational squared aspect here.

For the formal exact-resonant Hamiltonian statement, take canonical complex mode amplitudes a_k with quadratic Hamiltonian sum_k omega(k)|a_k|^2. The sector phase rotation generated by I_(q,rho)=sum_{k in sector} j_k |a_k|^2 multiplies each mode of that sector by exp(±i j_k theta) under the conventional sign choice and leaves other modes alone. An exactly resonant monomial in the modes and their conjugates is invariant under every such rotation, by (1), whether or not it uses modes in several sectors. This proves the separate Poisson invariants; multiplying by the positive common sector factor gives a separate linear energy invariant. Equivalently, if a monomial uses an initially empty sector, it must contain at least two amplitude factors from that sector, and so cannot source a nonzero component there at the zero state. Positivity and finiteness give the obstruction for exact polynomial truncations, not for the full dynamics or resonant scattering in other depth/aspect settings. Some kinematically admitted monomials might have zero water-wave coefficients; the obstruction does not assume nonzero coefficients.

## Exact controls

- Square, alpha=7/1259: (12,5)+(12,-5)=(24,0). Radii 13,13,24 sit strictly on opposite sides of the gravity/capillary crossover alpha r^2=1 (input ratio 1183/1259<1, output 4032/1259>1). Sector (1,31746), weights 1,1,2. The squared frequencies are 31746/1259, 31746/1259 and 126984/1259. This is a noncollinear *kinematic* example, not a new claim about physical exchange.
- Square, known harmonic-type input (1,0)+(1,0)=(2,0): exact at alpha=1/2, sector (1,3) and weights 1,1,2. At alpha=1 the same momentum identity fails frequency matching (same q=1 but different rho=2 and 10). Integer directions alone or a single squarefree-radius class would not exclude it.
- Rational squared reciprocal aspect U/D=1/2 (irrational aspect itself), alpha=7/1978: (17,12)+(17,-12)=(34,0), radii 19,19,34. Both use sector (2,171190), with exact elliptic-point and factorization checks in the replay. Demonstrates clearing the geometry denominator without assuming a square.
- Mixed-sector angular quartet at alpha=1 in the square: (1,1)+(2,-1)=(1,-1)+(2,1), with sectors (2,3) and (5,6). The cancellation occurs inside each sector, consistent with the separate invariants. A slogan claiming that no modes from different sectors ever occur in one resonant vertex would be false.
- Limit: square, (4,3)+(4,-3)=(8,0), exact at alpha=1. For alpha=1+1/M rational, the same triad is off resonance but its detuning is exactly
  12(1-alpha) / (2 sqrt(5(1+25 alpha)) + sqrt(8(1+64 alpha))),
  by taking the difference of squared frequency totals. Thus the detuning is -3/(sqrt(130) M)+O(M^-2). There is **no** positive lower detuning uniform over rational parameters even for this one triad in a fixed compact interval around 1. No statement about a fixed parameter's small divisors follows from the class theorem. Mixed-order near-resonances, finite physical linewidth, forcing, finite depth, nonlinear frequency renormalization, and dissipative mechanisms are outside it.

## Prior-art guardrail

- [Kartashova, 1998, *Wave Resonances in Systems with Discrete Spectra*](https://www.academia.edu/1150744/Wave_resonances_in_systems_with_discrete_spectra), [bibliographic DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/TRANS2/182/04): Sec. 1 introduces additive class partition and pure-wave examples; Sec. 3.3 gives a mixed-wave local-neighbor/one-triad analysis based on an integer radius parameter. The theorem above is about an entire possibly multi-edge cluster and all-order action constraints, not just bounded immediate neighbors. The broad q-class strategy is not new.
- [Kartashova, *Nonlinear resonances of water waves* 2009 v2, Sec. 2.1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.0050v2): mixed gravity–capillary q-class construction explicitly mentioned; numerical isolated triads for selected parameter choices are reported, and reference [32] (Kartashova & Kartashov on exact and quasi-resonances among gravity–capillary waves) is marked in preparation. The exact title and alternative terminology were searched; no full publication or integral-point sector result located during this bounded scan. It would be unsafe to assert priority on the mixed q-class factorization. [Kartashova–Nazarenko–Rudenko 2008](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.78.016304) classifies linked scale/angle quartets for pure gravity and is not this mixed effect. Classic pure-gravity and pure-capillary cases drop out of the elliptic argument when one restoring term is zero (singular curve); never extrapolate the sector finiteness to them.
- [Feola–Montalto–Murgante, Apr 2026, Secs. 3.1/6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20968v1) studies square deep-water quasi-resonant estimates and quadratic lifespans for almost all capillarity parameters; its analytic block reductions are not the rational exact-resonance classification. [Ionescu–Pusateri](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02932) already gives generic low-order modulation constraints; no novelty claimed for generic absence. [Berti–Feola–Franzoi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05424) treats finitely many cubic resonances for a one-dimensional periodic surface.
- [Guan, JFM 2026 Sec. 4](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2026.11815) already studies noncollinear and standing-wave mixed resonances and calls out rational frequency ratios; the explicit lattice triads here are controls, not claims of novel mechanism. [Chabane–Choi](https://doi.org/10.1111/sapm.12249) shows precise conditions in which an exact mixed triad can exchange no energy. [Aubourg–Mordant](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.114.144501) and [Falcon et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1103/2w88-kh5j) handle mixed crossover and finite-size/linewidth behavior in physical systems; the arithmetic calculation is complementary and makes no measurement claims.
- [Zuccoli–Kadri, Oct 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2026.12161) adds a finite-depth compressible acoustic mode, explicitly outside the single surface-branch model. [Pezzi, Jan 2026 author manuscript](https://catenadiatomica.altervista.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/chain_of-res_capillary_gravity_waves.pdf) constructs chains on a continuum 1D resonance curve after imposing physical cutoffs and a selection rule. It is neither a lattice proof nor the absence of an unbounded chain on the full uncut lattice.
- Parked a simpler finite-depth transcendence angle: [Kartashova’s 2010 book, Remark 2.2](https://nzdr.ru/data/media/biblio/kolxoz/Kartashova%20E.%20Nonlinear%20Resonance%20Analysis%20%28CUP%2C%202010%29%28ISBN%200521763606%29%28O%29%28241s%29_PNc_.pdf) already proposes tanh/exponential Laurent elimination and Lindemann–Weierstrass for finite-depth resonances, and [Abassi–Wilkening, 2025, author preprint v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00844v3), Lemma 3.1, explicitly excludes algebraic normalized depths for its pure-gravity harmonic condition. Neither is used in the present proof.
- Precise contribution: the elliptic integral-point finiteness argument, yielding a global finite-support obstruction for exact-resonant mixed Hamiltonians of arbitrary finite order on a potentially resonant rational slice. No direct duplicate found, but no novelty or breakthrough claim is certified.
