# Finite-depth shell balance at algebraic parameters

Companion to the [deep-water arithmetic note](https://github.com/ConfusedDuckCollectibles/Research1/blob/c2ad057981ed31995d0607de829353a84b4f551a/discoveries/gravity-capillary-resonance/proof.md). The exponential and transcendence approach to finite-depth gravity waves is classical; see the source notes below.

**Frequency statement.** Fix a positive algebraic number h. For any finite set of distinct positive algebraic numbers r_i and nonzero algebraic factors A_i, the numbers
`A_i sqrt(tanh(r_i h))`
are linearly independent over the field of algebraic numbers. Square roots of the positive tanh values are taken positive. In particular, for a flat-bottom, irrotational gravity–capillary fluid with algebraic dimensionless depth h, algebraic dimensionless capillarity α≥0 and algebraic positive-definite horizontal quadratic form, every finite signed frequency resonance among nonzero lattice wavevectors balances positive and negative counts separately at each radius. The frequency, after removing a common frequency unit, is
`ω(r)=sqrt(r(1+α r²) tanh(rh))`. The same holds in the pure capillary case `ω(r)=r^(3/2) sqrt(tanh(rh))`. Here h is normalized by the wavenumber unit: if horizontal physical frequencies use κ times the normalized wavevector, then h=κ times the physical depth, α is the surface tension per unit density times κ²/g, and the frequency unit in the mixed case is sqrt(gκ). Algebraicity is a condition on the normalized parameters, not on their dimensional values.

Momentum is a separate requirement for a translation-invariant Hamiltonian term. In canonical Fourier variables a_k, radius balance is exactly the condition that the monomial Poisson-commute with each shell action `J_r=Σ_{|k|=r}|a_k|²`. A formally frequency-resonant Hamiltonian therefore preserves each J_r, has no odd-degree monomials (on the nonzero modes), and may redistribute action between different modes of the same radius. It need not decouple shell equations: even cross-shell terms can preserve both shell actions. Individual motions of the physical PDE, bounds on nonzero divisors, convergence of a canonical transformation or actual conservation of J_r outside this formal resonant Hamiltonian are not asserted. On a finite union of initially active shells the resonant polynomial truncation has invariant support, since every monomial that uses an omitted shell has at least two factors in that shell. These shells are finite on a lattice with positive-definite metric, and the finite-dimensional polynomial Hamiltonian flow is global at a fixed finite order because their preserved sum of nonnegative actions bounds the amplitudes.

## Proof and exceptional depths

Absorb the A_i into coefficients c_i and discard zero coefficients; order the remaining distinct radii as 0<r_1<…<r_s. The case s=1 is immediate. For s≥2 set X_i(H)=sqrt(tanh(r_i H)) for H>0 and form
`P(X)=Π_{ε_2,…,ε_s=±1} (c_1 X_1 + Σ_{i=2}^s ε_i c_i X_i)`.
This includes the all-plus factor. The individual signs of X_i for i>1 permute the factors, and flipping X_1 permutes them with a factor `(-1)^(2^(s−1))=+1`. Thus P is a homogeneous polynomial of degree `d=2^(s−2)` in X_i². For each signed factor, as H→∞, the substitution X_i(H) gives its constant term minus `c_1 exp(-2r_1 H)`, plus a term that is little-o of `exp(-2r_1 H)`. Even if its constant term cancels, the factor is nonzero for all sufficiently large H. The product is therefore not identically zero.

Using `X_i(H)^2=(exp(2r_i H)−1)/(exp(2r_i H)+1)` and multiplying by `Π_i(exp(2r_i H)+1)^d` now gives a nonzero exponential polynomial E(H). The numerator before substitution has individual degrees at most d in the variables exp(2r_i H); collect all equal exponents and remove zero terms, so `E(H)=Σ_{j=1}^L b_j exp(β_j H)`, with distinct real β_j. This handles all rational dependencies among the radii; no independence assumption on their rational span is needed. Here b_j and β_j are algebraic. At H=h the β_j h remain distinct algebraic numbers. The linear-form version of Lindemann–Weierstrass gives E(h)≠0. All denominators are positive at h, so the all-plus signed sum cannot vanish. Grouping a signed resonance by radius finishes the frequency claim; the reverse direction holds tautologically.

For fixed distinct positive radii and a fixed coefficient list with all c_i nonzero, both allowed to be real rather than algebraic, this argument still gives a nonzero E(H) with `L≤(d+1)^s`. A nonzero exponential polynomial of L real exponentials has at most L−1 real zeros, counted with multiplicity: divide out the lowest exponential, differentiate, and apply Rolle's theorem inductively. There is a zero of order at least d at H=0, because the homogeneous polynomial in tanh vanishes there to that order. Away from zero the zeros of E are symmetric, with multiplicity: polynomial homogeneity gives `E(−H)=(-1)^d exp(−2d H Σ_i r_i) E(H)`. Thus the fixed unbalanced list has at most `floor((L−1−d)/2)` positive exceptional depths; this bound is for a single list, not the union over a lattice. For a countable lattice and fixed weights independent of H, that union is countable, possibly with accumulation. If all distinct radii are rational multiples of one positive algebraic radius, a choice of common radius unit also makes E a polynomial in one exponential; an exceptional positive depth then has that exponential algebraic. The theorem already establishes transcendence of every exceptional positive depth when radii and weights are algebraic, without that commensurability restriction.

## Explicit finite-depth mixed triad

On the square torus with positive unit gravity and α=1/2, let `u=(4,3), v=(4,-3), w=(8,0)`. Then u+v=w, with radii 5,5,8. The frequency condition 2ω(5)=ω(8) is exactly `tanh(5h)/tanh(8h)=44/45`. The ratio increases strictly from 5/8 to 1: its logarithmic derivative is the difference of `2r/sinh(2rh)` at r=5 and r=8, positive as a function of radius is decreasing for h>0. Consequently there is a unique critical depth, approximately 0.4415313803619726. Setting X=exp(2h)>1, it satisfies
`X^13+89X^5−89X^8−1=0`, with `2.41829503<X<2.41829504` by exact rational substitution. The polynomial's root X=1 is excluded (the ratio substitution would be indeterminate there). This h is transcendental by Hermite–Lindemann, since X>1 is algebraic.

The momentum and frequency conditions here give an actual nonzero coefficient at cubic order on the flat-bottom torus. Use normalized Haar measure and the cubic Hamiltonian
`H_3=(1/2)∫η (|∇ψ|² − (G_0 ψ)²)`,
obtained from the first Dirichlet–Neumann expansion; `G_0` has symbol `λ_r=r tanh(rh)`. The surface-tension energy is even in the surface gradient and has no cubic term. Use canonical coordinates
`η_k=E_r (a_k+conj(a_{−k}))`, `ψ_k=−i E_r ρ_r (a_k−conj(a_{−k}))`, with `E_r=sqrt(λ_r/(2ω_r))` and `ρ_r=ω_r/λ_r`. The three Fourier momenta for `a_u a_v conj(a_w)` are u,v,−w. For each choice of which factor comes from η the two orders of ψ cancel 1/2. In a ψ pair with momenta p,q the multiplier before the ψ phases is `−p·q−λ_{|p|}λ_{|q|}`. The two positive a terms in ψ have phases (−i)²=−1 and dot product u·v=7. A pair with a and conj(a) in ψ has phase +1 and dot product u·(−w)=v·(−w)=−32. The coefficient divided by the positive `E_5² E_8 ρ_5²` is
`7+λ_5² + 2(ρ_8/ρ_5)(32−λ_5λ_8)`.
At resonance `ρ_8/ρ_5=2λ_5/λ_8` and `λ_5/λ_8=(5/8)(44/45)=11/18`. Therefore the normalized coefficient is `7−3λ_5²+128(11/18)>92/9`, since λ_5<5. Opposite signs on the two phase conventions or a different overall Fourier normalization do not affect nonvanishing when the conventions are used consistently.

## Small-divisor contrast in pure gravity

The known deep-water trident at parameters (2,1) has momenta `(49,0),(−9,0)` versus `(20,15),(20,−15)` and frequencies `7,3` versus `5,5`. Multiply all momenta by N. At a fixed finite h>0 with **zero** surface tension, the signed detuning is
`Δ_N=√N [7 sqrt(tanh(49hN)) + 3 sqrt(tanh(9hN)) − 10 sqrt(tanh(25hN))]`.
The deep-water terms cancel; `sqrt(tanh x)=1−exp(−2x)+O(exp(−4x))` at positive large x gives
`Δ_N ∼ −3√N exp(−18hN)`.
Hence these are nonzero for all sufficiently large N (indeed for all N if h is algebraic, by the theorem). With maximum wavenumber 49N this excludes a positive inverse-power lower bound on all nonzero momentum-compatible pure-gravity quartic phases, even at fixed positive algebraic depth. No claim about their nonlinear coupling at quartic order or a bound at fixed nonzero surface tension follows. The calculation combines a known deep-water quartet with the standard finite-depth exponential correction.

In their one-dimensional horizontal setting and at positive algebraic parameters, the exact cubic resonance exclusion here can be combined with their Lemma 4.4 to obtain their uniform cubic phase gap; this does not extend our argument to higher orders or two horizontal dimensions. [Feola–Montalto–Murgante (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20968) address the two-horizontal-dimensional **infinite-depth** problem, near resonances, and PDE lifespans, rather than this arithmetic finite-depth condition. The Dirichlet–Neumann expansion used here goes back to [Craig–Sulem](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcph.1993.1164).

The additive statement is the explicit fixed-algebraic-parameter linear-independence proof for arbitrary finite lists of distinct algebraic radii and weights, with controlled per-list exceptional depths and an illustrated nonzero mixed triad. The method, the pure-gravity partition antecedent, and shell-action conservation when radius balance holds are not claimed as new. Priority for the full scope is not asserted.
