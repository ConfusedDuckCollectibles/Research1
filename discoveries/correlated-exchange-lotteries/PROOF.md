# Common-law maximin guarantees for prefix chains

## Model and theorem

Let V be a finite set of paired vertices, with distinct non-loop directed arcs between them. Non-directed sources have specified outgoing arcs to paired vertices, all with success probability 1. Every risky paired arc e has an exogenous binary availability X_e with marginal 1/2. All joint laws on the risky arc indicators with those marginals are admissible. Nature may know the lottery but the joint law is fixed before, and independently of, its realized draw. A feasible deterministic plan is an allowed vertex-disjoint packing of simple source chains and nontrivial directed cycles. The donation prefix ends on the first unavailable arc and there is no fallback, recourse, or testing after choice that changes the plan. Cycles give all or no unit-payoff receipts atomically. Each successfully performed edge counts one and all other values are zero; not a health-adjusted utility. D is the maximum over allowed pure plans of their worst expected realized count, and L is the analogous maximum over lotteries with the same admissible pure plans. No individual preference constraints are embedded in D or L.

Let n_j(P) count the chains of P with at least j recipients. For each fixed plan the worst expected realized count is exactly n_1 + n_2/2, for any chain cap at least two. The first arc in each chain succeeds for sure and the second has marginal 1/2. Upper attainment for this fixed plan: in each chain with at least three recipients use a fair bit and its complement on the first two risky arcs, so they cannot both succeed. In each cycle choose any two distinct risky arcs and use another complementary pair, killing the cycle. The components are vertex-disjoint, so their chosen arcs do not conflict; their fair bits and unconstrained remaining arcs can be extended arbitrarily with correct marginals. This constructs one law admissible for that particular plan, not a single law that kills all plans.

Now use **one law simultaneously for all plans**: draw a uniform total order of the paired vertices. A risky arc succeeds if and only if its source is earlier than its recipient in that order. Edges have the prescribed marginals; no directed cycle has all arcs increasing. The r-th receipt in a simple chain requires all r paired vertices along its prefix to occur in their specified order, an event of probability 1/r! (the initial source has a certain arc, is not ranked, and is not part of the event). The law's expected realized count on P is therefore the sum of n_j(P)/j!. For cap 3 this is at most n_1 + n_2/2 + n_3/6 ≤ (10/9)(n_1+n_2/2) ≤ (10/9)D. The same law applies to all P, so the bound survives any private lottery and L ≥ D by using an optimal pure plan. For arbitrary chain cap l ≥2, this proves L ≤ (2/3) (sum of 1/j! for j=1..l) D, using n_j≤n_2 and n_1≥n_2; no assertion of general sharpness past l=3. Even with no finite cap this upper factor is at most (2/3)(exp(1)-1), about 1.1455. No assertion for heterogeneous rewards, self loops, uncertain first links, policy-dependent marginals, or fallback.

## Fractional cut-cover certificate for one chain at cap 3

Assume exactly one chain can be used (no cycles in this subproblem), and there is a legal length-three chain. Shorter choices are pointwise no better in conditional expectation under any admissible law than a full choice, whose value is at least 3/2 everywhere. Associate every full choice with the unordered pair of its risky arcs. Make H with risky arcs as vertices and each possible such pair as an edge, merging duplicates. A lottery assigns weights w_e to these pair-edges, totaling one.

For any two risky arcs i,j, equal marginals imply P(X_i=X_j=1)=P(X_i=X_j=0), hence their common success probability is half the probability that a binary split does not cut the pair-edge. Conversely any law of splits, symmetrized with its complement with equal probability, is a valid law with marginals 1/2 and represents that same expected uncut weight. Consequently a given lottery has exact robust value

    3/2 + (1 - maximum weighted cut of H at weights w)/2.

Let tau(H) be the minimum total nonnegative mass on cuts to cover every edge with at least mass one (the standard fractional cut-cover number). For nonempty H, finite minimax gives max_w min_cut uncut weight = min_Q max_edge P(edge not cut). Dividing a feasible cover of mass tau by tau gives a law covering every edge with probability ≥1/tau, and conversely any cut law with minimum coverage a>0 scales to a cover of mass 1/a. Thus the exact optimal bonus is (1-1/tau)/2. There is no bonus precisely when H is bipartite: a cut covers all edges iff the graph is bipartite, and an average of cuts cannot cover every edge with probability one unless a positive-weight cut covers all. Any odd cycle of length k alone certifies a bonus at least 1/(2k), by uniform lottery on that cycle. The directed physical graph need not contain any cycle. The criterion is stated for unweighted one-chain utility with no cycles, not all multi-source or priority variants. In cap-three chain-only models with several sources, the same cut substitution still gives the exact value of a fixed lottery: use its background expected first and second positions plus one-half the minimum uncut weighted full-prefix count, where weights may now sum to more than one. Similarly each label's guaranteed receipt has its own cut expression using only prefixes ending in that label; separate minima are legitimate because each floor is required under every law.

For a k-cycle H, k odd, the uniform lottery guarantees one edge of the cycle uncut in every split. Nature can symmetrically choose the edge left uncut and choose an alternating split along all other edges, so each pair has expected success 1/(2k) regardless of plan. An even k-cycle has a full cut. The directed triangle V={0,1,2} with the three circulating risky arcs realizes H=C3 and achieves factor 10/9. Its optional 3-cycle cannot improve the universal upper, so sharpness also holds in the theorem's fuller plan class. Disjoint copies remain sharp at arbitrary size by additivity and the same upper bound. The DAG with risky arcs 0→1, 1→2, 2→3, 3→4, 1→3 has H=C5 and achieves factor 16/15; roots are reachable by a certain source arc and no return edge is allowed. A directed four-cycle gives H=C4, a negative control.

## Triangle with variable marginals and optional priority floors

Consider only the directed triangle with one certain source and all three risky arcs sharing arbitrary marginal p. Write h_i=P(X_i=X_(i+1)=1), f=max(0,2p−1), and g=max(0,3p−1−3f). If M is the number of available risky arcs, then sum h_i=E[choose(M,2)]≥3p−1 (choose(M,2)≥M−1 for each M). Also h_i≥f. For any lottery w over full chains starting at i, its worst-case third-position contribution is exactly f+g min_i w_i. To attain:
- For p≤1/3, use only empty and singleton success sets, giving all h_i=0.
- For p≥2/3, use all-success probability 3p−2, plus each of the three sets with one failure with probability 1−p, giving all h_i=f.
- Otherwise assign masses h_i=f to the exact two-success sets, put the entire excess g on a minimum-weight pair, and assign each singleton e probability p minus the sum of the two pair masses containing e. These are nonnegative and sum to one by construction. There is no all-success event in this middle range.

Uniform gives exact lottery optimum 1+p+f+g/3, and pure optimum is 1+p+f. Short chains cannot improve the utility under any law. Adding the atomic triangle as an optional alternative does not improve these optima: the symmetric upper-witness law has no all-success when p≤2/3; when p≥2/3 it gives cycle expected 9p−6 ≤ 3p, the full-chain result. Relative advantage in the full interval p∈[.45,.55] is at least min(94/87,106/99)=106/99. This p curve pertains only to the triangle.

At p=.5 each recipient i has worst probability w_i + w_(i−1)/2, since a single adversarial coupling can zero its late conjunctive receipt and supports no successful triangle cycle. These are simulated acceptance constraints, not measures of welfare or ethical rules. For floor b_0=.60 and b_1=b_2=.35, if m=min_i w_i and z is optional lottery mass on the atomic cycle, the necessary bound is .60≤w_0+w_2/2≤1−z−3m/2; shorter chains may be extended without reducing any recipient probability. Under the adverse law concentrated on the minimum-weight conjunction, aggregate value is at most 3(1−z)/2+m/2. The optimum is therefore at z=0, m=4/15, attained by w=(7,4,4)/15 and value 49/30. Individual robust receipt guarantees are .60, .50, .40. No pure plan satisfies all positive floors, so the reference 3/2 is the *unconstrained* pure utility, not a nonexistent feasible pure optimum.

## Longer complete-digraph benchmark

For n=3,4,5 take every directed paired arc (no loops) and one source; here allow a single chain but no simultaneous cycles. Chain cap n. Any shorter chain is dominated pointwise by completing that same order to length n. In the resulting symmetric zero-sum game, averaging any optimal full-order lottery over every vertex permutation gives the uniform full-order lottery (concavity of the worst-law value and invariance to relabeling). Likewise, to evaluate uniform one may average any adverse mask law over labels, enforcing equal individual marginals by requiring expected mask size m/2 for m=n(n−1). At each mask cardinality k, the least uniform-order return comes from the minimum of exactly enumerated masks. The exact lower convex envelope of those minima at m/2 needs at most two sizes. Both exhaustive implementations agree; each minimum seed mask at m/2 and its uniform relabeling attain the values, 5/3, 5/3, 17/10 respectively. The uniform-random-rank upper bound is not relatively sharp on the size-4 and size-5 complete benchmarks even though it is universal.

## Information and policy limits

If the adverse law is allowed to depend on the **realized** length/chain choice, every plan faces its own fixed-plan worst case and the unconstrained utility of randomization is at most D; no utility gain. If instead law is known, linear expectation by itself likewise needs no lottery (separate probability-floor objectives can still differ). If selected outcomes are affected by assignment, or if only on-trajectory uncancelled edges are observed, the joint exogenous ambiguity cannot be verified from a raw failure percentage. At cap three, a hypothetical publication giving the joint availability probabilities of relevant successive pairs and any cycles, under a common exogenous measurement definition, would be enough to identify these expected counts; no higher moments are needed for that restricted target. The proof does not establish how to collect that in practice, what a clinically valid feasible set is, or whether the strong baseline assumptions fit any program.
