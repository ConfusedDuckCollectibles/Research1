# Independent audit: a common-law lottery for prefix chains

**Result.** The cap-three \(10/9\) upper factor, triangle sharpness, fractional cut-cover formula, acyclic \(C_5\) example, triangle priority-floor and variable-\(p\) controls, and complete cap-four/five benchmarks check. They are synthetic graph-model theorems only. The paper should cite the existing kidney-exchange DRO literature and distinguish its metric and information order, without implying novel general DRO, randomized fairness, actual clinical proposal, or global priority.

## Model and sharp universal bound

Fix a finite directed graph of paired vertices, specified certain initial arcs from non-directed sources, non-loop risky arcs, and disjoint packings of simple source chains and possibly atomic cycles. A performed arc earns one, never a weight for patient utility or resources. The chain receives its successful initial prefix and cancels everything downstream of its first failure; there is no testing, recourse, reassignment, or fallback. Each risky binary arc has marginal one-half; every coupling is feasible. Nature chooses **one joint law**, not conditional on the realized lottery action. The draw is independent of that law and the latent availabilities.

For any fixed packing \(P\), let \(n_j(P)\) be the number of chains with at least \(j\) recipients, counting the certain receipt as the first. Its guaranteed count is \(n_1+n_2/2\). To make it exact for that particular packing, use complementary fair bits on the first two risky arcs of every length-at-least-three chain, and on any two distinct arcs of each cycle. All coordinates chosen this way are disjoint between components; fill remaining coordinates with independent fair bits. The lower bound holds for any admissible coupling by positivity. This argument does not claim a single coupling kills all packings; that would be false in the triangle example.

There is instead a simultaneous comparison coupling: sample a uniform order of the paired vertices and let a risky arc succeed iff it points forward. It has the correct marginals and kills all directed cycles. A \(j\)-receipt prefix needs the specified ordering of its \(j\) distinct paired vertices, probability \(1/j!\); the non-directed donor is not part of this order. Thus any cap-three plan scores at most
\[
n_1+n_2/2+n_3/6\ \le\ (10/9)(n_1+n_2/2).
\]
The same coupling bounds the expected score of every lottery. This is an inequality, including when the optimal pure score \(D\) is zero. For a cap \(\ell\ge2\), \(\sum_{j\ge3}n_j/j!\le n_2\sum_{j\ge3}1/j!\) and \(n_1+n_2/2\ge3n_2/2\) gives the stated factorial extension. The upper factor for longer caps is not shown to be sharp.

## Why one chain reduces exactly to fractional cut covering

Assume a legal full chain, only one chain per plan, and no cycles. Shorter choices are no better in expected unit count under **any** admissible law than any full choice. Project a full chain onto the unordered pair of its two risky arcs, merging duplicates into an edge of \(H\) whose vertices are the risky arcs. No loops occur. Given any realization of a binary split, its cut on \(H\) records where the indicators differ. Since the two margins are equal, the joint-success probability for any edge of \(H\) is one-half the probability of NOT cutting that edge. Conversely, a deterministic split and its complement, sampled ever with equal probability, produce exactly these marginals and leave the cut unchanged. Thus for a plan lottery \(w\) over edges of \(H\), the exact worst count is \(3/2+(1-\max_Cw(C))/2\).

Write \(\tau\) for the standard fractional cut-cover number. A cover of total mass \(\tau\), normalized to a cut law, guarantees every edge coverage at least \(1/\tau\); any law with worst-edge coverage \(a>0\) scales back to a cover of mass \(1/a\). Finite minimax on edges and cuts shows that the best lottery value is \(3/2+(1-1/\tau)/2\). The graph has \(\tau=1\) iff all edges can be cut together (bipartiteness); one needs no convexity claim about arbitrary packings with atomic cycles. The fixed-lottery formula without normalization extends to multiple simultaneous chains with no cycles by replacing the baseline by the expected first and second receipts and using the expected count of every full-prefix pair as its cut weight.

A directed triangle with every starting vertex accessible yields \(H=C_3\) and exact value \(5/3\) (pure \(3/2\)), saturating the upper factor. A single NDD is used at most once, so adding the atomic triangle cannot improve the pure value; the general same-law upper bound rules out an additional mixed gain as well. With one source accessing all roots, for the DAG arcs \(a=0\to1,b=1\to2,c=2\to3,d=3\to4,e=1\to3\), the full pair graph is exactly \(a-b-c-d-e-a\). Indeed the paths can use \(a\) then \(b\) or \(e\), \(b\) then \(c\), \(c\) then \(d\), and \(e\) then \(d\); no others compose. A uniformly random sole uncut edge coupling makes any individual full choice score \(3/2+1/10\). Uniform plan weights give this same lower bound by the odd cycle. The ratio is \(16/15\). The even cycle is an exact no-gain control, not a claim that all bipartite original graphs or cyclic graphs share a robustness value.

## Extensions audited

- For the directed triangle with uniform marginal \(p\), write \(h_i\) for successive two-arc joint success, \(f=\max(0,2p-1)\), \(g=\max(0,3p-1-3f)\). Then \(\sum_i h_i\ge3p-1\) since the number of two-success arc pairs in a mask with \(m\) successes is \(\binom m2\ge m-1\). With each \(h_i\ge f\), any lottery's worst third step is at least \(f+g\min_iw_i\). For \(p\le1/3\) singletons/empty attain it. For \(1/3\le p\le1/2\) put all pair probability \(3p-1\) on a minimal-weight pair: its two singleton residuals are \(1-2p\), its other singleton is \(p\). For \(1/2\le p\le2/3\) put probability \(1-p\) on that pair, \(2p-1\) on the other pairs and one singleton residual \(2-3p\); for \(p\ge2/3\), all-success weight \(3p-2\) and equally placed one-failure masks attain all \(h_i=f\). No negative probability or endpoint exception. Optional three-cycle comparison also holds: its worst outcome has no triple success below \(2/3\), and at/above \(2/3\) expected \(9p-6\le3p\). On the .45 to .55 window the ratio is increasing to .5 and then decreasing, with endpoint minimum \(106/99\).
- For the priority illustration at \(p=1/2\), the label-\(i\) robust floor is \(w_i+w_{i-1}/2\) if full chains are used, even with optional cycle mass \(z\), because one fair law can kill both the label's late two-arc conjunction and the cycle simultaneously. Shorter chains can be extended pointwise in this triangle. With \(m=\min_iw_i\), the lead floor \(3/5\) implies \(3/5\le1-z-3m/2\). The worst objective is \(3(1-z)/2+m/2\) by assigning all third-position potential to one minimum-weight path and killing the cycle. Thus \(49/30\) is an actual upper bound attained at \(w=(7,4,4)/15,z=0\) with individual floors \(3/5,1/2,2/5\). These are **separate** worst-law floor guarantees, with potentially different worst laws. An unconstrained pure benchmark is \(3/2\); no pure plan satisfies all three positive stated floors.
- In the complete bidirected \(n=3,4,5\) controls, one chain only, all shorter words can be extended, and group averaging any lottery retains or improves its worst-law value (concavity); the full words form a transitive orbit, hence uniform is optimal. The uniform-plan value of a latent mask is invariant under relabeling. Averaging any fixed mask over permutations makes every risky edge marginal its cardinality divided by \(m=n(n-1)\). Therefore sums across a general law need only enforce expected mask size \(m/2\) once symmetrized. In the new independent replay, use one representative per complete arc-mask permutation orbit, count ordered simple paths via subset DP, and weight each \(j\)-vertex path by \((n-j)!\), the number of full words extending its successful prefix. Per-cardinality coverage is checked against \(\binom{m}{k}\), be it one or many orbits. For the minimum unnormalized score (numerator out of \(n!\)), affine supports with integral slopes 2, 4, 14 respectively certify that mixing different cardinalities cannot improve the midpoint minima 10, 40, 204. Each explicit midpoint minimizing mask, randomly relabeled, attains the bounds, hence exact values \(5/3,5/3,17/10\). These are not exact universal cap-four or cap-five factors; the samples only refute a proposed sharpness mechanism.

## Closest prior and novelty limit

- [Delage–Kuhn–Wiesemann, *Dice-sion Making under Uncertainty*](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/5582.pdf) already treats strict advantages from an independent randomization device under ambiguity. [Delage–Saif](https://biblos.hec.ca/biblio/libreacces/23686707.pdf) gives general mixed-integer randomized DRO bounds, algorithms, continuous relaxation conditions, and assignment examples. The common-law vs realized-action timing distinction is an instance of this established phenomenon, not a new model class.
- [McElfresh–Dickerson–Ren–Bidkhori, WSC 2021](https://duncanmcelfresh.github.io/files/Distributionally_Robust_Cycle_and_Chain_Packing_With_Application_To_Organ_Exchange.pdf) is particularly close and should be cited directly: it already applies DRO to the size-limited kidney-exchange cycle-and-chain packing problem, over unknown distributions of match *qualities* through CVaR and data-driven ambiguity, not unknown common fair binary prefix survivals and independently mixed plans. [McElfresh–Bidkhori–Dickerson, AAAI 2019](https://duncanmcelfresh.github.io/files/robust_kex_aaai19.pdf), §4 and Appendix B, does model binary edge existence uncertainty, but over a deterministic failure-count-budget set with a different post-failure objective/structure, not a fixed-marginal distribution game.
- [Anderson–Ashlagi–Gamarnik–Roth, PNAS 2015](https://rma350.github.io/assets/publications/PNAS-2015-FindingLongChainsInKidneyExchangeTSP.pdf), SI p. 9 “Stochastic Optimization for the KEP,” explicitly assumes no independence between edge availability bits, but assumes joint sampling and permits next-stage testing/reoptimization. [Carvalho et al., IJOC 2021](https://researchportal.ulisboa.pt/en/publications/robust-models-for-the-kidney-exchange-problem/) and [Blom et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08565) are robust attacker-defender recourse models. Claiming first use of correlated exchange failures or first robust exchange is wrong.
- [St-Arnaud–Carvalho–Farnadi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00241) already uses plan lotteries for ex ante fairness, without this common marginal-only uncertainty game. [Šámal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1571065305052510) gives fractional cut-covering itself; [Proença et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15346) gives the weighted MaxCut connection. The standard minmax/cover result is not novel; the new-looking results are its prefix-specific reduction, the factorial rank bound and tight constructions. No closely identical theorem surfaced in these primary sources, but literature priority remains unestablished.
