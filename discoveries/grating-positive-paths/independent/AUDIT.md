# Independent audit: stationary positive-path grating separator

**Verdict.** The exact fixed-time cone inequality, its angular sharpness, and the proposed gray continuum counterexample pass. This is a rejection rule for a precisely calibrated carrier-path null, not a general passivity test, a hydrodynamic-phase discriminator, or a claim about a published material waveform. The latest primary comparisons below already contain the same forward gray Callaway equations and related sign/phase diagnostics. The sharp finite two-wavevector constraint and the positive-residual violation are the meaningful combination.

## Null, sharpness and the exact attainable body

Let \(Z=(Vt)^{-1}\int_0^t v_s\,ds\); if \(S\) is independent uniform on the time interval, \(A=v_S/V\) has the calibrated normalized marginal \(\pi\) and \(E[A\mid Z]=Z\). In fact one-time stationarity is stronger than necessary: time-averaged marginal \(\pi\) suffices. Thus \(J(f)=E f(A)-E f(Z)\ge 0\) for convex \(f\). At fixed physical \(t\), the same random \(Z\) is required for both wavevectors. For \(f_c=-\cos(cx)/c^2\), \(J(f_c)=D(c)/c^2\), \(f''_c=\cos(cx)>0\) if \(0<c<\pi/2\). On the full support, \(\cos(c_2 x)/\cos(c_1 x)\) decreases strictly as \(|x|\) runs from zero to one: for \(x>0\) its log derivative is \(c_1\tan(c_1x)-c_2\tan(c_2x)<0\). Hence \(f_{c_2}-(\cos c_2/\cos c_1)f_{c_1}\) and \(f_{c_1}-f_{c_2}\) are convex. These are exactly the two normalized residual inequalities. Strict Jensen implies \(D(c_1)=0\) forces \(A=Z\) almost surely, hence all \(D(c)=0\) at that time, not merely the two small-\(c\) values.

The sharpness construction works for fixed uniform \(\pi\): freeze velocities outside a narrow active interval and use ordinary positive-rate Poisson refreshing to that interval's conditional equilibrium within it. Its detailed-balance jump-law pairs agree; strict Jensen is positive from nonconstant paths and positive occupation of the active interval. Paths stay in the interval, where the ratio of curvatures lies arbitrarily close to the desired edge for intervals near \(|x|=1\) or near zero. For an inversion-invariant null use mirrored intervals and equal controls at the two endpoints. Adding a small common full-space Poisson reset makes each process irreducible and preserves its marginals and reversibility; its probability of changing the finite path is at most its rate times \(t\), so let its rate go to zero faster than the local Jensen residual. The wedge is the **sharp closed conical hull** of these residual pair directions, not an unbounded set of attainable data pairs. Constants need not be sharp for an arbitrary fixed marginal whose support or atoms preclude concentrating a continuous refreshed interval at the extremal curvature.

I also audited the optional fixed-time converse. By [Strassen 1965, Theorem 8](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177700153), any \(\nu\preceq_{\rm cx}\pi\) on \([-1,1]\) admits \((Z,A)\) with these marginals and \(E[A\mid Z]=Z\). Choose the jointly measurable conditional quantile \(Q_z(u)\) of \(A\mid Z=z\) (regular disintegration on the real line). Let \(U\) be independent uniform on the circle, and set
\[
 v_s/V=Q_Z\!\left(2\min\{\langle U+s/t\rangle,1-\langle U+s/t\rangle\}\right)
\]
where brackets denote fractional part. The fold preserves uniform measure and is even on the circle. Thus the path is stationary, time reversible, and its average over a full period \(t\) exactly equals \(Z\). Conditional quantile endpoints of measure zero are immaterial. The same path produces all wavevectors at the chosen time, but the construction sets its period to this time and need not fit a collection of observations at multiple times. It is generally not Markov on velocity alone, although latent phase and \(Z\) furnish a deterministic augmented-state evolution. If the true same-direction response is required to have zero imaginary part, for symmetric \(\pi\) symmetrize \(\nu\) and the path sign; all cosine data stay unchanged.

For any finite number of \(c_j\), the body of responses at the chosen \(t\) is the compact convex image of \(\{\nu:\nu\preceq_{\rm cx}\pi\}\). Its support for \(F(x)=\sum w_j\cos(c_jx)\), with unrestricted real \(w_j\), is \(\inf_{h\text{ convex},\ h\ge F}\pi h\), where continuous \(h\) suffice. To check: over compact probability measures \(\nu\) and the convex cone of continuous convex \(\phi\), the Lagrangian \(\nu F+(\pi-\nu)\phi\) is separately continuous affine; its infimum over \(\phi\) is \(\nu F\) on the feasible order set and \(-\infty\) otherwise by scaling. The minimax interchange gives infimum \(\pi\phi+\max(F-\phi)\), precisely the convex-majorant formula by shifting \(\phi\) by the maximum. This is a fixed-time broad-path exact characterization, not a characterization of kinetic generators. For \(\pi\) symmetric on \(\{-1,1\}\), every symmetric probability on \([-1,1]\) is convex-dominated. When \(c_2=2c_1\), put \(a=\cos c_1\), \(m=\Theta_1\), \(n=(1+\Theta_2)/2\). The body is exactly \(a\le m\le1,\ m^2\le n\le(1+a)m-a\), by the variance and the chord of \(y^2\) on \([a,1]\); all intermediate \(n\) arise by blending \(Y=m\) with a mixture of endpoints preserving \(m\), and lifting symmetrically through \(\cos(c_1 Z)=Y\). This confirms why not to call the cone the whole pair-feasibility region.

## Physics, exact replay and countercontrols

For energy-conserving single-mode RTA, distinguish true energy readout \(\int h\,d\pi\) in heat-capacity normalized \(h\) from the common collision pseudo-temperature \(\int \gamma h\,d\pi/\int\gamma\,d\pi\). If \(0<\int \gamma\,d\pi<\infty\) and regular finite jump rates, the generator \(\gamma_i(\int h\,d\pi_\gamma-h_i)\) is a stationary reversible positive reset process, with jump balance \(\pi(di)\gamma_i\pi_\gamma(dj)\) symmetric, and tilted propagation exactly the path expectation. All zero rates just give ballistic behavior. This claim is about the matched preparation/readout weight and a conservative RTA; a nonthermal optical mode weighting, loss boundaries, signed off-diagonal collective operators, coherent transport and changes between gratings do not reach that conclusion. In the calibrated null the wavelengths intentionally probe the same path law. The model warning is substantive, not a claim that all electrodynamic or passive heat mechanisms reduce to independent carriers.

In gray 3D Debye there is a common speed \(V\) and \(z=\hat k\cdot\hat q\) uniform on \([-1,1]\). The linear variation of the displaced Bose state, after normalizing the modal energy by mode heat capacity, is \(\delta T+T_0(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf u)/\omega=\delta T+(T_0/V)\hat k\cdot\mathbf u\). Constant rates and energy/momentum moment constraints give orthogonal projections onto \(1,z\) for normal scattering in the longitudinal sector, and onto \(1\) for resistive scattering. Total linear energy is conserved; only the **normal part** conserves momentum (the resistive term damps it). \(L=\Gamma_N(I-P_{0,1})+\Gamma_R(I-P_0)\) is nonnegative self-adjoint in this mode-energy norm, and streaming is skew-adjoint, so the linear evolution is passive in this precise dissipativity sense. This does not imply positive evolution of signed excess energy. Its putative angular collision gain kernel \(N+R+3N zz'\) is explicitly negative for intervals of near-antipodal \(z,z'\) at the test rates. This is compatible with positive *occupations* sufficiently close to equilibrium and a locally meaningful displaced Bose equilibrium for drift below \(V\). No equivalence to ab initio scattering of any particular graphite sample is claimed.

The independent check.py uses the exact Legendre basis, not the author's monomials nor a finite-angle grid. In the basis \((-i)^kP_k(z)\), collisional eigenvalues are \(0,R,N+R,N+R,\ldots\); the identity \(zP_k=((k+1)P_{k+1}+kP_{k-1})/(2k+1)\) makes streaming tridiagonal. The constant coefficient is the energy mean and each \(A^n1\) stays in degree \(n\). The exact 52-term-and-constant propagation has remainder at most \((N+R+c)^{53}/53!\), since for \(A=-tL-ic z\) the semigroup is contractive and \(\|A\|\le N+R+c\). Alternating exact rational truncations separately bracket \(\cos c\) and \(\operatorname{sinc}c\). All comparisons use exact fractions; the replay decimals report rounded endpoints only.

The certified pair is \(\Theta_1\simeq0.94211835636462,\ \Theta_2\simeq0.77717759160307\), with \(D_1\simeq0.00104756737290,\ D_2\simeq0.00047835329704\) and cone discrepancy \(K D_1-D_2\simeq0.00136135483647\) for \(K=4\cos(1.2)/\cos(0.6)\simeq1.75617\). Subtracting the exact worst additive-error cost \((1+K)0.0004\) leaves \(>0.00025\) margin and each residual strictly positive. This budget presumes exact \(c_i\) and bounds on *the entire residual errors*, including any errors in the ballistic subtraction insofar as included in that budget; it does not apply to unpropagated speed, temperature amplitude calibration, or true-time uncertainties. The positive rate resistive-only \(N=0\) control lies strictly inside both inequality directions. Low-order \(t^3q^2\), \(t^5q^4\) comparisons and sign conventions independently check; the short-time factor \(32\) is sufficient for this model's moment criterion, not a transport-regime threshold.

**Adversarial control: bounded positive displacement alone can reproduce this exact comparator.** Even for this witness, \(a<m<1,\ m^2<n<(1+a)m-a\) passes the preceding two-moment band numerically by exact inequalities, so some positive bounded displacement law matches the two amplitudes, just not one coming from the calibrated uniform stationary velocity. Explicitly let \(e=1-m\), \(r=1-2m+n\). Use \(1-Y\) equal to \(r/e\) with probability \(e^2/r\), else zero; lift \(Y=\cos(c_1 Z)\) to symmetric \(Z\) with support strictly inside \((-0.9,0.9)\), also certified by exact inequalities. If merely initial uniform velocities were assumed, take \(v_s/V=A\) uniform for the first \(0.01t\), then \(v_s/V=(Z-0.01A)/0.99\) with independently sampled \(Z\). This remains bounded, has the wanted initial law, and produces the identical integrated displacement. The example need not represent any material; it shows what the calibration premise excludes.
