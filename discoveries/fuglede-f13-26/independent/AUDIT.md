# Independent audit: no 26-element spectral set in F13 cubed

**Result.** The finite proof closes the entire cardinality-26 branch in \(\mathbb F_{13}^3\), not only a subclass or a fixed projection. It does not prove the finite Fuglede equivalence in \(\mathbb F_{13}^3\) at other cardinalities. The new computations use exact integer arithmetic in bounded domains; independent generation and equations reproduce all stated counts, orbit classes, and planar limits. The overall method should credit the analogous 22-point proof of Ferguson and Iosevich for \(\mathbb F_{11}^3\). The new part is the specific prime, the finite classifications, and use of both members and local quotient injectivity to discard surviving profile types.

## Geometric reductions and the initial direction bound

Write \(D(B)\) for projective directions of differences in \(B\), \(U(B)\) for projective normal directions with all thirteen parallel affine planes of \(B\)-cardinality two. A spectral pair \((E,A)\) has a scaled unitary character matrix, so both its columns and rows are orthogonal; the prime cyclotomic relation applied to the plane population polynomial gives \(D(A)\subseteq U(E)\) and \(D(E)\subseteq U(A)\). A full projective good line is impossible: its affine pencil through a line with \(a\) points has total \(26+13a\), while uniform occupancy would give \(14\cdot2\). Any affine line in one member lies inside some uniform plane of that member, by pigeonholing the other member into thirteen planes normal to the given line direction and taking a nonzero in-plane difference. Thus each member is a cap (no three collinear). Similarly a plane with fourteen points would produce every plane direction by pigeonhole over its thirteen parallels in each direction, contradicting absence of a complete good line for the other member. Initial plane cap is thirteen.

For a pencil of projective normals \(W\), quotient along its one-dimensional annihilator, using arbitrary linear coordinates in the quotient. Let \(f\) be the integer fibre counts, at most two. Suppose \(L\) profiles are not designated as uniform and the other \(14-L\) are constant two. Choose one good family of affine lines to be vertical. Each remaining profile is \(g_i(y-s_i x)\). Summing the fourteen lines through a point counts every other point once and the point itself fourteen times, giving
\[
\sum_i g_i(y-s_i x)=2(L-1)+13f(x,y).
\]
The formula still holds when some of the \(L\) undesignated profiles are in fact uniform. For \(L=1\), the plane cap thirteen forces \(f\le1\), and two parallel affine quotient lines are filled; thus the original member lies in two parallel planes with thirteen points each.

Neither member can lie in two parallel planes. Such a pair of layers must each be a 13-cap, with 78 pairs and at most six per direction. At least thirteen directions and no full projective line imply precisely thirteen distinct directions, each with six pairs and one leftover vertex on each layer. The other spectral member then has thirteen good profiles on the associated pencil, so is also contained in two parallel planes by the \(L=1\) case. For each of the thirteen layer directions \(d\) of the first set, good normals perpendicular to \(d\) must put the two leftovers together: all other pairs contribute even occupancy. The singleton difference is between layers, not parallel to \(d\), so there is at most one projective normal per line \(d^\perp\). These are thirteen out of the fourteen lines through the first layer normal, and the remaining line has at most thirteen good directions. Therefore its good set has size at most 26. Conversely, the second set's two layers have at least thirteen intra-layer directions and strictly more than thirteen cross-layer directions: identifying the latter with \(Q-P\), equality \(|Q-P|=13\) would force \(Q\) invariant under all differences in \(P\), of which a nonzero one would imply \(Q\) lies on an affine line. This contradicts \(D(A)\subseteq U(E)\).

For any pencil with at least nine uniform profiles, designate exactly nine and set \(L=5\). The modular polynomial bound below gives degree at most three. The canonical nonconstant minimum masses for degrees one, two, three are 78,52,39 by complete exact enumeration, whereas ordinary mass is 26 and dominates canonical mass. Consequently the five polynomials are constant modulo 13. Since ordinary profile values lie in \([0,13]\), each is either constantly 2 or has exactly two entries 13 and all others zero. The constant congruence is \(2k=8\bmod 13\) for the number \(k\) of the first type among five, hence \(k=4\). There is exactly one genuinely bad direction, the case just excluded. Every good pencil therefore has size at most eight.

## Explicit finite gate completeness

Use reduced representatives \(\deg g_i\le12\). Each substituted polynomial has individual variable degrees at most 12, so vanishing on the whole affine plane modulo 13 is a formal identity. If \(c_{i,j}\) is coefficient \(j>0\), each degree yields
\(\sum_i c_{i,j}s_i^e=0\) for \(0\le e\le j\), since the corresponding binomial coefficient is nonzero. Distinct slopes make the Vandermonde rank full for \(j\ge L-1\), so each profile degree is at most \(L-2\). For lower \(j\), the coefficient vector has \(L-j-1\) free coordinates: every complementary set of \(j+1\) coordinates is an invertible square Vandermonde system. Thus whenever the free coordinates are fixed from their allowed values, the rest are uniquely fixed. The author's weighted-Lagrange construction of this same kernel follows from vanishing of the top Lagrange coefficient for any polynomial of degree at most \(L-2\), but the independent implementation does not use it.

The gate table uses exactly \(L\) bad nonconstant profiles and \(M<13\), so values are unique canonical residues. Choose a uniform direction to be vertical. Transform the finite bad slopes by any \(s\mapsto a s+b\) with \(a\ne0\), realized by an invertible linear map preserving the chosen vertical family. Visiting affine orbits only omits equivalent cases, not additional restrictions on which good direction was chosen. Fix the top-minus-one coefficient at zero for the first two distinct bad profiles. Their arguments shift by independent amounts under a single planar translation, because the slopes are distinct. Each polynomial has a positive degree \(d<13\), giving the uniquely available shift where \(c_{d-1}+d\,c_d h=0\).

The new exact profile runner builds every candidate by free **values** at the first \(L-1\) cells subject to the height and mass bounds, then uses vanishing finite differences to extend to each of the other cells. Recover monomial coefficients from the Newton binomial basis, checking point evaluation. In each case the actual slope mask evolves by applying all affine maps to its orbit. ## Coupled-member bootstrap and final pair contradiction

Nine planar cap points would produce at least nine directions by pairing counts, incompatible with the initial ceiling eight on every good pencil of the partner; an eight-cap cannot have at most eight directions by replay. Thus each member has plane cap seven. The \((7,7)\) gate excludes exactly seven good directions on any projective line.

For a projective normal \(v\) **nonuniform for \(E\)**, necessarily \(v\notin D(A)\); otherwise spectrality would demand it be uniform. Consequently the quotient of \(A\) along \(v\) is injective. This is a local statement for the pencil \(v^\perp\), not a global projection or direction-set bound, and makes no orthogonal decomposition assumption if \(v\) is isotropic. No such pencil has eight good directions for \(A\) by the double-fibre obstruction of \((6,8)\), or seven by the earlier gate. Every \(E\) planar slice normal to \(v\) has its difference directions in that very pencil of \(U(A)\), so cannot be a seven-cap (21 pairs, up to three per direction). Uniform \(v\) has planes of size two anyway. Apply this argument symmetrically: both plane caps drop to six.

A size-six slice requires exactly six directions (the five-cap obstruction on a subset rules out fewer), which then exhaust the good normals for the partner in that pencil. The slice directions' class 189 is impossible for the partner gate \((8,6)\), whose good classes are 95 and 111. Hence the planes all contain at most five. At that point \((6,8)\) also excludes eight good directions globally, since surviving profiles have peak six; exactly seven was already excluded. The \((8,5)\) gate now excludes six good directions. The five-cap obstruction therefore reduces the affine plane intersection cap to four. Then \((9,4)\) excludes five good directions globally.

Now \(|D(E)|\le43\): every projective line has at most four \(D(E)\) points, and the fourteen pencil lines through a selected point of \(D(E)\) partition the remaining directions into at most \(14\cdot3\). For any direction \(d\) in \(D(E)\), each affine secant has just two points. Two parallel secants lie in an affine plane with four points. There their six chord pairs use at most four directions because \(D(E)\subseteq U(A)\). Equal directions occur only on disjoint chord pairs, so at least two opposite edge pairs are parallel. This forces a parallelogram: labeling one vertex 0 and two adjacent ones \(u,v\) independent, the last lies on the lines \(u+\langle v\rangle\) and \(v+\langle u\rangle\), hence equals \(u+v\). Thus the original parallel secants have identical displacement up to sign.

Orient all \(d\)-parallel secants by the same nonzero displacement \(w\) and choose their initial points. Their quotient by \(\langle w\rangle\) is a cap, since three collinear projected points would lift six points into a plane. For any projected chords parallel to one another, use difference lifts \(a,a'=\lambda a+\mu w\) with \(\lambda\ne0\). The first two secants give four distinct directions in their span, \([w],[a],[a+w],[a-w]\), which exhaust the available directions there. The second pair gives \(\{(\mu-1)/\lambda,\mu/\lambda,(\mu+1)/\lambda\}=\{-1,0,1\}\). Summing gives \(\mu=0\) and summing squares gives \(\lambda^2=1\) in \(\mathbb F_{13}\). The equal-chord replay then bounds the number of secants in each direction by seven. Summing proves the contradiction \(325=\binom{26}{2}\le43\cdot7=301\).

## Source and replay assessment

- [Fallon–Mayeli–Villano](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02936) prove the full \(p=5,7\) cases and the geometric framework. It does not claim the \(p=13,26\) branch. Their separate [arXiv:2610.36417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36417) treats binary dimensions, not this odd prime.
- [Malikiosis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43670-023-00078-2), Theorem 1.4, excludes sizes strictly above \(p^2-(p-1)\sqrt p\) and below \(p^2\), not \(2p=26\) at \(p=13\). Its conclusion explicitly discusses the limitations at this low cardinality. Searches for size 26, \(2p\) at \(p=13\), and current papers did not surface a matching result; this is not an exhaustive priority guarantee.
