# Independent audit: exact two-branch partial-voltage/linear-impedance alias

## Scope and disposition

The theorem checks within its declared two-electrode equilibrium and calibrated linear diffusion closure. At a single known transferred-charge center, even a **labeled, separated** positive/negative slope measurement plus a full noiseless equilibrium voltage curve does not globally identify the two capacities and center lithiations for the displayed known quadratic OCPs. Both states are strictly admissible; both inverse branches are locally nonsingular. A further calibrated scalar observation separates the two. No diagnosis of any cell, operational procedure, or empirical estimate is made here.

The charge \(q\) is measured transferred charge relative to the common **observed local center**, increasing on discharge; it is not a rescaled state of charge using unknown aged capacity. On discharge the positive electrode gains inserted lithium and the negative loses it, \(x_p=a_p+bq,\ x_n=a_n-cq\), with \(b,c>0\), and \(V=U_p-U_n\) so that \(-V'=g_p+g_n\). Capacities \(1/b,1/c\) and inventory \(a_p/b+a_n/c\) are effective parameters of these active domains and this normalization; their losses relative to a common pristine baseline are not a chemical assay or microscopic attribution. Differential voltage and incremental capacity derived from the same exact monotone trace add no independent information.

## Independent derivation and exact witness

For quadratic known OCPs of opposite curvatures \(U_p''=\alpha>0\), \(U_n''=-\beta<0\), write \(X=\alpha b^2,\ Y=\beta c^2,\ K=X+Y=V''>0\). The labeled central slopes are \(u=g_p(0)>0,\ v=g_n(0)>0\). Completing squares gives
\[
2(V(0)-V_\star)=D=u^2/X+v^2/(K-X),\qquad 0<X<K,
\]
where \(V_\star=(A_p-\gamma_p^2/(2\alpha))-(A_n+\gamma_n^2/(2\beta))\). Positive \(b,c\) follow uniquely from \(X,Y\); \(a_p=(\gamma_p-u/b)/\alpha,\ a_n=(v/c-\gamma_n)/\beta\). This equivalence reconstructs the complete quadratic trace, including its linear coefficient \(-(u+v)\). It proves at most two physical preimages, with the formal zero/one/two count subsequently pruned by domain and baseline constraints. The right side is strictly convex, tends to infinity at both ends, and has minimum \((u+v)^2/K\) at \(Ku/(u+v)\). Its discriminant is \([DK-(u+v)^2][DK-(u-v)^2]\); above the fold the positive root separation is the square root of that expression divided by \(D\). The coordinates \((u,v,K,X)\) have a smooth inverse on the stipulated positive domain, so local rank is equivalent to \(\partial D/\partial X\ne0\). Strict inequalities and two simple roots give an open ambiguous set in the **four quadratic observation coordinates**, not in the ambient space of arbitrary voltage functions.

Use \(U_p=21/5-(8/25)x+x^2/10,\ U_n=3/10-(3/25)x-x^2/10\); both are strictly decreasing on \([0,1]\), derivatives at most \(-3/25\). On \(|q|\le1/20\), the two states \((a_p,a_n,b,c)=(19/25,1/10,5/6,1)\) and \((9/10,6/25,1,5/6)\) lie strictly inside \((0,1)^2\) at both endpoints and hence everywhere. With \(Q_{p0}=Q_{n0}=L_0=5/4\) and pristine center fractions both \(1/2\), all model losses are strictly positive. Independent symmetric interpolation gives \(V=93189/25000-(7/25)q+(61/360)q^2\), both central slopes \(7/50\), roots \(5/36,1/5\), and derivatives \(-32879/62500,+32879/62500\). The inventory is \(253/250\) versus \(297/250\), corresponding to effective LLI \(19.04\%\) versus \(4.96\%\). LAM changes from \((4\%,20\%)\) to \((20\%,4\%)\). This is not a permutation of the electrode potentials or labels; they and their distinct diffusion kernels are held fixed.

## What the impedance equality does and does not represent

For a linear, homogeneous spherical particle with uniform small boundary flux and zero initial perturbation, the regular radial Laplace solution is proportional to \(\sinh(wr/R)/r\), \(w^2=s\tau,\ \tau=R^2/D_{\rm diff}\). Direct integration over the ball gives surface/mean ratio \(w^2\sinh w/[3(w\cosh w-\sinh w)]\). Mean lithiation perturbation is \(\pm I/(sQ_i)\) by conservation and the declared discharge signs. Thus the **negative** voltage response per positive discharge current is
\[
Z_b(s,q)+g_p(q)G_p(s)+g_n(q)G_n(s),\qquad
G_i(s)=s^{-1}+\tau_i/15-\tau_i^2s/525+O(s^2).
\]
Distinct fixed \(\tau_i\) imply distinct kernels, including at sufficiently small nonzero frequencies, not useful conditioning at an arbitrary chosen frequency. Their labeling and absence of changes with bias/state are assumptions. With independent \(Z_b\) calibration and known total slope one complex frequency with nonzero \(G_p-G_n\) identifies \(g_p\); otherwise the ideal split itself is a stipulated observation. Consequently the two central whole spectra match under the calibrated closure, not for generic battery architectures. Changes in active particle counts can leave per-particle diffusion times unchanged while altering capacity; they may still alter charge transfer or electrolyte response. Those additional dependencies are not inferred away from the spectrum and can break equality outside the model.

**A separate exact counterexample when the baseline is unknown.** With the same known OCPs, take \(b=c=1\) and center pairs \((22/25,2/25)\), \((23/25,3/25)\). They give central \(u,v=(18/125,17/125)\) and its reverse, both \(K=2/5,\ X=Y=1/5\), and share the entire voltage \(23163/6250-(7/25)q+q^2/5\). Their inventories \(24/25,26/25\), capacities \(1,1\), and endpoints are strictly admissible on the same window under the same pristine normalization. Their time constants can again be fixed and distinct. Let \(H_i(s)=G_i(s)-1/s\), \(\delta=1/125\), and choose the admissible unknown \(q\)-constant backgrounds \(Z_{b,U}=1/20+\delta H_n\) and \(Z_{b,W}=1/20+\delta H_p\), respectively. Then both full spectra at **every** common \(q\) are
\[
\tfrac1{20}+\frac{7/25-(2/5)q}{s}+(18/125-q/5)(H_p+H_n).
\]
The nuisance is constant in **bias**, not in frequency, and permitted to differ between candidate worlds. It need not include another conservation pole: that common term was removed in \(H_i\). These spherical residuals are positive real: subtract the spatially constant mean mode from the Laplace concentration to obtain zero-mean \(f\). Testing the diffusion equation against \(\bar f\) makes the inward boundary flux times the conjugate surface \(f\) proportional, with positive normalization, to \(s\|f\|^2+D_{\rm diff}\|\nabla f\|^2\). For \(\Re s>0\) its real part is nonnegative, which gives positive-real surface response after dividing by squared input flux. The example also works with simpler positive-real modeled residuals, e.g. \(H_i=1/(1+s\tau_i)\). This is an admissible family of **allowed** passive nuisances, not a claim that a specific device admits it. An independently fixed baseline, a restricted known background shape, or an independent electrode/reference observation can rule it out. Differencing two biases at the same frequency removes an unknown bias-constant background and recovers \(X\) via slope change, but does **not** recover the absolute split or the inventory unless the center split is separately known.

## Extra-observation and local-order checks

A single independently calibrated absolute same-cell electrode potential at the center resolves the two lithiations via the known full voltage and the monotone known half-cell OCPs. The measured labeled slopes then give both capacities. This inversion works for any such differentiable strictly monotone known OCPs with nonzero local derivatives, not only quadratics. The exact benchmark positive-electrode gap is \(539/25000\) V (21.56 mV), with the exact worst-case one-measurement radius strictly below \(539/50000\) V. An unknown additive reference offset invalidates this one-point rule. With stable additive offset, comparing two points in that reference removes it; the quadratic gap is \(|\Delta X|q^2/2\), equal to \(11/144000\) V at the toy boundary, with half that as the hard radius.

Given the center split as in the theorem, one independent calibrated positive split at a distinct known \(q\) gives \(X=(u-g_p(q))/q\); the benchmark gap is \(11/3600\) in slope units at \(q=1/20\). Calibrated one-frequency \(Z\) at that bias has difference \(\Delta X q(G_p-G_n)\) up to branch-order sign, and thus norm \(|\Delta X q||G_p-G_n|\). For the purely mathematical RC example in the replay, the two component gaps are \(11/12000,-11/36000\) at \(s=i\). Exact deterministic hard balls in a norm must have radius **strictly** below half the prediction gap to be disjoint; equality permits the midpoint. This presumes all other hypotheses and data exact. If one estimates a difference using two noisy measurements, both errors, plus nuisance and slope uncertainties, must enter the bound. Do not compare reference-voltage and separated-slope numerical radii as the same sensor unit.

For known \(C^3\) shapes near the witness on a common neighborhood of both admissible windows, apply the inverse function theorem to \((V(0),V'(0),V''(0),g_p(0))\) at each nonsingular state; the observation map is \(C^1\) in the state variables and continuous with those shape perturbations in \(C^3\). Two nearby states exactly share the center jet/split; they need not share the **full** exact nonquadratic trace. If \(F=V_A-V_B\), the zero center jet through degree two gives \(|F|\le U_3w^3/6,\ |F'|\le U_3w^2/2\) over \(|q|\le w\), for \(U_3=\sup|F'''|\). Where both slopes have magnitude at least \(m>0\), \(dQ/dV=1/V'\) at the same \(q\) differs by at most \(U_3w^2/(2m^2)\). At a common voltage, the inverse charges differ by at most \(U_3w^3/(6m)\), so bounded curvature adds a third-order term to the incremental-capacity difference. The difference of positive split slopes in \(q\) remains nonzero near the witness; its contrast is \(\Theta(|q|)\) sufficiently near zero. The same is true of a corrected spectral channel with a fixed nonzero kernel difference, not a generic hardware channel. The cubic order is attained by adding any sufficiently small nonzero \(\lambda x^3\) to the positive known OCP, leaving the negative one unchanged: the continued branches have \(F'''=6\lambda(b_A^3-b_B^3)\ne 0\). Hence a noiseless complete nonquadratic trace in that example **does** separate despite a small cubic signature. The second harmonic formula is also correct for input \(\Re(Ie^{i\omega t})\), reporting voltage \(\Re(V_2e^{2i\omega t})\), ignoring other nonlinearities. It is not a characterization of physical nonlinear EIS.

## Primary provenance and calibrated scope

- [Lin and Khoo, 2024, §§2.1–2.5 and 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17331) already use the same charge-balance form (their Equation 4), derived losses and differential-voltage fractions, and assess window sensitivity via Fisher information. [Mohtat et al.'s earlier two-point study](https://websites.umich.edu/annastef/papers_battery/Mohtat2017ACC.pdf) and [their voltage-plus-expansion study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378775319303593) are closer instrument-design context. This is not priority for partial-window conditioning or an additional electrode-resolving observable.
- [Bizeray et al., §§ II–III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.02471) already derive spherical diffusion and linear EIS identifiability of grouped dynamics conditional on known initial electrode stoichiometries, nonzero known OCP gradients and ordered parameters. Giving exact shapes but not where on each shape the unobserved state lies is different; even the positive labeled charge-coordinate slopes then need not determine stoichiometries and capacities. [Kirk et al., §§2.3.1, 4.3, 5.2 and supplement S4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.03961) take electrode balancing as equilibrium inputs, use separate measured potentials, and analyze additional kinetic/second-harmonic physics. [Grandjean et al.](https://doi.org/10.3390/en10010090) already discuss RC-assignment ambiguity; this is not an assignment ambiguity. [Laue et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10800-021-01579-5) and [Hallemans et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10896) show calibration with three-electrode or multi-operating-point data. [Simultaneous dynamic voltage/impedance identification](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0013468626009084) is further contemporary context; accessible publisher snippets do not share the exact one-state idealized observation map.
- [Li et al., 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2590116826000512) and [their data/code inventory](https://github.com/REIL-UConn/Multi-Objective-Optimization-for-Lithium-Ion-Battery-Degradation-Diagnostics/blob/main/README.md) benchmark partly controlled true losses, not this joint calibratable-spectrum theorem. [Hofmann’s public dataset metadata](https://rdspace.uni-bayreuth.de/entities/researchdata/0bb95cc2-8ce9-4dcb-b7c0-4eda5949f9ed/full) and [dissertation Table 2 and Fig. 5](https://epub.uni-bayreuth.de/id/eprint/8980/1/Hofmann%20%282026%29%20-%20PhD%20Thesis.pdf) document raw and derived outputs separately, with some electrode EIS channels absent. No file from the large archive has been ingested here. The fitted labels are not independent chemistry ground truth; even a successfully matched raw reference electrode observable would validate only that observable, not establish the true model inventory or microscopic losses.

No primary source found in this bounded review states the exact calibrated-upper-information fold, with both strictly admissible regular branches and the cubic-versus-linear window result, but this is not a priority claim. The contribution is a model-specific identifiability counterexample and exact conditions under which one more observation separates, not a diagnosis or practical verdict on any EIS device or chemistry.
