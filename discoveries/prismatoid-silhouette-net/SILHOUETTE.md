# Exact silhouette unfolding criteria for convex polytopes and alternating prismatoids

This note gives a constructive sufficient criterion, an extension beyond Hamiltonian quasigeodesics for a family of nonnested prismatoids, and an exact ten-vertex example. It does not settle Dürer's edge-unfolding conjecture. All actual cut edges are specified without subdivision or pseudoedges.

## A. Quantitative quasigeodesic criterion

Assume the vertices of a full-dimensional convex 3-polytope have distinct orthogonal projections in strictly convex position: every projected vertex is an extreme vertex of one planar polygon. Lift the cyclic boundary of this polygon to the original polytope. Each edge of the projected polygon has a horizontal supporting normal attaining its maximum on exactly the two corresponding lifted vertices, so the lifted segment is an actual exposed edge. Their cycle is Hamiltonian and simple. A fiber over a point of the projected boundary is a singleton, since it lies in this exposed edge (or vertex). Over the interior, the upper and lower boundary surfaces are graphs over the projected polygon, meeting only along the silhouette. No facet is vertical: its planar projections would make three distinct projected vertices collinear, impossible.

For a facet F, let g_F be the gradient of its affine height over the projection. Let alpha_v in (0,pi) be the projected polygon's interior angle at vertex v. If
    alpha_v max_{F incident to v} sqrt(1+|g_F|^2) <= pi
for every v, then the silhouette is a Hamiltonian quasigeodesic. Indeed, the inverse of orthogonal projection on F is a linear lift with singular values 1 and kappa_F=sqrt(1+|g_F|^2). For an orientation-preserving 2x2 matrix L, the derivative of arg(L(cos theta,sin theta)) is det(L)/|L(cos theta,sin theta)|^2, bounded above by its singular-value ratio. Thus the angle in each facet at v is at most kappa_F times its projected angle. Each projected cap fan has total angle alpha_v, so the total at v on either side of the silhouette is at most pi. This is the required quasigeodesic condition. O'Rourke's Theorem 1 (arXiv:2206.05353v3) gives a net by cutting all but any one silhouette edge. Equivalently, each cap has no interior vertices, develops to a convex polygon by the angular condition, and the two polygons attach on opposite sides of the uncut edge. Strict inequality ensures strictly convex cap boundary angles.

In particular, every full-dimensional lift of a fixed strict planar polygon has this form of net after sufficiently small positive compression in the projection-normal direction: gradients scale linearly and projected corner angles do not change. This is an explicit small-height criterion in arbitrary numbers of vertices, not a claim that a generic polytope has any projection exposing every vertex. One possibly convenient all-rational sufficient test is: at corner v with nonparallel edge vectors e_-,e_+, write s=|g_F|² and require
    4 s² |e_-|² |e_+|² <= det(e_-,e_+)²
for each incident F. This means s≤sin(pi-alpha_v)/2. Then sqrt(1+s)≤1+s/2≤1+sin(pi-alpha_v)/4<1+(pi-alpha_v)/pi, which implies the displayed angle condition strictly.

## B. Two developed caps with a common supporting silhouette edge, beyond the angle criterion

Fix 2m points p_0,...,p_{2m-1} in strict cyclic convex position, m≥3, alternating between the two bases. Lift even vertices to height 0 and odd vertices to height h>0. All indices below are cyclic. Each base is a facet. The cap on the even-base side projects to its base polygon plus triangular ears (p_{j-1},p_j,p_{j+1}) at odd j; the other cap is analogous. To see this directly, the base chords between successive even vertices split the remainder of the projected polygon into exactly the triangular ears. The boundary graphs over each ear are the appropriate affine plane through its three vertices: across a base chord the ear fills the adjacent triangle, and these ears plus the base tile a projected disk without any choice of triangulation. Alternatively each ear plane is supporting, since its affine height increases away from the containing-base half-plane for a lower ear and decreases for an upper ear. All remaining projected vertices lie strictly on that containing side. Thus ears, bases and silhouette edges are all actual faces/edges.

Assume the perpendicular foot of each p_j on the line through p_{j-1},p_{j+1} is strictly inside the corresponding base segment. This is an explicit dot-product check. It holds, for example, when all projected points are cocircular in cyclic order with consecutive angular gaps less than pi: the ear's angles at the base endpoints are then acute, as each subtends one consecutive arc less than pi.

Unfold each of the two caps separately by keeping its base planar and putting each ear outside across its base edge. For L=p_{j-1}, R=p_{j+1}, W=p_j, e=R-L, N=(e_y,-e_x), and D=e·e, the unfolded tip is
    W' = L + [ ((W-L)·e) e + sqrt(((W-L)·N)^2 + h^2 D) N ] / D.
The component along e is the foot coordinate; the other component gives precisely the 3D altitude to the hinge, moved into the plane on the outside. Each open ear lies in the exterior normal strip over the relative interior of its own base edge. Exterior points in that strip have as unique closest point of the convex base their perpendicular foot on that edge: the squared distance to any other base point is strictly larger by the supporting half-plane inequality. Thus the strips of different edges are disjoint. In fact every ear point outside its hinge has an interior foot, so each developed cap is a simple embedded polygon, whether convex or not.

Now check whether some silhouette edge is **strictly supported in both cap developments**: in each, the line of the edge has every other developed vertex strictly on one side. If so, join those cap developments along that actual edge, placing the two polygons in opposite half-planes. They meet exactly along the hinge. Cutting precisely the other 2m−1 silhouette edges gives a spanning path of the actual polytope graph and an injective net. Strict support is sufficient, not necessary. Failure to find it says nothing about arbitrary edge cut trees.

## C. Exact control with ten projected crossings and height interval up to 30

Use these ten projected integer points, in cyclic order starting at index zero:
    (65,0), (63,16), (39,52), (16,63), (-25,60),
    (-56,33), (-65,0), (-52,-39), (-16,-63), (33,-56).
Alternating vertices are the two 5-vertex bases, with heights 0 and h. All are on the circle of radius 65. The two projected base boundaries meet in ten strict transversal crossings; thus this is not a nested prismatoid, nor does it satisfy the at-most-two-crossings condition explicitly stated in Radons' 2019 abstract. The feet are strictly inside all ten ear hinges. At least one lateral triangle is obtuse even at h=30 (its apex dot-product is the projected dot-product plus h²).

The same seam between silhouette vertices 8 and 9 is strictly supported by both developed caps for **all** 0<h≤30. The standalone companion verifier uses directed-rounded fixed-denominator BigInt intervals for all 1,920 contiguous height intervals of length 1/64. The least lower cross-product bound, in the given coordinate units, is 485572870250/10^9 > 0. This is exact everywhere on the whole interval, not sampling. Within an interval, radicands are bounded by endpoint monotonicity in h²; every square-root endpoint is outward rounded via integer square root; the signed interval products and divisions use floor/ceil. The analytic formula in §B and the interval signs prove the positive net claim, without claiming no net beyond 30.

For comparison, the full silhouette is a strict quasigeodesic at h=13, but at h=14 the cap angle on its own base at vertex 1 exceeds pi. This demonstrates why §B adds coverage even to the exact quasigeodesic condition in §A, not merely to its conservative gradient upper bound. At each vertex in this alternating family, the opposite-base side has just one ear angle (<pi); the own-base side consists of base angle alpha plus incident ear angles beta_1,h and beta_2,h, each in (0,pi/2). The two beta angles are formed by a horizontal base vector u and a sloping silhouette ray with planar component v. Their cosines are (u·v)/(|u|sqrt(|v|²+h²)); hence under the foot hypothesis both increase strictly with h (the angles increase, the positive cosines decrease). At h=0 the total is the strictly convex projected angle; at infinite h it tends to alpha+pi>pi. Thus each vertex has a unique finite threshold for convexity on its base side, and the silhouette quasigeodesic heights are exactly h≤the least threshold. The exact control compares the imaginary part of a product of three unnormalized complex angle factors: for each angle the factor is dot+i*cross-norm. The total angle is in (0,2pi), so the sign distinguishes below and above pi. For one mixed angle the cross-norm is sqrt(det(u,v)^2+h²|u|²), and the actual confirmed signs place the example's least threshold strictly between 13 and 14.

## D. Source boundaries and version corrections

- [Ghomi, Affine unfoldings of convex polyhedra](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.3231) proves unfoldability after a suitable affine transformation of every convex polyhedron, not for the original metric nor via a prescribed silhouette. [O'Rourke, Hamiltonian Quasigeodesics yield Nets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05353), latest v3 June 2026, Theorem 1, supplies the general quasigeodesic-to-net implication used in §A.
- [Radons, Edge-unfolding nested prismatoids](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comgeo.2023.102033), 2024, proves the complete nested case; [his 2019 near-flat abstract](https://dmv2019.math.kit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Radons_EdgeUnfoldingNearlyFlatPrismatoids.pdf) reports the at-most-two-crossings case and says some conditions can be relaxed under additional structural assumptions without specifying them there. A matching theorem for the above common-support test and exact beyond-quasigeodesic family was not found in the checked primary sources. No priority claim against unpublished Radons details.
- [Bian–Demaine–Madhukara, Edge-Unfolding Prismatoids: Tall or Rectangular Base](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14262) proves existence via petals for sufficiently far-separated levels, and for rectangular base with nonobtuse triangular other faces. This does not reduce to that second class; the example has pentagonal bases and persists with an obtuse lateral facet. Do not infer from this alone that no given height might also admit a petal net. [O'Rourke, Unfolding Face-Neighborhood Convex Patches](https://www.science.smith.edu/~jorourke/Papers/PrismatoidsCCCG13Full12Jun13.pdf), introduction, invokes [Val Pinciu’s 2007 volcano-unfolding theorem](https://cccg.ca/proceedings/2007/01a4.pdf), which already proves nonoverlap for a convex polytope face and its neighbors across edges. The contribution in §B concerns a common supporting actual edge of the two developed caps, not the separate petal nonoverlap. Radons also uses supporting-line gluing in the nested setting.
- [O'Rourke, Prismatoid Band-Unfolding Revisited v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09813v4) was **withdrawn on 27 October 2026** after a counterexample by Sadegh Merati to Lemma 8. The version on the author's website still advertises v2; it is not a proved benchmark. [Overlapping Unfoldings of Cones and Convex Polyhedra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09606), July 2026, constructs some unfoldings with arbitrarily thick overlap, not a convex polytope whose every unfolding overlaps.
- [ProofAtlas Dürer dossier](https://www.proofatlas.ai/collaboration/durer-edge-unfolding-conjecture/) records source-reported reciprocal coverage through three interior vertices, and an active four-interior frontier. It is distinct from the actual-edge silhouette mechanism here; neither status report nor the reciprocal preprint estimates are premises. No generic or metric counterexample claimed.

## E. No Hamiltonian quasigeodesic at height 14

The independent audit certifies more than failure of the silhouette angle condition. At height 14, the actual graph has 56 Hamiltonian cycles up to reversal, none with both directed angle sums at most π at every vertex. At height 13 only the silhouette qualifies. The seam net at height 14 still exists. See the [independent audit and replay](INDEPENDENT-DURER-AUDIT.md). This particular example lies outside the hypothesis of the known Hamiltonian-quasigeodesic theorem, not outside the full range of other possible nets.
