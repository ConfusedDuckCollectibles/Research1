# Independent audit: silhouette zipper unfolding

**Scope.** Verified a quantitative shallow-silhouette construction and a strict seam criterion for alternating two-level convex prismatoids. The exact irregular pentagon-on-pentagon fixture admits a simple edge zipper along the given silhouette with one edge spared at every height \(0<h\le 30\); at \(h=14\) its actual edge graph admits **no** Hamiltonian quasigeodesic. This is a limited sufficient construction, not an answer for every nonnested prismatoid or to Dürer's problem. The exclusion concerns closed Hamiltonian quasigeodesics using 1-skeleton edges, not arbitrary geodesics on the surface.

## Geometry

1. **Actual edges and true face disks.** Let a full-dimensional 3-polytope have distinct vertex projections, all strict vertices of the 2D projected polygon \(Q\). Every vertical supporting plane above a projected polygon edge exposes exactly the two lifted endpoints and their segment. Each boundary fibre of the projection has this unique height, and no facet is vertical: a vertical supporting plane projects to a support line, which has at most two projected vertices. Over \(\operatorname{int}Q\) there is an interval of positive length on each vertical fibre; this follows by taking a convex combination of an interior fibre of positive length and any point beyond the target from it. Upper and lower piecewise-affine surfaces are therefore face disks, each homeomorphic to \(Q\), meeting precisely at the actual Hamiltonian silhouette. This holds for general vertex lifts as well as prismatoids.

2. **Shallow angle certificate.** At a projected hull vertex let \(\theta_i\in(0,\pi)\) be its internal angle. The projected face sectors on either disk partition that angle. For a face \(z=g_F\cdot p+c\), the graph map \(L_F\) has induced metric \(I+g_F g_F^{\mathsf T}\); identifying the target face plane orthonormally and with the induced orientation gives \(\det L_F=\sqrt{1+|g_F|^2}\) and least singular value 1. The derivative of transformed argument of the planar unit direction \(u_\phi\) is \(\det L_F/|L_Fu_\phi|^2\le\sqrt{1+|g_F|^2}\). Thus either angular fan at vertex \(i\) is at most \(\sqrt{1+g_{\max}^2}\theta_i\), where \(g_{\max}=\max_F |g_F|\). If this is \(<\pi\) at all vertices (or at most \(\pi\) for the weak criterion), the silhouette is an edge Hamiltonian quasigeodesic. For two levels the side-face projected distance \(d_F\) between the parallel level lines is positive and \(g_F=h/d_F\); include caps with slope zero. A direct bound is \(h<d_{\min}\sqrt{(\pi/\max_i\theta_i)^2-1}\), and face combinatorics is unchanged under positive vertical scaling. O'Rourke's Thm 1 yields a net by cutting every actual silhouette edge except any one. No acute triangulation required.

3. **Strict alternating ears and one-edge gluing.** Every base polygon edge has one other-colour hull vertex on the short projected arc outside that edge; all remaining projected vertices lie strictly on the interior side. The affine function through the edge at the first height and the outside vertex at the other height is therefore an upper supporting face if the first base is upper, and lower if the first base is lower. This gives exactly each base plus one triangular ear per edge, since the ears and core tile \(Q\) in projection. On developing a disk the tip's projection along the edge is unchanged and its perpendicular altitude \(d\) becomes \(\sqrt{d^2+h^2}\) on the exterior side. If each tip's foot along that base edge is strictly interior, the whole ear minus its base stays in the convex core's normal strip over that edge's relative interior. The nearest core point is unique; distinct ears therefore meet, if at all, only at a common base endpoint, and neither meets the core except along its own base. In particular the patch is an embedded simple polygon, even when its silhouette is reflex. If all developed vertices other than endpoints on a proposed common true seam lie strictly in the same open half-plane of its oriented line, in each patch's drawing orientation, reflect one patch after matching the two labelled endpoints. Their only intersection is the seam. Their union is a simple polygon, with cut graph the original silhouette minus that edge, an actual spanning path. A single supporting edge on each disk suffices; convexity of both disks is not required.

* Check convex polygon supporting signs, equal radius 65, all strict interior foot inequalities, 10 transverse crossing pairs between the pentagon boundaries, and eight side triangles even at \(h=30\) with strictly obtuse apex from the integer 3D dot-products. Their \(h^2\) dependence shows they stay obtuse below that height.
* For each integer \(h=0,\ldots,30\), separately draw the two projected patches developed via the exact ear formula, with component error \(<2\cdot10^{-6}\) from integer square-root/division bounds. Every point moves at most \(h\) and as a function of \(h\) is 1-Lipschitz. Developed chord lengths are at most 190 up to \(h=30\); physical rounding error in each determinant is less than 1. The seam determinants have derivative modulus at most \(2\cdot190+2\cdot190=760\), so testing 31 integer heights suffices: the least certified-grid approximation is \(485.663051613242>381\), with half-step loss at most 380 and rounding smaller than 1. Seam [8,9] is strictly supported throughout the full interval. Height zero appears only as the limiting flat projection in this check, not as a three-dimensional example.
* Independently enumerate all supporting facets from 3D integer triples at both \(h=13,14\): exactly the two pentagons and the ten indicated ears. The graph has cyclic neighbours \(i+(-2,-1,1,2)\) at vertex \(i\), with the wrap sector the base facet. For adjacent 3D rays \(u,v\) the Gaussian-complex factor \(u\cdot v+i\|u\times v\|\) has exactly the facet angle as positive argument. Each proper fan has total in \((0,2\pi)\) by positive vertex curvature, so its product imaginary part is positive exactly if its sum is \(<\pi\). Compute square roots by downward integers at scale \(10^6\); the modulus of each factor is \(<34200\), bounding length-\(k\) product error by \(k(34200)^{k-1}10^{-6}\). Both fans of all six local unordered pairs at each vertex are resolved strictly. The exact DFS enumerates 56 actual Hamiltonian cycles modulo reversal in each graph (start at 0). At \(h=13\) with 33 accepted local pairs, precisely the silhouette passes. At \(h=14\) with 32 accepted local pairs, none passes. This strengthens the fact that the chosen silhouette itself is not quasigeodesic. All local signs, seam tests, and strict projected configuration inequalities persist in an open neighbourhood of this height and in small planar-coordinate perturbations preserving the two levels.

## Primary priority and wording

* [O'Rourke, arXiv:2206.05353v3, Theorem 1 and §3 Lemma 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05353v3) gives the same two-disk Hamiltonian-quasigeodesic argument and explicitly demonstrates its shallow congruent regular antiprism case. Our exposed-projection test generalizes this to strict irregular projected configurations, not a new quasigeodesic unfolding method. Here one irregular case is inapplicable to that sufficient theorem because no edge Hamiltonian quasigeodesic exists.
* [Pinciu, CCCG 2007, Theorem 1](https://cccg.ca/proceedings/2007/01a4.pdf) proves the volcano unfolding of a face and its edge-neighbour faces is nonoverlapping for every convex polyhedron. [O'Rourke, CCCG 2013 introduction](https://www.cccg.ca/proceedings/2013/papers/paper_22.pdf) cites exactly this result; the two alternating ear disks separately are those neighbour patches. The strict-foot proof is a direct strict embedding certificate, not priority for nonoverlap of individual petal disks. The whole step is their common true-seam gluing and an explicit configuration without a quasigeodesic to rely on.
* [Radons, Computational Geometry 116 (2024), 102033](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comgeo.2023.102033), [author arXiv v3 §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.00555v3), proves all **properly nested** two-plane prismatoids. His Observations 3.1/3.3 use supporting-line conditions for gluing differently assembled nested patches. Radons's earlier [2019 DMV conference abstract](https://dmv2019.math.kit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Radons_EdgeUnfoldingNearlyFlatPrismatoids.pdf) expressly reports a sufficiently flat result for at most two projected boundary intersection points and mentions unspecified relaxations under structural assumptions. The ten-crossing fixture is outside this stated hypothesis; no priority assertion about those unpublished relaxations. [O'Rourke arXiv:2603.09813v2, §8(3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09813v2) focused on nested classes and also listed general nonnested unfolding as open, but [the native latest v4 record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09813v4) expressly withdraws the work on 27 October 2026 after Sadegh Merati’s counterexample to Lemma 8. Use this only as historical context, not as a proved criterion.
* [Bian, Demaine, Madhukara, CCCG 2021, Theorems 1.1, 1.2](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/Prismatoids_CCCG2021/paper.pdf) cover rectangular base/nonobtuse triangles and sufficiently tall prismatoids. The fixture's base polygons are pentagons, with obtuse lateral triangles. Their numerical sufficient tall-height threshold is at least \(2d_{AB}/\pi=260/\pi>82\) here, since the projected diameter is 130; that threshold does not cover this checked interval. This is a comparison with published sufficient criteria, not a claim no other prior method can make any fold of these polyhedra. [Demaine–Demaine–Uehara, CCCG 2013](https://cccg.ca/proceedings/2013/papers/paper_10.pdf) handles Hamiltonian zipper unfoldability of nested prismoids and characterizes several related restricted classes; the alternating triangular band here is not a prismoid in that terminology.

**Assessment.** Good narrowly scoped constructive note, especially the exact h=14 case and strict open robustness. Do not claim a first general half-plane gluing principle, first nonnested prismatoid class, new isolated-petal lemma, or a global solution.
