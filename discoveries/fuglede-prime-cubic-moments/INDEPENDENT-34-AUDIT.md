# Independent audit: initial \( \mathbb F_{17}^{3} \) ledger and the 34-point pencils

**Status.** The arithmetic survivor list and both conditional 34-point caps are verified. If a 34-point spectral pair exists, each member is an affine arc with at most eight points on a plane, and each uniform-normal set meets any projective line in at most eight points. This does not exclude cardinality 34 or any other item in the survivor list.

## Scope and source inheritance

A spectral pair \((E,A)\) has a square character matrix, unitary after normalization, so either member is also a spectrum of the other. For \(B=E,A\), let \(D_B\) be its projective directions of differences and \(U_B\) its normals with constant plane occupancy. The prime cyclotomic polynomial gives \(D_E\subset U_A\), \(D_A\subset U_E\). The elementary ambient reduction and plane and line caps used here are [Ferguson–Iosevich, §§8–9](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1), whose geometric reductions credit [Fallon–Mayeli–Villano](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02936), and whose §9 studies size 22 over \(\mathbb F_{11}\), not size 34 over \(\mathbb F_{17}\).

The surviving sizes outside \(1,17,289,4913\) first have the form \(17m,\ 2\le m\le16\): a nontrivial Fourier zero makes size a multiple of 17, and a set larger than \(17^2\) determines every direction, forcing the spectrum to be the whole group. Tiles only have prime-power sizes; at those four sizes spectrality and tiling are equivalent by the same §8 reductions. [Malikiosis, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.13592) excludes the open interval \(289-16\sqrt {17}<|E|<289\), covering \(m=14,15,16\) but not \(m=13\). If \(m\) is odd and nonsquare, let \(q<17\) divide \(m\) to odd order. The determinant \(\Delta\) of the unscaled character matrix satisfies \(\Delta\overline\Delta=(17m)^{17m}\). Frobenius \(q\bmod17\) has nontrivial order in the cyclic group of order 16, so its group includes complex conjugation; every prime ideal above the unramified prime \(q\) is conjugation-fixed. Such an ideal sees even valuation on the left and odd on the right. This is the classical self-conjugacy obstruction, not a new exclusion method; see [Brock (1988)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(88)90054-4) and [Winterhof–Yayla–Ziegler (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.6548). Hence the residual multipliers are exactly \(\{2,4,6,8,9,10,12\}\) **under those reductions**; possible further results are not asserted away. Focused literature checks found no external primary paper stating the present 34-point eight-pencil gate. No priority assertion beyond that search is warranted.

## Two-layer preliminary contradiction

At \(m=2\), every line has at most two points and every plane at most \(p=17\), by the elementary pencil argument in §8.2. For clarity, a full uniform pencil about an affine line of occupancy \(a\) would imply \(2(p+1)=2p+pa\), impossible. A plane with more than \(p\) points would determine every one of its \(p+1\) tangent directions and force a full uniform pencil for the partner. For any affine line a partner difference lies in its orthogonal plane since \(2p>p\); thus some plane containing the line is uniform, and the line has at most two points.

If one member is supported in two parallel planes, each slice is a \(p\)-point planar arc. For each direction there are at most \((p-1)/2\) disjoint pairs, so each slice determines at least \(p\) directions, and their union is exactly \(p\) because a full direction line is impossible. Consequently the partner has a pencil with \(p\) uniform labels. Project along the pencil's perpendicular axis; the Radon identity below has one nonuniform profile and forces the multiplicity to depend only on that profile's coordinate. In detail, it gives \(g=p f\), hence \(f\) takes values 0 or 1 by \(g\le p\), and exactly two levels are occupied. The partner therefore also has two full layers.

For each of the \(p\) common tangent directions of the first member, each slice consists of \((p-1)/2\) disjoint pairs and one singleton. For a uniform normal orthogonal to that tangent direction, the two singletons must occupy the same 2-point level, since all pairs occupy their levels whole. Their difference is independent of the tangent direction, so the corresponding normal line contains at most one point of \(U_E\). These \(p\) lines plus one residual line form a pencil. The residual line contains at most \(p\) points of \(U_E\); thus \(|D_A|\le |U_E|\le2p\). Yet in the two-layer partner the \(p\) tangent directions and cross-layer differences are disjoint. With the layers identified as \(P,Q\subset\mathbb F_p^2\) at heights 0 and 1, cross-layer directions are indexed by \(Q-P\). If \(|Q-P|\le p\), then all \(p\)-element translates \(Q-a,\ a\in P,\) coincide, making \(Q\) invariant under a nonzero translation and hence an affine line. This violates the 2-point line cap. Therefore \(|D_A|>2p\). This part is the odd-prime specialization of Ferguson–Iosevich's Lemma 9.1, not a distinct method.

## The polynomial identity and exact finite gates

Consider one pencil of 18 projective normals on either member. Let \(K\) be the number of uniform labels, \(L=18-K\) the number of nonuniform labels. When \(K>0\), select a uniform vertical direction; the \(L\) other labels have finite slopes \(s_i\) and integer profiles \(g_i(t)\) of mass 34. Projection multiplicity \(f(x,y)\) is not presumed Boolean. Summing the occupied masses on the 18 lines through \((x,y)\) gives
\[
\sum_i g_i(y-s_i x)=2(L-1)+17 f(x,y).
\]
Modulo 17 interpolate the left profiles separately into polynomials of degree at most 16; the polynomial identity is formal since each exponent is at most 16. If \(c_{ij}\) denotes the coefficient of \(t^j\) and \(w_i=(\prod_{\ell\ne i}(s_i-s_\ell))^{-1}\), the degree \(j>0\) identities are \(\sum_i c_{ij}s_i^h=0\) for \(0\le h\le j\). For \(j\ge L-1\) the Vandermonde rank gives zero. For \(j\le L-2\) the exact solution space is \(c_{ij}=w_iR_j(s_i)\), \(\deg R_j\le L-j-2\), by Lagrange and dimension. A nonuniform profile constant modulo 17 necessarily has two 17s and all other levels zero: all counts lie in \([0,17]\), and a positive constant residue would have to be the uniform value 2. The two-layer contradiction thus excludes all constant-residue bad profiles.

For each degree normalize the coefficient immediately below the leading coefficient to zero by argument translation, and take leading coefficients up to multiplication by \(d\)-th powers by scaling the argument. The independent program enumerates every remaining nonconstant coefficient tuple exactly. It rotates the 17-value histogram to try all constant shifts, minimizing the sum of the canonical residues. This sum is bounded above by the actual nonnegative mass. The degree 1 to 7 minima are \(136,102,85,51,51,34,34\). In degrees 6 and 7 every retained catalogue member has residue mass exactly 34, so there is no hidden possibility of a lift by adding 17 to one of its entries. Centered sextics are even with exactly one for each of the 16 leading coefficients. There are five centered monic septics (five for each leading coefficient after scaling), with maxima \(4,6,6,8,10\).

* \(K\ge11\) gives \(L\le7\), hence degree at most 5, impossible for any bad profile.
* For \(K=10\), every bad profile is sextic and \(c_{i6}/w_i=\lambda\ne0\). The \(c_{i5}/w_i\) are affine in \(s_i\), so one affine translation of the quotient coordinates centers them simultaneously (changes by \(6\lambda(b-a s_i)\)). There are \(\binom{17}{8}=24310\) slope masks and 16 nonzero \(\lambda\). Exactly 1360 pass the constant condition \(\sum_i c_{i0}=14\), and none pass the degree-4 identities. This is a necessary filter, not a test of realizability of any generally surviving profile.

## Arc directions and the K9 gate

Any arc of \(n\ge3\) points in an odd-characteristic affine plane has at least \(n\) determined directions. Here is an elementary proof independent of hyperfocused-arc classifications. Suppose there are exactly \(n-1\), so each point sees every direction once. Normalize three to \(A=(0,0),B=(1,0),C=(0,1)\). For every remaining \(R=(x,y)\) the three nonzero ratios \(y/x,\ (x+y-1)/y,\ x/(x+y-1)\) multiply to 1. Expressed by the slopes of \(AR,BR,CR\), respectively, the ratios are \(s,(s+1)/s,1/(1+s)\). Multiplying over all \(R\), slopes besides \(0,-1,\infty\) cancel. The extra slope \(-1\) at \(A\), \(\infty\) at \(B\) and \(0\) at \(C\) give \(-1\cdot1\cdot1\), contradiction. Thus \(K\le9\) already implies that both members' affine-plane cap is 9, via the directions contained in the partner's uniform pencil.

Now let \(K=9\). Choose two distinct bad finite labels as 0,1 by an affine slope change preserving the vertical label. There are \(\binom{15}{7}=6435\) masks. If one profile is septic then all are, since \(c_{i7}/w_i\) is a single nonzero constant. A common dilation sets it to 1 because seventh powering permutes the nonzero scalars. A common translation kills the affine \(c_{i6}/w_i\). Of the five available centered profiles per leading coefficient, cap 9 retains four. For each mask independently test all choices of the actual first two values of \(c_{i5}/w_i\) and all 17 possible quadratic coefficients; these determine every remaining value. Retain all profile assignments in each resulting bucket, including coincident \(c_{i5}/w_i\). This gives 984555 quadratic trials, 513 allowed quadratics, 18909 profile assignments, 81 satisfying \(\sum_i c_{i0}=16\), and zero surviving the quartic identities.

The independent pure-sextic check uses a stronger gauge than the initial enumeration. The coefficient ratio \(c_{i6}/w_i\) is projectively a homogeneous linear form and has a unique projective root; that root cannot be a bad direction because profiles there would have degree at most 5. Justification of the transformation: for a representative \(v_i=(-s_i,1)\) of a bad label, write \(w_i=(\prod_{k\ne i}\det(v_k,v_i))^{-1}\). Rescaling representatives by \(v_i\mapsto a_i v_i\) changes the ratio \(c_{ij}/w_i\) by \(a_i^{L-2-j}\prod_k a_k\), since the profile's argument rescales inversely. A common invertible coordinate change multiplies all determinant factors by the same determinant. Thus the ratios evaluate a homogeneous degree \(L-2-j\) form, up to one common constant, in any projective coordinates.

Send this uniform root to the vertical label, giving \(c_{i6}/w_i=\lambda\ne0\) in the finite chart. Set \(\lambda=1\) or 3 by common dilation (sixth powers are exactly squares) and again put two bad labels at 0 and 1. At each slope the unique centered sextic is \(P_i\) with the given leading coefficient \(a_i=\lambda w_i\), translated as \(P_i(t+h_i)\). Since \(c_{i5}/w_i\) is quadratic in the slope, so is \(h_i=c_{i5}/(6a_i)\). A common translation sets \(h(0)=h(1)=0\), giving \(h_i=H s_i(s_i-1)\). Test all 17 \(H\). Use \(P_i(h_i)\) for the constant and \(c_{i4}^{\rm centered}+15a_i h_i^2\) for the quartic term; the latter formula uses the zero centered \(t^5\) term. The 218790 trials give 12538 constant matches, and none satisfy the quartic identities. The first failing quartic moments \(h=0,1,2\) account for 11694,700,144, respectively. Therefore \(K\le8\); nine points in a plane would supply at least nine directions in the partner's uniform pencil, so the affine-plane cap is 8.
