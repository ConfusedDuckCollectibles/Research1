# A two-loop test for slow slip, and its depth limit

Could deformation from slow fault motion be separated from changes in surface or subsurface pressure? In the stated flat isotropic elastic model, measuring horizontal displacement around two equal-area loops on opposite sides of the fault gives a contrast that cancels scalar-pressure deformation and additive affine frame errors. For a known slip direction and source footprint, the proof gives exact worst-case gains, including how much reverse slip the sign test can tolerate.

There is also an exact limit to what this measurement can tell us about depth. In the illustrative geometry, an alternative source 10 km shallower can match the whole transverse-displacement field only if at least **19.61% of its nonnegative slip mass** lies outside a 50 km-radius disk. That is a constraint for ideal full-field data, not a guarantee that real sensors can distinguish the two depths.
