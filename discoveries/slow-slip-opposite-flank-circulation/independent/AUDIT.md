## Scalar load null and exact depth equivalence

At a nonzero lateral Fourier vector \(k\), rotate coordinates to \(k,k^\perp\). The \(k^\perp\) component of a normal surface traction and of \(\nabla(\alpha(z)p)\) is zero. In isotropic plane layering, the antiplane elastic equation is \((\mu W')'-\mu|k|^2W=0\). The top shear traction is zero, and interface flux and displacement continuity cancel the interface terms in the integration by parts; the deep flux boundary term must vanish in the admitted solution class. Then the energy \(\int\mu(|W'|^2+|k|^2|W|^2)\) is zero. A transversely isotropic plane-layer coefficient can instead use two distinct positive shear moduli \(C_{44},C_{66}\); the printed one-\(\mu\) ODE is for locally isotropic layering. Do not rely on mere boundedness in arbitrary depth-dependent coefficients. The \(k=0\) frame translation contributes no curl.

With homogeneous moduli and a prescribed upper-minus-lower tangential displacement jump \(a\,s\), the top Neumann solution is \(A\cosh(|k|z)\), the bottom decay is \(B e^{-|k|(z-d)}\); continuity of \(\mu W'\) gives \(B=-A\sinh(|k|d)\). The jump fixes \(A=e^{-|k|d}(e_T\cdot s)\hat a\) with **no missing prefactor or reversed sign**. For \(s=e_x\) the horizontal curl is \(-\partial_y(P_d*a)\) at the surface. Above the sheet at receiver depth \(z\), its scalar smoothing kernel is the half-sum \( (P_{d-z}+P_{d+z})/2\). This also makes analyticity and the absence of new noiseless SH depth identifiability from another receiver above both sheets explicit; independent noise measurements can still help precision.

For \(d>d'>z\), cancelling the strictly positive cosh factor and the rake factor off its zero line gives \(\hat b=e^{-|k|(d-d')}\hat a\) there. A finite measure has a continuous Fourier transform, so equality extends across the missing rake-parallel line and the DC value; the masses agree. Fourier uniqueness gives \(b=P_{d-d'}*a\) and proves sufficiency. At fixed elevated receiver the exponential lateral smoothing gives a real-analytic curl field, so ideal exact equality on a nonempty open aperture propagates, not stably through a finite/noisy sampling problem. Rake is fixed and known: arbitrary vector jumps can be SH-invisible. For nonzero finite signed compact sources, exact compact matching at a different depth is also impossible by analyticity of \(P_\delta*a\), though the quantitative mass bound requires positive slip.

For \(\delta=d-d'>0\), integrating radially yields disk tail \(\delta/\sqrt{\delta^2+R^2}\). Every translate of the strictly radial-decreasing unit-mass \(P_\delta\) puts at most as much mass into a centered disk as the centered kernel, by overlapping concentric superlevel disks; integrate against the positive measure \(a\). The strict extremizer among point measures is at the disk center, approximable by smooth concentrations. The derivative norm for truncating the equivalent shallow source is indeed \(3(4/5)^{5/2}/(4\pi d'^3)\), since \(|\partial_yP_{d'}|\) peaks at \(x=0,|y|=d'/2\); restoring removed mass costs at most a second such term by the triangle inequality. This truncation is an error **upper** bound for one proposed mimic, not a stable lower detectability/certificate.

## Exact loop sign, sharp extrema and rake qualifications

Take both rectangles CCW with the same area. For well-defined line traces/Stokes, the scalar load kernel gives zero circulation separately, and a fixed additive affine nuisance gives constant curl \(A_{21}-A_{12}\), which cancels in the difference. This is not coordinate invariance under arbitrary rotations/gains or a statement about a gradient in the reference-frame yaw itself. Integrating \(-\partial_yP_d(x-s_x,y-s_y)\) over the positive-\(y\) rectangle and subtracting the negative one gives the sum of its two reflected positive endpoint differences.

For any \(t,L>0\), the even function \(u\mapsto p(t)-p(t+L)\) is strictly decreasing in \(|u|\). Its shifted integral over \([-X,X]\) decreases for \(s_x>0\) by comparing its two endpoint values at \(|X+s_x|>|X-s_x|\). The exact third derivative of \(C(D+t^2)^{-3/2}\) is \(15Ct(3D-4t^2)/(D+t^2)^{9/2}\), with \(C=d/(2\pi)\) and \(D=d^2+u^2\). The stated far-flank condition makes it nonpositive at all arguments and thus makes \(G(t)\) convex. The even sum of the two reflected arguments then minimizes at \(s_y=0\), maximizes at \(|s_y|=S_y\); combining gives precisely the proposed \(K_{\min},K_{\max}\). Strict positivity is immediate from the Poisson kernel falling with transverse distance. Sharpness uses masses at the extrema or smooth arbitrarily concentrated approximations; it assumes no pointwise-slip cap. Reverse signed masses give exact worst case \(K_{\min}M_+-K_{\max}M_-\) when the distinct extrema are available in nondegenerate support; the sign threshold is strict, and equality gives no uniform positive lower margin.

The y-rake contribution has absolute value at most \(r_w K_x\), \(r_w=(X+S_x)/(Y-S_y)\), by bounding the ratio of the two derivatives pointwise on both flanks, where \(K_x\) is a sum of absolute contributions of its derivative. Thus globally
\[
V\ge(1-mr_w)\int K_x\,da_x .
\]
For \(mr_w\le1\), this implies the printed \((1-mr_w)K_{\min}M_x\); for a negative bracket one may instead use \(K_{\max}\), not substitute \(K_{\min}\). The quoted positive threshold \(m<8/7\) for the benchmark is unaffected and is sufficient only. As a check, there is a more specific exact transverse kernel \(K_y=B(s_x,Y-s_y)-B(s_x,Y+s_y)\), where \(B(c,t)=\int_{t}^{t+L}(P_d(X-c,v)-P_d(X+c,v))\,dv\). For \(c>0\) its integrand is positive and strictly decreases in \(v>0\), so \(K_y\) has the sign of \(s_xs_y\) and vanishes if either coordinate is zero. At the benchmark corner \(K_y/K_x\approx0.16517\), below the safe gross bound \(7/8\), not a claim of an optimal rake ratio.
