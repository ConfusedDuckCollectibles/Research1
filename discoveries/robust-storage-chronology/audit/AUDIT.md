# Independent audit: robust scalar storage from coarse chronological totals

**Conclusion.** The mathematical result passes. It gives sharp robust feasibility and capacity for the specified one-store periodic model, together with finitely many envelope-only candidate cuts. This is a narrow deterministic uncertainty theorem, not a finding on real reliability, not an estimation of a site's needed reserve, and not a new claim to the known-trace sequent-peak method. I read the author's PROOF.md independently of writing check.py. The manuscript's initial ancillary Singh citation needed correction (below).

## Exact conventions and fixed-trace test

Let one measurable bounded real input \(p\) repeat with period \(T\). Each chronological bin independently fixes a raw integral and the raw range is \([-P_-,P_+]\). The nonnegative store has \(X'=g-\rho X-z\), \(g=f(p)\), \(0\leq z\leq g_+\). For converter losses use \(f(p)=\eta_c\min(p_+,P_c)-p_-/\eta_d\), requiring enough discharge power to serve \(P_-\) (ratings stated on the appropriate external side); \(\eta_c\eta_d\leq1\) suffices for the concavity, satisfied by physical efficiencies in \((0,1]\). There is no import, shedding, ramp, additional parasitic load or state-dependent efficiency.

For \(a\le b\), write \(I(a,b)=\int_a^b e^{\rho(t-a)}g(t)\,dt\). Variation of constants gives \(X(a)\ge -I(a,b)\) whenever \(X(b)\ge0\). Periodicity gives \(I(a,a+T)\ge0\) at every phase \(a\). Even from a nonperiodic finite initial state, a negative weighted cycle makes any all-future nonnegative trajectory impossible: at the same phase after \(n\) cycles iteration bounds the state above by \(e^{-n\rho T}X(a)+I(a,a+T)(1-e^{-n\rho T})/(e^{\rho T}-1)\) for \(\rho>0\), and by \(X(a)+nI(a,a+T)\) for \(\rho=0\). In this fixed-trace model the condition for nonnegative unbounded periodic state is exactly all-start \(I(a,a+T)\ge0\), not just the (unweighted or weighted) total at the calendar anchor.

When all cycles pass, remove whole cycles at the **end**, not at the start. If \(b-a=kT+r\), integrate the first \(r\) then the \(k\) nonnegative full-cycle integrals, with their own positive factors. Consequently all-time maximal deficit from each start is already attained by \(b-a\le T\).

Here is an independent sufficiency and periodic-state proof. Let \(H(t)=\int_0^t e^{\rho v}g(v)\,dv\), \(W(t)=\inf_{s\ge t}H(s)\). The infimum equals \(\min_{s\in[t,t+T]}H(s)\) by the preceding reduction. Thus \(W\) is locally Lipschitz (shift the compact interval and use the local Lipschitz constant of \(H\)), nondecreasing (use its future-infimum definition), and locally constant off the contact set \(W=H\). On that level set \(H'=W'\) a.e. Therefore
\[
 X_{\min}(t)=e^{-\rho t}(H(t)-W(t))=\max_{0\le r\le T}[-I(t,t+r)]
\]
is nonnegative, periodic, and solves the state equation with admissible rejected charging \(z=e^{-\rho t}W'\le g_+\); in particular the scalar deterministic minimum capacity really is \(\max_t X_{\min}(t)\). This construction is anticipatory and demonstrates pointwise minimality; no clairvoyance is needed for the actual feasible cap policy.

For a causal implementation starting at \(X(a_0)=x_0\), set \(U(t)=x_0+\int_{a_0}^{t}e^{\rho(v-a_0)}g(v)\,dv\), \(C_t=E e^{\rho(t-a_0)}\), and subtract the running overflow \(R(t)=\sup_{a_0\le s\le t}(U(s)-C_s)_+\); then \(X(t)=e^{-\rho(t-a_0)}[U(t)-R(t)]\). This is the upper reflection: where the regulator increases, \(X=E\) and \(z=g-\rho E\in[0,g_+]\) a.e. With \(x_0=E\), the formula is the infimum, over \(a_0\le s\le t\), of \(e^{-\rho(t-s)}[E+I(s,t)]\), each nonnegative. This proves one trace-independent causal rule and one common full initial state suffice for indefinite service; fullness at arbitrary initial phases is **not** an assertion of an immediately periodic trajectory. The author's periodic upper-regulator proof also checks. An arbitrary imposed low initial state is a different problem.

## Worst chronology, exact endpoints and threshold

Write \(c=f(P_+)\), \(d=-f(-P_-)\); from raw integral \(A_j\), the maximum-surplus duration is \(h_j=(A_j+P_-\Delta_j)/(P_-+P_+)\); deficit duration \(\delta_j=\Delta_j-h_j\). The chord under \(f\) turns each weighted integral into an affine functional in normalized deficit mass, with equality at both raw endpoints. Under \(\int_{j}\theta=\delta_j\), the exact worst weighted integral of that chord is obtained by filling largest weights in this bin (choosing the length-matched level set; choose arbitrary points on a tie). On an unwrapped arc of at most one period the weights are zero off its physical projection and increasing in its unwrapped order inside. A bin split around the period cut has one shared deficit budget; fill its later piece first. A raw-endpoint trace achieving the worst value remains admissible and repeats. Maximizing over arcs and over profiles commutes; there is no claim one witness realizes every arc optimum.

The finite cut claim checks. For endpoint bins distinct, fill the latest portion of a suffix ending at a fixed boundary or of a prefix ending at \(b\); their threshold lines are \(a=R-\delta\) and \(b=L+\delta\). If the same bin is entered and exited without wrap, the shift in regime is the line \(b-a=\delta\). If it is split, the two shifts are \(b=L+T+\delta\) (the later piece reaches the deficit budget) and \(b-a=T-\Delta+\delta\) (total entered length reaches it). For partially filling the earlier piece after the later one is full, its lower limit is \(b+\Delta-\delta-T\). All integration endpoints are consequently constants or fixed translates of one endpoint; after multiplying by \(\rho\), weighted deficit on each cell is \(A+B e^{-\rho a}+C e^{\rho(b-a)}\). In transformed variables \((U,V)=(e^{-\rho a},e^{\rho(b-a)})\), cell boundaries \(a,b,b-a=\mathrm{const}\) become straight lines \(U=\mathrm{const}, V=e^{\rho b}U,V=\mathrm{const}\). Images are full compact convex coordinate polygons (the inverse map recovers the corresponding half-plane inequalities), including possible line segments. Vertex tests suffice; at zero leakage all cell pieces are affine already in \((a,b)\). A quadratic number of cuts is indeed sufficient; a straightforward implementation may do \(O(J)\) arithmetic per cut unless using cumulative precomputed full-bin contributions.

With one bin let \(h=h_1\), \(\delta=T-h\). The exact minimum weighted cycle is \(((c+d)e^{\rho h}-c-d e^{\rho T})/\rho\), giving the claimed threshold. For arc length \(q\le\delta\), deficit is \(d(e^{\rho q}-1)/\rho\). Above \(\delta\) it is \([c+e^{\rho(q-\delta)}(d(e^{\rho\delta}-1)-c)]/\rho\). The bracket coefficient is negative whenever the cycle passes and \(c>0\); \(q=T\) would otherwise give positive deficit. Thus the maximum really is \(d(e^{\rho(T-h)}-1)/\rho\), including equality at threshold and limit \(d(T-h)\) for \(\rho=0\). The arguments also cover all-positive/all-negative budget endpoints (empty receiving or giving portions). Formal cases \(c=0<d\) and \(d=0<c\), if permitted, reduce to respectively no actual deficit or no capacity needed. If both endpoints vanish the internal drive is zero in this physical model.

## Independent replay and a falsification control

Run python3 check.py. Exact fractions and no imports from the author. The program enumerates every compatible unit-slot trace over seven raw levels, including intermediate converter regimes and an active upper charging clamp, compares every weighted arc with independent largest-weight allocation, independently enumerates cut vertices, and verifies exact fixed points of the capped period map for the adverse discrete witnesses whenever feasible. The prose proof, not slot sampling, covers all measurable completions.

For \(\rho=\log2\), raw bounds \([-1,8]\), \(\eta_c=3/4,\eta_d=1/2,P_c=6\), take two bins of lengths \(2,3\) and endpoint surplus-duration budgets \(1,2\). The scaled worst full-cycle deficits at observed bin boundaries are \(-45/2,-19/2\) (both pass), but the interior start \(t=1\) gives \(+33/2\), attained by the repeated raw trace \((-1,8\,|\,8,8,-1)\) on unit slots. Checking only the metering boundaries would misclassify infinite robust capacity as finite. Separately the author's two symmetric-rate controls both check exactly. The same-repeated-chronology assumption is substantive: for one bin of length four with \(h=3\) under the clipped rates above, repeating the same trace needs \(2/\rho\); allowing different chronologies each cycle lets the last-slot deficit touch the following first-slot deficit and requires at least \(6/\rho\).

## Source comparison and recommendation

[Lele 1987](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR023i010p01819), also [author-institution abstract](https://archives.atree.org/id/eprint/866/), explicitly gives exact sequent-peak-style capacity calculation with storage-dependent losses and known period inflows, going beyond the lossless [Gidley equivalence](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1694(86)90146-0); [Klemeš 1979](https://watermanagement.ucdavis.edu/download_file/view_inline/733) highlights much earlier exact mass-curve methods with convex losses. Cite Lele, not 10.1080/02626668709491180 (a Singh flood-forecasting article). [Fekete et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fenvs.2023.1076830) use known traces and iterative loss-adjusted cumulative deficits. [Günther–Bensmann–Hanke-Rauschenbach 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42162-026-00676-z) use essentially the same proportional differential storage-loss model; their Appendix A explicitly omits cyclicity for the known event profiles. [Bahl et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compchemeng.2018.01.023) derive design objective bounds with the underlying full series; [Castro Gómez–Werner–Wogrin 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06083v1) derive exact aggregation errors using active sets but §II.A footnote 3 omits storage coupling in that paper. [Lechl et al.](https://d-nb.info/1380991250/34) frame cumulative storage/energy uncertainty as stochastic network-calculus envelopes and have power limits and charge/discharge losses, but not this exact repeated coarse-total deterministic uncertainty set. [Le Boudec–Tomozei 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-28540-0_23) already provide exact perfect-battery deterministic feasibility and a causal schedule, plus sufficient sizing under arrival and service curves. [Ghiassi-Farrokhfal–Keshav–Rosenberg 2015](https://www.ece.uwaterloo.ca/~cath/tsgy14.pdf) treat nonideal scalar storage with clipping and efficiency in stochastic network calculus, but §III specifies a discrete-time charge-independent subtraction, not proportional leakage. [Zou–Lin–Aliprantis–Chen](https://staff.ie.cuhk.edu.hk/~xjlin/paper/infocom18-robust-storage-tech.pdf) address causal robustness with network and ramps, not these exact scalar leaky periodic cuts. None of these verified sources was found to state the specific coarse-bin robust certificate; this is a bounded literature comparison, not an absolute priority determination.

Recommendation: suitable for a modest mathematical methods note once the incorrect citation is replaced. Lead with the exact coarse-data impossibility test and certifying critical cuts, give a positive-mean counterexample to data averaging, and cite known-trace mass curves, loss-aware storage and robust control explicitly. No application to actual grids follows without their additional constraints and data assumptions.
