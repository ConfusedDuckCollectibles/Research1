# Which sizes are still open on the 13³ grid?

The standard reductions for `F₁₃³` already handle the tiling sizes 1, 13, 169 and 2197. All other possible spectral cardinalities are multiples of 13 from 26 to 156. Here is the current exclusion ledger:

- **26, 39, 52, 104, 117:** the exact geometry and computation in the linked Research1 notes: [26](../fuglede-f13-26/), [39](proof.md), [52](../fuglede-f13-52/), [104 and 117](../fuglede-f13-104-117/).
- **65, 91:** applications of the existing Butson determinant obstruction, not new discoveries.
- **130, 143, 156:** [Malikiosis, Theorem 1.4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43670-023-00078-2), since each exceeds 169−12√13.
- **78:** the only cardinality not excluded by these arguments. Finite Fuglede on this grid remains open.

## The existing obstruction at 65 and 91

Let a putative spectral pair of size N have character matrix H over the 13th roots of unity, with determinant Δ in the integer ring of Q(ζ₁₃). Orthogonality gives Δ times its conjugate equal to Nᴺ. For a prime q≠13, the field is unramified at q and the decomposition group at every prime above q is generated by the Frobenius ζ₁₃ ↦ ζ₁₃^q. If a power of q is −1 modulo 13, complex conjugation fixes each of these prime ideals. Their valuations of Δ and its conjugate are equal, so the valuation of their product is even.

At N=65 take q=5, since 5² is −1 modulo 13. At N=91 take q=7, since 7³ is 5 and 7⁶ is −1 modulo 13. In each case q occurs to the first power in N and N is odd, so the valuation of Nᴺ at a prime above q is odd. This contradicts orthogonality. The self-conjugacy obstruction goes back to [Brock, Theorem 4.2(i)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(88)90054-4) and related work of de Launey. Its presentation, including the example q=5 in this exact cyclotomic field, can be found in [Armario, Egan, Kharaghani and Ó Catháin, Section 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16579#S2.SS3). The finite-field cardinality reductions can be found in [Ferguson–Iosevich, Section 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1). No priority is claimed for the two determinant exclusions.
