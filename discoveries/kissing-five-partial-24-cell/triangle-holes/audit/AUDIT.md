# Independent audit: three triangular holes in a fixed 24-cell

**Verified.** With squared norm 2 and mutual dot at most 1, suppose a code has exactly 21 named points of one equatorial 24-cell and the three absent roots have mutual dot 1. The size is at most **39**; **38** is attained. When both hemispheres have eight high points the size is at most **38**. The same upper bound of 38 holds if one side has eight and the other six high points. We do not close the 38/39 gap for all triangular triples. Combined with the existing exact nontriangle/pair exclusions and 23-point equality case, every 40-point code containing at least 21 vertices of one named 24-cell contains the whole cell and is one of the aligned $D_5,L_5$ constructions. This does not bound configurations without the fixed partial template.

## Coordinates and two types of low point

Let $D=\{\pm e_i\pm e_j:i<j\}\subset\mathbb R^4$ and write points as $(u,t)$; on either side use reflection to make $t\ge0$, counting $t=0$ on only one side. A point outside the equatorial root core is high for $t\ge1$ and low otherwise. Every low violates at least one hole inequality, since the full root polar has norm at most 1. Its unit shell consists of the signed-axis frame and the two parity half-cube frames. For example, with maximum coordinate magnitude $a$ the squared norm in the polar is bounded by $4a^2\le1$ when $a\le1/2$, and by $a^2+3(1-a)^2\le1$ when $a\in[1/2,1]$; equality gives the stated frames. Dot products across different frames are $\pm1/2$.

Three roots of mutual dot 1 have distinct two-element supports; each support meets each other in one coordinate with the same sign. Signed permutations reduce them to a positive coordinate triangle (face) or to a positive three-edge star. The orthogonal Hadamard matrix with rows $(++++),(++--),(+-+-),(+--+)$, divided by 2, preserves $D$ and turns the face $r_{12},r_{13},r_{23}$ into a star, where $r_{ij}=e_i+e_j$. Thus normalize to this face. Furthermore there is exactly **one** face frame for this triple: the displayed transform changes it to a star in one half-cube frame, and changing the sign of unused $e_4$ fixes the three roots and exchanges the two half-cube frames, so the other is also a star.

Call a low point ordinary if it violates only one or two holes, and assign it to a root it violates maximally, breaking ties consistently. The [existing cone construction](https://github.com/ConfusedDuckCollectibles/Research1/blob/1d79b0776f70551f36164708dde80d50c9c86ffe/discoveries/kissing-five-partial-24-cell/exact-holes/COMPANION.md) applies: for one violation all constraints besides that root hold, for two violations the mixed interpolation needs only the constraints besides their pair, and an absent but obeyed third constraint is equally available. For $r=r_{ij}$ use the basic rays $(r,0)$, $(e_i,1),(e_j,1)$ and the four signed half-cube lifts with positive coordinates $i,j$, and add $W_{r,s}=((r+s)/2,1/\sqrt2)$ for both other holes. Each generator has norm $\sqrt2$, all pairwise dots are at least 1, and the additional $W$–$W$ inner product is $7/4$. Any norm-$\sqrt2$ cone point has coefficient sum at least 1, strictly greater if at least two nonparallel generators are given positive weight. Among low single rays the apex is absent, and the distinct $W$ rays conflict. Consequently at most one ordinary low of each assigned type can occur on each side. The same existing ray tests show a matching signed axial high bans that ordinary type, and a height-one nonpolar high with one or two violations bans all ordinary lows assigned to its own maximal type.

A triple-violating high at height 1 also bans **all** ordinary types. In face coordinates write its horizontal part $(a,b,c,d)$. The first three coordinates are positive and every pair sum exceeds 1; the retained $e_i\pm e_4$ give $\max(a,b,c)+|d|\le1$. Its dot with each apex exceeds 1, and with any matching axial lift it is $1+a_i>1$. Its horizontal product with each matching half-cube lift is at least $(a_i+a_j-a_k-|d|)/2>0$. The horizontal product against each $W_{r,s}$ is $>1$ already. These strict ray inequalities also hold for any norm-$\sqrt2$ ordinary point in the cone.

## Same-side triple lows

For a triple-violating low $X=(a,b,c,d,t)$ in face coordinates, $a,b,c>0$, all pair sums exceed 1, $\max(a,b,c)+|d|\le1$, and $|d|<1/2$. The last follows from a pair sum $>1$. The vector $(a,b,c)$ is a positive combination of $r_{12},r_{13},r_{23}$ with coefficient sum $(a+b+c)/2$: every pair sum is $>1$ while the remaining coordinate is $\le1$. Thus against any other triple low $Y$ the first-three-coordinate dot is strictly greater than $(a+b+c)/2$. The norm constraint gives
\[
 a+b+c\ \ge\frac{2-t^2-d^2}{1-|d|}\ \ge 2-t^2+|d|.
\]
After multiplication the second inequality is $|d|(1-t^2)\ge0$. If $X$ has the smaller same-side height, the fourth-coordinate loss is at most $|d|/2$ while the vertical gain is at least $t^2$. Therefore $X\cdot Y>1+t^2/2$, including strictly above 1 at $t=0$. There is at most one triple low on each side. This fails for opposite heights, as the exact $(3/5,3/5,3/5,0,\pm\sqrt{23}/5)$ pair with dot $4/25$ checks.

## High configurations and counting

If there are at least two high points, no pole can occur. The normalized horizontal Gram matrix $G$ of a high layer of size $k$ has rank at most four and off-diagonal entries at most zero. Components of the graph of strict negative entries are mutually orthogonal. On each connected component the top eigenvalue of $I-G$ is simple, so each singular block has nullity one. There are at least $k-4$ singular components, each of size at least two. This gives $k\le8$, and for $k\ge6$ all heights equal 1, since every point has an orthogonal horizontal partner and their heights have product at most 1.

* $k\le5$: at most $k+3+1\le9$ extras.
* $k=6$, all polar: if no antipodal pair exists, the components must be two singular triples. In a polar singular triple the only possible off-diagonal entries are $0,-1/2$, and a determinant vanishes only when all are $-1/2$. It then uses all three frames, and no polar vector is orthogonal to it, contradicting the second component. An antipodal pair instead puts all six in one frame. With only two absent signed axes, at most one ordinary type is unblocked, giving at most eight extras. If any high is nonpolar, at least one ordinary type is blocked, so at most nine extras.
* $k=7$: at least three singular components, with at least two of size two, yield two antipodal horizontal pairs. They are polar because the hole triangle cannot contain an opposite pair. Their directions belong to one frame by cross-frame nonorthogonality. In that frame rotate the two pairs to coordinate axes 1 and 2. The other three high directions lie on the unit circle of coordinates 3 and 4. If all are axial, three of the four signed points match every sign quadrant. Otherwise a nonaxis direction violates the root of its quadrant (its only violated root). There is at most one hole supported on $\{3,4\}$, and two high directions cannot share an open quadrant. The nonaxis high therefore blocks the sole type that could escape the two antipodal axial pairs. Thus there are no ordinary lows, and at most eight extras. All arguments use the rotated roots, not unchanged coordinate labels.
* $k=8$: exactly four orthogonal antipodal pairs. For each equatorial root both signed horizontal constraints follow from either retained sign and high antipodality. Since no opposite root pair is deleted, all eight directions are polar and belong to the same frame. All ordinary types are blocked. At most nine extras.

The total is at most $21+9+9=39$. Reaching 39 requires on each side one triple low and (high, ordinary) counts in $\{(5,3),(6,2),(8,0)\}$. No point has been assumed to lie on a grid.

## Excluding two full caps and also a full cap opposite a six-high side at 39

A triple low beside a full frame cannot see the star orientation of the holes. In star coordinates its shared positive coordinate $a\le1-t$ from the cap. The other three coordinates are strictly positive, their pair sums are at most 1 by retained roots, so their squared norm is strictly less than 1. To check the last assertion: if their largest $b\le1/2$, the sum of squares is at most $3/4$; if $1/2<b<1$, it is at most $b^2+2(1-b)^2<1$; positivity of all three forbids $b=1$. The norm would give $2-t^2<1+(1-t)^2$, impossible for $0\le t<1$. Hence any full frame on a side with a triple low is the unique face frame.

Take a triple low on the full-cap side, in face coordinates, and put $m=\max(t,|d|)<1$. The cap and retained roots give $a,b,c\le1-m$, hence
\[
 a+b+c\ge(2-t^2-d^2)/(1-m)\ge2+2m.
\]
For an opposite-side triple low $Y$ with absolute height $t'$ and fourth coordinate $d'$, the same positive combination gives
\[
 X\cdot Y>1+m(1-t'-|d'|).
\]
Consequently such a $Y$ needs $t'+|d'|>1$. A full cap on that side forces $t'<1/2$ since $a',b',c'\le1-t'$ and the pair sums exceed 1. Also $|d'|<1/2$. This is impossible, and proves the conditional upper bound 38 for two full caps.

For the extra $(8,0)$ vs $(6,2)$ exclusion, suppose the six-high side also has two ordinary types. It cannot be all polar, nor contain a triple-violating high. All its nonpolar highs must be assigned to the one remaining hole $r$, as each bans its own ordinary type. There is at most one: unit horizontals $w,w'$ satisfying $w\cdot r,w'\cdot r>1$ lie in the same open $45^\circ$ cap, hence have $w\cdot w'>0$. Thus there are five polar highs and one nonpolar high. If the six highs had no antipodal pair, they must again be in two singular triples, of which one is entirely polar. That triple is in three different frames and precludes any polar direction orthogonal to it, contradicting the other triple. Every antipodal pair is polar from the absent-root conditions; its presence implies all five polar highs lie in one frame.

In this frame the two ordinary types require their corresponding matching signed axes (two per hole) all absent. Their union is three of the signed axes, exactly the three absent. The third hole is violated by the nonpolar high, so both of *its* matching signed axes must also be absent: otherwise the horizontal direction has strictly positive product with a matching signed axis, which is impossible between two height-one highs. Thus all three holes use only those three signed axes and form a face in this frame. In particular the signed pair $\pm e_4$ of the fourth, unused coordinate is present in the six-high side. Its triple low must obey $t'+|d'|\le1$, contradicting compatibility with the full-cap-side triple low. This adds a cross-pattern exclusion. Residual high-count pairs for a putative 39 are $(5,5),(5,6),(5,8),(6,6)$, up to reflection. No exclusion of any of those four patterns is claimed here.

## Exact controls and source scope

The 38-point construction uses the 21 retained face roots, both full axial caps, and $(1,3/5,4/5,0,0)$. Every triangle is congruent to this one under the above signed permutations and triality. The companion exact replay enumerates all 96 deletion triangles, checks their unique face frame and explicitly verifies the 38-point construction, the opposite-side example, and equality-sensitive seven-high controls. It is not an exhaustive test over additional points.

[Rankin](https://doi.org/10.1017/S2040618500033219) and [King–Mixon–Parshall–Wells, Theorem 2 and the $2d-1$ discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.12871v3), citing Kuperberg (2007), already give the orthoplex/nonpositive-code structure. [Cohn–Rajagopal, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00937v3#S2) give the three triality frames and the two isometry classes when the full equator is present. [wustep, Aug. 17 full-equator section](https://github.com/wustep/maths/blob/main/problems/kissing-5d/ATTACK.md#2026-08-17--the-d4-containing-bound) already proves the 40 bound with the full $D_4$ equator; the same author's later exclusions for large intersections with $D_5$ are restricted to a quarter-grid and count all $D_5$ coordinate roots, not the named 24-cell subset. [Tomar's near-$D_4$ theorem](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23602662) concerns Voronoi **volume in dimension four**, not five-dimensional code extension; [Tomar's recent global bound](https://www.mefyi.com/papers/kissing-numbers-five-seven/kissing-numbers-five-seven.pdf) is $\tau_5\le43$, and does not imply this conditional result. The targeted search found no matching continuum triangle-deletion result. This is not an exhaustive priority claim.
