# Triangle holes: 21 named 24-cell vertices force completion at 40

Private verification note, 25 November 2026.

**Claim.** A five-dimensional kissing configuration containing at least 21 vertices of one named equatorial 24-cell has at most 40 points. At 40 it contains the full named cell and is isometric to D5 or Leech's L5. For exactly 21 named vertices with triangular holes, the maximum is either 38 or 39. If its two high layers both have the maximum size eight, the sharp bound in the triangular case is 38.

The new case is exactly 21 named vertices whose three missing roots are pairwise adjacent. The existing [exact-hole companion to Research1 #24](https://github.com/ConfusedDuckCollectibles/Research1/blob/1d79b0776f70551f36164708dde80d50c9c86ffe/discoveries/kissing-five-partial-24-cell/exact-holes/COMPANION.md) gives bounds 38 for exactly 22 and 37 for exactly 21 nontriangular holes. Its base result gives the 40-point classification with at least 23 named vertices. It already constructs 38 points for each triangular mask. These are fixed-template results, not a global improvement of the five-dimensional kissing number.

## Normalization and the prior cone lemmas used here

Rescale to norm squared 2 and pairwise dot at most 1. Write an extra point as (u,t), with u in the equatorial R^4 and t the signed height. It is **high** if |t|≥1 and **low** otherwise. Allocate height zero to the upper side only; analyze a single side with nonnegative height τ=|t| by reflection. The named cell has roots D={±e_i±e_j:i<j≤4}. Any low has ||u||>1, so it violates r·u≤1 for at least one missing root r: the polar inequalities of the full D force ||u||≤1. The polar unit directions are precisely the 24 points F consisting of the eight signed axes and sixteen half-cube directions, split into coordinate, even, and odd orthoplex frames. Across different frames all absolute inner products are 1/2. The axes, half-cubes and triality actions are as in the companion and [Cohn–Rajagopal §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00937v3).

If the three holes form a triangle, normalize them to the face {e1+e2,e1+e3,e2+e3}. Indeed their signed supports are either a triangle or a three-edge star, and a triality isometry exchanges those forms. An **ordinary low** violates only one or two holes; assign it to a hole at which its horizontal product is maximal, choosing only one in a tie. A **triple low** strictly violates all three.

The companion's seven-ray cone for a single violation of r consists of (r,0) and the six lifts (v,1) with v∈F and v·r=1. The argument remains valid here because in a single-violation chamber every other missing inequality is obeyed. For two violations r,s with maximal one r, the companion's interpolation uses the same cone augmented by W_{r,s}=((r+s)/2,1/√2). Every inequality other than r,s is obeyed, including the third missing inequality. In particular in r=e1+e2, s=e1+e3 coordinates it uses a≥b≥c>1−a≥0, b+c≤1, and |d|≤1−a for u=(a,b,c,d); b+c≤1 now holds because the third hole is not violated. Both membership proofs also apply to height-one points, because the required ray coefficient (r·u−τ)/2 remains strictly positive when τ=1 and r·u>1.

Augment each r cone by both adjacent W rays. They have mutual dot 3/2+(s·s')/4=7/4, and each has dot >1 with the seven old rays. The remaining old ray pairs have dot ≥1. A sphere point represented by multiple distinct rays has coefficient sum >1 by strictness of the triangle inequality; the coefficient sum is always ≥1. The only single rays below height 1 are the apex and the W rays, whose mutual products are strictly >1. Thus each side has at most one ordinary low of each of the three assigned types, and no two height-one nonpolar highs with the same ordinary assignment can coexist.

## One triple low per side

For a triple violator x=(a,b,c,d,τ) in face coordinates, its first three coordinates are positive, their pair sums exceed 1, and max(a,b,c)+|d|≤1 from retained e_i±e4. Thus each first coordinate is at most 1−|d|≤1, and |d|<1/2. Since each pair sum exceeds the remaining coordinate, (a,b,c) is a nonnegative combination of the three horizontal face roots, with weight sum S/2=(a+b+c)/2. For another triple violator y, its first-three-coordinate dot with x is strictly above S/2. Also
    S ≥ (2−τ²−d²)/(1−|d|) ≥ 2−τ²+|d|.
The first inequality is a²+b²+c²≤(1−|d|)S. For the second, cross multiplication leaves (1−τ²)|d|≥0.

If two triple lows are on the same side take x to have the smaller height τ. The fourth-coordinate product loses at most |d|/2, and the signed height product adds at least τ². Thus the full dot is strictly above 1+τ²/2≥1, a contradiction. In particular the argument also covers height zero.

## High-layer bookkeeping

A high layer contains at most eight points, by nonpositivity of its normalized horizontal directions and the rank-four orthoplex bound; a pole excludes all other highs on that side. For use below, split the horizontal Gram matrix into components by its negative entries. They are mutually orthogonal; each component has nullity at most one by Perron–Frobenius on its irreducible nonnegative I−G. A singular component has size at least two, and one of size two is an antipodal pair. This is the standard decomposition, see [King–Mixon–Parshall–Wells, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.12871v3). With m≥6 highs, at least m−4 components are singular, so there is an orthogonal neighbor for every point. Their heights are at least one and have product at most one, giving height 1 for all of them.

We record the blocking checks, to distinguish a nonpolar high from an ordinary or triple low. A height-one polar high (w,1) with w·r=1 sends w to a matching axis in triality coordinates and has dot ≥1 with every old r ray, and strictly >1 with either W since w·s∈{0,1} for an adjacent s. Hence it blocks every r-assigned ordinary low (the exact-hole assumption removes the apex). A nonpolar high with one or two violations belongs to its assigned ordinary cone as above; more precisely it dots strictly above 1 with every one of its assigned rays even if the generator W corresponds to a deleted neighbor that the high does not violate. The old ray checks follow from a,b>0 and a+b>|c|+|d| in r-normal coordinates. Any r-adjacent hole has form e_i±e_j, i∈{1,2}, j∈{3,4}. With a sole violation, its dot with the high is at least a+b−1>0 by the cross bounds. In the mixed chamber it is at least min(b−c,b−|d|)≥0. Thus the W dot is above 1/2+1/√2>1 after adding its height term.

For a nonpolar high with three violations, every ordinary type is blocked. In face coordinates a,b,c>0, and the remaining face coordinate plus |d| is at most 1. For any r using two face coordinates, their sum is >1, so the high dots above 1 with each matching half-cube lift and both axial lifts; the apex and both W rays give strictly >1 from the hole violations.

Count extras on each side:
- **At most five highs:** at most three ordinary lows and one triple low, hence at most nine.
- **Six highs:** If all polar, they are in one frame. To see this, a polar antipodal pair forces all the other polar highs into that frame by orthogonality. Without such a pair, two singular components must be two triples; each singular polar triple must have every off-diagonal −1/2 by its Gram determinant, hence uses all three frames and has no polar orthogonal neighbor, a contradiction. A six-of-frame layer leaves at most one ordinary type unblocked, since both signed axes forming that root must be among the two absent. If instead there is a nonpolar high, it blocks at least one ordinary type as above. Therefore there are at most two ordinary lows and one triple low, giving at most nine.
- **Seven highs:** At least two antipodal horizontal pairs occur, because at least three components are singular and have at least two points. They must be polar: a nonpolar antipodal pair would require an opposite pair of deleted roots. Two polar pairs share one frame, so put them along ±e1 and ±e2. The three remaining directions lie in the e3,e4 plane. A nonaxis direction violates the sole root of its open quadrant. The hole triangle uses three distinct coordinate supports after every change of triality frame, so at most one nonaxis high fits this plane; two within the same quadrant would have positive dot. If none, the three circle axes block every available type. If there is one, the other two circle axes are opposite its two signs, covering other quadrants, and the nonaxis high blocks its own. Roots using coordinate 1 or 2 have their matching axes. Thus only the triple low is possible and extras total at most eight.
- **Eight highs:** The layer is a polar frame, since it consists of four antipodal pairs and the deleted roots contain no opposite pair. Each ordinary type has a matching high axis after rotating this frame to axes. Thus at most one triple low remains, for at most nine.

Summing gives 21+9+9=39. Any 39-point configuration in this case would have on each side one triple low and a (highs, ordinary lows) count in {(5,3),(6,2),(8,0)}.

## Both-full bound of 38, independent of lower-layer refinements

A triple low beside a full high frame cannot see a star of holes in that frame's coordinates. For the standard star the shared positive coordinate is at most 1−τ by the cap, and the remaining three coordinates satisfy full pairwise absolute-sum bounds ≤1, so their squared norm is ≤1. The norm identity would give 2−τ²≤(1−τ)²+1, impossible for 0<τ<1. At τ=0 equality forces the shared coordinate to be 1 and the other three to lie on a unit axis, contradicting the other strict violations. Every hole triangle is a face in exactly one of the three triality frames. For a coordinate face the normalized symmetric Hadamard exchanges axes and one half-cube and maps that triangle to a star; a sign flip of the unused axis preserves the original triangle and interchanges half-cube parity. Thus two full layers supporting their own triple lows would have to use the same frame.

A useful stronger cut needs only one full layer. If x is a triple low beside a full layer of face orientation, its first three coordinates are ≤1−m for m=max(τ_x,|d_x|), by the cap and retained e_i±e4. Hence its sum S is at least
    (2−τ_x²−d_x²)/(1−m) ≥ 2(1−m²)/(1−m)=2+2m.
For any opposite-side triple low y, the positive-combination argument gives
    x·y > 1+m−m(τ_y+|d_y|).
In particular m=0 is impossible in such a pair, and any coexisting y must satisfy τ_y+|d_y|>1 and τ_y>1/2. If y is itself beside a full layer, it is in the same frame, and its pair sums >1 with each first coordinate ≤1−τ_y imply τ_y<1/2; contradiction. Thus both full high layers can contribute at most one extra low overall, giving ≤38. The existing 38-point example has eight axial highs on each side, so this conditional bound is sharp.

## Optional refinement: a full side cannot face a nine-extra six-high side

If a side has six highs and two ordinary low types, at least one high is nonpolar (otherwise there is at most one ordinary type). Each nonpolar high must have only one or two violations and is assigned to the sole ordinary type not present. Two such highs conflict in the same ordinary cone. Thus exactly one high is nonpolar and the other five are polar.

Five polar nonpositive directions without an antipodal pair must have frame multiplicities (4,1,0). To justify this, within each frame the directions are orthogonal, and all cross-frame products are −1/2. Multiplicities (3,2,0) violate the squared norm of the two group sums by the Gram two-by-two minor; (3,1,1) would give the sum of the two minority directions squared norm 1 but squared projection 3 onto the orthonormal majority three; (2,2,1) gives zero for the sum of the four majority directions, impossible as each dots −1/2 with the fifth. Multiplicity five within one frame is impossible without a pair. In the surviving (4,1,0) profile the lone vector is −1/2 times the sum of the orthonormal four. Their positive dependence forces any further nonpositive direction orthogonal to all four, impossible. Consequently the five polar highs actually include an antipodal pair and all lie in one frame.

Rotate that frame to axes. Two unblocked adjacent ordinary roots can be normalized to e1+e2,e1+e3. Their three signed axes e1,e2,e3 must all be absent among the five polar highs, so those highs are exactly −e1,−e2,−e3,±e4. The sixth direction must be nonnegative on the first three coordinates and zero on the fourth. Its violated third hole cannot be a star root e1±e4, so the triangle is a face in these coordinates. By ±e4, any triple low on that side has τ+|d|≤1, contradicting the opposite-full cut if the other side also has a triple low. Thus for total 39 an (8,0) side, if present, must face a (5,3) side. There is no claim ruling out the remaining possibilities.
