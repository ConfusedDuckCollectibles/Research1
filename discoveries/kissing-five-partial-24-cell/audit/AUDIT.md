# Independent audit: a 22-vertex partial 24-cell in five dimensions

**Pass.** The exact continuum theorem in the author's checkpoint (audited sha256 6d822696a5b18743c49994c045f6149090b1ca03ec45f7802aab5b731d3ce332) survives. Among five-dimensional kissing codes containing at least 22 specified vertices of a common regular 24-cell, the maximum is 40. At least 23 specified vertices in a 40-point code forces the missing vertex to be present and the code to be D5 or L5. This does not prove a new unrestricted upper bound or classify 40-point codes under the 22-only hypothesis.

## Exact proof checks

Use points $(u,t)\in\mathbb R^4\times\mathbb R$ of squared norm 2 and distinct pairwise inner products at most 1. The equatorial root system $D$ is all $\pm e_i\pm e_j$ for $i<j$. Classify an extra as high for $\tau=|t|\ge1$ and low otherwise. Assign the equator to one side only.

1. **High orthoplex, including opposite holes.** The full polar is given by $|u_i|+|u_j|\le1$ and has maximum norm 1, with equality at the axes and all sixteen half-signs. If the largest coordinate in absolute value is $a\ge1/2$, test $a^2+3(1-a)^2\le1$ on $[1/2,1]$; both roots of the equality are endpoints. If $a\le1/2$, equality needs all four coordinates of magnitude $1/2$. Same-side high projections are pairwise nonpositive when scaled to unit directions. A pole cannot share a side with any high. On $m$ projected unit vectors the rank-four trace inequality and $z^2+z\le0$ for $z\in[-1,0]$ give $m^2/4\le 2m$; equality forces every distinct dot to be 0 or $-1$, so precisely four antipodal orthogonal lines and eight points. For each projected direction some orthogonal partner forces both raw heights to equal 1. The eight horizontals then satisfy both root signs from any one retained sign, unless both signs of that root are deleted. For two opposite holes $\pm r$, Parseval gives $\sum_i(r\cdot w_i)^2=2$, so at most one of four orthogonal basis lines can violate $|r\cdot w_i|\le1$. Three lines in the full polar belong to the same frame (axes against halves never orthogonal; orthogonal half-signs have the same parity) and force their fourth line to be the last line of that frame. This removes the apparent antipodal-deletion exception. The signed frames are exactly axes, even half-signs, odd half-signs.

2. **A full same-side cap suppresses nonzero lows.** The standard symmetric normalized Hadamard permutes $D$ and swaps axes and even signs; one coordinate sign flip swaps the two half-sign classes. Rotate to the axial frame. The eight highs give $|u_i|\le1-\tau$. There are three perfect matchings of the four coordinate indices; deleting at most two roots affects at most two matchings, because each support pair lies in only one matching. A clean matching gives two disjoint constraints $|u_i|+|u_j|\le1$. If $\tau>1/2$, the coordinate bounds already force $\|u\|^2<1$; otherwise optimize the two-coordinate square norm over the box cut by the sum constraint to get $2-\tau^2\le2((1-\tau)^2+\tau^2)$. For $0<\tau\le1/2$ this contradicts $5\tau^2-4\tau<0$. At zero, equality on both pairs gives a root, necessarily one of the holes. This proof uses the matching only after triality, not merely in the original coordinates.

3. **Individual deleted-root cones.** In coordinates $r=e_1+e_2$, the eight roots $e_i\pm e_j$ with $i=1,2$ and $j=3,4$ suffice; all have raw dot 1 with $r$. Their constraints imply $0<u_1,u_2\le1$ and $m:=\max(|u_3|,|u_4|)\le1-\max(u_1,u_2)$ whenever $p=u_1+u_2>1$. The exact base below the apex is the convex hull of $e_1,e_2$ and $(1/2,1/2,\pm1/2,\pm1/2)$. It is obtained by weight $2\min(h_1,h_2)$ on the square with first coordinates $1/2,1/2$ and the remaining weight on an axis. For $1<p<2$, $u=(p-1)r+(2-p)h$ for $h$ in that base. Both convexity of the lifted norm ball and directly $\|u\|^2\le p$ give $\tau\ge2-p\ge|u_1-u_2|+2m$. The seven-ray cone then follows from $(u,\tau)=A(r,0)+\tau(H,1)$ with $A=(p-\tau)/2>0$ and $H=(u-Ar)/\tau$ in the base. Its generators all have norm $\sqrt2$ and all mutual dots at least 1. A sphere point with two positive generator coefficients has coefficient sum strictly above 1 by strictness of the Euclidean triangle inequality. The only single-ray low among the original seven is the apex; all pairs of distinct assigned lows thus have dot above 1. This covers one deletion, and all two deletions except raw dot 1, because these are the only holes that can remove any of the eight sufficient roots.

4. **Adjacent mixed cone.** Take $r=e_1+e_2$, $s=e_1+e_3$ and assign a low to the violated root with largest dot, tie broken once. In the $r$ chamber without a second violation, the old cone proof still applies: the only deleted one of its sufficient constraints is $s$, and that constraint was obeyed. For two violations write $u=(a,b,c,d)$, $\kappa=1-a$. Retained inequalities imply $a\ge b\ge c>\kappa\ge0$, $b+c\le1$, $|d|\le\kappa$: notably $a+c>1\ge b+c$ establishes $a>b$. Add $W=(1,1/2,1/2,0,1/\sqrt2)$ to the old rays. Its squared norm is 2; its dot with the apex is $3/2$ and with each other old ray is at least $1/2+1/\sqrt2>1$. The five coefficients in the author's interpolation produce the announced horizontal $u$ identically. The smallest potentially problematic coefficient is the $e_1$ coefficient at $h=2\kappa$, equal to $1-b-c\ge0$. All other coefficients are nonnegative because $|d|\le\kappa\le h/2\le c\le b$. Their total is $a+h/2$, hence exactly one at $h=2\kappa$. At this endpoint the lifted vector of height $T(h)$ lies in the norm ball and $0\le T(h)\le\tau$. The vertical derivative is $3/2-1/\sqrt2>0$, with $T(2c)=a-b+2c=|a-b|+2\max(c,|d|)$; interpolate if the requested height is below this endpoint, use the old criterion if above. The only single-ray lows in this augmented cone are $r$ and $W$, which already conflict. Reflection and swapping coordinates 2 and 3 gives the other side and root. Thus at most one low **per deleted-root type per side**; not at most one low overall. The control code gives two compatible same-side lows of different types with dot $63/65$.

5. **Counting and rigidity.** With $k\in\{1,2\}$ deletions and no eight-high side, there are at most $14$ highs and $2k$ lows, giving $38+k$. If a side has eight highs, every low there is a missing root. Each such insertion forbids a low assigned to that root on either side by the defining strict violation. Remaining types can occur only on the opposite side, so there are at most $k$ lows overall and $16$ highs. For $k=1$, equality must use the latter branch with both caps full and the missing root inserted. For $k=2$, the only extra equality possibility is 7+7 highs and 2+2 lows; the theorem does not claim it feasible or infeasible. Restoring the equator permits any of the nine ordered triality frame pairs because different-side caps have dot at most 0. Their same/distinct frame isometries are D5/L5; exact antipodal counts are 20 versus 12. Deleting a single root from any of these nine witnesses shows 39 is sharp when the named missing root is genuinely absent. The compactness corollary is qualitative: use the compact manifold of orthonormal four-frames together with the compact labeled 41-code space, including each of finitely many choices of 22 matched labels. A sequence of distances to the aligned template tending to zero would converge to an exact overlap, contrary to the theorem. No explicit robustness radius follows.

## Prior and scope

- [Cohn–Rajagopal, §2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00454-026-00841-x) already gives the full-24-cell D5/L5 layered constructions, all three cap frames, triality and the antipode distinction. [Wustep's 17 August log](https://github.com/wustep/maths/blob/main/problems/kissing-5d/ATTACK.md#2026-08-17--the-d4-containing-bound) explicitly gives the full-D4 orthoplex upper bound, and also later finite-grid restrictions. Its superficially similar 27 August exclusion with at least 22 D5-type points applies only to the finite $(1/4)\mathbb Z^5$ candidate graph and does not assume 22 roots in a common four-dimensional section. The present theorem neither absorbs all those grid restrictions nor relies on a lattice.
- [Nyam-Osor's abstract and source record](https://aixiv.online/abs/2611.01607) give at least six antipodal pairs $\Rightarrow$ at most 42, plus upper bounds on antipodal pair counts in 41- and 42-point codes. An exact partial 24-cell contributes at least ten pairs but need contribute no others, so none of the stated pair bounds establishes 40 in this regime. The indexed abstract reports release of an additional root-local supplement; direct Zenodo source/PDF could not be retrieved (cache miss/429). Exhaustive priority is not claimed.
- [Kriebel's independent research dossier](https://aleckriebel.github.io/Math/research/kissing-number-5/) treats neighboring D5/angle/Gauss/special configurations. A bounded targeted search of this and the above did not reveal an equivalent uniform continuous 22-root theorem or forced 23-root completion. [Tomar's near-D4 theorem](https://www.mefyi.com/papers/twenty-four-cell-all-contact/) instead addresses Voronoi volume in dimension four, not the occupancy of a five-dimensional code with a partial equator. [Tomar's global current preprint](https://www.mefyi.com/papers/kissing-numbers-five-seven/) gives $\tau_5\le43$ by an exact semidefinite certificate; [Mittelmann–Vallentin](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.1105) is the earlier refereed upper bound of 44. The 22-root result is distinct from those global results and does not improve them.

**Recommendation:** Worth a narrowly scoped exact note after editorial review. A strong and accurate lead is “A five-dimensional kissing code with 22 vertices of one 24-cell has at most 40 points.” Give known full-core constructions their own citation and identify the extension as stability under two exact deletions, not resolution of the 5-D kissing problem.
