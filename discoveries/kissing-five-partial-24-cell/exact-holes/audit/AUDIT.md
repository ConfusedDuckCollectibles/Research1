# Named holes in a 24-cell section: independent rigidity audit

**Conclusions.** Fix a regular 24-cell in an equatorial subspace of a five-dimensional kissing configuration. If exactly 22 of its named vertices are present, the configuration has at most **38** points, sharp for every pair of holes. If exactly 21 named vertices are present and the three missing roots do not form an adjacency triangle, the maximum is **37**, sharp for every such triple. This improves the proposed 39 bound and includes every path with two edges. There are 1,928 such triples out of 2,024. For adjacency triangles, a direct 38-point example refutes the same 37 bound and the per-type low-point lemma does not extend; no triangle upper bound is claimed here. Together with [the earlier at-least-22 theorem](https://github.com/ConfusedDuckCollectibles/Research1/blob/83a61b036ad943b1297091922d823758b4b1e019/discoveries/kissing-five-partial-24-cell/CHECKPOINT.md), a 39-point code with a named 22-vertex partial 24-cell therefore contains at least 23 named vertices, and the 40-point equality conclusion is unchanged. These are fixed-template statements, not an unrestricted bound.

## Coordinate facts

Use squared norm 2 and pairwise inner product at most 1, with $D=\{\pm e_i\pm e_j:i<j\}$ in the horizontal four coordinates and $M\subset D$ the absent roots. Distinct roots are adjacent when their dot is 1. Reflect the lower side for the proofs and count height zero on only one side. Call a point with positive-side height $t\ge1$ high, and any other extra point low. The full horizontal polar is $|u_i|+|u_j|\le1$ and its unit shell $F$ consists of three orthoplexes: signed axes, even half-cube signs, odd half-cube signs. Different classes have inner products $\pm1/2$ (between half-cubes by odd Hamming distance). Inside one class, distinct directions have inner product 0 or $-1$. Triality maps the three classes onto each other and preserves $D$. A low must violate at least one missing root since its horizontal norm exceeds 1.

### No nonadjacent simultaneous violations

If $|M|\le3$ and $r,s\in M$ have dot less than 1, no compatible horizontal $u$ can satisfy both $u\cdot r>1$ and $u\cdot s>1$. For dot $-2$ this is immediate; for dot $-1$ use $\|r+s\|=\sqrt2$ and $\|u\|\le\sqrt2$. For dot 0 with equal supports, $r+s=2\epsilon e_i$. There are two coordinates outside the support, and at least one pair $\epsilon e_i\pm e_j$ is fully retained, since at most one other hole is available. Their sum is $r+s$, excluding two strict violations. For disjoint supports, pair the four signed coordinates in either of the two other matchings. The resulting two candidate pairs have disjoint root sets and each has sum $r+s$; at least one pair survives the one other deletion. Therefore a _nontriangle_ triple allows at most two simultaneous violations.

### Rays for points assigned to one hole

Assign any violating point to a maximally violated root, with consistent tie-breaking. For $r=e_1+e_2$ the seven basic rays of the positive-side $r$ cone are
\[
 (r,0),\quad (e_1,1),(e_2,1),\quad ((1/2,1/2,\pm1/2,\pm1/2),1).
\]
For every adjacent hole $s$ allow the extra ray $W_{r,s}=((r+s)/2,1/\sqrt2)$. Under the above nontriangle hypothesis this covers every $r$-assigned low: for one violation the eight sufficient cross constraints of the [previous proof §§3–4](https://github.com/ConfusedDuckCollectibles/Research1/blob/83a61b036ad943b1297091922d823758b4b1e019/discoveries/kissing-five-partial-24-cell/CHECKPOINT.md) all hold. For two violations they are adjacent; take the other violated root to be $e_1+e_3$. Every constraint used in the previous mixed-chamber interpolation holds except these two by definition of a double violation, even if a third hole was deleted but obeyed. In particular $a\ge b\ge c>\kappa=1-a\ge|d|$ and $b+c\le1$ for $u=(a,b,c,d)$ are justified by these retained **or obeyed** constraints. The interpolation is the identical one and uses only $W_{r,s}$. The resulting cone can include the additional ray for an alternative adjacent hole without spoiling the conflict proof.

All generators have norm $\sqrt2$ and pairwise dot at least 1. For a W ray against the base, the smallest horizontal contribution is $1/2$, and $1/2+1/\sqrt2>1$ after including height; W's dot to the apex is $3/2$. The only new cross-check is
\[
 W_{r,s}\cdot W_{r,t}=3/2+(s\cdot t)/4>1.
\]
Here distinct $s,t$ adjacent to $r$ cannot be antipodal, so $s\cdot t\ge-1$. A spherical point on more than one distinct generator ray has coefficient sum above 1 by strict Euclidean triangle inequality. Its dot to any other point of this cone of norm $\sqrt2$ exceeds 1 because the other's coefficient sum is at least 1. The single-ray lows (apex and any W rays) conflict pairwise as well. Thus **at most one assigned low per hole per side**. Absence of each apex is exactly the fixed-template hypothesis, not an additional assumption.

### Two tests for high points

A signed axial lift matching a signed coordinate of $r$ dots at least 1 with every basic ray and above 1 with every added W (that coordinate is either 1 or $1/2$). It forbids every $r$-assigned low except the absent apex. The test is unchanged under triality.

A height-one nonpolar high $h=(w,1)$ forbids every low assigned to its own maximal violation $r$. If it violates $r$ only, the cross constraints give $w_1,w_2>0$ and $|w_3|+|w_4|\le 2(1-\max(w_1,w_2))<w_1+w_2$. Thus its dot exceeds 1 with every basic ray, including the apex. The inequality against each W follows from $w\cdot s>0$ for any $s$ adjacent to $r$: a matching coordinate $w_i$ plus a signed other coordinate is at least $w_1+w_2-1>0$. If the high violates both $r,s$, their canonical mixed coordinates give $a\ge b\ge c>\kappa\ge|d|$; hence $a+b>c+|d|$, again making all basic ray dots strictly above 1. For any other $r$-adjacent $t$, the matched positive coordinate is at least $b$ and the absolute other coordinate at most $c$, so $w\cdot t\ge0$. In all these cases $h\cdot W_{r,t}=(w\cdot r+w\cdot t)/2+1/\sqrt2>1$, even if $t$ is an obeyed hole. This strict test works for both W single rays and arbitrary combinations.

## Large high layers and counts

No pole can coexist with another high on its side. For a side of $m\ge6$ highs the normalized nonzero horizontal Gram matrix $G$ has rank at most four and nonpositive off-diagonal. The graph of its strict negative entries decomposes into orthogonal components. On a connected block of $I-G$ the top eigenvalue is simple by Perron–Frobenius and no eigenvalue exceeds 1; hence each Gram block has nullity at most one. There are at least $m-4$ singular blocks, each with at least two vertices. Thus for $m\ge6$ every point has a neighbor in a different orthogonal component. Their two heights are at least 1 and their product at most 1, so all heights are exactly 1. The rank bound gives $m\le8$. This decomposition is prior, not a novel Gram lemma.

* **Eight highs:** there are four antipodal horizontal pairs along an orthonormal basis. If both signs of no root are absent, those pairs imply the full polar conditions from the retained sign of each root. Otherwise only one opposite pair can be absent with $|M|\le3$. Parseval for its missing root $r$ gives $\sum_i(r\cdot w_i)^2=2$, so at most one horizontal basis line violates its absolute inequality. The other three orthogonal lines are in the same frame of $F$, forcing the fourth in that frame as well. Thus the eight highs constitute all axial lifts after triality, banning every low.
* **Seven highs:** there are at least three singular blocks, at least two of size two, hence at least two antipodal horizontal pairs. A nonpolar antipodal pair forces a missing opposite pair; in the nontriangle case its two highs ban two different low types (their violated sets are disjoint). Thus no lows for two holes, and at most one for three. Otherwise select two polar antipodal pairs and rotate them to signed $e_1,e_2$. The remaining three highs lie on the coordinate circle in $e_3,e_4$. Roots using coordinate 1 or 2 are banned by a present matching lift. A nonaxis circle direction violates exactly the root matching its open quadrant, and two such directions cannot share a quadrant. With no nonaxes the three axes cover every quadrant. With exactly one nonaxis the other two must be the two axes opposite its signs: its quadrant is banned by nonpolarity and the other three by axes. With exactly two nonaxes the remaining axis is opposite a common sign of both; their two distinct quadrants are banned by nonpolarity and the remaining two by that axis. Three nonaxis directions require three holes and ban all three. Thus at most one low remains in the three-hole case and none in the two-hole case.
* **Six highs:** for two holes the low total is already at most two. For three holes, any nonpolar high forbids a hole type, leaving at most two lows. Otherwise all six are in $F$. They must have an antipodal pair: without one, the at-least-two singular components have exactly three vertices each. A singular triple with its offdiagonal entries in $\{0,-1/2\}$ consists of three pairwise $-1/2$ directions (the three-by-three determinant vanishes only when all three are $-1/2$). It therefore has members of all three frames. No vector in $F$ is orthogonal to all three, contradicting the second orthogonal component. With an antipodal pair present, its other four points are orthogonal to it and in the same frame. At most one missing-root type uses two absent signed axes of this frame, so there can be at most one low here.
* **At most five highs:** addition of at most three lows always leaves at most eight extras.

Consequently each side has at most eight extras under either hypothesis, yielding $24-|M|+16$. These bounds are attained simply by deleting the specified holes from $D_5$.

## Triangle control and credit

For missing triangle $\{e_1+e_2,e_1+e_3,e_2+e_3\}$, keep both complete axial caps and add the equatorial point $(1,3/5,4/5,0,0)$ to the 21 retained roots. It has squared norm 2, violates only the three named roots, and has dot at most 1 with all cap points. Total is 38 with exactly 21 named vertices. The failed per-type lemma has a stronger elementary diagnostic: the same equatorial point and $(1/10,0,1,0,\sqrt{99}/10)$ both assign uniquely to $e_1+e_3$, but their mutual dot is $9/10$. These two points are feasible with the 21-root core (not simultaneously with the full caps in the 38-point witness). Hence extending the preceding cone conflict argument to all triples is not valid.
