# Proof: growing triple deletions with one shared marker

**Theorem.** The masked Gram construction gives the arbitrary-anchor two-weight lift. For $n\ge100$, any simple 3-uniform deletion hypergraph of maximum degree $D\le n/8$ leaving one old coordinate untouched is covered, with no regularity or special incidence partition assumed. The bound is a sufficient range, not an optimal cutoff. This is a specific weighted-theta equality, giving a restricted case related to Conjecture H. It is not a general proof of H, and not a new proof of ordinary Chvátal.

## Gram and row fit

Use $S$ for surviving old triples, $X$ for their incidence, $x_i$ for its columns, $d_i$ for deletion degrees, $K=\binom{n-1}{2}$, $a=\binom{n-2}{2}$, $b=\binom{n-3}{2}$, $q=\binom{n-4}{2}$. In the residual graph give triples weight 1 and selected positive-degree bridge $i$ weight $w_i=d_i$. Ignore zero-weight vertices. Let $\alpha=\sum_i w_i$, $L=K-\alpha>0$, and $t_i=K-d_i$. Let $\lambda_i=1$ for bridge anchors, otherwise $t_i/K$.

For each $C_i$, put zero on triples containing $i$, and start at $1/a$ on all other rows. On those rows fitting its old incidence demands is an exact linear solve on the remaining $n-1$ coordinates. The full incidence Gram is $bI+(n-3)J$, with inverse infinity norm at most $2/b$. Removing triples changes each absolute row sum by at most $3D$, giving inverse norm at most $2/(b-6D)$. Each starting error is the difference of the two nonnegative quantities $\#\{\text{deleted }U:j\in U,i\notin U\}/a$ and $1-\lambda_j$; both are at most $D/a$. This yields the stated $c=|1/a-1/K|+6D/[a(b-6D)]$ bound on avoiding rows. For each positive-degree unbridged $i$, fit $V_i$ likewise, putting $1$ on incident rows. The other incidence demands are negatives of the actual surviving codegrees, bounded by $n-2$; the bound off incidence is $v_0=6(n-2)/(b-6D)$.

Put $g=\mathbf1-Cw$, $F=\sum_{i\ \mathrm{unbridged}}d_i V_iV_i^\top/[K(K-d_i)]$ (positive-degree terms only) and $B=C\operatorname{diag}(w)C^\top+gg^\top/L+F$. Direct substitution gives $g^\top x_i=L\lambda_i$ and
$$
 Bx_i=\begin{cases}\mathbf1-d_i C_i,&i\text{ bridged},\\
   \lambda_i\mathbf1+(d_i/K)V_i,&i\text{ unbridged}
 \end{cases}
$$
with a zero $V_i$ term when $d_i=0$. In both cases $(Bx_i)_U=1$ whenever $i\in U$. Those are compatibility identities for **all** coordinates, not merely tight stars. The full normalized Gram blocks are $B+P,C,C^\top,\operatorname{diag}(1/w)$; its Schur complement is $P+gg^\top/L+F$. Its locked entries use the **intersecting**, not the disjoint, distinct pairs. The marker bridge block is diagonal since every bridge intersects every other bridge. For any tight weighted star $s$, the full Gram $H$ satisfies $Hs=\mathbf1$, $s^\top Hs=K$; Cauchy–Schwarz implies $KH-J\succeq0$. Multiplication on both sides by $\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt w)$ gives the ordinary theta upper matrix at value $K$: diagonal $K-w_i$, distinct intersecting entries $-\sqrt{w_iw_j}$. An untouched star attains $K$. Dropping or reducing some of the allowed bridge weights keeps the same residual value by monotonicity and the untouched lower bound. This observation is about the residual, not arbitrary whole-face weights.

## Compatible mask and perturbation

For the full old triple layer use $P_*=I+A_{\rm disj}/q-J/K$. The inclusion filtration $f_R(U)=1_{R\subseteq U}$ gives the disjoint-adjacency eigenvalues $(-1)^j\binom{n-3-j}{3-j}$, $j=0,1,2,3$, by inclusion-exclusion. The zeroth and first layers vanish in $P_*$; the residual eigenvalues are $1+(n-5)/q$ and $1-1/q$. For the survivor incidence subspace put $\Pi=X(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top$, $E=I-\Pi$, $P_0=EP_{*,SS}E$. Zero-padding a vector in $\ker X^\top$ to the deleted coordinates proves its gap $\mu=1-1/q$. No inherited nullspace assertion is made for the uncompressed survivor principal block.

Mask $M$ keeps symmetric entries with nonempty intersection, including diagonal. On this mask, compatibility is $(QX)_{U,i}=0$ at every $i\in U$. For those entries all terms in the incidence sum already lie on the mask. Hence $Q_0=M(I-B-P_0)$ is compatible and $T(Q)=M(EQE)$ preserves the compatible subspace. Its exact quadratic form is $\|EQE\|_F^2\ge\|Q\|_F^2-2\|\Pi Q\|_F^2$. For the column at $U$, pad its vector by zeros on deletions and restrict to full old triples meeting $U$. The three inside incidence columns annihilate it. The local outside-column Gram after projecting off the inside columns is
$$
 3(n-4)I+\frac{3(7-n)}{n+3}J\ \preceq\ 3(n-4)I .
$$
The elementary complete Gram count here counts all triples through an outside point and meeting $U$ as $3(n-3)$, and two outside points as 3. Since the survivor $X^\top X$ is at least $(a-3D)I$, summing the column projection estimate gives $T\succeq\kappa I$ on the compatible subspace, $\kappa=1-6(n-4)/(a-3D)$. This is a symmetric Frobenius-space inequality. The correction $P=P_0+E\,T^{-1}(Q_0)\,E$ solves all locks and is PSD at $\|Q_0\|_F<\mu\kappa$. Matrix entries are rational, not outputs of a numerical SDP. Full positivity is analytic; no claim that the replay materializes the giant Gram.

The error terms in the foundation check as follows. With $W=C-J/K$, the terms linear in $W$ cancel in $B-J/K$ exactly, leaving $W\operatorname{diag}(w)W^\top+(Ww)(Ww)^\top/L+F$. Each row has at most three incident anchors and three incident unbridged sites. Row Cauchy–Schwarz gives $R$. For $G=X^\top X$, $G_f=aI+(n-2)J$, the full approximation to $x_U^\top G^{-1}X_D^\top$ has coordinates $(|U\cap d|-\omega)/a$, $\omega=6/(n-1)$. Here survivor and deletion are distinct, so overlap is 0–2 and total overlap is at most $3D$; the squared overlap error bound is $(6D+m\omega^2)/a^2$. The inverse identity gives the pre-incidence row error $6H_0/[a(a-6D)]$ in $\ell_1$, $H_0=3D+m\omega$. Multiplication by $X_D^\top$ has $\ell_1\to\ell_2$ norm at most $\sqrt D$. The two coefficients $13/12,13$ in $U_2$ are Young's inequality. For the first-order term, each column of $P_{*,DS}$ has entries $-1/K$ on deletions that meet its survivor and $\delta=1/q-1/K$ otherwise; the overlap-sum bound $3D$ applies to the former and the full $\ell_1$ sum is at most $H_0$. Each component of $X_D^\top P_{*,DS}$ is bounded by $D/q$; this gives the remainder of $A$. The quadratic term uses $X^\top P_{*,SS}X=X_D^\top P_{*,DD}X_D$ and $\|P_{*,DD}\|_2\le1+m/q$. Thus every masked error is bounded by $R+2A+(1+m/q)U_2$.

## Explicit error functions

Take $e$ and $\sigma$ as upper bounds on the maximum and total unbridged deletion degrees; $e=D,\sigma=3m$ always suffice. The entrywise estimates are

    R = 3D/K² + alpha*c² + (3D/K + alpha*c)²/L
        + (3e + sigma*v0²)/(K*(K-e))
    A = (3D/K + H0*delta)/a + 6D*H0/(a*(a-6D)*q)
    U2 = (13/12)*(6D+m*omega²)/a² + 13D*(6*H0/(a*(a-6D)))²

Here $H_0=3D+m\omega$ and $\delta=1/q-1/K$. For $n\ge100$ and $D\le n/8$, each of $K,a,b,q$ is at least $9n^2/20$; $m\le n^2/24$, $L\ge3n^2/10$, and $K-D,a-6D,b-6D\ge 2n^2/5$. This gives $c\le10/n^3$, $v_0\le15/n$, $\omega\le7/n$, $H_0\le2n/3$, $\delta\le15/n^3$, and $1+m/q\le11/10$. The three bounds are $R\le4/n^3+185/n^4$, $A\le2/n^3+30/n^4$, and $(1+m/q)U_2\le5/n^3+13/n^4+900/n^5$.

## Explicit ranges and exact controls

The independent replay checks the seven rational roundings, in coefficient order for $R$, $A$, and $(1+m/q)U_2$:
$$
 425/108<4,\quad 39575/216<185,\quad 50/27<2,\quad2300/81<30,
 \quad715/162<5,\quad35035/2916<13,\quad71500/81<900 .
$$
The elementary binomial lower bounds, $m\le Dn/3$, $\alpha,\sigma\le3m$ and $e\le D$ give $R+2A+(1+m/q)U_2\le13/n^3+260/n^4+900/n^5$. The number of ordered locks is at most $\binom n3\,3K\le n^5/4$. The resulting upper Frobenius bound decreases with $n\ge100$ and is at most $1569/2000$; meanwhile $\mu\kappa\ge(4499/4500)(17/20)=76483/90000$. All inequalities are strict at the right comparison, even when $D=n/8$ exactly. The fixed-$D$ monotonic argument also checks: after substituting $m,\alpha$ proportional to $n-1$, $H_0$ is constant, scaled positive terms $n^4R,n^4 A,n^4(1+m/q)U_2$ are nonincreasing, and $\sqrt{\binom n3(\binom n3-\binom{n-3}3)}/n^4$ decreases once $n\ge10$. Both $\mu,\kappa$ increase for fixed $D$ in the required range. The exact squared sufficient ratios at $n=41,D=3$ general and $n=36,D=3$ distinct tips are approximately 0.921068 and 0.989714. The latter needs bridges at all deletion-anchor endpoints with a distinct nonanchor tip per deletion; omitted endpoints use the general bound, not $e=1$. No smaller-order failure follows from failing the sufficient test.

The paired audit files are [replay.cjs](replay.cjs) and [expected.json](expected.json). Run `node replay.cjs`; no dependencies are required. At $n=112$ the replay deletes 515 out of the 518 triples in 14 Latin-style classes on 111 coordinates, leaving **exactly one untouched**. Deletion degrees 12, 13 and 14 occur, with all three among the irregular subset of 37 selected anchors and 74 positive-degree unbridged sites. The code constructs the survivor incidence Gram from the actual 227,405 triples and solves the exact fits independently modulo 65521; it checks 33,600 coupled old incidence coordinates (900 locked) and 24 old/marker/empty/crossing score cases. The exact rational perturbation ratio for this fixture is approximately 0.334612061. The small $n=10$ control constructs the full projected Kneser block, a nonzero compatible $Q_0$, and nonidentity $T(Q_0)$: 1,150 null incidence entries and 345 compatibility checks pass. That small control is **not** a positivity theorem below the sufficient range. Modular identities are crosschecks, not a proof of real PSD; the strict BigInt comparisons and the preceding analytic bound provide the real certificate.

## Lift, boundaries, and prior

The underlying graph is on nonempty faces (equivalently remove the looped empty vertex as in EFF §5). Put $S_0=n+K$. The weighted old-center star at $i$ is $S_0(u+v)-d_i u+\mathbf1_{\{i\text{ chosen}\}}v$; the marker star is $(r+1)v$, $r$ the number of chosen anchors, and never wins under the given $n$. For nonempty anchors let $k=\min_{\rm anchors}d_i$. An untouched center gives the baseline and a minimum-degree anchor gives the other side of the formula. For empty anchors the correction is zero.

For $k>0$, at $u:v=1:k$ combine the residual certificate (reduce bridge weights to $k$), $k$ times the full old triple layer, and $(k+1)$ times the full old singleton/pair layers; subadditivity of weighted theta follows from its maximum of weighted linear scores in a common handle representation. This yields $(k+1)S_0$ before the marker. The marker addition can be proved directly with a dual Gram, avoiding ambiguity about the word clique. For crossing cap $s=(k+1)S_0$ set $t=s/k$. The $r$ bridge vectors have fixed metric $tI-J$ in the unscaled dual Gram. Add the marker vector with prescribed inner products $-1$ against every bridge; its projection has squared norm $r/(t-r)$. Its required squared norm is $t-1$, attainable by an orthogonal residual because $t\ge r+1$. All other old faces are disjoint from the marker and impose no fixed dual entries.

Pure inner: restrict the full old rank-layer certificates at $S_0$. Pure outer for nonempty anchors: triple cost $K$; color old pairs $i+j$ and bridges $2i$ modulo odd $M\in\{n,n+1\}$, distinct incident colors, and fit singletons in missing colors of an $n+1$ palette. Every color class consists of pairwise disjoint faces, giving lower-rank cost $n+1$. This works for any anchor subset and also for odd $n$, when the extra unused color outside the residue palette is available. If $k=0$, an untouched anchor is a common maximizer of both endpoints; if the anchors are empty, the marker is adjacent to every old face in the disjointness graph and costs nothing extra (or use the unscaled dual from the full old system with an unconstrained cross block). Zero weights are either omitted or taken by monotonicity/continuity, not inverted. Interpolation of the crossing and appropriate pure endpoint gives exactly
$$
 \vartheta(G,w)=S_0(u+v)+\max\{0,v-ku\}
$$
when anchors are nonempty, with $S_0(u+v)$ otherwise. The coefficients in this formula are nonnegative; it does not permit nonnested layers or multiple markers.

Closest primary comparator: [Ellis–Filmus–Friedgut §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28404v3#S5) explicitly separates their general projection-packing result from theta/weighted Hoffman tightness; their general theorem does not supply the latter certificate. Their Conjecture H is weighted Hoffman optimization for the unweighted downset graph, not by wording the universal arbitrary-positive-face-weight proposition. [Chang–Liu–Liu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19123) and the older rank-three result [Czabarka–Hurlbert–Kamat](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00494) address intersecting-family cardinality, not these semidefinite equalities. The uniform Kneser core goes back to [Lovász](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1979.1055985); the masked local inverse and PSD strategy are explicitly inherited from the [Research1 cycle proof](https://github.com/ConfusedDuckCollectibles/Research1/blob/main/breakthroughs/spectral-chvatal-cycles/proof.md), whose underlying derivation is reproduced above. A focused primary check did not surface the same irregular, growing-deletion theorem, not an exhaustive priority judgment.
