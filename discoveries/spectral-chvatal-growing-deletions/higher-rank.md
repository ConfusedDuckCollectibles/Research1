# A weighted theta certificate for near-complete top layers of every rank

**Theorem.** For integer $r\ge3$, $n\ge100r^4$, $K=\binom{n-1}{r-1}$, let old nonempty faces of rank smaller than $r$ have weight one, old $r$-sets independent weights $p_U\in[0,1]$, and additional faces consist only of new singletons and old–new pairs with individual weights in $[0,1]$. Write $d_i=\sum_{U\ni i}(1-p_U)$, $D=\max_i d_i$, $w_i$ the total bridge weight at old $i$, $W=\max_i w_i$, $B_*=D+W$, $\alpha=\sum_i w_i$. At $B_*\le K/(20r^3)$, $\alpha\le K/8$, weight-zero vertices omitted, the theorem proves
$$
\vartheta(G,w)=\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}\binom{n-1}{k-1}+\max_i(K-d_i+w_i).
$$
This is a near-complete class with arbitrary *top-rank* weights under specified aggregate bounds, not arbitrary downsets, arbitrary lower-rank weights, or general Conjecture H. For each fixed $r$ it permits positive density of actual deletions; that allowance decreases with $r$. The earlier rank-three theorem has substantially better quantitative bounds in its special case and should remain separately stated.

## 1. Uniform Kneser operator and weighted positivity

Put $a=\binom{n-2}{r-1}$, $b=\binom{n-3}{r-1}$, $q=\binom{n-r-1}{r-1}$. The successive factor ratios for $q/K$ are $1-r/(n-1-j)$, $0\le j\le r-2$. The product deficit is at most $r(r-1)/(n-r+1)<1/100$; the other two ratios are no smaller. The complete old incidence Gram is $aI+\binom{n-2}{r-2}J$. Avoiding one site gives $bI+\binom{n-3}{r-2}J$. Each weighted loss has absolute row sum at most $rD$; hence the inverse row absolute-sum norms are at most $2/(a-2rD)$, $2/(b-2rD)$ by the preliminary complete inverse and Neumann inversion. Both denominators are $\ge0.97K$, since $2rD/K\le1/(10r^2)$. The weighted full Gram's smallest eigenvalue is $\ge a-rD$.

Here is the independent spectral check. On the inclusion span generated by $f_T(U)=1_{\{T\subseteq U\}}$ with $|T|=j$, disjoint adjacency multiplies the indicator of disjointness from $T$ by $\binom{n-r-j}{r-j}$. Inclusion–exclusion puts its highest graded coefficient at $(-1)^j\binom{n-r-j}{r-j}$. The spans are nested (sum the next-level indicators), exhaust at $j=r$, and self-adjointness gives these eigenvalues on the orthogonal level differences. The first two levels together are the old incidence span. The next-to-leading ratio relative to $q$ at $j=2$ is $\eta=(r-1)/(n-r-1)$. Later absolute values decrease, with quotient $(r-j)/(n-r-j)\le1$. On constants the factors $\binom{n-r}{r}/q=(n-r)/r$ and $\binom nr/K=n/r$ show exact cancellation. Thus $P_*=I+A_{\rm disj}/q-J/K$ annihilates the first two levels and has gap $\mu=1-\eta>0.99$ on their complement. This uses the rank-$r$ spectrum, not an extrapolated numeric rank-three eigenvalue.

As in the already audited heterogeneous rank-three seed, use complete incidence $X$, $F=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt p)$, $f=F\mathbf1$, $\Delta=I-F^2$, $\widehat X=FX$, $L_*=I-P_*$, $R=F P_*F+\Delta$. It is PSD, with locked entries $I-ff^\top/K$. If $y\perp \widehat X$, $Fy\perp X$, so $R$'s quadratic form is $\ge \mu\|Fy\|^2+y^\top\Delta y\ge\mu\|y\|^2$. Zero rows of $F$ give isolated unit rows in $R$; delete them before reciprocal scaling.

Write $G=\widehat X^\top\widehat X$, $\Pi=\widehat XG^{-1}\widehat X^\top$, $E=I-\Pi$. From $L_*X=X$ the exact projection identities are
$$
 R\widehat X=F L_* \Delta X,\qquad
 \widehat X^\top R\widehat X=X^\top\Delta X-(\Delta X)^\top L_*(\Delta X).
$$
Each entry of $L_*$ is at most $1/q$ in magnitude, so each entry of the first right-hand side is at most $D/q$. The row absolute norm $2r/(a-2rD)$ gives $\|\Pi R\|_{\max}\le3rD/K^2$. The second identity's matrix is PSD; $\|\Delta X\|_2^2\le\|X^\top\Delta X\|_2\le rD$, and $L_*\succeq-\eta I$. Hence its norm is at most $rD(1+\eta)$. Each incidence row times $G^{-1}$ has Euclidean norm at most $\sqrt r/(a-rD)$. This yields $\|\Pi R\Pi\|_{\max}\le2r^2D/K^2$. These hold with zero/unequal weights, using the actual sums of deficits rather than number of nonunit rows.

## 2. Rank mass estimates and compatible inverse

Let $t_i=K-d_i,\ M=\max_i(t_i+w_i),\ \lambda_i=t_i/(M-w_i)$, and $L=M-\alpha$. One has $|M-K|,\,M-w_i-t_i,\,w_i\le B_*$, and $M,t_i,M-w_i\ge0.99K,\ L\ge0.8K$. Fit $C_i$ supported on avoiding $r$-sets to weighted targets $\lambda_j$ at $j\ne i$ and zero at $i$, starting at $1/a$. The error before fitting is the difference of weighted losses divided by $a$ and $1-\lambda_j$, both nonnegative and at most $B_*/(0.99K)$. Use $|M-a|\le rK/n+B_*$, since $(r-1)/(n-1)\le r/n$, then
$$
 |C_i(U)-1/M|\le c=2r/(nK)+4rB_*/K^2\quad(U\not\ni i).
$$
In detail the $B_*/K^2$ coefficient is at most $1/(0.99)^2+2r/(0.97\cdot0.99)<4r$. For a slack site $V_i$ starts at one on incident rows and is fit to $t_i e_i$. Its avoiding correction uses codegree $\binom{n-2}{r-2}\le rK/n$ and inverse row bound $2/(b-2rD)$; hence $v=3r^2/n$ bounds its avoidance magnitude.

Scale column entries by $f_U$, and use the earlier multiple-marker rank identities (exhibiting each actual bridge at $i$ by a copy of $\widehat C_i$ and reciprocal diagonal $1/w_e$). In brief $g=f-\widehat C w$, slack Gram $\sum_i(1-\lambda_i)\widehat V_i\widehat V_i^\top/t_i$, and the old rank part $B_{\rm rank}$ gives $g^\top\hat x_i=L\lambda_i$ and $B_{\rm rank}\hat x_i=\lambda_i(f-w_i\widehat C_i)+(1-\lambda_i)\widehat V_i$. The $V_i$ fits include positive but slack anchors. This is $f_U$ whenever $i\in U$; duplicated bridges precisely cancel their aggregate term at a tight old star.

The centered expansion at $ff^\top/M$ cancels all linear deviations and yields the three stated rank bounds. To check their uniform constants, use $\alpha\le\min(nB_*,K/8)$. For $B_*>0$, after division by $rB_*/K^2$, the first term is bounded by $1/0.99^2+8r/n+4rB_*/K<3$, by Young's squared inequality. In the second, $rB_*/M+\alpha c\le(1/0.99+2+1/2)rB_*/K<5rB_*/K$, so its normalized coefficient is at most $32rB_*/K\le32/(20r^2)<1$. The third has coefficient at most $(1+9r^3/n)/0.99^2<2$. All bounds extend at $B_*=0$ by zero perturbation. The signed shift on the mask is $(1/K-1/M)ff^\top$ and costs at most $2B_*/K^2$; both signs are allowed. With $P_0=ERE$,
$$
 \|\mathcal M(I-B_{\rm rank}-P_0)\|_{\max}
 \le (12r+2+2r^2)B_*/K^2
 \le 7 r^2 B_*/K^2 .
$$
Zero top-weight rows have identically zero defect. For positive weights the signed shift cannot be dropped unless $M=K$.

For a symmetric compatible masked $Q$, $\widehat X^\top Q_{\cdot,V}$ is zero at each $i\in V$. Each outside sum has at most $r\binom{n-2}{r-2}\le Kr^2/n$ terms, since a support face must contain $i$ and some coordinate of $V$; factors $f_U$ are at most one. Therefore $\|\Pi Q\|_{\max}\le3r^3\|Q\|_{\max}/n$; absolute column sums of $\Pi$ are at most $3r$, using at most $K$ incidences per old coordinate. The map $T(Q)=\mathcal M(EQE)$ preserves the compatible subspace, with $\|I-T\|_{\max\to\max}\le(2+3r)3r^3/n\le12r^4/n\le0.12$. Hence it can be inverted there. The correction $EQE$ has spectral norm at most the sparse symmetric $Q$'s norm, itself at most $rK\|Q\|_{\max}$; $EQE$ is not assumed sparse. The resulting bound $7r^3B_*/[K(1-0.12)]\le35/88<\mu$ proves real PSD on the weighted complement. The normalized Gram, then physical reciprocal diagonal after scaling each positive row by $1/f_U$, gives theta $M$ on the top layer and bridges via its exact tight-star action, as in the prior audit.

## 3. Lower layers and new singleton completion

For $2\le k<r$, $H_k=I+A_{\rm disj}^{(k)}/q_k$ with $q_k=\binom{n-k-1}{k-1}$ is PSD by the complete $k$-layer spectrum; its minimum adjacency eigenvalue is $-q_k$. It takes each layer star indicator to ones, by a diagonal contribution at a containing row and exactly $q_k$ disjoint containing rows otherwise. For singletons use $J$, whose distinct entries are all free because distinct singletons are disjoint; any singleton star produces ones. The direct block sum preserves cross-layer intersecting zeros and maps a common maximizing old star to ones. Its exact weight is $s=M+\sum_{k<r}\binom{n-1}{k-1}$; no rank-two-only coloring assumption is carried into higher ranks.

The old/bridge cap-$s$ dual Gram has bridge block $s\operatorname{diag}(1/w_e)-J$. For a new singleton approaching only its incident bridge constraints, take projection coefficients $-w_e/(s-q_b)$ on its own bridges, $q_b=\sum_{e\ni b}w_e$. The forced inner products are $-1$; required projection norm squared $q_b/(s-q_b)\le s/u_b-1$, since $q_b+u_b\le n+1<s$. Attach each singleton with its own additional orthogonal residual. Other bridge/marker entries are disjoint and free, including when bridges share old endpoints. Empty singleton support or zero weight needs no inverse. Each new star weighs at most $n+1$; equality is attained by the old maximizing star.

## 4. Independent exact controls and primary context

Run `node higher-rank-replay.cjs` and compare the [expected transcript](higher-rank-expected.json). The [source](higher-rank-replay.cjs) requires no dependencies. The standalone code independently adapts the prior rational audit to a different **rank-four at $n=9$** instance, and adds direct complete lower-layer PSD checks. It uses weights $0,1/9,1/4,4/9,9/16,1$, two zero 4-faces, six positive bridges across distinct markers, a tight old site with two bridges, a positive bridge with slack, and a bridge-free singleton. It checks 32,256 direct inclusion counts for eigenvalues $5,-4,3,-2,1$; 630 outside incidence-mask counts; 1,134 first-order, 81 second-order, 15,246 locked and 252 zero-row seed entries. A weighted-complement trade has Rayleigh numerator $13$ versus exact gap lower $13/4$. Complete layer Grams have exact rational LDL ranks $1,28,76$ for $k=1,2,3$ and map all 1,161 star entries correctly. Bridge scales 1 and $1/8$ give opposite shifts $11/48,-97/96$, with 496 actual positive-weight locks each, all violated if the offset is omitted. Exact scalar checks cover 51 ranks up to 137; the analytic inequalities, not that finite sample, cover every $r$. Tiny controls verify identities, not positivity of a corrected inhomogeneous Gram outside the theorem range.

An actual theorem-domain rank-four family at $n=25{,}600=100\cdot4^4$ partitions the old coordinates into twenty blocks of 1,280, arranged in five disjoint square-free block quartets. Set all 4-sets using one element from each of the four blocks of four quartets to zero; in the remaining quartet give those 4-sets weight $1/2$; all others weight one. Every coordinate has positive deficit; $10{,}737{,}418{,}240{,}000$ actual deletions have exact fraction $1677721600/2795547353599$ (about 0.06%). Place the six finite fixture bridge weights within old coordinates of the half-weight quartet. $D=1280^3,\ W=17/12,\ \alpha=13/4$ satisfy the exact budgets; the exact star/theta is $33537917337617/12$. This family scales at fixed $r=4$. No huge Gram is materialized; the symbolic proof certifies positivity.

The complete layer ingredients are classical from [Lovász, Example 6.1.12](https://lovasz.web.elte.hu/kurzusok/orth-theta-16.pdf), with nearby exact symmetric generalized Johnson results in [L-Systems and the Lovász Number](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-025-00136-4). Their complete/symmetric graphs are not the present arbitrary heterogeneous perturbations. The [published rank-three foundation](heterogeneous-weights.md) supplies the rank fit, diagonal-corrected seed, and multi-marker attachment; the all-rank constants and complete lower layers are the verified additions. [Ellis–Filmus–Friedgut §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28404v3#S5) explicitly separates projection packing from spectral H and leaves general H outside this scope. No exact overlap was found in a focused comparison; this is not a priority claim and the result has not been externally peer reviewed.
