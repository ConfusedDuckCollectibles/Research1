# The finite-size transmission law of a resonant dimer with rare pairing breaks

## 1. Model, target and transmissivity convention

Take a real one-particle nearest-neighbor chain with hopping \(+1\), meaning that \(E\psi_j=V_j\psi_j+\psi_{j-1}+\psi_{j+1}\). Attach identical semi-infinite leads of onsite value \(v=6/5\) and hopping \(+1\) to the two ends. The sample contains \(N\) independent two-site tiles, with \(A=0\), \(B=v\), and tiles \(AA,BB,AB\) of probabilities \((1-p)/2,(1-p)/2,p\), respectively. \(AB\) means that the \(A\) site is encountered first from the left. No positions inaccessible to the model are changed between the hypotheses. The comparison is intact pairing \(p=0\) versus a specified positive density of pairing breaks \(p>0\); next we scale \(p\) with length.

For an energy \(E\) inside the lead band, let \(c=(E-v)/2=\cos k,\ s=\sqrt{1-c^2}>0\). Single sites transfer \((\psi_j,\psi_{j-1})\) to \((\psi_{j+1},\psi_j)\) by \(L_{V_j}(E)=\begin{psmallmatrix}E-V_j&-1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}\), and the rightmost matrix of a word is the first site encountered. Conjugate all transfers by the same real flux normalization
\[
 C_E=\begin{pmatrix}s&0\\-c&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 C_E L_v(E) C_E^{-1}=R_k=\begin{pmatrix}c&-s\\s&c\end{pmatrix}.
\]
If \(M\) is the full transfer in these coordinates, the single-channel two-lead transmission probability is
\[
 T(M)=\frac{4}{\|M\|_F^2+2}
      =\frac{4\mu^2}{(1+\mu^2)^2},\qquad \mu=\|M\|_{\rm op}\ge1.                 \tag{1}
\]
To check the flux interpretation, the real rotation coordinates transform by the normalized circular eigenvectors of \(R_k\) to equal-current right- and left-moving waves; identical leads cancel the common current factor. A real determinant-one matrix in this traveling-wave basis is of the form \(\begin{psmallmatrix}\alpha&\overline\beta\\ \beta&\overline\alpha\end{psmallmatrix}\) with \(|\alpha|^2-|\beta|^2=1\). A wave incident on an open lead with none incoming from the opposite lead has transmitted modulus \(1/|\alpha|\), by solving the two-component transfer equation. The Frobenius norm is unchanged by this unitary basis change and equals \(2(|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2)\), giving (1); its second form uses the reciprocal singular values of a real \(SL(2,\mathbb R)\) matrix. Outer rotation factors preserve the result. Mismatched leads are not silently modeled as these contacts.

At **\(E=0\)**, \(AA\) transfers as \(-I\), \(BB\) is the rotation \(R_\theta\) with \(\theta=2k\), and \(AB\) is
\[
 D=(C_0 L_v(0) C_0^{-1})(C_0 L_0(0) C_0^{-1}).
\]
Here \(\cos k=-3/5,\sin k=4/5,\cos\theta=-7/25,\sin\theta=-24/25\), and \(D\) has norm \(2\) and \(T(D)=16/25\). These are direct exact rational controls. The angle \(\theta/\pi\) is irrational: if \(e^{i\theta}\) were a root of unity, its sum with its inverse, \(-14/25\), would be a rational algebraic integer and hence an integer. Thus \(p=0\) has \(T=1\) for every sample and every length at this specific energy. This is an on-resonance transport statement, not a claim of an absolutely continuous band or an absence of pure-point spectral components in the intact random dimer.

Conversely, **each fixed \(0<p<1\)** has almost-sure positive exponential transmission attenuation at \(E=0\). The closed group generated by the tile transfers contains all real planar rotations (the closure of the powers of \(R_\theta\)); no finite family of lines is invariant. If that group were compact, averaging a positive definite metric over it would give a metric invariant under all its elements, and rotational invariance forces that metric to be a multiple of the identity. \(D\) is not orthogonal, contradicting compactness. The integrability condition is automatic for a finite alphabet. [Furstenberg's product theorem](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1963-0163345-0) gives \(\gamma(p)=\lim_N \log\|M\|/N>0\) almost surely per two-site tile. By (1), \(\lim_N -\log T/(2N)=\gamma(p)\) almost surely. This is a transport localization criterion at one energy; no new statement about dynamical or spectral localization, many-body behavior, or a real material is inferred.

## 2. How the coherent dilute law emerges without imposing random phase

Let \(p=p_N\), \(Np_N\to\Lambda\in(0,\infty)\), and let \(K\) be the number of \(AB\) tiles. Its marginal has the Poisson limit. To compute the *coherent* transmission controlled by \(K\), condition on \(K=j\). The \(j\) defective tiles in the tile word are a uniform \(j\)-subset of \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\). Equivalently the \(j+1\) background gap lengths \(g_0,\ldots,g_j\) are uniformly distributed over weak compositions of \(N-j\) into \(j+1\) parts. Conditional on gaps, the background choices remain fair and independent. Outer gaps have no effect on (1). For the interior gaps write \(\phi_\ell\) for their total rotation angle, and set
\[
 z_m=\tfrac12((-1)^m+e^{im\theta}).
\]
For \(m\ne0\), \(|z_m|<1\). The conditional joint Fourier coefficient for interior modes \((m_\ell)\) given the gaps is \(\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1} z_{m_\ell}^{g_\ell}\). For each fixed active \(\ell\) and \(m_\ell\ne0\), the unconditional coefficient conditional on \(K=j\) has absolute value bounded by
\[
 E[|z_{m_\ell}|^{g_\ell}\mid K=j]
 \ \le\ \frac{j}{N(1-|z_{m_\ell}|)} .                              \tag{2}
\]
Indeed for any gap, \(\Pr(g_\ell=l\mid K=j)=\binom{N-l-1}{j-1}/\binom Nj\le j/N\) for \(0\le l\le N-j\). Thus all nontrivial finite Fourier coefficients tend to zero. By density of trigonometric polynomials on the torus, for fixed \(j\) the interior phases converge **jointly in distribution** to independent Haar angles. This is not total-variation convergence of the finite discrete law to a continuous angle distribution, nor an assumption of a uniform reflection phase in the final chain. (2) gives a finite-error certificate for any specified trigonometric-polynomial probe by summing its coefficient error bounds. The same estimate holds for each nonzero Fourier mode multiplied by a bounded continuous function of the rescaled ordered defect positions, with an extra factor its sup norm, because positions are fixed by gaps. For the zero Fourier mode the rescaled ordered uniform \(j\)-subset converges to the order statistics of \(j\) independent unit-interval uniforms (sampling with replacement collides with probability \(O(j^2/N)\)). Hence the interior phases become independent even of the limiting marked positions. Taking a finite \(j\)-truncation then controlling the count tail gives the Poisson mixture below.

**Microscopic limit to a known random-phase law.** The following radial recursion and Poisson-count model are not new as phenomenological transport models: see [Vagner et al. (2003), Model II](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.67.165316). Equation (2) derives when that law in fact emerges from the specified paired-site lattice, including the quantitative fixed-\(j\) phase control. Draw \(K\sim{\rm Poisson}(\Lambda)\); if \(K=0\), return \(T=1\); otherwise return the coherent transmission of \(D R_{\phi_{K-1}}D\cdots R_{\phi_1}D\) with independent uniform interior angles. Equivalently write \(X=2/T-1\) and \(x_d=2/T(D)-1=17/8\): start at \(X_0=1\) and use the scalar distributional recursion
\[
 X_{j+1}=x_d X_j+\sqrt{x_d^2-1}\sqrt{X_j^2-1}\cos U_{j+1},
 \qquad U_{j+1}\ {\rm iid\ uniform\ on}\ [0,2\pi] .                 \tag{3}
\]
Use \(T=2/(X_K+1)\). This follows by multiplying the \(SU(1,1)\) traveling-wave matrices: the squared modulus of the new \(\alpha\) is the sum of the two squared magnitudes of its terms plus an interference term, with uniform phase after any prior rotations. The first transition is deterministic. The same derivation applies to independent randomly marked defects of finitely many strengths if each strength has fixed conjugated transfer and gaps are independent of these marks; replace \(x_d\) by the marked value. Phase randomization is a limit of propagation through the stated background, not physical dephasing between defects.

Two exact checks illustrate why the law is not independent attenuation of an intensity through a Poisson count. Conditional on exactly two breaks,
\[
 \frac1T=\frac{353}{128}+\frac{225}{128}\cos U,\qquad
 \frac{64}{289}\le T\le1 .
\]
A second scatterer can therefore return some samples almost to perfect transmission, rather than always multiplying the one-defect \(16/25\) factor. For \(j\ge2\) the conditional law is non-atomic: the first random transition (\(j=2\)) is nondegenerate, and beyond it \(X_j>1\) a.s., so the next arcsine conditional distribution again has no atom. In the mixture, the atom at \(1\) is exactly \(e^{-\Lambda}\), and the atom at \(16/25\) is exactly \(\Lambda e^{-\Lambda}\). In particular the upper median of \(T\) stays exactly 1 for \(\Lambda\le\log2\), even though every fixed positive \(p\) produces almost-sure attenuation as length tends to infinity. This scaling compares a *family* of devices, not fixed \(p\) with \(K\) kept finite.

**Commensurate control.** If \(v=1\) is substituted throughout, \(k=2\pi/3\) and the background \(BB\) rotation has order three. The same conditional Fourier proof on this finite subgroup gives a uniform internal gap phase on the three residues, not Haar on the full circle. Direct rational-metric replay gives \(T(D)=3/4\) and \(T(DR_\theta^bD)=3/7,1,3/7\) for \(b=0,1,2\pmod3\). Thus two defects have exactly unit transmission with limiting conditional probability \(1/3\), and the atom at 1 in the Poisson mixture is at least \(e^{-\Lambda}(1+\Lambda^2/6)\). This is a control of the role of commensuration (related low-density phase anomalies are known), not an exception to the stated irrational example.

The moments of the limiting law give independent checks:
\[
 E(1/T)=\tfrac12[1+\exp(9\Lambda/8)],\qquad
 E[-\log T]=\Lambda\log(25/16).
\]
The first follows by averaging (3) at each step. For the logarithm, the \(\alpha\) update is a leading term of modulus \(|\alpha_d\alpha|\) times \(1+\rho e^{iU}\) for some \(|\rho|<1\). The mean of \(\log|1+\rho e^{iU}|^2\) is zero by the convergent power series or Jensen's circle mean identity, so the conditional means add exactly per defect. In these formulas moments of the original finite-\(N\) distributions also converge: \(\|M\|\le2^K\) and \(1/T\le4^K\) give uniform integrability from the exponential moments of the binomial count when \(Np_N\) stays bounded.

As a standard low-density consistency check, the projective stationary measures of the fixed-\(p\) transfer chain tend weakly to Haar as \(p\downarrow0\). Any subsequential limit is invariant under \((I+R_{\theta*})/2\) on projective space, hence invariant under the irrational rotation. The background tiles contribute zero to the Furstenberg integrand, so \(\gamma(p)/p\to\int\log\|D u\|\,du=\log(5/4)\), where \(u\) is the unit direction with Haar law and the integral uses the same circle mean. This matches known leading low-density Lyapunov reasoning; it is not claimed as a new general first-order formula.

**Unavoidable discrimination ceiling.** For any test of the defect-free model versus the specified alternative that observes only a statistic whose conditional law with \(K=0\) is the same as under the null, including the matched transmission or jointly noisy readout, a test of size at most \(\alpha\) on \(m\) independent length-\(N\) samples has power at most \(1-(1-\alpha)(1-p)^{Nm}\). With no defects in all samples the null law is exactly present in the alternative. In the ideal noiseless Poisson limit the ceiling \(1-(1-\alpha)e^{-m\Lambda}\) is attained by rejecting when some \(T\ne1\) and randomizing on the all-unit outcomes. A hard finite error blurs this ideal test; the two exact finite calibrations below do not rely on exact equality of observed transmission.

## 3. Finite probability certificate for the locked 400-block benchmark

Fix \(N=400\), \(p=1/400\), the decision \(T_{\mathrm{reported}}<0.80\), and independently certified absolute readout error \(\le0.02\). This hard bound does not require stochastic independence of the readout errors. The repeat samples must have independent disorder draws, not repeated readouts of one chain.

At \(E=0\), \(K=0\) gives \(T=1\), and \(K=1\) gives \(T=16/25\). For \(K=2\), an inner gap of length \(g\) contains \(b\) \(BB\) cells with conditional binomial law. There are exactly \(N-1-g\) defect-position pairs with that gap. Up to sign and external rotations compute \(T=T(D R_\theta^b D)\) by exact rational arithmetic. Thus the conditional mass of the event \(T(0)\le0.73\) is an exact sum
\[
 \frac{1}{\binom N2}\sum_{g=0}^{N-2}(N-1-g)2^{-g}
  \sum_{b=0}^{g}\binom gb\,{\bf 1}_{\{T(DR_\theta^b D)\le0.73\}} .             \tag{4}
\]
The included rational replay yields \(0.786229681\ldots\) (rounded). No Haar substitution is used for this finite-N decision. All \(K=1\) events and the subset (4) of \(K=2\) events already give detection probability \(>0.5131\) per chain and \(>0.9438\) with four independent chains (reject if any crosses threshold). Other \(K\) events are not needed for these lower bounds.

### Deterministic energy margin for those events

Take \(e=|E|\le10^{-5}\) and work first in \(C_0\) coordinates. One site changes in operator norm by \((5/4)e\), since the perturbation \(C_0 E_{11} C_0^{-1}\) is rank one of that norm. The onsite \(A\) and \(B\) transforms at zero have norms 2 and 1. Thus each two-site tile changes by at most \(((5/4)e)(4+(5/4)e)<6e\). Its unperturbed norm is 1 for \(AA,BB\) and 2 for \(AB\). Telescoping a product or expanding by subsets of changed factors gives a product error bound \(2^K[(1+6e)^N-1]\). The correct lead normalization at \(E\) is a further similarity by \(F_E=C_E C_0^{-1}\), satisfying \(\|F_E-I\|\le e\): its only changed column is \((s(E)/s(0)-1,(c(0)-c(E))/s(0))^T\); \(|s'(E)|=|c(E)|/(2s(E))<0.4\) and \(|c'(E)|=1/2\) over the larger interval used below. Hence \(\|F_E\|\|F_E^{-1}\|\le(1+e)/(1-e)\), with an additional change from the zero-energy similarity of at most \(2e/(1-e)\cdot 2^K\). Overall the error in the full flux-normalized matrix is bounded by
\[
 2^K\left[\frac{1+e}{(1-e)(1-6Ne)}-1\right]<2^K(0.025),
\]
using the binomial inequality \((1+a)^N\le (1-Na)^{-1}\) for \(Na<1\). At \(K=0\) the observed transmission remains above 0.80, regardless of the readout error in the allowed box; at \(K=1\) the norm remains above 1.95 and forces a reported value below 0.80. For the certified \(K=2\) subset, \(T(0)\le0.73\) implies \(\mu(0)>1.77\) by (1). With \(4(0.025)\) of possible norm change one still has \(\mu(E)>1.67\), also forcing reported transmission below threshold. Thus the four-chain benchmark's deterministic zero false-positive gate and power lower bound survive this energy error. The readout and energy tolerances are dimensionless contractual requirements, not independently achieved instrumental precision.

## 4. A larger stochastic detuning window without invoking the Haar approximation

A second check widens the energy range by 50-fold at \(N=400\), with a small allowed false-positive probability. The scaling \(|E|=O(N^{-1/2})\) follows here from a two-moment bound rather than worst-case amplitude expansions. This section tests the same two classes of disorder when measured slightly off their nominated resonance. It does **not** imply that an intact pairing model at a fixed detuned energy has no thermodynamic attenuation.

For any **fixed energy chosen independently of the disorder**, \(e=|E|\le5\times10^{-4}\), use the correct \(C_E\) coordinates and define \(G=C_E L_0(E) C_E^{-1}\). The background \(AA\) tile is \(F=G^2=E G-I\) by Cayley–Hamilton. The \(BB\) tile is exactly a rotation. The similarity and single-site perturbation bounds just proved give \(1.99<\|G\|<2.01\). For independent fair background tiles let \(\overline X_n=E_{\rm disorder}(M_n M_n^T)\); appending another tile acts by the positive linear map
\[
 \mathcal L_E(X)=\tfrac12 F X F^T+\tfrac12 R_{2k}X R_{2k}^T .
\]
Decompose \(X=aI+Z\) with \(a=\operatorname{tr}X/2\) and \(b=\|Z\|\) in the normalized Frobenius metric \(\|Z\|^2=\operatorname{tr}(Z^2)/2\); \(Z\) is traceless symmetric and \(a\ge0\) in the application. On the traceless block the average of \(I\) and the action \(Z\mapsto R_{2k} Z R_{2k}^T\) has norm \(|\cos 2k|<0.29\), because the action rotates the two traceless coordinates by \(4k\). Replacing the first identity action by \(X\mapsto F X F^T\) changes the averaged operator by at most \(\tfrac12(2\|E G\|+\|E G\|^2)<3e\); thus all radial/traceless off-diagonal couplings are bounded by \(3e\) and the traceless norm by 0.31.

For the radial input \(I\), use \(\operatorname{tr}G=E\) to get
\[
 \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(F F^T)-1
     = E^2(\|G\|_F^2/2-1).
\]
Since \(\|G\|_F^2=\|G\|^2+\|G\|^{-2}<4.4\), and only one of the two background choices contributes, the radial coefficient in \(\mathcal L_E\) is at most \(1+e^2\). Consequently
\[
 a'\le(1+e^2)a+3 e b,\qquad b'\le3e a+0.31b .
\]
Start at \(a_0=1,b_0=0\). The two bounds \(a_n\le L^n,\ b_n\le(3e/0.69)L^n\) hold by direct induction with \(L=1+15e^2\), because \(1+9/0.69<15\) and \(L\ge1\). In particular, for a background segment of \(g\le N\) cells,
\[
 E_{\rm disorder}(1/T-1)\le \frac{(1+15e^2)^g-1}{2}\le
 B_g:=\frac{15g e^2}{2(1-15N e^2)} .                                     \tag{5}
\]
Only (5) for fixed independently controlled \(E\) is used; it provides no supremum-across-scanned-energy confidence or independence between shots on the same chain.

At \(p=0\), a threshold crossing entails \(T<0.82\), so Markov bounds its chance by \(B_N/(1/0.82-1)\), and the size in eight chains by eight times this. At \(p=1/400\) condition on \(K=1\) and its position. The two background segments have total length \(N-1\). With probability at least \(1-B_N/[(1/T(1.08))-1]\), both segment norms are at most 1.08, by (5) and a union bound (here \(T(\mu)\) means the norm expression in (1)). The defect itself has norm \(\|R_k G\|=\|G\|>1.99\). For determinant-one matrices \(\|B^{-1}\|=\|B\|\); hence the full norm is at least \(\|R_k G\|/(\|B_\mathrm{left}\|\|B_\mathrm{right}\|)>1.99/(1.08)^2\), which forces a threshold crossing with the same external readout allowance. This lower bound makes no claim about \(K\ge2\) at this energy setting. The exact rational certificate for eight independent chain realizations at \(N=400\) gives **false alarm below 0.028 and power above 0.95** uniformly over each fixed \(|E|\le5\times10^{-4}\) chosen independently of the disorder. The scope is to the specified model/contacts and hard readout resolution.

## 5. Evidence boundary

The full distribution distinguishes a tuned dimer resonance from its specified rare-pairing-break alternative. Positive Lyapunov attenuation in one infinite chain and finite-sample probability of low transmission are not equivalent; the limit law explains why a healthy-looking sample can be produced by a locally nontransparent disorder mechanism, and why a process with known phase correlation cannot be represented by multiplying mean intensity attenuations. One should not infer a diagnosis of generic localization from a fixed low-transmission sample. Unknown lead matching, absorption, dephasing, choice of probe energy after seeing outcomes, unequal onsite values or hopping outside the contract, and different defect processes are not covered. No device or archive science data have been fit; the rerunnable numbers are conditional, exact model results.
