# Primary studies and precise overlap

- [Dunlap, Wu and Phillips, *Absence of Localization in a Random-Dimer Model*, PRL (1990)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.65.88) introduced the correlated two-site binary model and resonant delocalization. [Jitomirskaya, Schulz-Baldes and Stolz, *Delocalization in random polymer models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0405024) study commuting critical transfer matrices, the quadratic vanishing of the Lyapunov exponent at generic critical energies and dynamical lower bounds, even though related models exhibit pure point spectrum. The defect-free resonance and the distinction of spectral and dynamical/localized transport properties are not new here.
- [Farchioni and Grosso, *Electronic transport for random dimer-trimer model Hamiltonians* (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.56.1170) study mixtures of random monomers, dimers, and trimers and transmittance scaling. The use of local correlation violations to change transmission is not new here.
- [Schulz-Baldes, *Low density expansion for Lyapunov exponents*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0607027) (2006) treats dilute strong impurities rather than weak amplitudes, including distinct rational quasi-momenta. The dilute first-order Lyapunov coefficient and the existence of rational-phase anomalies are prior. The calculation here concerns the **finite expected defect-count law**, not a new generic low-density Lyapunov expansion. [Furstenberg, *Noncommuting Random Products* (1963)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1963-0163345-0) supplies the almost-sure positivity/irreducibility result for the fixed-positive-density comparison.
- **Closest mathematical/physical overlap:** [Vagner, Markoš, Moško and Schäpers, *Coherent resistance of a disordered one-dimensional wire*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.67.165316) (2003; [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0207636v2)), §§II–III Model II, already combines a Poisson count with the same one-channel radial recursion and moments in a random-phase treatment; Eq. (14) is their phase-randomization condition. Their §IV numerically compares the phase treatment with explicit continuum disorder. The limiting distribution is therefore not claimed as novel. The addition is a rigorous derivation from the broken lattice-pair model (with conditional Fourier errors and an exact commensurate countercontrol) and the finite calibrated rejection certificates. [Felderhof and Ford, *Growth of resistance with density of scatterers in one dimensional wave propagation*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01021091) (1986) supplies earlier continuum count/spacing ensembles and resistance moments.
- [Culver, Sathe and Roy, *Scattering expansion for localization in one dimension*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.109.064201) (2024; [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13368)) derives exact one-channel coherent composition formulas and studies long-chain joint log-transmission and reflection-phase distributions in the weak-scatterer expansion. Neither the radial addition rule nor the importance of phase is new here. The law in this note is a finite-number-of-strong-breaks Poisson limit, with the internal phases **derived from conditional microscopic gap combinatorics**, not posited as an independent random phase approximation. [Beenakker et al., *Nonalgebraic length dependence of transmission through a chain of barriers with a Lévy spacing distribution*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.0297) (2008) is adjacent work on random barriers with fluctuating spacings, but on a heavy-tail spacing model.
- [Naether et al., *Experimental observation of superdiffusive transport in random dimer lattices*](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/15/1/013045) (2013) give a physical optical waveguide realization of related binary dimers. Their measured wave-packet spreading is not the matched-lead, energy-resolved stationary transmission test here. [The random-dimer disordered SSH experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05207) is a different lattice and detuning problem. No data from either are reused.
- Recent but distinct diagnostics: [*Anderson Localization: A Density Matrix Approach*](https://doi.org/10.1103/y5kd-7prs) (2026) researches localization length and finite-size convergence in other disordered models, [*Studying energy-resolved transport with wavepacket dynamics on quantum computers*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16180) (2026) reports finite lattice wave-packet diagnostics, and [*Charge transport capacity as a probe of resonances in models of many-body localization*](https://doi.org/10.1103/l7n9-ql9y) (2026) covers interacting finite many-body systems. The present result should not be transferred to any of them without a new model and proof.

The narrow addition is a derived microscopic limit to the known phenomenological Poisson random-phase law, with a conditional Fourier bound, interference and atoms, plus exact finite-length high-power readout bounds under separately stated detuning contracts. This limited review does not constitute an exhaustive priority claim. The package contains original proofs and code, no third-party raw files or model fit to measured devices.
