# Independent audit: rare breaks in a resonant random dimer

**Result.** The exact microscopic joint dilute limit, one-/two-defect law, fixed-density Lyapunov claim for \(0<p<1\), calibrated 400-block discrimination calculation, and stochastic widened energy theorem all check. An independent exact integer-count/weighted-metric replay agrees. The random-phase-plus-Poisson count law itself is known in continuum models; the new-looking contribution is deriving it rather than proposing it for this discrete correlated model, and the specific finite-error guarantees. This is no claim about actual samples or generic localization diagnostics.

## Microscopic transfer and the order of limits

With hopping \(+1\), an onsite \(v\) transfers by \(L_v(E)=[(E-v,-1);(1,0)]\) on \((\psi_j,\psi_{j-1})\). Encountering \(A\) before \(B\) gives \(L_B L_A\), not the reverse. Let \(c=(E-B)/2\), \(s=\sqrt{1-c^2}>0\), \(C=[(s,0);(-c,1)]\). Then \(CL_B C^{-1}\) is the usual planar rotation of angle \(k\) with cosine \(c\) and sine \(s\). Its normalized circular eigenvectors give equal-current left/right traveling-wave coordinates, the common scale canceling in identical leads. For real \(M\in SL_2\) in these normalized coordinates the complex two-mode transfer has paired diagonal components \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\), squared moduli balance \(|\alpha|^2-|\beta|^2=1\), and through probability \(T=|\alpha|^{-2}=4/(2+\|M\|_F^2)\). Equivalently \(T(\mu)=4\mu^2/(1+\mu^2)^2\), \(\mu=\|M\|_{\rm op}\ge1\). In raw coordinates the needed norm squared of a product \(P\) is \(\operatorname{tr}(P^T QP Q^{-1})\), \(Q=C^TC\); the independent checker uses this rational formula at zero.

At \(E=0\) and \(B=6/5\), \(AA=-I\), \(BB=R_\theta\) with \(\cos\theta=-7/25,\sin\theta=-24/25\). It is irrational modulo \(\pi\): \(2\cos\theta=-14/25\) could not be a rational algebraic integer of the form \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) for a root of unity. The raw \(AB\) matrix is the parabolic shear \([(-1,6/5);(0,-1)]\), and its flux form \(D\) has largest singular value 2. Thus \(K=0\) gives exact \(T=1\); \(K=1\) gives \(16/25\) for any placement/background. These do not presume dephasing.

For fixed \(0<p<1\), \(D\) powers grow unboundedly, proving noncompactness without any averaging argument. Positive probability of \(BB\) adds an irrational rotation, which has no finite invariant subset of the real projective line. This is strong irreducibility of the generated group. Integrability is automatic for the finite alphabet. Standard [Furstenberg](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1963-0163345-0) positivity therefore yields a positive almost-sure Lyapunov exponent \(\gamma\) **per two-site tile**. The matched probability has \(-\log T/(2N)\to\gamma\); no uniform poor transmission in every finite word follows. The endpoint \(p=1\) cannot be included: the deterministic \(D^N\) has only polynomial norm growth. Nothing here establishes spectral type from a single energy.

## Joint Haar/Poisson law and known random-phase formulas

Condition on \(K=j\) for fixed \(j\ge1\). The locations are equiprobable \(j\)-subsets, hence all \(j+1\) spacings including the end gaps are uniform weak compositions of \(N-j\). Given them, all background types remain iid with \(AA,BB\) probabilities \(1/2\). For a gap of length \(g\) its angular Fourier coefficient at integer frequency \(m\) is \(z_m^g\), \(z_m=(({-1})^m+e^{im\theta})/2\). Gaps' phases are conditionally independent. Nonzero \(m\) entails \(|z_m|<1\). Any particular gap size \(l\) has probability \(\binom{N-l-1}{j-1}/\binom Nj\le j/N\); thus the absolute expected coefficient for any nonzero multi-frequency is at most \(j/[N(1-|z_m|)]\) using one of its active coordinates. For \(j=0\), there is only one gap, of length \(N\). This proves conditional joint weak convergence to independent Haar marks even including both end gaps. To include locations, multiply by any bounded continuous location test: the same nonzero-mode bound is multiplied by its supremum norm; the zero mode gives convergence of locations to sorted independent uniforms. Thus the location point process is asymptotically Poisson with independent mark phases. Endpoint rotations are relevant for the full normalized transfer, but disappear from the scalar \(T\); for \(j\ge1\) exactly \(j-1\) Haar factors remain. Truncating a uniformly tight binomial count and using \(Np_N\to\Lambda\) proves the Poisson mixture. This is weak convergence, not total variation from a finite discrete angular law to a continuous one.

For two defects, reduce \(D\) by singular-value rotations to \(S=\operatorname{diag}(2,1/2)\). Then
\[
\|SR_\varphi S\|_F^2=(16+1/16)\cos^2\varphi+2\sin^2\varphi.
\]
Consequently \(1/T=353/128+(225/128)\cos U\) for uniform \(U\), and \(T\in[64/289,1]\). For general \(j\), \(X=2/T-1=2|\alpha|^2-1\) obeys the usual random-phase series rule \(X'=x_dX+\sqrt{x_d^2-1}\sqrt{X^2-1}\cos U\), \(x_d=17/8\); new phase is independent of previous transfer by the intermediate Haar factor. The conditional law is non-atomic for \(j\ge2\) because the first arcsine step is nondegenerate, and \(X>1\) almost surely each subsequent step. Therefore the only atoms are \(T=1\) and \(16/25\) with exactly their zero-/one-defect Poisson weights. Their reported statistical median and ceiling follow. The mean resistance formula uses \(E[X\mid K=j]=x_d^j\); the mean log formula uses the vanishing circle average of \(\log|1+\rho e^{i\theta}|^2\) for \(|\rho|<1\). Uniform integrability follows from \(1/T\le4^K\) and \(\sup_N E[16^K]<\infty\) in this scaling. The fixed-\(p\) stationary law weakly approaches Haar as \(p\to0\): any limit is invariant under \((I+R_{\theta*})/2\), hence under \(R_\theta\); the Furstenberg integrand only has a nonzero defect term. The circle average gives \(\lim\gamma(p)/p=\log(5/4)\), a known style of low-density calculation.

The power ceiling requires that the conditional law when all samples have \(K=0\) actually equal the null, including the measurement/error law if noisy. In that common component a size-\(\alpha\) test cannot reject with probability more than \(\alpha\). The ceiling \(1-(1-\alpha)(1-p)^{Nm}\) is therefore an upper bound; exact attainment is asserted only for ideal noiseless limiting \(T\) outcomes, not arbitrary finite noisy tests.

At the negative-control onsite \(B=1\), \(BB\) has order 3, and together with \(-I\) its finite angular group has order 6. The nontrivial Fourier modes of this finite group all decay (the step-angle difference generates it), so the long-gap limit is discrete Haar on that group. In projective transmission coordinates only the \(BB\) count mod 3 matters. Exact two-defect values by residues 0,1,2 are \(3/7,1,3/7\) and the one-defect value is \(3/4\). Thus \(K=2\) itself adds an atom at \(T=1\) in the commensurate control. Continuous Haar and exactly two atoms are not universal.

## Finite thresholds and distinct energy assumptions

**Exact \(N=400,p=1/400\), fine deterministic tolerance.** The independent replay tracks counts of words with exactly two \(AB\) blocks by the number of \(BB\) cells strictly between them. Its three-state integer stage recursion does not use the author's gap-summation implementation and is checked separately by exhaustive enumeration for \(2\le N\le7\). The norm is evaluated with the raw rational metric \(Q\); no numerical angle or presumed angular mixing is used in the 400-block gate. The exact mass with \(T_0\le .73\) given two defects is 0.786229681154 (rounded). Combining only that event and all one-defect words gives lower probability 0.513139867671 per chain and 0.943815457199 over four independent chains. Other defect counts can detect too; these are lower bounds, not exact full-power claims.

For the finite offset \(|E|\le e=10^{-5}\), in fixed \(C_0\) coordinates a site changes with norm \(5e/4\); each two-site product changes by less than \(6e\) from the zero-energy unperturbed values (site norms 2 and 1). Expanding the block product difference with \(K\) defects gives \(2^K[(1+6e)^N-1]\), since the background zero-energy norms are one. The correct lead frame is further conjugated by \(F=C_E C_0^{-1}\), and its changed first column has displacement at most \(e\). Hence the combined matrix change is bounded by \(2^K[(1+e)/((1-e)(1-6Ne))-1] < 2^K\cdot .025\), using the norm bound plus the extra zero-energy similarity displacement and a geometric majorant for the binomial expansion. Consequently \(K=0\) forces the reported value above .80 with an absolute error .02; \(K=1\) has \(\mu>1.95\), which forces a report below .80; \(K=2,T_0\le.73\) implies \(\mu_0>1.77\) and \(\mu_E>1.67\), also forcing a report below .80. All final inequalities replay in exact rational arithmetic. This proof is pointwise in the word and permits offsets chosen per word within the fine window.

**Wider moment tolerance.** At fixed \(|E|\le e=1/2000\), let \(G\) be the energy-correct normalized \(A\) site. Then \(1.99<\|G\|<2.01\), and \(S=G^2=EG-I\) since \(\operatorname{tr}G=E,\det G=1\). Background expectation map \(\mathcal L(X)=(SXS^T+R_{2k}XR_{2k}^T)/2\) acts on \(X=aI+Z\), traceless \(Z\) norm \(b=(\operatorname{tr} Z^2/2)^{1/2}\). For the unperturbed identity-plus-rotation average on \(Z\), rotation acts at twice the geometric angle, giving norm \(|\cos2k|<.29\). The replacement of the identity conjugation by \(SXS^T\) has averaged normalized-Frobenius operator error at most \(e\|G\|+e^2\|G\|^2/2<3e\). The radial coefficient from \(aI\) is exactly \(1+E^2(\|G\|_F^2-2)/4<1+e^2\); the linear trace cancels precisely because \(\operatorname{tr}G=E\). Thus \(a'\le(1+e^2)a+3eb,\ b'\le3ea+.31b\), and by induction \(a_n\le(1+15e^2)^n,\ b_n\le(3/.69)e(1+15e^2)^n\). This yields the bound \(E[1/T-1]\le B_g=15g e^2/[2(1-15N e^2)]\) for a segment of \(g\le N\) blocks. Markov yields the stated null false-alarm size given that a report below .80 implies true \(T<.82\). Conditional on one \(AB\), apply Markov separately to both sides and add their lengths, at most \(N-1\). Outside the resulting bad event both norms are at most 1.08. The defect norm exceeds 1.99; determinant one gives reciprocal inverse norm, so the full norm exceeds \(1.99/1.08^2\), forcing the declared threshold even with the readout error. Exact final bounds over eight independent realizations: false alarm at most .027374394926 and power at least .955201122784 (rounded). The wider result is uniform for each fixed energy selected independently of disorder. It does not bound the probability of a crossing at *some* energy in an adaptive scan. Readout errors can be correlated if hard-bounded; the power product requires independent physical disorder draws (or conditional independence at fixed shared energy).

## Primary source scope

- [Dunlap–Wu–Phillips, PRL 65, 88](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.65.88) is the random-dimer resonance antecedent; [Jitomirskaya–Schulz-Baldes–Stolz](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0405024) addresses critical energies and transport in random polymer models. [Schulz-Baldes](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0607027) already derives first-order low-density Lyapunov behavior and uses oscillatory rotations, identifying correlated polymer backgrounds as an adaptation. The weakly mixed fixed-\(p\) check and the stochastic background scaling are not first such investigations.
- The nearest explicit comparator is [Vagner–Markoš–Moško–Schäpers, cond-mat/0207636v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0207636), §§II–III Model II: their continuum dilute wire mixture already uses a Poisson impurity count and the same random-phase resistance transition with conditional and mixture moments. The analytic treatment assumes phase randomization, tests it numerically in §IV, and does not prove this correlated lattice rotation limit. Accordingly neither the Poisson mixture, angular composition rule, associated formal moments, nor the caution against multiplying intensities are standalone new results. [Felderhof–Ford](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01021091) already uses Poisson statistics in a varying-count continuum ensemble and studies low-density resistance. [Khachatrian et al.](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.62.13501) studies mean coherent resistance for random spacings, including finite chains. No matching primary source for this discrete microscopic proof and its concrete calibration gates surfaced.
- All comparisons use the specified matched contacts, unit nearest-neighbor hopping, fixed two-site tile laws, and identical coherent noninteracting single channel. No empirical signal, calibration achievability, arbitrary unknown mechanisms, nonresonant null equivalence, or actual hardware is implied.
