# Independent audit: exclusion of the full-plane branch at size 52

**Disposition.** Pass. No spectral pair of size 52 in \(\mathbb F_{13}^3\) can have a 13-point affine plane in either member, using the already-audited quintic theorem and planar gates. This leaves the nonlayer branch (plane cap at most ten, no uniform pencil above nine under the earlier reductions). It does not settle size 52 or the full conjecture.

## From the audited reductions to a finite pair of graphs

The earlier audit proves that a full plane forces four full parallel layers in both members, in linked dual coordinates. Opposite projections are injective. Each layer is a graph on \(\mathbb F_{13}\) with line cap four. The verified mixed moments through degree five isolate \(\sum f,\sum x f,\sum f^2\) for each graph, by multiplying by the cubic indicator in its layer coordinate before summing. In particular \(\sum f=\sum x f=0\). The same holds for \(g\), any *distinct* layer of the same member. By the four-Fourier-minor argument and the independently audited 13-point planar cap-four gate, at least 11 shifts \(s\in\mathbb F_{13}\) give \(|\operatorname{im}(f(x)-g(x-s))|\le3\). None have exactly two values (sum zero), and two constant shifts would make the finite difference with a nonzero step of \(g\) constant, forcing an affine \(g\), contrary to cap four. Thus at least ten shifts have exactly three values. A cyclic set of ten or more good positions out of 13 must include a run of four: with \(b\in[1,3]\) bad positions, a max run of three has at most \(3b\le9\) good positions, and with \(b=0\) the run is immediate. Pretranslate the input of \(g\) to assume that shifts \(0,1,2,3\) are among these. This preserves the needed moments and both line caps. This and subsequent transformations are reductions of necessary conditions on a pair, not assumptions about other layers.

Set \(a(x)=f(x)-g(x)\), \(b(x)=f(x)-g(x-1)\). Each is a ternary word. For \(d=b-a\) one has \(d(x)=g(x)-g(x-1)\). The cyclic field identities are
\[
\sum_x d=0,\qquad \sum_x x d=-\sum_x g,\qquad
\sum_xx^2d=-2\sum_x xg-\sum_xg.
\]
Therefore both words have zero zeroth and first weighted moments and have the same second weighted moment (not a zero second weighted moment of either word). Conversely these conditions suffice to recover a cyclic \(g\) with the required first two moments, from \(g(0)=0\) and its 12 recurrence steps; consistency at the wrap follows from \(\sum d=0\). Then \(f=a+g\). The third and fourth differences are \(b(x)+d(x-1)\) and \(b(x)+d(x-1)+d(x-2)\). No higher-moment condition is invoked for the final contradiction.

Allowed normalizations are simultaneous vertical scaling by any \(\lambda\ne0\), separate vertical offsets on \(f,g\), common shear \(cx\), joint cyclic input rotation, and a common constant to fix \(g(0)\). Under the first three, \(a\mapsto\lambda a+c_0\), \(b\mapsto\lambda b+c_0+c\), so first reduce \(a\)'s unordered three-letter alphabet under the affine group, then \(b\)'s separately by translation only. Joint rotation preserves both alphabets, permitting the lexicographically minimum \(a\)-rotation, with no independently chosen \(b\)-rotation. For any shift, the vertical-affine moves scale/translate its output uniformly and hence preserve image size, and they preserve graph line caps and zeroth/first weighted moments. A shifted input leaves \(\sum xf\) unchanged because \(\sum f=0\); the other normalizations add only low field power sums. Even the optional square moment survives, since mixed correction terms are multiples of \(\sum f\), \(\sum xf\), or \(\sum1,\sum x,\sum x^2\), all zero. Square moments are not needed by either successful checker.
