## Remaining branch: geometric degree-seven rigidity

Put D=D(E) and suppose every projective line has ≤8 points of D, all affine plane intersections with E have size ≤10, all affine line intersections size ≤4, and |E|=52. For supposed nonzero homogeneous degree-seven F vanishing on D, plane projective incidence gives sum n_L=14N and sum n_L²=N²+13N for N=|D| (ordered distinct pairs determine one projective line). If n_L≥4 everywhere, sum (n_L−4)(n_L−7)=N²−141N+5124≥615/4. Restriction to a nonfactor projective line is a nonzero binary form of degree seven with ≤7 rational zeros, counted with multiplicity. Only ≤7 distinct factor lines can contribute positively and each gives ≤4. This is impossible.

Otherwise some direction line has n_L≤3. Every four noncollinear planar points in odd characteristic give at least four directions: with a collinear triple, the other point emits three different off-line directions; with no collinear triple and only three directions, each direction is a perfect matching on the four points. Two matchings give a parallelogram via affine coordinates with those two directions as axes; the two diagonals are not parallel in odd characteristic, so cannot furnish the third matching. A slice of ≥5 points would also contain four noncollinear points by line cap four. Thus the thirteen parallel affine planes each have exactly four collinear points, since average occupancy is four. At least five of the thirteen four-point lines share one direction d (there were at most three directions). Their quotient locations modulo d are distinct and form a five-cap: a quotient line with three locations would lift to an affine plane with twelve points. The five-cap yields at least five quotient directions, since each direction supports disjoint pairs and at most two on five points. For each such direction choose two corresponding parallel affine 4-lines. Relative to a fixed quotient displacement, their cross directions are indexed bijectively by a difference set of two four-element height sets, with at least seven elements by Cauchy–Davenport. Along with d, this forces at least eight roots on each of at least five distinct projective lines through d, so each divides F.

Now fix one of the original four-point affine lines ell of direction d. Any selected point off ell spans a unique plane with ell and gives four distinct non-d directions in it. If its direction line is not a factor, restrictions of those five distinct previous factors are five nonzero linear forms, all vanishing at d. Hence a root of multiplicity ≥5 at d and four separate roots contradict degree seven. Every point is therefore in factor planes through ell. Each direction factor corresponds to at most one affine plane containing ell; these planes intersect pairwise only in ell and have at most ten E-points. Total at most 4+7(10−4)=46, contradiction. Multiplying by a nonzero linear power excludes all lower degrees as well. For the partner, degree-j directional moment is zero on D because the parallel planes have four points each and sum t^j=0 for 1≤j≤7. It is thus the zero polynomial; multinomial coefficients are invertible since j<13. Degree zero is 52=0. Symmetry gives both members’ moments.
