# Size 52, nonlayer branch: a nine-uniform pencil forces one fixed shadow on both sides

Step 3 of the size-52 proof. [Step 2](../02-layers/PROOF.md) excludes full planes. This step shows that every hypothetical size-52 spectral pair over \(\mathbb F_{13}^3\) is in exactly one of the following branches:

* Both maximum uniform pencils have size at most eight, and at least one has size eight.
* The maximum uniform pencil of each member has size nine. On each member a nine-uniform pencil gives, after an affine normalization, the same specified 52-point Boolean planar shadow. All mixed moments through degree seven vanish for both members.

The mixed case is excluded. The result depends on two labelled projected-section gates and two exact finite height arguments, not on an unrestricted 3D search.

## Inputs

Let \(D(B)\) be the projective nonzero difference directions of \(B\), and \(U_B\) the projective normals with all plane sizes four. For a spectral pair in dual spaces \(D(E)\subset U_A\) and \(D(A)\subset U_E\). We use the earlier audited size-52 checkpoints for plane cap ten, line cap four, and uniform pencils with at most nine normals. The high-pencil impossibility at exactly ten is an application of [Qihang Wang §§2–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20495v1), as credited earlier. The framework is from [Ferguson–Iosevich §8 and §11](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1), [Fallon–Mayeli–Villano](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02936), and [Kiss–Somlai](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-024-01404-y). The precise finite size-52 claim here is not the prime-11 theorem. No publication priority claim.

Throughout, vertical projection means along a direction \(d\) normal to the full two-dimensional pencil of normals under discussion; its kernel is one-dimensional. The projected pair-member multiplicity in the quotient is kept as an integer function. Projective tangent lines of \(E\)'s differences passing through the point \(d\) correspond to the fourteen quotient slopes, and their \(D(E)\)-sections are disjoint once \(d\notin D(E)\). A single slice of \(E\) above any quotient affine line determines a subset of \(D(E)\) in this corresponding tangent line.

## 1. The fixed K9 shadow

For a nine-uniform pencil the five nonuniform slope families have integer profiles \(g_i\) of mass 52, cap 10, and satisfy
\[
 13 f(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^5 g_i(y-s_i x)-16 .
\]
The field profile coefficients at positive degree \(j\) are \(w_iQ_j(s_i)\) with \(w_i=\bigl(\prod_{k\ne i}(s_i-s_k)\bigr)^{-1}\), \(\deg Q_j\le3-j\); all higher coefficients vanish. This is the previous exact cubic Radon atlas: all three PGL2 bad-slope orbit representatives, simultaneous centering if \(Q_3\ne0\), and otherwise \(Q_2\) times an affine shift used to remove the redundant linear-profile coefficients. Least nonnegative residues are the actual profiles under cap 10. There are 40 normalized integer projections; every reported projection has all 14 line masses independently rechecked. These are possible projections, not spectral pairs.

Each mass-ten line of \(f\) lifts to a 10-point affine planar slice, with line cap four and at most nine determined directions because its tangent line meets \(U_A\) in at most nine. The projection of that slice has point multiplicity at most two: the pencil formula gives \(f\le\lfloor5(10-4)/13\rfloor=2\). Normalize two missing directions of any allowed 10-point planar set to the axes and any two distinct points to \((0,0),(1,1)\). Exhaust slope masks extending their true directions to nine; a search in increasing horizontal coordinate checks every pair and collinearity count. After removing duplication from mask extensions there are 750 distinct normalized point arrays. Project each along every one of its 14 directions that gives multiplicity at most two, and canonically record the 13-entry array modulo affine permutations of its coordinate. The four strings are
\[
 0000120211012,\quad 0001002201211,\quad 0001011020122,\quad 0011011111111 .
\]
The last is the only Boolean type. Matching each mass-ten row of the 40 Radon projections to these exact masks leaves just four normalized projections, all Boolean, scalar homothetic by the direct replay. The representative \(B\), encoded \(13x+y\), is printed by the verifier. Thus \(d\notin D(E)\) and \(E\) is the graph of a function \(h\) on \(B\).

The five bad quotient slopes are \(0,1,2,3,5\), with maximum sizes \(10,7,10,7,9\). The other nine slopes \(4,6,7,8,9,10,11,12,\infty\) each have thirteen size-four sections. A size-nine cap-four plane needs at least eight directions (same normalized gate; zero survivors for at most seven), giving eight on each of the three largest tangent slopes. For each size-seven fibre at the two remaining bad slopes, the Boolean 1D shadow in coordinate \(x\) has affine canonical string \(0000110101111\). This string does not occur among projections of the 3,108 normalized cap-four 7-point arrays with at most six directions when projecting along a missing direction. Thus each of the two medium tangent slopes has at least seven D-points. The sum over the five high slopes is at least \(8+7+8+7+8=38\).

## 2. Quartets, rank and a universal directional lower bound

For a size-four section in any of the nine good quotient slopes, a collinear lift means its heights are affine in the line coordinate. This gives two linear equations in the four heights. A four-point noncollinear set in odd characteristic determines at least four directions (including the case of three collinear and a fourth point). Consequently if a whole tangent line of \(D(E)\) contains at most three directions, all 13 lifted quartets are collinear; call this a low slope. Exhaustive exact rank test: imposing all 26 quartet equations on any three distinct good slopes has rank 49 on the 52 height variables, and their kernel is exactly the three-dimensional space of global affine heights. The latter would put all 52 in a plane. Thus at most two good slopes are low.

We claim \(|D(E)|\ge74\) for any cap-ten, cap-four lift of \(B\), assuming every tangent direction line has at most nine, as above. If not, its nine good slopes contribute at most \(73-38=35\), so at least one is low (else at least \(9\cdot4=36\)).

* Two low slopes: for each pair of slopes impose both full quartet systems and subtract the affine height agreeing on three noncollinear base points. The resulting kernel dimensions are one for 35 pairs and three for one pair. Every nonzero vector up to a common nonzero vertical scalar is covered by choosing its first nonzero nullspace coordinate as 1. There are \(35+(13^3-1)/(13-1)=218\) exact nonaffine lifts counted with their pair anchors. Reconstruct their full \(D\)-sets from all point differences; every model has at least 146 directions. This even exceeds the separate bound 91 for any spectral lift, but only 74 is needed here.

* Exactly one low slope: at least six of the other eight slopes have at most four directions. Otherwise they contribute at least \(1+3\cdot5+5\cdot4=36\) together with the low slope. For each of these six slopes, its set of normalized vertical slopes is contained in a 4-element palette. It is **not** valid to infer collinearity just from an asymmetric base quartet, since three collinear points plus a fourth also give four directions. Instead fix one of the seven affine orbits of 4-subsets of \(\mathbb F_{13}\) as the palette. On each individual base quartet subtract its first height and enumerate all \(4^3\) possible relative heights determined by the slopes from the first point to the other three. Keep only tuples whose other three pair slopes are also in the palette. Enumerate all linear constraints on these tuples that annihilate constant and linear heights on this fibre: equivalently, after eliminating the line through its first two heights, test every projective linear functional on the remaining two residual heights. This is a necessary relaxation, with no assumption about the shape of a four-point set. All actual palettes are covered by the seven affine representatives because the stencil annihilates both constant/linear shifts and is homogeneous in height: changing the palette by \(u\mapsto a u+b\) changes the quartet by \(h(x)\mapsto a h(x)+b x+c\), leaving its linear-stencil constraints unchanged. Each good slope may use a different representative, and no compatibility is presumed from that.

  Exhaust the position of the single full-collinear slope, the choice of six other slopes, and a stencil from each of their palette lists. Add constraints by incremental exact modular elimination; prune at rank 49 since all constraints annihilate affine heights, and deduplicate only identical echelon-basis strings. The replay visits 6,779 nodes and has 2,374 rank-48 leaf tuples. After affine gauge and choice of the sole projective height, these are nine distinct residual height vectors. All nine have \(|D|=63\), but each has a 49-point affine plane, checked by enumerating all 183 normal directions. Thus none has the necessary plane cap ten. This excludes the final case of \(|D|\le73\) without calling the linear relaxation sufficient.

## 3. Duality closes the mixed case; degree-seven moment consequence

For a normal \(v\) of \(E\), let \(q(v)\) be the number of unordered pairs of equal level and \(k(v)=(q(v)-78)/13\ge0\), zero precisely on \(U_E\). Let \(V_E,V_A\) be the respective complements of \(U\). Double counting pairs gives
\[
 \sum_v k(v)=330,\qquad
 \sum_{v\perp d} k(v)=18+t_E(d),
\]
where \(t_E(d)\) counts unordered differences of direction \(d\). For \(d\in V_A\) the latter pair count is zero by spectrality. If the partner \(A\) has maximum uniform pencil at most eight, every projective line has at least six points of \(V_A\). Sum over \(d\in V_A\):
\[
 18|V_A|=\sum_v k(v)|V_A\cap v^\perp|\ge6\cdot330,\qquad |U_A|\le73.
\]
It cannot contain \(D(E)\) from Section 2. Thus if either member has a nine-uniform pencil then both do. As a side remark even at maximum nine the same bound with five gives \(|U_A|\le91\).

For a hypothetical degree-seven nonzero homogeneous \(F\) vanishing on \(D(E)\), the three large slopes all have at least eight roots, so their projective lines are factors. Those lines are concurrent at the missing vertical point \(d\). On any other tangent line through \(d\) not dividing \(F\), these factors give a zero of multiplicity at least three at \(d\), separate from its points of \(D(E)\). Each of the two other high slopes has at least seven non-\(d\) roots, forcing both factors too. Now any of the nine good slopes having a noncollinear quartet produces at least four more roots, forcing its line as a factor (at least fivefold at \(d\) already). There are at most two such slopes, so at least seven slopes have every quartet collinear, contradicting rank 49 even for three. Multiplying by a nonzero linear power covers lower degrees too. Directional moment polynomials of \(A\) through degree seven vanish on \(D(E)\) by uniform equidistribution, so their coefficients all vanish (invertible multinomial coefficients in characteristic 13); exchange the two sides to obtain both mixed moments.

## 4. At least one pencil of size eight

If both have all uniform pencils of size at most seven, the zero (9 points, at most 7 directions, line cap 4) planar gate gives plane cap eight for both. A four-point line in one forces its pencil to have at most two uniform normals: \(104\le 4K+8(14-K)\) yields \(K\le2\). Parallel slices of the partner in the corresponding tangent direction have average four; if any had at least four noncollinear points they would determine at least four directions. Since the partner already has line cap four, all 13 slices must therefore consist of exactly four collinear points in at most two slope directions. Two such lines are parallel; in their common plane their cross-directions number at least seven by Cauchy–Davenport on two four-element sets, giving eight together with their common direction. This forces a pencil of at least eight uniform normals in the original member. Thus both line caps would be three. The zero (8,7,3) gate gives plane cap seven, though the final count only needs line cap three. Every tangent plane has a size-at-least-four parallel slice, noncollinear under line cap three; its four directions give every projective line at least four points of each \(U\). Counting lines through any external point gives \(|U|\ge14\cdot4=56\). But the weighted inequality above, now using at least seven points of \(V_A\) on every line, gives \(|V_A|\ge129\), hence \(|U_A|\le54\), contradiction. Therefore some family has a pencil of eight or nine, and Section 3 rules out nine paired with anything smaller than nine. Here the possible “at least one eight” subcase does not by itself force both maxima eight without an extra argument: the no-nine branch is accurately stated as max at most eight on both and **at least one** reaches eight.

## 5. Reproduction

Run `node check52-nonlayer.js | diff - check52-nonlayer.expected`, with no external dependencies. The source directly enumerates the 10-plane atlas, 7-plane shadow gate, Radon grids, all three-slope ranks, the two-low exact heights, and the palette-linear relaxed solutions. Reported runtimes are for V8 (about 16 seconds including all primary passes); no time cutoff is used. The optional K8 pilot restricts to the eight-direction class that can accommodate a 10-point plane of exactly eight directions (all such 10-slice masks are projectively one orbit). At cap 10 the tested K8 mask has 900 normalized Radon projections, 300 compatible with the projected 10-plane atlas, 228 of those Boolean. Booleanity is mandatory if the common pencil was induced by an actual size-10 plane of the partner: its affine normal \(d\notin U_A\) and hence \(d\notin D(E)\). The surviving Boolean projections split by peak sizes 8/9/10 as 60/96/72. This is a positive projected model catalogue, not an exclusion or an atlas of all K8 cases. The optional `--k8` mode is an exploratory control and is not needed for the theorem.

The [next step](../04-both-k9/PROOF.md) excludes the common K9 case. A separate final argument then closes the remaining K≤8 case without a K8 projection atlas.
