# Independent audit: the nonlayer size-52 pencil dichotomy

**Disposition: pass, conditional on the previously audited full-layer exclusion, plane/line caps and \(K\le9\).** The repaired one-low argument is valid; the earlier inference from an asymmetric quartet is not used. Every hypothetical size-52 spectral pair in \(\mathbb F_{13}^3\) either has \(K(E),K(A)\le8\) with at least one equal to 8, or \(K(E)=K(A)=9\), with each nine-pencil giving the displayed Boolean shadow after its own affine normalization and both members having zero mixed moments through degree seven. This is not exclusion of size 52 or 78.

## The repaired palette proof is complete

If a good tangent slope has at most three directions, all its four-point slices are collinear (four noncollinear points in odd characteristic determine at least four, including three-on-a-line cases). Three such slopes enforce rank 49, with precisely the 3D global affine subspace as their kernel. Two low slopes were independently replayed: after quotienting by all affine heights, 35 anchor pairs have dimension one and one pair dimension three, hence 218 projectivized heights. The minimum direction count is 146.

Under a contrary total of at most 73, the good slopes contribute at most 35; if there are no low slopes they contribute at least 36. If exactly one slope is low, at least six of the other eight have exactly four directions, since otherwise their total is at least \(1+3\cdot5+5\cdot4=36\) including the low slope. On a given group of quartets for one such slope, use a single palette of four field values. Any actual palette is in one of seven affine classes. For a local quartet, choose its longitudinal coordinate \(t\) as \(x\) on finite slopes and \(y\) on the vertical quotient slope. The full relative-height enumeration (equivalently, all 169 residual pairs after zeroing the first two heights, and all four allowed first-edge slopes) retains every configuration consistent with that palette. A change of palette by \(z\mapsto\alpha z+\beta\) changes local heights by \(\alpha h+\beta t+\gamma\), so every linear annihilator derived after quotienting by local affine heights is invariant. No assumptions about symmetric base quartets or the rank of their noncollinear lifts are used. The constraint is **necessary**, not sufficient: residual span may contain height pairs not in the raw enumeration, and discarding further constraints cannot invalidate an exclusion.

Gauge by subtracting the unique global affine height passing through any three noncollinear base points. This is an invertible shear on graphs for a fixed height, preserving plane/direction counts, and the selected rows all kill global affine heights. Only identical echelon strings are deduplicated in the original (this may retain duplicates but cannot lose genuine cases), insertion does not modify old pivot rows, and rank 49 before gauge forces affine, so pruning is valid. Branches choose each low slope, each six-of-eight candidate subset and each of their palette stencils. The alternate code forms a true reduced echelon string, uses different gauge anchors at the start, independent local span calculation, and separate branching order; it independently obtains nine classes of relaxed nongauge height vectors from 2,374 retained leaves. Each has exactly 63 directions and a 49-point plane by checking all 183 projective normals, violating cap ten. It also matches all 84 triple ranks, all 218 two-low projective heights and minimum 146. Thus \(D(E)\) has at least 74 directions whenever it has a nine-pencil.

## Weighted partner bound, degree seven and the low case

With \(q(v)=\sum_t\binom{n_{v,t}}2\), \(k(v)=(q(v)-78)/13\) is nonnegative, zero exactly at an equipartition, by convexity and total 52. No assumption of integrality of \(k\) is needed here. An unordered pair is counted by 14 normals, so \(\sum q=14\binom{52}{2}\), giving \(\sum k=330\). On the pencil \(v\perp d\), ordered direction equality is not needed: each **unordered** pair parallel to \(d\) contributes 14 and each other pair contributes one, so \(\sum_{v\perp d} k(v)=18+t_E(d)\). For \(d\notin U_A\) this is 18. Summing over those \(d\) gives \(18|V_A|=\sum_v k(v)|V_A\cap v^\perp|\ge r\cdot330\) if every projective line has at least \(r\) such points. With \(r=6\) it gives the exact \(|U_A|\le73\), contradicting \(|D(E)|\ge74\) in a mixed case. For \(r=5\) it gives \(|U_A|\le91\). This is the weighted identity of Ferguson–Iosevich with a simple inequality instead of their stronger divisibility hypotheses.

For degree seven, three tangent lines with eight nonvertical roots are distinct factors. They concur at the vertical point, and restriction to any other nonfactor line gives multiplicity at least three there even though it is not a direction. The two seven-root sections then force two further line factors. Every good slope with a noncollinear quartet forces yet another via four nonvertical roots and multiplicity at least five. At most two good factors remain available, so seven or more good slopes would have all collinear quartets, contradicting the triple rank. A smaller-degree polynomial multiplies to a nonzero seventh-degree one. Cyclotomic equidistribution gives each opposite member’s directional moment polynomial vanishing on the directions, and the coefficients of its expansion are units because degrees are below 13. The conclusion holds on both sides by working in the verified both-nine branch.

Finally assume both \(K\le7\). The nine-point zero gate gives both plane caps eight. A four-point line in \(E\) forces at most two uniform normals in its pencil from \(104\le4K+8(14-K)\). The partner \(A\)'s 13 parallel slices with that **line direction as normal** partition 52 points and have differences in that pencil. Each slice of at least four points must be collinear or produce at least four directions, so all slices have exactly four collinear points using at most two directions; the source line cap four is applicable here. Select two using the same direction. They are distinct parallel affine lines, and Cauchy–Davenport gives at least seven differences of their two four-element along-line coordinate sets. These give seven distinct cross-directions, since the transverse separation is fixed and nonzero, alongside the shared line direction. Their affine span has an eight-direction projective line in \(D(A)\subset U_E\), impossible. Thus both line caps at most three. Every parallel-plane family then has a noncollinear size-at-least-four slice, hence at least four directions in that projective line. Through a point not in \(U\) the 14 projective lines cover its elements disjointly, giving \(|U|\ge56\), while \(r=7\) in the weighted count gives \(|U|\le54\). This proves some max reaches 8 in the no-nine branch, not that both maxima reach 8.
