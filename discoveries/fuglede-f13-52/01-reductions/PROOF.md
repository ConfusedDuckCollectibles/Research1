# Initial reductions: quintic rigidity and the high-pencil atlas

This is the first step of the [size-52 proof](../README.md). Let E and A be a spectral pair in dual copies of F₁₃³. D denotes projective difference directions; U denotes normals whose 13 affine levels each have four points. Character orthogonality and the cyclotomic relation give D(E) ⊂ U_A and D(A) ⊂ U_E. There are 183 projective normals and 14 per pencil.

The exact planar inputs needed below are that no \(n\)-point planar set of line cap \(c\) determines at most \(d\) directions for
\[
 (n,d,c)=(8,5,4),\ (11,9,4),\ (13,10,4),\ (10,9,3).
\]
Choose two missing directions as axes, then move two distinct points to \((0,0),(1,1)\). Every other point has both coordinates in \(2,\ldots,12\), with no repetitions in either coordinate. Enumerate every set of \(d\) available finite slopes containing 1 and excluding 0, then every compatible point addition in increasing \(x\), checking every new pair direction and the collinearity cap. Input sets with fewer directions have their direction masks extended to \(d\). The printed positive controls are (8,6,4) and (13,11,4); in particular the p11 eight-point six-direction exclusion does not transplant.

## Quintic theorem, with a shorter exceptional case

Check pencil facts first. The 14 affine planes through a line \(\ell\) with \(a\) selected points have total occupancy \(52+13a\). A full pencil of uniform-normal planes would total 56, impossible. A plane with 14 points would determine all 14 directions by the parallel-line pigeonhole principle and hence force the opposite full uniform pencil. Also each line lies in at least one plane with uniform normal, by pigeonholing its partner's points into 13 values of evaluation in the line's direction. This gives plane cap 13 and line cap four. These are prior reductions.

A polynomial of smaller degree would give a nonzero quintic by multiplication with a linear form power. If there is no 4-line, projection along any pencil gives an affine plane with at least four points by averaging, and four noncollinear points in odd characteristic determine at least four directions. With three collinear use the fourth; with no three collinear, three directions would pair the six edges into three parallel perfect matchings, forcing a parallelogram with parallel diagonals, impossible in odd characteristic. For \(N=|D(B)|\), the projective incidence sum \(\sum_L(|L\cap D|-4)(|L\cap D|-5)\) is \(N^2-113N+3660\), using 14 lines through each point, 183 total lines, and one line through any pair. At most five factor lines can contribute, each at most \((13-4)(13-5)=72\), giving 360 against minimum \(1871/4\).

For a 4-line \(\ell\) with direction \(d\), the checked planar \((8,\le5,\mathrm{cap}\,4)\) gate means each nonfactor plane in the pencil holds at most seven. Total 104 forces at least one factor. If at least two distinct factors pass through \(d\), their product forces a double root at \(d\) on every nonfactor projective line through \(d\). Any off-\(\ell\) point in its corresponding affine plane yields four distinct further direction points on that projective line. A nonzero quintic restriction cannot have those six roots counting multiplicity. All points would then be covered by the at most five factor planes, yielding at most \(4+5(13-4)=49\). Therefore **each affine 4-line is in exactly one factor plane through it**, which has exactly 13 points; the remaining 13 planes have exactly seven each by equality of 104 with \(13+13\cdot7\). Counting distinct factors is enough, even if there are repeated factors.

In one such 7-plane use the line as \(y=0\), base abscissae \(T\), centroid \(c\). Each of three other points \((x,y)\) forms the same four-value slope set \((x-T)/y\) (reciprocal-slope coordinates), since the base direction itself is a fifth. Its average is \((x-c)/y\), so the three points and \((c,0)\) are collinear on a transversal. The transversal's direction must be in the four-value slope set, forcing \(c\in T\). All three nonzero \(y\) values are distinct and their ratios stabilize \(T-c\). The stabilizer acts freely on the three nonzero members, hence has order exactly three. The base and transversal are \(c\) plus a scaled \(\mu_3\) orbit. Independently exhaust all 55 normalized base sets \(T\supset\{0,1\}\), placing one off-line point at \((0,1)\) by shear and vertical scaling. Four exact configurations survive; all share the stated orbit shape.

Fix the sole full factor plane \(P_0\) through \(\ell\). The 13 nonfactor planes of the pencil yield 13 three-point rays outside \(P_0\), all centered at the common \(c\). Each is itself a 4-line and thus has a unique assigned full factor plane \(Q\ne P_0\). Any such \(Q\) contains a selected noncentral point on a given ray iff it contains that entire ray, so its assigned rays are exactly its contained rays. If \(r\) rays are assigned to \(Q\), then \(13-3r\) selected points lie on \(Q\cap P_0\), including \(c\), and the line cap forces \(r\in\{3,4\}\). **But \(r=3\) yields an actual 4-line shared by \(Q\) and \(P_0\), violating the uniqueness already proved for every 4-line.** Thus every group size is four, which cannot partition 13. This immediately closes the case.

For a spectral pair, \(M_j(v)=\sum_x(v\cdot x)^j\) vanishes on the opposite direction set for \(1\le j\le5\) by equidistribution, hence by rigidity is a zero polynomial. Its multinomial coefficients are units mod 13, giving every mixed moment through degree five, plus degree zero since \(52=0\bmod13\).

## Size-52 dichotomy and exact \(K=9\) atlas

Let a projective line in \(U_B\)'s ambient plane contain exactly \(K\) uniform points. Preserve multiplicity \(f\) in projection along the perpendicular, with \(L=14-K\) nonuniform line profiles \(g_i\) and label forms \(y-s_ix\) (choose a uniform family vertical). Counting all lines through a planar point gives the integer Radon identity
\[
 13f(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{L}g_i(y-s_ix)-4(L-1).
\]
As in [Ferguson–Iosevich Lemma 8.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1#S8), compare homogeneous degrees modulo 13 using invertible multinomial coefficients for degree \(<13\). For each positive degree \(j\), the profile coefficient is \(w_iQ_j(s_i)\), where \(w_i=(\prod_{k\ne i}(s_i-s_k))^{-1}\) and \(\deg Q_j\le L-j-2\); beyond that degree all those coefficients are zero by Vandermonde.

If \(L\le3\), a nonconstant affine profile has integer mass at least 78, so the nonuniform profiles must be \(0/13\) with four 13s. The constant equation requires \(L=1\) and gives \(f\) the indicator of four full parallel lines (four full affine planes upstairs). If \(L=4\), no affine nonconstant profile is possible; either all four residues are constant zero, already impossible, or all four quadratic leading coefficients are nonzero and \(Q_2\) is constant. The linear/leading ratios are affine in \(s_i\), so one affine translation simultaneously centers the profiles to \(a_i t^2+d_i\). The exact admissible centered constants are \(4\) for square \(a_i\) and \(8\) for nonsquare, with no 13 elevations. Four such constants cannot sum to 12 modulo 13. Thus \(K=10\) is impossible; \(K\ge11\) forces layers and in fact \(K=13\).

**Priority:** [Qihang Wang, arXiv:2609.20495v1 §§2–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20495v1) already has the same quadratic classification and exclusion for ordinary unweighted 52-point sets in the plane. His proof applies without change under the line-mass cap 13 even when the projected point masses are not Boolean. This is a direct application, not a new four-special-directions result. [Kiss–Somlai](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-024-01404-y) gave the prior projection framework and described the three-direction case. [Ghidelli–Kiss–Somlai](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21779) give asymptotic few-special-directions results and general unrestricted multiset constructions; those do not supply this mass-52, cap-10 atlas.

If either member has a full 13-point plane, the (13,10,4) gate and spectrality give its partner \(K\ge11\), so the partner is four-full-layered and therefore itself has a full plane. Reversing shows both layered. Otherwise both \(U\)'s have \(K\le9\) everywhere; in fact both plane caps improve to 10, since 11 points of line cap four already determine at least 10 directions and would force an excluded high pencil of the partner.

For \(K=9\) in the nonlayer branch, \(L=5\) and the cap 10 atlas has **40 normalized exact projections**: all use five nonzero cubics; 16 projections are Boolean, and 24 have exactly two double fibres and 48 singleton fibres. All have some size-10 plane. No projection survives cap 9. The energy signatures in output are \((3,3,4,4,4)\) (12 Boolean tuples), \((3,3,3,3,6)\) (4 Boolean tuples), \((3,4,4,4,5)\) (24 double-fibre tuples), where a profile's energy is \(\sum_t(g(t)-4)^2/26\). Counts are normalized profiles, not affine orbits of 3D lifts. A 10-plane always contains a 4-line in the nonlayer branch because (10,9,3) excludes any such plane with line cap three.

The executable enumerates the three projective \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(13)\)-orbits of five bad slopes by translation, scaling by 2, and reciprocal. For a nonzero cubic leading \(Q_3\) (constant), first translate so the two coefficients of affine \(Q_2\) vanish by simultaneous centering; allow all degree-two \(Q_1\). If \(Q_3=0\) and \(Q_2\ne0\), a translation moves \(Q_1\) by \(2Q_2\) times an arbitrary affine polynomial, so one can normalize \(Q_1\) to a constant when \(Q_2\) is linear or to a multiple of \(s^2\) when \(Q_2\) is constant. In both cases all profiles must be genuinely nonconstant: since cap 10 <13, a residue-constant profile is uniform, contrary to its being a bad slope. The case \(Q_3=Q_2=0\) has no allowed affine nonuniform profile. For each positive-degree coefficient choice, exhaust all mass-52 profile constants with sum \(16\bmod13\) and check all 169 Radon values. Surviving projected arrays are independently verified by all 14 line-mass profiles. Replays at caps 11 and 12 are extra projection controls, not claims about the nonlayer spectral branch.
