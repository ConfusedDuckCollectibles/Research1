## Analytic checks

Nonzero modes on the square torus with periods $2\pi,2\pi$, unit gravity and fixed $\alpha>0$. Let $f(r)=\sqrt{r(1+\alpha r^2)}$, $\omega_r=f(r)\sqrt{\tanh(rh)}$, $w=\omega_1$, $F=\omega_{r_3}-\omega_{r_2}-w$, and $r_1=1,r_2=|(m,b)|,r_3=|(m+1,b)|$ for integers $m\ge0,b\ge1$.

- **Global sign:** $S=(\omega_{r_1}+\omega_{r_2})/\omega_{r_3}$ strictly increases. For each $r_i<r_3$, the log derivative of the corresponding positive term is $r_i/\sinh(2hr_i)-r_3/\sinh(2hr_3)>0$, since $x\cosh x-\sinh x$ starts at zero and increases strictly. This includes $r_1=r_2$. Therefore a root is equivalent to $S(0^+)<1<S(+\infty)$, strictly, and at that root $F'=-\omega_{r_3}S'<0$. There is no assumption of global $F'<0$ away from roots.
- **Support:** On a compact positive-depth interval $I$, the high-mode corrections $\omega_r-f(r)$ and their first $h$ derivatives are $O_{I,\alpha}(b^{5/2}e^{-2h_- b})$ uniformly in $r\ge b$ once $b$ is large; $r^{5/2}e^{-2h_-r}$ is eventually decreasing. This gives $F'=-w'+o(1)$, uniformly for every $m$, with $w'>0$ bounded below. Let $M=m+\tfrac12$. From $r_3-r_2=2M/(r_3+r_2)$, $r_3/r_2\le2$ and $f'(r)\asymp_\alpha\sqrt r$, the deep difference is $\asymp_\alpha M/\sqrt{r_2}$. For small $|F|$ relative to $\min_I w$ that difference is bounded above and below by positive constants. If $m\ge b$, it grows at least as $\sqrt m$, impossible for large $b$; otherwise $r_2\asymp b$ and $m+1\asymp_{I,\alpha}\sqrt b$. Also for each fixed small $b$ the phase tends uniformly to $+\infty$ as $m\to\infty$, so bounded phases involve only finitely many $m$.
- **Count and rate:** In the upper support band $r_i=b+O_I(1)$; the mean value theorem with $f'(r)=\tfrac32\sqrt{\alpha r}(1+O_\alpha(r^{-2}))$ gives $F=\kappa M/\sqrt b-w(h)+O_{I,\alpha}(b^{-1})$, $\kappa=\tfrac32\sqrt\alpha$. Necessary and sufficient grid windows differ by $O(b^{-1})$ at their endpoints, while the grid spacing is $\kappa/\sqrt b$. The count on the *closed* $I$ is $(w(h_+)-w(h_-))\sqrt b/\kappa+O(1)$, including possible endpoint roots. Comparing adjacent phases in this band gives a strict difference $+\kappa/\sqrt b+O(b^{-1})$. This verifies distinctness of $m$-roots **on a fixed compact for sufficiently large $b$**. Counts across $b$ are counts of pairs, not claimed distinct depths. For a target interior $h_0$, choose $b$ as the nearest integer to $(\kappa M/w(h_0))^2$ as $m\to\infty$; the mesh in $b$ gives mismatch $O(1/b)$. The uniform negative derivative and opposite endpoint signs ensure a root at distance $O(1/b)$.
- **Baire and metric:** Each nonempty sublevel $\{|F|<t_b\}\cap I$ with $t_b\to0$ has diameter at most $2t_b/c_I$ once $b$ is large; there are $O(\sqrt b)$ candidates. The tails of the summed $s$-powers converge to zero for $t_b=e^{-b}$ and every $s>0$, hence dimension zero. At $t_b=b^{-p}$ total lengths are summable when $p>3/2$; a.e. lower bound follows by Borel–Cantelli and the finite remaining exceptions after excluding exact roots at that depth. Exhaust compacts and use a countable $p\downarrow3/2$ for all $p>3/2$. For each $B$, the union of open sublevels over $b\ge B,m\ge0$ is dense by the construction even for arbitrary positive $t_b$. Its limsup is residual. Group any finite unbalanced signed frequency sum by distinct radii: the constant deep-water sum is followed by the nonzero exponential from the smallest radius with nonzero grouped coefficient, since $\sqrt{\tanh(rh)}=1-e^{-2rh}+O(e^{-4rh})$. It is nonidentically-zero analytic, hence has countable zeros. The union over finite integer signed lists is meager and null; removing it gives $0<|F|<e^{-b}$ infinitely often and still a residual set. This does not exclude balanced equal-shell relations.

## Coupling and formal/scalar distinction

For $\lambda_i=r_i\tanh(hr_i)$, $E_i=\sqrt{\lambda_i/(2\omega_i)}$, $\rho_i=\omega_i/\lambda_i$, first differentiate harmonic extension at the displaced boundary. The first-order boundary trace correction is $-\eta G_0\psi$, which gives $G_1(\eta)\psi=-G_0(\eta G_0\psi)-\nabla\cdot(\eta\nabla\psi)$. Thus $H_3=\frac12[\psi G_1(\eta)\psi]_0$; capillary potential is even in $\nabla\eta$. The three $\eta$ assignments at momenta $k_1,k_2,-k_3$, with $\psi$ phases $-i,-i,+i$, give the coefficient divided by $E_1E_2E_3$:
$$
 C=\rho_1\rho_2(m+\lambda_1\lambda_2)
   +\rho_1\rho_3(m+1-\lambda_1\lambda_3)
   +\rho_2\rho_3(Q-\lambda_2\lambda_3),\qquad Q=k_2\cdot k_3.
$$
The two $\psi$ placements per term cancel the factor $1/2$, not the signed phases. Collect the low-$\lambda$ products as $-\rho_1\lambda_1(w+F)=O(1)$. From the cross product of size $b$, $Q-r_2r_3=-b^2/(Q+r_2r_3)$. In the near-resonant band $Q+r_2r_3=2b^2+O(b)$, $\rho_{2,3}=\sqrt{\alpha b}(1+O(b^{-1}))$, and $M/\sqrt b=(w+F)/\kappa+O(b^{-1})$. Inserting these and $\rho_1 w=1+\alpha$ gives
$$
 C/b=4/3+5\alpha/6+O_{I,\alpha}(|F|+b^{-1}).
$$
Since $E_2E_3\sqrt b\to1/(2\sqrt\alpha)$, the canonical coefficient $H_{123}=E_1E_2E_3 C$ is positive and of order $\sqrt b$ at the large roots and along the indicated near-resonances.

For the scalar obstruction it suffices to require that restrictions to finitely supported data solve the coefficientwise cubic homological equation. Canonical $a_k=(\eta_k+i\psi_k/\rho_k)/(2E_k)$ are independent complex coordinates of the real phase space: reality of $\eta,\psi$ does not impose $a_{-k}=\overline a_k$. Set only the three $a_{k_j}$ nonzero, with values $\langle k_j\rangle^{-s}e^{i\theta_j}$. Norm is $\sqrt3$. Extract the $(1,1,-1)$ Fourier coefficient on this three-angle torus; degree exactly three isolates the one monomial times $\prod_j\langle k_j\rangle^{-s}$. A continuous homogeneous cubic is bounded on the radius-$\sqrt3$ ball by continuity at zero and scaling (not compactness). Thus the coefficient is at most $O_s(b^{2s})$. The diagonal quadratic Hamiltonian acts on the monomial by $\pm iF$, forcing coefficient magnitude at least $c\sqrt b\,e^b$ on the residual sequence. This already contradicts the finite-mode restrictions; no infinite-dimensional Poisson bracket need be defined. The same rules out a $C^3$ scalar with such a cubic Taylor coefficient. It does not forbid a formal coefficient array or measurable dependence on $h$. Mere Borel measurability in amplitudes is insufficient: paste the finite-support polynomials on the countable union of finite-coordinate subspaces and zero off it, yielding a Borel homogeneous function but not a continuous polynomial or a generator. This does not exclude partial or quasi-resonant transformations, nor address PDE solutions.
