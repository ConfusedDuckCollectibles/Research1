# A three-ray compact plasma screen can match a point-mass FRB echo pair on an octave
Research1, 25 November 2026 UTC. Conditional one-screen model theorem; not an astronomical fit, classification, or abundance result. Independently checked; not peer reviewed.

## Observable and result
Freeze a point source at one position and an unmagnetized, lossless, scalar, static thin cold-plasma screen in Euclidean paraxial optics. Compare a vacuum gravitational point mass at one nonzero dimensionless impact y, to the plasma lens. The observable is the two leading ray terms of the unresolved coherent field: differential phase over a full octave, differential group delay, their voltage-amplitude ratio, and relative Morse phase. Allow a frequency-independent unknown common amplitude (the intrinsic unlensed burst flux is not observed); the two-ray ratio also eliminates any common phase, including phase not representable by a time shift plus standard dedispersion. Do not allow arbitrary chromatic gain in a claim about calibrated intensity. No measurement of resolved image angles, position changes, intrinsic spectral phase, Faraday rotation/absorption, or multiple independent source positions is assumed.

**Constructive theorem.** For every finite y>0 and nonzero point-mass delay, there exists a nonnegative smooth compact 2D electron column giving exactly three nondegenerate real stationary rays over the closed octave. The minimum and saddle exactly match all the relative gravitational observables just listed at every frequency. The third is a maximum, with voltage amplitude at most 1/(K−1) uniformly for any finite K≥200, and can be made arbitrarily faint relative to either desired ray with no change in their relative observables. The column support radius and sup norm can stay bounded independent of K in the chosen normalized coordinates, but the Hessian is not uniformly bounded. At y=1 an exact control uses 1/100 common **intensity** gain; K=4096 makes the maximum less than 0.6% in voltage relative even to the weaker primary (intensity ratio below 0.000036), in geometric optics.
 
**Complement.** No single smooth compact cold-plasma screen, including this one, has an exact full-wave intensity proportional by a fixed nonzero constant to that of a vacuum point mass of finite y>0 on any open interval. For each finite K, a uniform finite-wave approximation by the three stationary terms has an explicit O(C(K)/Λ) bound in the high-phase family below; it is not an arbitrarily close full-wave statement for a fixed radio frequency range and fixed measured delay.

## Lens conventions
Write ρ=ω/ω_min ∈[1,2], u=(2/ρ)^2∈[1,4]. Thus u=1 at the high end; plasma-column unit uses ω_ref=2ω_min. Angular lens coordinate is dimensionless x∈R², radial r=|x|, azimuth θ. Source β=(1,0), Q=|x−β|²/2, S=Q−uN, and phase is ω τ₀ S(x,u). Group lag on a stationary branch is τ₀(S−2u S_u)=τ₀(Q+uN); branch changes in x drop out by stationarity. Ray field amplitude is |det D²S|^(−1/2) and Morse multiplier exp(−iπ m/2) where m is number of negative Hessian eigenvalues. Set τ₀>0 later.

A vacuum point mass uses dimensionless Fermat potential 1/2|ξ−y e₁|²−log|ξ|, positive impact y. Associated positive intensities μ_g^±=((y²+2)/(y√(y²+4))±1)/2, the brighter ray is a minimum and the fainter a saddle. Differential lag is (4GM_z/c³)·[y√(y²+4)/2+log((√(y²+4)+y)/(√(y²+4)−y))] with redshifted mass M_z. At y=1 the fainter/brighter intensity ratio is (3−√5)/(3+√5)≈0.145898, voltage ratio ≈0.381966, Morse multiplier −i. Absolute point-mass calibration is not matched; for y=1 the plasma rays have intensities g μ_g^±, g=1/100.

## 1. Exact global radial construction and all stationary points
Let c_−=441/400, c_+=1/100, k=11/(50 log 4), so 0.15<k<0.16. Let δ=1/1000, a=3/20, b=11/10, R=1+√(23/100)<1.48. Prescribed rays r_−(u)=√(c_−+k log u)−1 ∈[.05,.15], θ=π, and r_+(u)=1+√(c_++k log u) ∈[1.1,R], θ=0. For the radial part n(r), write q=n′ and H_u=r−u q. On the two tracks take
q_−(r)=(r+1)exp[(c_−−(r+1)²)/k], q_+(r)=(r−1)exp[(c_+−(r−1)²)/k],
which solve H_u(r_−)=−1 and H_u(r_+)=+1. For the radial base, S_θ=r sinθ, so any noncentral stationary ray is on one of those axes, at H_u=+1 or −1 respectively. At the origin S_x=−1 due C∞ radial behavior and the noncentral source.

On 0≤r≤2/K set q=Kr. The only crossing there is H_u=−1 at r_c=1/(uK−1)<2/K. Blend smoothly from Kr to 2 on [2/K,3/K], stay at 2 until .04, blend smoothly to q_− over [.04,.045], then use q_− through r=a. Both functions in each join have q>r+1, so H_u<−1 outside the central root and before the start of the active r_− interval. On [.05,.15], q_−>0 implies H_u<1 and the equation H_u=−1 has just the prescribed branch because exp[(c_−−(r+1)²)/k] strictly decreases. Similarly use q_+ on [b,1.49], blend q_+ to zero on [1.49,1.50], then zero until r=2. Throughout [b,1.49], q_+≤r−1, hence H_u≥4−3r>−1; for r>R on that interval q_+<(r−1)/4, hence H_u>1. The cutoff to zero preserves that latter inequality. Outside r=2 a nonincreasing positive column taper produces q≤0 and hence H_u≥r>1. At r≥3 let n=0.

The gap [a,b] must have strict −1<H_u(r)<1 for its interior and all u. Its pointwise condition is L(r)<q(r)<U(r), where L=max(r−1,(r−1)/4), U=(r+1)/4. Let L_ε=(5(r−1)+3√((r−1)²+ε²))/8 with ε=1/100. Then 0≤L_ε−L≤3/800, and U−L≥.425 throughout. Two smooth baseline candidates are Q_lo=.99 L_ε+.01 U and Q_hi=.01 L_ε+.99 U. Join either to the q_− extension over [a,a+δ] (exact q_− to all orders at a), and to q_+ over [b−δ,b] (exact q_+ at b). The extensions are within (L,U) in the open collars: at the lower collar q_−<U after a because q_−/(r+1) strictly decreases from 1/4, while q_−>0>L; in the upper collar q_+>L because r<b and r>1, and q_+<.102<U (use log bound, k>.15). Gluing uses convex C∞ cutoffs, e.g. the normalized cumulative integral s(t) of exp[−1/(t(1−t))] on (0,1), extended as 0/1. Thus q lies within (L,U) everywhere interior after either join. 
The needed integral is exactly \int_a^b q dr = −(k/2)(1−1/4)=−3k/8; this ensures equal branch columns since dn(r_i)/du=k/(2u²) on each. One has \int_a^b L=−273/3200=−.0853125, \int_a^b U=1235/3200=.3859375. Relative to these endpoints the relaxed smoothing costs at most .95(3/800+.01·.53), .95(.01·.53), respectively, and two collars cost ≤.002 in either direction (uniform values between −.25 and .53). Consequently the target −.06<−3k/8<−.05625 lies strictly in between the integrated candidates. Set the *unique convex weight* by their integrals. This is an exact definition, not a numerical fit. A primitive n=1+\int_0^r q dr up to r=2 and its plateau times a smooth decreasing taper from r=2 to 3 completes the radial column. It is ≥.75 up to r=2 (q is nonnegative except possibly in the gap, where q>−.25 and gap width .95), bounded above independently of K (e.g. |q|≤3 before r=1.5, n≤5.5), and smooth across the origin since n=1+K r²/2 there. The global radial screen has exactly three isolated roots described above. In particular the undeflected location x=β is interior and not itself stationary; it has q(1)>L(1)=0.

## 2. Tune both desired magnifications without creating any rays
At either intended root, set h_+=r−1 or h_−=r+1. Its radial Hessian eigenvalue is λ_r=2 h_i²/k>0, by differentiating H. Unperturbed angular eigenvalue is +1/r on the positive axis, −1/r on the negative axis, since \(S_{θθ}=±r\) there and these are critical points. Introduce
A_i(r) = (r²/u_i(r)) [k/(2 h_i² g μ_g^i)−1/r],  u_i(r)=exp[(h_i²−c_i)/k]
evaluated on each ray track. Choose g>0 small enough that A_i>0 throughout both closed tracks (the unperturbed intensities k r/(2 h_i²) have strictly positive minima). Extend this same smooth formula just beyond each end and multiply by a nonnegative C∞ cutoff identically one on the track and supported in two disjoint annuli that avoid the origin. For the y=1 g=.01 control take supports (.049,.151) and (1.099,1.481). On these slightly enlarged supports u_i>.98 by e^(−t)≥1−t, μ_g^−∈(.17,.18), μ_g^+∈(1.17,1.18), and both A_i>0 with \max (A_++A_−)<2000 by the rational inequalities in the replay.

Use χ(v)=1−s(2v−1), and φ(v)=χ(v)\int_0^v [1−s((t−ε_θ)/ε_θ)]dt for v∈[0,2]. It equals v in a neighborhood of zero, is identically zero for v≥1, has 0≤φ≤2ε_θ, and derivative φ′≥−32ε_θ, since 0≤s′≤2e^(4/3)<8. Define the full column
N(r,θ)=n(r)−A_+(r)φ(1−cosθ)+A_−(r)φ(1+cosθ).
The total height perturbation is at most 2ε_θ \max(A_++A_−), chosen <.01; then N≥0 everywhere. Choose ε_θ so \(4·32ε_θ \max(A_++A_−)\) is less than the minimum radius over both supports. For the reference y=1 use ε_θ=10⁻⁷, giving .0256<.049. For all nonzero r,
S_θ / sinθ = r+u[A_+(r)φ′(1−cosθ)+A_−(r)φ′(1+cosθ)] >0 (extend this quotient across axes).
Thus NO off-axis roots can occur; on either axis each cap is identically zero with all radial derivatives, and the radial root equations are unchanged. Mixed Hessian term vanishes on either axis by reflection. The positive-ray angular eigenvalue changes to +(r+u A_+)/r²; the negative-ray one to −(r+u A_−)/r². Together with λ_r these give exactly g μ_g^i and enforce minimum, saddle with multiplier −i respectively. At r_c caps vanish and the central quadratic has Hessian (1−uK)I, so the unique extra ray is a maximum (Morse factor −1), intensity 1/(uK−1)². It is not an additional positive-time-template component assumed absent. The positive-degree requirement +1 at infinity (identity outside support) forces at least one additional ray for any smooth compact screen with a min–saddle pair and only nondegenerate roots; this construction has the minimum total count. If a calibrated class imposes ∥D²N∥_op≤H, every real nondegenerate extra ray has amplitude ≥1/(1+u H). That is a per-ray floor, not a floor for unresolved coherent amplitude when several rays cancel; curvature calibration and sufficient independent resolvability are needed for a discrimination test.

## 3. Relative waveform and observables that distinguish the models
Both tracks have N_i=N₀−k/(2u) for one N₀ by the just-enforced integral. Also Q_i=(c_i+k log u)/2, so
S_i = c_i/2 + (k/2)(log u+1)−u N₀.
Hence ΔS=S_−−S_+=437/800>0 exactly and differential group lag is τ₀ ΔS; choose τ₀ to match the particular point-mass delay. Relative phase is ω τ₀ ΔS−π/2 at every frequency across the octave and ray voltage ratio is √(μ_g^−/μ_g^+). The *absolute* two terms agree with the point-mass pair only up to a common factor (amplitude √g and frequency-dependent unit phase). This common phase includes ω log u and is not solely a time shift or ordinary cold-DM phase. The differential ray columns agree but independent absolute dispersive or intrinsic wave phase information may break this equivalence. True third relative voltage amplitude for the y=1 example obeys 1/[(K−1)√(g μ_g^−)]<.006 at K=4096. Proof does not discard any other branch.

Nonmatching astrometric control: signed separation x_+−x_−=√(c_++k log u)+√(c_−+k log u) grows from 1.15 to 1.15+√(.23), unlike a pure gravitational point mass in geometric optics; an unresolved spectral data set has no such comparison. More generally, no pair of distinct smooth ray branches lying on the same fixed scanned source axis, on an open rectangle of axial source positions β and u can have achromatic nonzero ΔS throughout that rectangle: by stationarity ∂_β ΔS=−(x₂−x₁) is u-independent; ∂_u ΔS=0 implies N(x₂)=N(x₁), so ΔS=[(x₂−β)²−(x₁−β)²]/2. Nonzero u-invariant difference fixes their sum as well; fixed rays across u would obey x_i−β=u N′(x_i), so each equals β, contradiction. Applies to axisymmetric screens or other models whose relevant branches are known collinear there, not an arbitrary asymmetric screen or a restriction on group lag alone with unknown frequency-independent relative phase offset. Not a test inferred from any native FRB.

## 4. Finite waves: impossibility of exact equality, and uniform sequential approximation
Physical plasma prefactor in the true normalized Fresnel operator:
F_p(ω)=1+[ωτ₀/(2π i)]∫ d²x e^{i ωτ₀ Q(x)}(e^{−i τ₀ ω_ref² N(x)/ω}−1).
The integral is compact; this defines a holomorphic function of complex ω away from zero. At fixed τ₀, ω_ref and fixed physical screen, ω→+∞ makes \(ω(e^{−i τ₀ ω_ref² N/ω}−1)\to −i τ₀ ω_ref² N\) uniformly on its compact support. Changing to radial coordinates around β and variable s=|x−β|²/2, Fourier Riemann–Lebesgue on the compact integrable radial average proves F_p→1. Therefore real analytic intensity \(I_p(ω)=F_p(ω)\overline{F_p(\bar ω)}\) tends to 1. The true point-mass intensity \(I_g\) is real analytic for positive ω, by the exact Gamma/confluent hypergeometric point-mass formula with a positive-real log branch; its two-ray high-frequency asymptote has nonvanishing contrast \(2\sqrt{\mu_g^+\mu_g^-}\) at distinct nonzero delay, hence no high-ω limit. If \(I_p=C I_g\) with C>0 on any open band, real-analytic continuation gives equality on all ω>0, contradiction. Equal exact voltage up to any arbitrary common unit-modulus function and constant amplitude would imply the same intensity equality. This is a mathematical infinite-precision result for calibrated transfer, not stable analytic extrapolation from noisy or discrete frequencies or unknown burst spectral gain.

For approximation at fixed y, g, geometry, smoothing, and K, take Λ=ω_min τ₀ large while the dimensionless profiles and ρ stay fixed on [1,2]. The phase family has three distinct nondegenerate smooth roots uniformly in ρ. Compactly subtract the corresponding free integral with a cutoff equal to one around all support and the free stationary point. Compact stationary and nonstationary neighborhoods give
sup_{ρ∈[1,2]} |F_p − ∑_j e^{i Λρ S_j−iπ m_j/2}/√|det D²S_j|| ≤ C_K/Λ.
A specified finite constant can be obtained as follows (no numerical finite-frequency threshold is certified). Use parameter-smooth Morse coordinates \(S=S_j+\tfrac12 z^T D_j z\), \(D_j\) diagonal ±1, each with compact cutoff, and let b_{j,ρ} be the integrated smooth amplitude including Jacobian in these coordinates. A signed Fresnel Gaussian Fourier integral shows each local remainder at κ=Λρ is ≤ \|(1−Δ)^3 b_{j,ρ}\|_1/(16πκ): use |e^{−i ξ^T D_j ξ/(2κ)}−1|≤|ξ|²/(2κ) and ∫_{R²}|ξ|²/(1+|ξ|²)^3 dξ=π/2. On a compact nonstationary remainder b, two integrations by parts give ≤\|L^* L^* b\|_1/(2πκ) with \(L^* b=−\nabla·[b\nabla S/|\nabla S|²]\), defining that coefficient only near the support of b. Apply both also to the free term; their sup over ρ is finite by compactness and fixed K, and gives a reproducible bound in terms of chart and cutoff norms. The free stationary term cancels the explicit 1. The point mass at fixed y also has the ordinary high-phase two-ray approximation after scaling its mass for the chosen nonzero matched lag. Thus, first choose finite K to hide the extra ray in geometric optics; then increase Λ (and the corresponding gravitational delay at fixed radio band) for the uniform wave approximation. The constants can grow sharply with K or small cap widths. At fixed delay and band these arguments **do not** permit arbitrary wave closeness, and the physical ν→∞ limit above is not the same parameter family as fixed ρ increasing Λ.  

A separate [bounded-class result](FIXED-CLASS-GAP.md) proves a strictly positive, qualitative minimum uniform intensity mismatch over a fixed band and delay if the support and maximum column are bounded. It still allows arbitrary screen derivatives; it provides no numerical sensitivity threshold.

## 5. Physical conversion, prefit archive stop, and primary prior
Physical projected lens x_phys=a x with a²=c D_eff τ₀, \(D_eff=D_dD_{ds}/D_s\) in Euclidean distance; physical column \(N_e=(τ₀ ω_ref²/(2π c r_e))N\) from phase −r_e (2πc/ω) N_e. A product with a smooth nonnegative normalized line-of-sight profile produces this column mathematically; positivity alone does not guarantee physical transparency or the thin-screen regime. The simultaneous constraints below are not shown uniformly in K. Check plasma frequency ≪ observing band, slab thin compared with geometric distances and negligible transverse shift inside it relative to small-scale structures, small deflections, point-source coherence, negligible magnetic rotation/multiple scattering/absorption, and any density/gradient/stability bounds before applying. The construction is finely tailored and does not claim equilibrium or population plausibility. At a purely illustrative Δt=10 μs, 400–800 MHz, D_eff=1 kpc, τ₀≈18.3 μs, a≈2.75 AU, and one unit of N is ≈0.00282 pc cm⁻³ (uses reference 800 MHz), so support <8.3 AU. Near-center core width 2a/K and angular features are much narrower. Taking a higher Λ by increasing τ₀ at fixed band/distance scales transverse lengths by √τ₀ and columns by τ₀, and changes the matched mass/delay; independent physical bounds may rule out following this family. No observational claim for these dimensions.
