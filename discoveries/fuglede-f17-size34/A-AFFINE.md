## A1. Large uniform pencils: reduction and K≥8

#### Prior parametrized reduction and general no-two-layer argument

In any 2p spectral pair of odd prime dimension-three field, neither member can be contained in two parallel planes. This is the proof of [F–I Lemma 9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1#S9), with p replacing 11, applied here at $p=17$. For if E is layered, each slice has p points and is a cap. Counting pairs shows at least p tangent directions per slice, since each direction gives at most (p−1)/2 pairs. D(E) contains no full projective line: a full line in U(A) would force mp+pa=(p+1)m for a line occupancy a via the pencil identity, impossible. Therefore both slices use the same p directions, each making (p−1)/2 pairs and one singleton. For any such direction d a uniform normal perpendicular to d must put the two slice singletons in the same plane, since the p−1 pairs occupy full two-point bins. Their difference is transverse to d, leaving at most one uniform normal on each of the p projective lines d^perp. The remaining pencil line has at most p uniform normals. Thus |D(A)|<=|U(E)|<=2p. By the known parallel-plane criterion (F–I Lemma 8.3), the p uniform normals in the tangent projective line of A force A itself into two layers. They give p in-plane directions and >p transverse directions: otherwise their two p-point projected sets P,Q satisfy |Q−P|=p, all Q−x for x in P coincide, and invariance under a nonzero period makes Q a full affine line. This contradicts the cap bound. 

For a pencil with K>=8 uniform normals, project along its annihilator. Write f(x,y) for multiplicities (not assumed Boolean); make one uniform normal vertical and label the L=18−K actual nonuniform normals by slopes s_i in F_17. Their integer occupancy functions g_i(t) have mass 34, are between zero and the current plane cap, and obey the following Radon identity and coefficient conditions (generalization of F–I Lemma 8.4):
    
    sum_i g_i(y−s_i x) = 2(L−1)+17 f(x,y).
    w_i = 1 / product_{h != i}(s_i−s_h).
    c_(i,j)/w_i = R_j(s_i), degree R_j <= L−j−2, for j>=1.

The identity counts every other quotient point once and the base point 18 times, then drops K uniform fibres of mass two. Reduce it modulo 17, represent profiles by degree <=16, and match homogeneous coefficients: sum_i c_(i,j)*s_i^h=0 for every 0<=h<=j. The Lagrange/Vandermonde nullspace yields the displayed degree bound. In particular all bad profiles have degree <=L−2, and the constants sum to 2(L−1) modulo 17. Translation of (x,y) shifts each argument by h+k*s_i and preserves all constraints. Common rescaling of x,y multiplies the coefficient of degree j by u^j, preserving slopes, admissible integer profile multisets and plane cap. In characteristic 17 a polynomial of nonzero leading degree d<=8 has a unique centered translate (coefficient of d−1 zero).

Given a plane with n>=3 points and no three collinear, its direction set has size at least n: otherwise it must be exactly n−1 and would be a hyperfocused arc, impossible over odd q. [Giulietti and Montanucci, On hyperfocused arcs, §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0601488) cites the classical result. An elementary argument uses an affine triangle (0,0),(1,0),(0,1) and the other points (x,y). The product per point of y/x, (x+y−1)/y, and x/(x+y−1) equals 1. In the n−1-slopes equality case each triangle vertex sees each direction once. These factors in local slopes s are s, (s+1)/s, and 1/(s+1), respectively. For slopes outside {0,−1,infinity} they cancel in the product over points. The extra terms for slopes −1 from the first vertex, infinity from the second, and 0 from the third multiply to −1, a contradiction. Thus a bound K_max on pencils of U transfers to the partner's plane cap, with no realization gap.

## A2. Seven uniform normals and the plane cap

### Definitions and independent plane-cap refinement

From [A1](A-AFFINE.md#a1), both mates have line cap two, plane cap seven, and at most seven uniform normals per pencil. The cyclotomic identity gives both dual inclusions $D(X)\subset U(Y)$ and $D(Y)\subset U(X)$.

There is already a sharper affine plane cap of six. If seven points of E occupy a plane, no three are collinear. The 21 pairs contribute at most three per direction, so their D has at least seven and, from the partner cap, exactly seven. This is impossible over F17: the classical [Beutelspacher–Wettl 1993, Theorem 2.3(d)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01388411) applied to the arc and all its 11 missing points on infinity gives the necessary divisibility of 16,17,or 18 by seven. The finite replay is self-contained even without importing that theorem. Up to PGL(2,17), there are ten possible seven-direction masks, normalized to contain infinity. Any hypothetical 7-cap determines every allowed direction, so translate one vertical secant to begin at (0,0) and scale both coordinates by a common field scalar to make its other endpoint (0,1); this leaves slopes unchanged. The remaining points are enumerated in lex order, allowing only permitted directions and no collinear three. The ten recursion counts are 125,156,109,111,97,133,101,126,105,117 (1,180 total); no case reaches seven. Thus moving forward all occupancy profiles have values in [0,6].

### Exactly seven uniform normals: algebra and gauges

Suppose a projective line has exactly seven uniform directions for E. Project onto the quotient plane along the annihilator of this line. Let f be the resulting integer multiplicity (0,1,2 by the line cap; it need not be Boolean). Select coordinates with a uniform normal vertical. The eleven actual bad normals are the distinct finite slopes s_i, and g_i(t) are their parallel plane populations, each mass 34 and values between zero and six. The integer identity is

    Σ_i g_i(y−s_i x) = 20+17 f(x,y).

It counts all masses once, the reference point 18 times, and subtracts the seven uniform line masses. Write g_i as its reduced polynomial modulo 17. For each j>0, comparing homogeneous terms gives Σ_i c_(i,j)s_i^h=0 for 0≤h≤j. With w_i=1/Π_{k≠i}(s_i−s_k), the nullspace is exactly c_(i,j)/w_i=R_j(s_i), deg R_j≤9−j for 1≤j≤9, and c_(i,j)=0 if j≥10. This is the usual Lagrange basis argument, as in F–I Lemma 8.4; binomial factors are invertible at all reduced degrees. Constants must sum to 20 mod17. The projection formula is needed only as a necessary condition: no choice passes even the requisite modular coefficients; the proof does not infer realizability from any partial system.

We normalize the bad 11-mask using all projectivities mapping an ordered pair of bad points to 0,1 and a good point to infinity. The resulting normalized finite masks (0,1 contained) have count C(15,9)=5005, covered by ten orbits. Any normalized image with these labels has distinct preimages of the stated type, so the traversal is exhaustive. The alternate all-projective-mask enumeration has 31,824 masks: seven orbits of size 2,448 and three of size 4,896. These coordinate changes act on actual plane profiles by argument scales, not merely by exchanging raw coefficient labels. The implementation takes numeric slopes in each representative and regenerates the allowable profiles at their dictated leading coefficients, so the scale is respected.

A common simultaneous scaling of x and y scales the degree-j coefficient of every g_i by u^j for any u≠0 and leaves the slopes fixed. A common translation shifts the argument on each slope by h+k*s_i; in a profile of degree d its next coefficient changes by d times its leading coefficient times that shift. Thus that coefficient can be killed on any chosen two slopes with nonzero leading coefficient; alternatively an everywhere proportional affine pattern can be killed everywhere. Individually, every degree d≤9 profile has a unique centered translate (coefficient degree d−1 zero); raw bounds and mass survive these shifts/scales.

The least possible residue mass for nonconstant degrees 1..6 is 136,102,85,51,51,34 (verified by complete centered coefficient census). Degree zero gives constant occupancy two because all individual values are strictly below 17, so cannot be a bad slope. Therefore the possible highest degree is 6,7,8,or 9.

### Highest-degree six branch

All eleven profiles have degree six. The leading weighted values Q(s_i)=c_(i,6)/w_i form a polynomial Q of degree≤3, nonzero at all slots. Sixth powers give the nonzero squares, so normalize first actual lead c_(0,6) to 1 or 3. The weighted degree-five coefficient has form R(s)=s(s−1)H(s), deg H≤2 by translating to kill it at the first two slopes. For a given actual leading coefficient there is exactly one centered sextic of mass 34 (sixteen total, all cap six and even), and its shift is h_i=R(s_i)/(6Q(s_i)). Let A_i=Q(s_i)c_(i,4,centered)/w_i. Taylor expansion gives

    Q(s_i) R_4(s_i) = A_i − R(s_i)^2,

because binom(6,2)/6²=−1 mod17. Thus the A_i interpolate a polynomial A of degree≤8, independent of the shift, and R²=A modulo Q. These necessary checks are followed by explicit quartic coefficient equations even when deg Q<3 (divisibility alone need not constrain quotient degree to five). Among 53,360 leading gauges, 170 pass degree eight. There are 276 shift choices matching the congruence, 152 pass quartic equations, four pass constants, and zero meet the remaining degrees.

### Highest-degree seven branch

Let Q=R_7 have degree≤2, allowing up to two zero slots, which must be sextics. Normalize the first nonzero actual septuple lead to 1 (seventh powers permute nonzero elements). There are 307 valid leading gauges per mask including all positions of first nonzero. Translate to kill R=R_6 at the first two nonzero-leading slopes. Thus R of degree≤3 is a product of their two linear roots and an arbitrary linear factor. For every septic slot take any of its three centered cap-six profiles; put A_i=Q(s_i)c_(i,5,centered)/w_i. As binom(7,2)/7²=−2 mod17, 

    Q(s_i) R_5(s_i) = A_i − 2R(s_i)^2.

At septic slots, A_i must interpolate a degree≤6 polynomial (at least nine septic slots, so there is uniqueness). Generate it from seven and check each remaining septic slot, retaining all centered forms matching its prescribed coefficient. Check that A−2R² is divisible by Q with quotient degree≤4. At any sextic root, R(s_i) must be nonzero and gives actual leading coefficient w_i R(s_i); the quotient R_5 gives the next coefficient and hence unique translate of the centered sextic. At each septic slot the shift is R(s_i)/(7Q(s_i)). Check the resulting profiles. Across ten masks, 6,714,090 first-seven assignments yield 52,833 degree-six polynomial matches and 48,537 complete profiles after enforcing the weighted fifth; zero meet the quartic coefficient equations.

### Highest-degree eight branch

Translate to kill R=R_7 at the first two octic slopes, making it rho times the product of their two linear factors. Therefore all octic A_i must interpolate a polynomial A of degree≤4; pick any five slots (smallest domains in the replay), interpolate and test all remaining octic slots, returning all shapes with each needed c6. ## A3. Rich sections and the forced Boolean hexagon quotient

### 1. Prior inputs and notation

By [A1–A2](A-AFFINE.md#a1), each member is a cap, plane occupancies are at most six, and each uniform pencil has at most six directions. Use $D(X)\subset U(Y)$ and $D(Y)\subset U(X)$. [Ball, Theorem 4.2(ii)](https://web.mat.upc.edu/simeon.michael.ball/blocking.pdf) gives $|U|,|D|\le86$, as in the main proof.

The closest primary method is [Ferguson–Iosevich §9, especially Lemmas 9.3–9.4 and Theorem 9.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1), which applies low-direction planar rigidity, then profile enumerations, and finally an equal-parallel-chord obstruction for size22 in F11^3. The new bridge below uses an orthogonality graph and complementary matchings instead of assuming all coplanar quadruples are parallelograms. No published F17 size34 closure was located.

### 2. Both members cannot have plane cap four

Suppose both plane caps were four. At any secant direction v of E, fix an endpoint e of an actual secant. The rays of the other 33 E points from e are distinct (the affine line cap is 2). Every projective line contains at most three of these rays (e plus at most three others in an affine plane). Among the 18 projective lines through ray v, at least 16 must meet one of the other 32 rays, since each can meet at most two of those. Thus at most two are tangent to this 33-ray set at v. Every uniform E normal perpendicular to v gives such a tangent: its level plane through e must contain exactly the two endpoints on v. This bounds the number of opposite A difference directions perpendicular to v by two. Interchanging E and A gives the dual bound.

Form the bipartite graph with sides D(E) and D(A), edges between perpendicular directions. Give each direction weight equal to the number of unordered actual point pairs in that direction in its own member. For each vertex, the weights of its neighbors sum to 17: in the opposite set its 17 level planes are uniform, with one pair each, and these are exactly the pairs whose direction is perpendicular to the vertex. All weights are positive. No vertex is isolated, every vertex has degree ≤2, and there is no 4-cycle, since two distinct projective points have at most one common orthogonal ray in dimension three.

An endpoint of a path forces its neighbor's weight to be17. Any weight17 vertex has only degree1 neighbors by the same sum rule. Hence path components are an edge or a two-leaf star, with total weight17 on each side. In a cycle the weights alternate t,17−t along either part, so the part size is even (17 is odd) and at least four (no 4-cycle).

For an actual point set X and direction d, its parallel secants define a matching M_d on X because of affine line cap2. For a degree-two vertex w on one side, its two neighbors d,d' give disjoint perfect coverage by M_d and M_d': every point of X is in exactly one of the 17 two-point planes perpendicular to w. For a cycle this forces the sets of endpoints of the selected opposite matchings to alternate between some set S⊂X and X−S. With part size at least four, there are at least two distinct direction matchings covering S and at least two distinct ones covering X−S. The union of two perfect matchings in distinct parallel directions on the same support splits into alternating polygons. Every such polygon lies in an affine plane parallel to the span of its two directions. Plane cap4 forces each polygon to have length exactly four. Thus |S| and |X−S| are multiples of four, impossible since |X|=34. This excludes all cycle components. The same argument shows that on either part there cannot be two weight17 vertices, since two complete parallel matchings of X would make 34 a multiple of four. There are at most two path components in total (each needs a weight17 vertex), so either part has total weight ≤34, contradicting the 561 unordered pairs. This proof does not depend on an unresolved projective arc classification.

### 3. The forced six-pencil, and its hexagon geometry

Every five-point affine cap determines at least five directions (its ten pairs contribute at most two per direction). A six-point cap likewise determines at least five (15 pairs at most three per direction). The exact small planar gate shows that no such five-point cap can have at most five directions. The same excludes six-point caps with ≤5 since they contain a five-point cap. At ≤6 directions, all actual 5/6-point caps use exactly six, in a single PGL orbit represented by G. Every such 5-cap extends to a 6-cap with the same directions, and every such 6-cap is affine-equivalent to a centered hexagon {±u,±v,±(u+v)} with u,v independent. An extension point need not belong to the original spectral member. By §2 some member has a 5- or 6-point plane, so its directions force exactly the six uniform normals G, up to projectivity, in the corresponding opposite pencil.

The standalone atlas normalizes a chosen noncollinear triple to (0,0),(1,0),(0,1). Every possible allowed palette contains their slopes {0,∞,16}. Enumerate all 105 size-five and 455 size-six palettes; within each, enumerate cap extensions in lex order. Node counts 189 and 1510 leave respectively zero and 30 five-point sets, and zero and 10 six-point sets. The latter palette support has ten masks, all in the 408-element G orbit. All 30 five-point sets extend within the same palette, and all ten six-point sets pass the centered affine-hexagon check.

### Follow-up: the forced projection is Boolean

If the six-direction pencil on A comes from a five- or six-point section of E perpendicular to d, then d is not uniform for E: a uniform normal would give two points per plane. Spectral duality D(A) ⊆ U(E) implies there are no A secants parallel to d. The pencil projects A along d, so this particular projection has **zero** double sites.
