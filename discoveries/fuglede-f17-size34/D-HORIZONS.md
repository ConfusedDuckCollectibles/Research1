## D1. The perfect seventh-moment pairing and power kernels

### 1. The degree-seven residue pairing is perfect on the actual horizon

Let X and Y be the two mates. Each consists of 34 smooth affine points on an integral ACM septic curve of arithmetic genus five, has zero affine moments through degree six, and has evaluation dimensions (1,4,10,17,24,30,33) in degrees 0 through 6. For either side, write C for the curve, H∞ for its degree-seven scheme of intersection with the actual horizon h=0, A_j for affine restrictions of all polynomials of degree≤j to C, V_j for their evaluations at the 34 marks, and J_j for the evaluation kernel in A_j. There is a uniquely determined quintic kernel line J_5=span G, whose homogeneous section on C cuts the 34 marks plus the distinguished smooth rational point P_*.

Choose a rational linear form L on the horizon not vanishing on any geometric support point of H∞. Such a form exists because there are at most seven support points, each excluding a proper linear subspace of choices over F17. Trivialize the degree-j twisting sheaf on H∞ by L^j, to identify it with the length-seven coordinate algebra B of H∞. Write R_j⊆B for homogeneous degree-j restrictions under this trivialization. ACM and the hyperplane restriction sequence identify A_j/A_(j−1) with R_j. Explicitly one homogenizes through degree j using h, then restricts to h=0; the kernel consists of sections divisible by h on C, which come from sections through degree j−1 by ACM. We have dim R_1=3 and dim R_2=6 (no quadratic relation on the septic and h⁰(O_C(1))=4). For j≥3 the sequence is surjective onto the whole B because H¹(O_C(j−1))=0 by duality and 7(j−1)>8. Thus dim R_j=7 for j≥3.

Define a linear functional λ:B→F17 by taking degree-seven sections, dividing by h^7 on the affine chart, and summing their evaluations on the 34 marks. This depends only on the element of B since all moments through degree six vanish. The multiplication pairing λ(bc) on B is nondegenerate: the dot product between V_3 and V_4 in F17^34 has rank at least 17+24−34=7, and all lower total-degree blocks are zero. Its top block descends exactly to R_3×R_4=B×B, using J_3=J_4=0, under the same normalization by L. Hence its rank is exactly seven, proving the assertion directly even for a nonreduced or nonsplit scheme. This also proves M7 is nonzero; it does not rely on counting ordinary residues only at simple points.

Let g∈B be the restriction of the homogeneous version of G in degree five, normalized by L^5. This is nonzero because otherwise G would have an affine restriction through degree four. At a geometric support point it has nonzero value except precisely P_* if P_* is on H∞, since G cuts exactly the marks plus P_* and no mark lies on the horizon. In the perfect pairing, the annihilator of R_2 is exactly span g: multiplying a quadratic by G in degree seven gives zero evaluations and hence λ(g R_2)=0, and R_2 has codimension one.

### 2. Exact paired fifth, fourth and third power kernels

The two horizon schemes H_X,H_Y sit naturally in the dual projective coordinate planes for the spectral pairing. Define K_j∈B_X⊗B_Y by restricting the biform (u·v)^j of bidegree (j,j), divided by L_X^j L_Y^j under the above charts. Then
    K_5 ∈ g_X⊗B_Y + B_X⊗g_Y,                                      (1)
and in addition rank K_3≥4 while rank K_4≤3. Ranks here are exact tensor ranks for two factors, equivalently ranks of the flattening from B_X^* to B_Y. For the exclusion below only (1) is needed.

To prove (1), use zero M7^Y(a−b) for ALL a,b∈X, including a=b, by spectral uniformity (each field value is taken exactly twice along every actual direction) and the seventh power sum over F17. Weight the a variable arbitrarily in the annihilator of V_1(X), and the b variable arbitrarily in the annihilator of V_4(X). Expand (y·(a−b))^7 before summing over y, a and b. Every bidegree vanishes except (2,5), and its binomial coefficient is a unit. The degree-two weights realize the full dual of homogeneous quadratics in three variables, since the degree-two evaluation is injective and has difference of dimensions 6. Thus their outputs include every homogeneous quadratic function of y (the factorial scalings in the coefficient pairing are invertible). On the degree-five side, the quotient V_5/V_4 is exactly B_X/span g_X: one first takes the graded horizon restriction, and the only new kernel is the leading restriction of G. It follows that contraction of K_5 against any element of B_X^* killing g_X gives an element annihilating R_2(Y) in the perfect λ_Y multiplication pairing. That annihilator is span g_Y, proving (1). This argument does not invert g_X or g_Y on nonreduced schemes.

For the quartic rank bound, apply the same derivation using a weights killing V_2 and b weights killing V_3 to isolate bidegree (3,4). Evaluations through degree four have zero kernel, and the graded restrictions R_3,R_4 both equal B. Thus the two contraction images of K_3,K_4 inside B_Y are orthogonal in its perfect multiplication pairing. Their ranks sum to at most seven. The kernel K_3 is obtained by restricting the perfect cubic coefficient pairing on the 10-dimensional space of ternary homogeneous cubics to the two 7-dimensional duals of the graded quotients. Therefore rank K_3≥7+7−10=4, giving rank K_4≤3. These additional restrictions, as well as (1), are valid without splitting or general-position assumptions.

## D2. Mixed local jet reductions before the Frobenius argument

### 1. Work over the algebraic closure; normalize only by units

Base change to K=algebraic closure of F17 preserves all tensor ranks and identities. Denote the normalized bilinear form between opposite horizon vectors by l(u,y)=u·y, with rational affine charts as in [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1). Projective representatives can be rescaled by units of their finite local rings. This only scales the corresponding power-tensor rows or columns by invertible multiplications, so power ranks are invariant. A unit in a local Artin K-algebra has a fifth root: take a root of its scalar value and use the finite binomial expansion for the nilpotent remainder (length at most seven and all relevant integers invertible). Equivalently lift successively since the derivative of the fifth power at a unit is nonzero. At units of g_X we use this to rescale a local representative until g_X=1. In that normalization write A(t)=a_X(t)/g_X(t); this is one function of that local parameter, independent of the y coordinate.

A geometric horizon of length seven with injective plane quadratics has no line section of length five or more. Indeed for the restricted line equation e, length B/eB≥5 gives dim(eB)≤2, so multiplication by e kills some nonzero projective linear form and yields a nonzero plane conic vanishing on the scheme. In particular any at least five of the seven distinct points of a geometrically reduced horizon span the plane. Any statement below that a union of two lines cannot contain all of a reduced horizon refers to this same quadratic injectivity.

### 2. Embedding dimension two and individual curvilinear jets

Suppose Y is geometrically reduced. At an embedding-dimension-two point u of X, g_X is a unit: a g_X-zero point could only be smooth P_* on the septic, where the horizon is curvilinear. Normalize g_X=1. In the two-dimensional tangent space at u choose a nonzero tangent annihilating dA, possible because dA takes values in the scalar field. The derivative of the separated fifth-power identity gives
    l(u,y)^4 l'(u,y) = 0 for every y in Y.
The tangent is nontrivial projectively, so l' is a nonzero linear form independent of l(u,·). Since Y is reduced, their product vanishes on all of Y, a forbidden conic. This does not assume rationality of u or any tangent.

Now let u be curvilinear of length m≥2. Choose a local parameter t. Write L(t,y)=L_0(y)+t L_1(y)+... with projective vector L_1 not proportional to L_0. When g_X is a unit, normalize as in Section 1 and use L(t,y)^5=A(t)g_Y(y)+a_Y(y). Its coefficient of t has A'(0)≠0: if it vanished the previous conic L_0 L_1 would again contain reduced Y. Reparameterize so that t=A(t)−A(0). On the eligible set T of Y points with g_Y≠0, the first derivative now also shows L_0(y)≠0. There are at least six such y. Define b(y)=g_Y(y)/L_0(y)^5. Then
    (L(t,y)/L_0(y))^5 = 1 + b(y)t.
Consequently the coefficient of t^j in L/L_0 is binom(1/5,j) b^j.

If g_X is not a unit, u=P_* and we may use t=g_X as parameter, since g_X has a simple zero there. Write A(t)=a_X(t) in the unscaled chart. Then
    L_0(y)^5=A(0)g_Y(y).
If A(0)=0 the nonzero linear form L_0 vanishes on all reduced Y points, which is impossible. Thus for the same eligible set T, L_0 never vanishes. We now have
    (L(t,y)/L_0(y))^5 = 1 + b(y)t + Σ_(j≥2) c_j t^j,
where b(y)=A'(0)/A(0)+a_Y(y)/(A(0)g_Y(y)), and c_j are scalars independent of y. The fifth root has coefficient of t^j a polynomial in b of degree j with leading coefficient binom(1/5,j): any term involving t^i, i≥2, uses at most j−2 of the b-terms. This applies through j=m−1. In both normalizations the analogous coefficients of the fourth-power ratio have the same triangular structure with binom(4/5,j).

The exact mod-17 fifth-root coefficients for j=0,...,6 are (1,7,4,1,1,4,7), and the fourth-power ratios through 2 have (1,11,4). In particular all are nonzero, as follows directly since 1/5=7 and 4/5=11 in F17. Let b_1,...,b_s be the distinct values on T. The restricted matrix of the first j+1 bilinear jet rows, divided by the nonzero L_0(y), has rank min(j+1,s) by Vandermonde, for j≤m−1. For each fixed actual support point w of X, regardless of g_X(w), its normalized ordinary row L_w on T also satisfies
    (L_w/L_0)^5 = affine linear polynomial in b,
as follows from the separated fifth-power relation. In the unit-at-u case subtract g_X(w) times the appropriate normalized row at u after writing a_Y=L_0^5−A(0)g_Y; in the P_*-at-u case use L_0^5=A(0)g_Y and the affine definition of b. This only uses values, not any assumption of independent fourth-power coefficients beyond the proved rank bound.

#### A noncollinear length-three jet is impossible

If m≥3 and L_0,L_1,L_2 are independent linear forms, any two y∈T giving the same b also give the same values of L_1/L_0 and L_2/L_0, hence the same projective point. There are therefore at least six distinct b values. The first three fourth-power jet rows, divided by L_0^4, span 1,b,b² and are independent, so they exhaust the allowed rank three on T. For any actual support point w of X, write L_w/L_0=P(b) with deg P≤2 using the basis L_0,L_1,L_2. The fourth-power rank bound gives P(b)^4=R(b) on T for some deg R≤2. Also P(b)^5 is an affine polynomial in b. The polynomial P R, degree at most four, agrees on ≥6 distinct b with that affine polynomial, hence agrees identically. If P or R is zero, the evaluations give L_w=0 on all T, impossible since T spans and w is a projective point. Otherwise deg P+deg R≤1. If P were nonconstant, it would have degree one and R be constant, contradicting P^4=R on ≥6 values (degree four). Thus P is constant and w is projectively u. This holds for all support w. It forces the entire length-seven scheme of X to be the one curvilinear local ring at u, but then the seven bilinear jet rows have rank at least six on T by Vandermonde, contradicting rank three of the actual bilinear pairing. Therefore no such local jet exists.

#### A collinear length-three jet when g_Y is a unit is impossible

If m≥3 but the first three linear forms have span dimension two, their rank on T is two. Vandermonde implies at most two distinct b values on T. The ratio L_1/L_0 is a nonconstant linear polynomial in b with nonzero slope, so T is contained in a union of at most two projective lines (equations L_1=constant L_0). If g_Y is a unit, T is all seven reduced geometric points. This contradicts quadratic injectivity. Thus under the extra unit hypothesis ALL nonreduced points of X, if any, are now exactly double curvilinear points. With g_Y vanishing at P_* on its horizon the conclusion here is only that the six eligible points lie on two lines; one must not silently include P_* in that conic.

### 3. Two distinct fat points when Y is reduced and g_Y is a unit

Assume Y is geometrically reduced, g_Y is a unit everywhere, and X has two different fat points. By Section 2 these are doubles. Write L and M for their value linear forms and L' and M' for their genuine first-jet linear forms. The derivative of the fifth-power relation has already shown that L,M are units on all seven points of Y. Use r=L/M (a nonzero function on all seven).

#### Both points have unit g_X

Normalize g_X=1 separately on the two local algebras. The value identities give
    L^5−M^5 = Δ g_Y
where Δ is the difference of the fixed normalized a_X values at the two points. The derivative identities give L^4 L'=c g_Y and M^4 M'=d g_Y with nonzero constants c,d (absorbing 5). If Δ=0, these identities imply L'M is proportional to M'L everywhere on Y: since r^5=1, the ratio of the derivatives is proportional to r^(-4)=r. This is a plane conic vanishing on Y. It is not identically zero: L and M are independent linear forms, and if L'M = const M'L as polynomials, unique factorization would imply L' proportional to L, contrary to the genuine projective tangent. Thus Δ≠0, and
    g_Y/M^5=(r^5−1)/Δ,
    M'/M = d (r^5−1)/Δ,
    L'/M = c (r−r^(-4))/Δ.
The four fourth-power value and derivative rows, divided by M^4, therefore span exactly the functions
    1, r^4, r^5, r^(-1).                                         (2)
They all belong to the rank-at-most-three fourth-power image.

#### One point has nonunit g_X

There is at most one such point, and take it to be the row L. At that point use t=g_X as parameter. With α the value of original a_X at this point, L^5=αg_Y, α≠0. At the other point normalize g_X=1, so M^5=a g_Y+a_Y and M^4 M'=d g_Y, d≠0. The derivative of the row L supplies 5 L^4 L'=a_Y+e g_Y for some scalar e. Consequently
    g_Y/M^5=r^5/α,
    M'/M=(d/α) r^5,
    L'/M=(1/5) r^(-4)+(e−a)/(5α) r.
Again the four quartic jet rows divided by M^4 span exactly (2). Here the r^(-4) coefficient is nonzero, so no separate Δ=0 case arises.

#### Finish both cases using repeated fibers, then the degree-six bound

Y cannot have at most two distinct r values, since then all its points lie on the union of two distinct lines given by L=rM, a forbidden conic. Suppose some two different Y points share r. The displayed derivative formulas show that M'/M and L'/M are determined by r. Hence neither M' nor L' can be independent of L,M; if one were, its ratio together with r in the chart M=1 would identify the projective point. In particular M'=a_1L+a_0M with a_1≠0, since its first jet is genuine. The nonzero coefficient of r^5 in the formula for M'/M now shows r^5 is a nonconstant affine linear function of r on Y. Consequently (2) contains independent functions 1,r,1/r: multiply a relation by r and use the at least three distinct r values. These three functions already exhaust the fourth-power image on Y. It follows that for every X support w, its row L_w^4/M^4 is constant on each r fiber. Also L_w^5/M^5 is constant there: the general fifth-power relation writes it as a linear combination of g_Y/M^5 and a_Y/M^5, each a function of r in either displayed case. Equality of fourth and fifth powers implies equality of the underlying scalars even at zero. Thus L_w/M is constant on each r fiber for every X support point w. For the fiber of two different Y points under consideration, it is also constant for L,M,L',M'. In this repeated-fiber case L',M' lie in span(L,M), so this last claim alone does NOT ensure that all linear forms are exhausted. But X has only exactly double local points by Section 2. If its geometric support fails to span the plane, it lies on the line spanned by L,M. The two known doubles have their tangent directions in that same line; any additional support would make a line section of length at least five. Thus there could be no additional support; and with only the two doubles the total length would be four, not seven. Therefore the actual support spans the plane, contradicting the two different Y points sharing all the normalized row values. No repeated fiber is possible.

There are now seven distinct nonzero r values. A linear dependence of the four functions (2), after multiplying by r, is a nonzero polynomial supported on exponents 0,1,5,6, of degree at most six. It cannot vanish at seven distinct values, over K just as over F17. This contradicts rank at most three, and proves there cannot be two fat points.

## D3. A mixed pair would have precisely one double point

Use the exact hypotheses of [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1) and [D2](D-HORIZONS.md#d2). Work over K, the algebraic closure of F17; all relevant identities extend. The two finite plane schemes have length seven and injective restrictions of plane quadratics. Their paired ordinary bilinear form l and its powers satisfy
    rank(l^4)≤3,                                  (1)
    l^5 = g_X⊗a_Y + a_X⊗g_Y.                      (2)
Each g is nonzero on every support point except the possible distinguished P_* on its horizon. At that point the containing septic is smooth and g has a simple zero, so it can be used as parameter on its local horizon scheme. The perfect multiplication functional λ_Y has λ_Y(g_Y R_2(Y))=0, and in the reduced scheme each point has a nonzero scalar weight in λ_Y. Indeed a zero weight would contradict perfectness by testing the corresponding primitive idempotent. From quadratic injectivity recall also that the total scheme length cut by any line is at most four.

[D2](D-HORIZONS.md#d2) already gives the asserted local conclusion when g_Y is a unit. In all cases it excludes embedding dimension two and any length-three jet not contained in a line. Thus assume below g_Y vanishes at q=P_*^Y and T=Y−{q} is its other six geometric points, all with unit g_Y.

### 1. Any collinear third jet would force two groups and constrain the entire X support

Suppose u∈X has a curvilinear local algebra K[t]/t^m with m≥3 and collinear length-three jet at u. Write L(t,y)=L_0(y)+t L_1(y)+⋯ for its ordinary dot row. L_0 and L_1 are independent linear forms and the third coefficient is in their span. Use the exact normalizations from [D2](D-HORIZONS.md#d2) §2:
* If g_X is unit at u, rescale the local projective representative by an invertible fifth root so g_X=1. The derivative of a_X cannot vanish (else L_0^4 L_1=0 on the reduced Y, implying the forbidden conic L_0 L_1). Take t=a_X−a_X(u). On T, L_0 never vanishes by the derivative equation, and (L/L_0)^5=1+b(y)t with b=g_Y/L_0^5 nonzero on T.
* If g_X vanishes at u, use t=g_X. Then L_0(y)^5=a_X(u)g_Y(y), with a_X(u)≠0 since otherwise all reduced Y is on one line. On T, (L/L_0)^5=1+b(y)t+Σ_(j≥2)c_j t^j, with b=a'_X(0)/a_X(u)+a_Y(y)/(a_X(u)g_Y(y)) and c_j scalars independent of y.
In both cases the first three ordinary jet rows divided by L_0 have degree 0,1,2 respectively in b, with nonzero leading coefficients. Since their rank is two, b has at most two distinct values on T. It cannot have just one: then all six points of T lie on the single line of its fixed L_1/L_0 ratio. Thus there are two distinct values, and the six eligible points lie on two distinct lines of the pencil whose equation space is W=span(L_0,L_1). These two groups are disjoint: their intersection has L_0=0 whereas every eligible point has L_0≠0.

Each group has size at least three. If one group had size one or two, a product of the other group's line and a suitable linear form would vanish at every eligible point but one. For a singleton take any linear form nonzero there; for a double take a line through one but not the other. This violates λ_Y(g_Y R_2)=0 since λ_Y g_Y has nonzero weights on all six eligible points and zero weight at q. So the split is 3+3. Any conic through these six points must contain both lines as factors, because restricting to either line gives a binary degree-two form with three distinct zeros. This makes their conic, and consequently the equation pencil W, unique, and q is not on this conic by quadratic injectivity on all seven points.

We now evaluate at q. If g_X is unit at u and L_0(q)≠0, equation (2) says L(t,q)^5 is constant, so L_1(q)=L_2(q)=0 and the first three normalized rows there have the vector for b=0. The two b values on T are nonzero in this case. Thus adding q gives three distinct Vandermonde columns, contradicting collinearity of that third jet. If g_X is nonunit at u, L_0(q)^5=a_X(u)g_Y(q)=0 directly. So in all cases L_0(q)=0. Also L_1(q)≠0, since otherwise q would be at the intersection of the two group lines and the conic would contain all seven points. For unit g_X at u, (2) at u,q gives a_Y(q)=0. For nonunit g_X at u, (2) locally gives L(t,q)^5=t a_Y(q), which has no t-linear coefficient on its left and therefore again a_Y(q)=0. Equation (2) at q now implies every geometric X support point lies on the projective line q^⊥.

Every length≥3 jet would be collinear by [D2](D-HORIZONS.md#d2). Applying the preceding construction to any such jet yields the same unique eligible conic and the same pencil W. Its base point on the X side is therefore the unique point W∩q^⊥ in projective space, since L_0(q)=0 and L_1(q)≠0. Hence u is the only support that can have local length ≥3. The same uniqueness shows u rational, but field splitting is not needed in what follows.

Finally l(t,q) on the u component has order exactly one, and l(t,q)^5=0 by (2) and both g_Y(q)=a_Y(q)=0. Therefore m≤5, since a unit times t^5 is nonzero in K[t]/t^m if m≥6.

### 2. A small algebraic fifth-root rigidity closes all these third jets

Use the affine fifth-root rigidity lemma proved in [D14](D-HORIZONS.md#d14): an invertible affine map taking three distinct fifth roots of unity into fifth roots has zero intercept. Its proof is algebraic over the entire algebraic closure of F17.

Since q^⊥ is a line of linear forms, choose a basis L_0,U for it. The form U is outside W because W∩q^⊥ is the L_0 direction. Fix one of the three-point groups of T and use the affine chart L_0=1 there. The coordinate U/L_0 is pairwise distinct on its three points: the independent coordinates L_1/L_0 and U/L_0 distinguish any two points, and L_1/L_0 is fixed on the group. Any X support w≠u has ordinary row on this chart A_w+B_w U/L_0 with B_w≠0. Its fifth power is constant on this group by (2) and the definition of b, as also proved in [D2](D-HORIZONS.md#d2) §2. Its three values are distinct, so the constant fifth power is nonzero. Dividing by one fifth root of that constant turns them into three distinct fifth roots of unity. For any two different supports w,w' other than u, the two normalized row functions induce an invertible affine map between their root triples. By the lemma its translation is zero. Thus the original affine functions are proportional on the fixed group and therefore, as linear functions of U/L_0, globally proportional. Their projective supports coincide. This proves X has at most two support points, one of them u.

The length total is seven, m≤5 and the other support of X has multiplicity at most two by uniqueness of u. Hence the only remaining split is exactly (5,2). At u the first two coefficient rows of L(t,y)^4 span the two group-constant functions on T after dividing there by L_0^4: their normalized values are a constant and a linear polynomial in b with nonzero slope. Both rows evaluate to zero at q. The fourth jet coefficient (the term of t^4) evaluates nonzero at q since L(t,q) has order one; on T the same row is still a function of b after dividing by L_0^4. Subtracting a linear combination of the first two rows makes it supported only at q, nonzero. These three rows now exhaust rank(l^4)≤3 from (1). Therefore all X support rows, when raised to the fourth power and divided by L_0^4 on T, are constant on each group. The fifth powers have the same constancy. Equality of fourth and fifth powers implies equality of underlying scalars, even at zero. It follows that each actual ordinary row L_w/L_0 is constant on one three-point group, so L_w∈W. Since L_w(q)=0, this forces every support w=u. This contradicts (5,2), excluding every third or higher jet.

### 3. The exceptional q has nonzero a_Y value, and at most one double survives

Now all local multiplicities of X are at most two, including if it initially had no fat point. Suppose a_Y(q)=0. Then (2) at q again puts all supports of X on q^⊥. The line cap of four bounds their number by four; length seven and all local lengths≤2 require exactly four supports, with three doubles and a simple point. Each double tangent is transverse to q^⊥: otherwise that double contributes at least two to the line-section length, which is already at least four from the four different support points.

Choose two of these doubles with unit g_X (at most one support can be its distinguished zero). Normalize both locals to g_X=1. Denote their value linear forms by L,M, and their genuine tangents by L',M'. On T they obey
    L^5−M^5=Δ g_Y,    L^4 L'=c g_Y,    M^4 M'=d g_Y,
where c,d are nonzero, as in [D2](D-HORIZONS.md#d2). In particular L and M are nonzero on T. If Δ=0 then r=L/M has r^5=1, and L'M=(c/d)M'L on T. The equality holds at q too because L(q)=M(q)=0. This is a nonzero conic (unique factorization and the projective tangents prevent it from being the zero polynomial), a contradiction. Thus Δ≠0 and M'/M=(d/Δ)(r^5−1) on T. Here L,M span the equation line q^⊥ and M' is outside their span by transversality. Thus r distinguishes the six points of T: equal r gives equal M'/M and hence identical projective coordinates in the chart M=1. Pick a third support w on the same line q^⊥, distinct from these two. Its row ratio is L_w/M=A+B r with A,B both nonzero. Equation (2) expresses its fifth power as an affine linear function of r^5 on T (write a_Y/M^5=1−a_X(M)g_Y/M^5 and g_Y/M^5=(r^5−1)/Δ). Therefore a polynomial of degree at most five, (A+B r)^5 minus that affine function, vanishes at six distinct r values. It would have to vanish identically, but its coefficient of r is 5A^4 B≠0. This contradiction proves a_Y(q)≠0.

If a double of X were at the possible distinguished g_X-zero, its local parameter is t=g_X. At q, (2) then gives L(t,q)^5=t a_Y(q); its scalar value at t=0 is zero, hence its fifth power is zero modulo t², impossible. Thus all doubles have unit g_X. If there were two distinct doubles, use the two-unit proof of [D2](D-HORIZONS.md#d2) §3, including its treatment of repeated r fibers. The only original use of g_Y as a unit there was to assure L,M nonzero at all Y values (and absence of local lengths above two). It is now fulfilled at q because, with g_X normalized to 1 at both supports, L(q)^5=M(q)^5=a_Y(q)≠0; on T the tangent formula assured it. All subsequent formulas remain valid at q, with zero g_Y(q) there. The degree-six sparse-polynomial root bound is on seven distinct r once repeated fibers are excluded with the original rank argument; in this final application all seven Y points take part. This gives at most one double, as asserted.

## D4. The Frobenius restriction and the unit-double calculation

Use the same perfect horizon multiplication pairings and power tensors as [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1). In particular the images of K_3 and K_4 in B_Y are mutually orthogonal with respect to λ_Y, not just numerically rank bounded. [D3](D-HORIZONS.md#d3) shows that X has exactly one double point u, rational because it is its only nonreduced support, and five simple geometric points. Y is reduced, so its length seven is seven distinct points over K, the algebraic closure of F17. The proof below is analytic; its only finite dependency is the μ5 rigidity proved in the preceding note.

### 1. The rational tangent chart and a general three-point projection obstruction

Apply the local normalization at u used in [D2](D-HORIZONS.md#d2) §2. If g_X is unit at u, normalize it to 1 by a unit fifth root. The fifth root of a unit in this rational double algebra is defined over F17: a scalar fifth root exists uniquely since gcd(5,16)=1, and the first nilpotent coefficient is lifted uniquely. Its first derivative of a_X is nonzero or else a product of two distinct linear forms would vanish on reduced Y. If g_X vanishes at u, take t=g_X as parameter. In this case g_Y is a unit by [D3](D-HORIZONS.md#d3), and the value equation is L_0^5=a_X(u)g_Y, with a_X(u)≠0 since Y is not on a line. In both cases L_0,L_1, the value and tangent linear forms at u, are independent and defined over F17. The form L_0 has nonzero value on every point of Y: for unit g_X its derivative equation gives this away from a possible distinguished q of Y, and at q its fifth power equals a_Y(q)≠0 by [D3](D-HORIZONS.md#d3).

Use the rational affine chart L_0=1 on Y, with coordinate x=L_1/L_0. Thus for every geometric support w of X, its normalized row L_w/L_0 is an affine linear function on this chart, and its fifth power is affine in x by (2) from [D3](D-HORIZONS.md#d3). This follows directly from the value and derivative equations at u (in the unit case g_Y/L_0^5 is a scalar multiple of x; in the nonunit case g_Y/L_0^5 is constant and a_Y/L_0^5 is affine in x). The fourth-power image on Y contains 1 and x, from the value and first derivative of the fourth power at u; the third-power image contains 1 by the value at u.

There cannot be three Y points sharing x. Otherwise fix such a line in the chart and three of its points. Let W=span(L_0,L_1) in the X vector space of its linear forms on Y. Each support row w outside W has three distinct values on the chosen line, and its fifth power is constant there, so this constant is nonzero. Dividing by a fifth root gives a triple of distinct fifth roots of unity. By the μ5 rigidity lemma, the normalized triples obtained from any two such outside rows are proportional: the unique affine map of affine-line values that takes the first normalized triple to the second has zero translation. Thus all support forms outside W are contained in the span of one such form and the equation of the chosen Y line, because their restrictions to that affine line are proportional. In the X projective plane this span is a line W'. All other supports lie on W, and the unique double u is contained **scheme-theoretically** in W (W is its projective tangent line). Therefore the nonzero quadratic product of equations of W and W' vanishes on the full X horizon, contradicting quadratic injectivity. If there is no support row outside W, W itself already contains the whole scheme, also impossible. Thus the tangent chart projects Y with fiber size at most two without any extension restriction.

Now suppose neither horizon support has a Frobenius orbit of degree four. The common splitting field of the coordinates has degree over F17 equal to a least common multiple of the individual degrees, all at most seven. It is not divisible by four because the only possible orbit degree at most seven divisible by four was excluded. Since 17 has order four modulo five, fifth powers are a bijection on this field (including zero). The rational chart and the support representatives used above lie in this field, so if two Y sites shared x, every support row's ordinary value L_w/L_0 would agree at the two points. The X support has six distinct points and therefore spans its plane, since no line section has length five. Hence these equal normalized coordinates would identify the two Y sites. Thus x is injective on all seven Y points.

Choose any X support row w outside W (it exists because the support spans). Put y=L_w/L_0, so (x,y) are affine coordinates on the Y chart. The equation y^5=A+B x has B≠0: if it were constant, power injectivity would make y constant on Y, putting Y on a line. As x is injective, y is injective on the seven Y sites too. In fact y being injective follows already from B≠0, because x is determined by y and (x,y) determine the point. Thus we can now use the seven distinct y values and x expressed as an affine function of y^5.

### 2. Three-dimensional quartic image forces a conic through all X supports

In the L_0=1 chart the quartic image contains the functions 1,x,y⁴, so it is exactly span(1,y⁴,y⁵). Indeed these three are independent on the seven distinct y values, since no nonzero polynomial of degree≤5 can vanish at all seven. For any X support point its row, expressed in the coordinate basis fixed above, has
    P(y)=a+b y+c y^5,
    R(y)=d+e y^4+f y^5 representing P(y)^4 on Y.
The equation for its fifth power implies P R is affine in y^5 on Y. Let A_7(Y) be the monic polynomial of its seven distinct y values, and let W_4,Z_4,S_6,T_6 be minus the y⁴ and y⁶ coefficients of the remainders of y⁹ and y¹⁰ modulo A_7, respectively, in the order
    W_4=−[y⁴](y⁹ mod A_7),  Z_4=−[y⁴](y¹⁰ mod A_7),
    S_6=−[y⁶](y⁹ mod A_7),  T_6=−[y⁶](y¹⁰ mod A_7).
The remainder of P R minus its known affine polynomial in y^5 is zero. Its coefficients at y⁴,y⁶ give
    (a−W_4 c)e−Z_4 c f=0,
    (b−T_6 c)f−S_6 c e=0.
This is exact because among the exponents of the product P R only 9 and 10 exceed six. If (e,f)≠(0,0), the determinant gives the single nonzero quadratic equation on the X row coordinates
    (a−W_4 c)(b−T_6 c)−Z_4 S_6 c²=0.                 (3)
Its leading coefficient on a b is one, so it is not the zero polynomial.

If e=f=0, then P^4=d is constant as a function on Y and d≠0 (a nonzero linear form on the chart cannot vanish at every Y point). Now P R=dP is affine in y^5 on Y. This is a polynomial relation of degree≤5, so b=0 by the seven y values. If c≠0, then P has seven distinct values because y^5 has seven distinct values (as x does), impossible for constant fourth power. Thus c=0 too, and the support is u in row coordinates (1,0,0), also satisfying (3). All X reduced support points therefore lie on (3).

If g_X is a unit at the double point u, equation (3) must in fact vanish on the entire horizon scheme, impossible. To see this without imposing a tangent to the new conic, its evaluated section in B_X vanishes at all reduced supports, so it is a scalar times the unique supported nilpotent at u in K[ε]/ε². The perfect multiplication functional λ_X is nonzero on ε: otherwise ε would annihilate every element in the bilinear pairing, since multiplication by any local element scales ε by its scalar part. Since g_X has a nonzero scalar value at u, λ_X(g_X times a nonzero multiple of ε) is nonzero. But λ_X(g_X R_2(X))=0. Therefore the evaluated section in B_X is zero, contrary to quadratic injectivity and the nonzero equation (3).

This conic argument actually works without the no-degree-four hypothesis as soon as some row outside W has a nonconstant fifth power (so y is injective) and the ratio x has at least five distinct values (constant fourth power could no longer allow c≠0).

### 3. The double at g_X=0 is likewise impossible without a degree-four orbit

If g_X vanishes at u, the value equation at the double point gives L_0^5=a_X(u) g_Y. In the L_0=1 chart the normalized g_Y is consequently constant and nonzero. For precision, on changing the representative by a unit L_0^(-1), replace λ_Y by λ'_Y(b)=λ_Y(L_0^7 b), g_Y by g'_Y=L_0^(-5)g_Y and R_2(Y) by L_0^(-2)R_2(Y). Then both orthogonality of the third- and fourth-power images and λ'_Y(g'_Y R'_2)=0 remain exactly valid. The perfect λ'_Y therefore vanishes on the whole quadratic coordinate subspace R'_2 in this chart. Since x is affine in y^5, the latter contains 1,y,y²,y⁵,y⁶ through the monomials 1,y,y²,x,xy. Orthogonality of the cubic and quartic images supplies the two missing degrees: the cubic row of w is y³ and 1 belongs to the quartic image, so λ'_Y(y³)=0; and the cubic row of u is 1 while y⁴ belongs to the quartic image, so λ'_Y(y⁴)=0. Hence λ'_Y vanishes on all monomials through degree six in y. They span B_Y by interpolation at its seven distinct y values. This contradicts perfectness.

## D5. Complete mixed-pair contradiction

### A. Mixed-case preliminaries

Work over the algebraic closure of F17. Use length seven and injective plane quadratics on each horizon. For nonzero line section e, length of its quotient algebra B/(e) is at most four: otherwise eB has dimension at most two, so a nonzero plane linear section is in the kernel of multiplication by e, giving a vanishing nonzero quadratic. This is scheme length, including first jets, not just number of points. The seventh-moment functional λ is perfect on B: the V3×V4 pairing has rank at least 17+24−34=7, and all lower total-degree terms vanish while the top graded restrictions both have dimension seven. The length-seven algebra comes from the actual horizon, not generic projection. In grade five the sole existing quintic kernel maps to g≠0; the unique extra point P_* of the marked quintic cut is rational and smooth, and if it is on the horizon the quintic has a simple zero there. Thus g is a unit except at that possible point, and there it is a valid local parameter on the smooth curve. The fourth-power image and third-power image on Y are orthogonal for λ, from the isolated (3,4) moment component; their ranks sum to at most seven, and the cubic pairing lower bound is four. The separated fifth-power identity uses the isolated (2,5) component and the one-dimensional annihilator of R2 generated by g. These are valid over extensions and for nilpotents.

The altered local representative at a g_X-unit is multiplication by the inverse of a unit fifth root, so powers and λ covariance are honest invertible operations. The g_X-zero parameter is used only at P_* (not substituted at another jet). The effective local third-jet coefficient family on the six eligible Y points is triangular in b with nonzero leading terms. Noncollinear third jets force ≥6 distinct b, then the rank-three quartic image makes P·R linear for a deg≤2 ordinary row and deg≤2 quartic representative; ≥6 distinct values force a polynomial identity, leaving only the same support and then too much bilinear rank. For a collinear third jet when g_Y has one zero q, the six eligible points lie on two pencil lines in a 3+3 split: a 1/5 or 2/4 split would give a quadratic singling out one nonzero λg weight. This conic is unique, q not on it. At q the local jet equations force L0(q)=0, L1(q)≠0, a_Y(q)=0. Any higher-jet support must be the same projective point, intersection of the two-dimensional pencil of forms with q^⊥; the L(t,q)^5 vanishes exactly to fifth order, bounding its local length by five. Outside rows on q^⊥ then all proportional on one triple by μ5 rigidity, leaving only multiplicities (5,2). At the length-five component the quartic t^4 coefficient at q is nonzero, and on the two eligible groups is a combination of the first two quartic coefficients; this fills rank three and forces the other support's ordinary row constant on both groups by combined fourth/fifth equality. This contradicts the split. No third or higher jet survives.

If then a_Y(q)=0, the ≤4 line-length argument forces three doubles and one simple on q^⊥, all doubles transverse. Two unit doubles have L^5−M^5=Δg_Y; Δ=0 gives a nonzero conic even at q, while Δ≠0 gives six distinct ratios r=L/M on T by the transverse derivative formula. A third support gives (A+B r)^5 affine in r^5, impossible by six distinct r and nonzero coefficient of r. Thus a_Y(q)≠0. A simultaneous g_X-zero double then gives locally L(t,q)^5=t a_Y(q) mod t², impossible. In the remaining two-unit-double argument the reused four rows 1,r⁴,r⁵,r^−1 remain defined even at q because its L⁵,M⁵=a_Y(q)≠0; the case of a g_X-zero double in [D2](D-HORIZONS.md#d2) is needed only when g_Y is everywhere a unit. Repeated r fibers really do force both tangent forms into span(L,M); if all supports were also there, the two length-2 tangents and a third support would break line-length cap. Once the r fibers are singletons, nonzero polynomial degree ≤6 cannot vanish at all seven. This verifies exactly one rational curvilinear double u, five simple points whenever X is nonreduced and Y reduced.

In that double chart take rational x=L1/L0 on Y; write ordinary chart rows as affine functions of (x,z). L0 is nonzero everywhere even at possible q by the preceding a_Y(q) conclusion. All rows have fifth power affine in x. The quartic image contains 1,x, and cubic image does too, from value/derivative at u. Set W=span(1,x) in the X projective plane of rows; the double u lies scheme-theoretically on W. If three Y points share x, all outside-W support rows on that fiber have three distinct values with a common (nonzero) fifth power. Rigidity makes their restrictions proportional. Thus all those outside supports lie on one projective line, the span of one such row and the fiber's equation; W covers the rest, including the double. This contradicts quadratic injectivity. Hence x fibers have size≤2. If neither support has degree-four orbit, the splitting degree is a least common multiple of orbit lengths at most seven, not divisible by four, and fifth powers are injective in that field. Distinct points in a common x fiber would agree on all ordinary rows, impossible since the six-strong X support spans by line cap. This verifies the Frobenius logic, including the possibility of extension degrees not prime.

For the determinant step assume some outside row y has nonconstant fifth power. Then x is affine in y⁵, and y is injective on Y because (x,y) are affine coordinates. The quartic image is exactly span(1,y⁴,y⁵), by seven distinct y values, the three indicated independent powers, and the quartic rank bound. For an arbitrary support row P=a+b y+c y⁵ let R=d+e y⁴+f y⁵ be its quartic function. Then PR is affine in y⁵. Reducing against the monic degree-seven y-polynomial, the y⁴ and y⁶ equations are exactly
  (a−W₄c)e−Z₄c f=0,  (b−T₆c)f−S₆c e=0,
where W₄,Z₄,S₆,T₆ are the negatives of the corresponding remainder coefficients of y⁹ and y¹⁰. No other power of PR needs reduction: its exponents are among 0,1,4,5,6,9,10. If (e,f)≠(0,0), the row lies on nonzero determinant conic (a−W₄c)(b−T₆c)−Z₄S₆c²=0. For (e,f)=(0,0), the constant fourth power d is nonzero and PR says b=0 (degree≤5 against seven distinct y). If c=0 this row is u and satisfies the conic. If c≠0, the x-values give distinct values of P, all with the same fourth power, so there are at most four x-values. If all support points lie on the conic and g_X is a unit at u, the conic evaluates to a multiple of the single nilpotent supported at u. Perfectness gives λ_X(ε)≠0 there; unit g_X and λ_X(g_X R2)=0 annihilate that multiple. Thus the conic vanishes on the entire scheme, forbidden. This verifies the distinction between set-theoretic and scheme-theoretic vanishing; when g_X vanishes at the double one must use the other argument.

The normalization on Y is λ′(b)=λ_Y(L₀⁷ b), normalizing g_Y by L₀⁻⁵ and R2 by L₀⁻². It preserves both annihilation and product orthogonality. If g_X(u)=0, then normalized g_Y is constant nonzero. When a nonconstant-fifth outside row y exists, λ′ kills 1,y,y²,y⁵,y⁶ from R2; the third/fourth images give y³,y⁴ against the value row 1. Those seven powers span B_Y, contradicting perfectness. This audit confirms the claimed degree-four necessity in the source. The following upgrades it to complete mixed exclusion.

### B. Analytic root rigidity

Let ζ be a primitive fifth root in characteristic 17. Its minimal polynomial over F17 is Φ5, because ord_5(17)=4. Normalize the first root of the source and target of an invertible affine map to 1 by scaling. For remaining roots write ζ^i,ζ^j and ζ^i′,ζ^j′, with distinct nonzero indices in each pair. An affine map exists only if
  (ζ^j−1)/(ζ^i−1)=(ζ^j′−1)/(ζ^i′−1).
Cross multiply and reduce exponents modulo five. The resulting degree≤4 coefficient polynomial vanishes at ζ, so is a scalar multiple of Φ5; its coefficient sum is zero, so it is zero. Thus δ_(j+i′)−δ_j−δ_i′=δ_(j′+i)−δ_j′−δ_i in F17[C5]. In either side the positive index is disjoint from both negative indices, which are nonzero; coefficient values distinguish sign and multiplicity. Therefore {j,i′}={j′,i} as multisets, forcing j=j′ and i=i′ since j≠i. The normalized affine map is the identity. Undo the scalings: any such map on three distinct roots has zero intercept. This replaces the finite μ5 rigidity check, including when its original coefficients were in the algebraic closure.

### C. Some outside row always has nonconstant fifth power

Assume instead every row outside W has constant fifth power. The constant is nonzero since Y spans its plane; an outside affine row cannot vanish identically. The dual tangent W can hold at most three geometric supports of X because u uses length two in its line-section length cap four. There are therefore at least three outside supports.

**Unit g_X at u.** Normalize to g_X=1 and use t=a_X−a_X(u). The derivative of the fifth identity gives normalized g_Y=5x. Thus λ′ annihilates x R2. Cubic and quartic orthogonality gives λ′(w⁷)=0 for every support row w, hence λ′(w²)=0 for every outside w by its nonzero constant fifth power. Take any such coordinate z. Then λ′(z²)=0; λ′(1)=0 by the rows at u, and λ′(x)=λ′(x²)=λ′(xz)=0 from x R2. If λ′(z)=0 then 1 and x are both in the annihilator of R2, impossible because the pairing is perfect and dim R2=6; they are independent since x is not constant. Thus λ′(z)≠0. Write another outside row w=a+b x+c z with c≠0. Expanding λ′(w²)=0 leaves exactly 2ac λ′(z)=0, implying a=0. All outside rows lie in span(x,z), a line; the others, including the double scheme, lie on W. Their union is a vanishing conic, contradiction.

**Zero g_X at u.** Here λ′ annihilates R2. Scale each outside row z_i so that its constant fifth power is 1. Orthogonality against the u value rows additionally kills λ′(z_i³), λ′(z_i⁴). Thus λ′ kills every polynomial in z_i of degree≤4. Interpolating over its at most five values shows the sum of λ′ weights on each of its fibers is zero. Since perfectness on reduced Y makes all individual weights nonzero, every fiber has size≥2. There are at least three values (otherwise two lines cover Y), so each row has exactly (3,2,2), with triple-level value c_i∈μ5. Two distinct outside rows cannot have proportional linear parts in the affine chart: then they are affinely related, and rigidity on their ≥3 values would make them scalar proportional. Choose three outside rows; in affine coordinates of the first two write z3=α z1+β z2+γ, αβ≠0. On each row's triple fiber the other two each take three distinct values, so the affine relation there has zero intercept. Consequently αc1+γ=βc2+γ=0 and c3=γ. At the intersection of triple fibers 1,2 the third value would be −c3, not a fifth root. Likewise at intersections 1,3 and 2,3 the omitted row would be −c2 and −c1 respectively. Thus the three triples are pairwise disjoint, impossible in seven. This shows a nonconstant-fifth outside row exists also in this case. The seven-power perfectness argument in A now rules out **all** mixed double points with g_X(u)=0.

### D. Unit double: the degree-four remnant also fails

Take the now guaranteed outside row y with nonconstant fifth power. If all supports satisfy the determinant conic, A already closes the case. Otherwise an exceptional constant-fourth-power row P=a+c y⁵, c≠0, witnesses that x takes at most four values. Its fibers have size≤2 in seven points, hence exactly four levels of multiplicities 2,2,2,1. The four levels are an affine image of μ4: the four distinct P values exhaust the fourth roots of one nonzero value, and P is a nonconstant affine function of x.

The four levels form a Galois-stable set because x is rational. Their centroid is rational, and the unique singleton level is rational. Since μ4 is contained in F17 and a singleton level differs nontrivially from the centroid, all four x-values are rational. Each fiber is Galois-stable of size at most two, hence all Y points are defined over F17². The degree-four orbit must therefore occur on X (also see the splitting argument in A). Its five simple supports consist of this orbit and a rational simple v. A rational outside row would take distinct values on a double x-fiber (as its affine slope there is nonzero) while having equal fifth powers there, impossible over F17². Thus v is on W, and the four conjugate supports are all outside W (W is rational and cannot contain the double plus four distinct points).

Use any rational transverse coordinate z on Y. Normalize one member of the four-orbit by its nonzero z coefficient, so its ordinary row is w=z+a+b x, with a,b∈F17⁴. Denote Frobenius by σ. At any double fiber, write its two row values as W1,W2. They are distinct, nonzero, and W1/W2 is a nontrivial fifth root. Since σ² fixes both Y points and inverts μ5, σ² on coefficients has
  (σ² W1)/(σ² W2)=W2/W1,     σ² W1−σ² W2=W1−W2.
These two linear equations give σ² W1=−W2, σ² W2=−W1. The midpoint of the two z-values on this fiber is rational, since the fiber is Galois-stable. It follows that (a+σ²a)/2 + (b+σ²b)x equals the negative z-midpoint on every double level, and its coefficients are rational because two such levels are distinct rational values. Absorb this rational affine function into z. Thus w=z+c(x), and on each double fiber the z-values are ±d_i with d_i≠0, d_i^17=±d_i. The function c is affine over F17⁴ and σ² c=−c.

Write q_i=c(x_i)/d_i. Equality of fifth powers on the pair says (q_i+1)^5=(q_i−1)^5. Expanding and dividing by 10 gives T_i²+2T_i+7=0 with T_i=q_i². Its two roots are nonzero, conjugate under σ (the discriminant 10 is nonsquare in F17), and not negatives of each other, since their sum is −2. Their nonzero quartic difference for w is
  Δ_i=8 d_i⁴ q_i(q_i²+1).
It is nonzero also by simultaneous fourth/fifth equality implying equality of the ordinary values. Hence quartic rank≤3 implies that the image is span(1,x,w⁴). The quartic row of σ w also belongs, so its pairwise difference divided by Δ_i is a single scalar independent of i. Put F(q)=q(q²+1). For σ w the q value is ±q_i^17 since d_i^17=±d_i. The square of the common difference ratio is therefore F(q_i)^34/F(q_i)². The root relation gives F(q_i)²=T_i(T_i+1)²=11T_i. Thus this square is T_i^17/T_i. The two possible T give distinct ratios (if equal the roots would coincide or be negatives). All three double levels must use the same T.

Now choose two of the three fibers with the same sign d_i^17/d_i, by pigeonhole. Their d-values have rational ratio, and their q-values differ at most by sign since the squares T agree. Thus the two nonzero c(x_i) have rational ratio. Distinct rational x_i and affineness give c(x)=η L(x) with nonzero η∈F17⁴ and rational affine L(x), by interpolation at those two levels. All four conjugate support rows are in the single projective line spanned by z and L(x). The double and rational v lie on W scheme-theoretically. The product of the two line equations vanishes on all of X, again violating quadratic injectivity. This excludes the final degree-four mixed case.

## D6. Universal scheme line cap and single-double exclusion

Use [C6](C-CURVES.md#c6), [C5](C-CURVES.md#c5), [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1), [D2](D-HORIZONS.md#d2) and [D5](D-HORIZONS.md#d5). Work over the algebraic closure K of F17. Each actual horizon is a length-seven finite plane scheme, with coordinate algebra B, after trivializing by a rational linear form nonvanishing at all supports. Restriction of homogeneous plane quadratics is injective. The moment functional λ has perfect multiplication pairing, and λ(g R_2)=0, where R_2 is the restricted quadratic subspace. The geometric vector pairing l and its powers satisfy
    rank(l)≤3,   rank(l^4)≤3,   λ_Y(K_3 K_4)=0,   λ_X(K_3 K_4)=0,
    l(x,y)^5 = g_X(x) a_Y(y)+a_X(x) g_Y(y).                         (1)
Here K_j means the one-side image of the j-th power tensor, and the orthogonality is of the two image spaces on the same side. The section g is a unit except possibly at the distinguished smooth rational point P_* if it lies on the horizon. At P_* it is a simple zero on the containing septic; the horizon local ring is curvilinear, and if its length is ≥2 then g is a generator of its maximal ideal. At a simple P_* the g value is zero. Thus Ann(g) has dimension at most one globally.

Throughout one may rescale the chosen local projective representative by a unit. Then degree-j evaluation scales by its j-th power, g and each a by its fifth power, and λ by the inverse seventh power, preserving every identity above. A unit in a finite local K-algebra of length≤7 admits a fifth root, by taking a scalar root and using the finite binomial expansion on its nilpotent remainder (characteristic 17). Thus locally a unit g may be normalized to 1. In a local Artin Gorenstein algebra with perfect λ, the one-dimensional socle has nonzero λ-value: if it did not, every element of that socle would be orthogonal to the whole algebra, since multiplication on it is by the scalar value.

### 1. Every line has scheme length at most three

Let e be its nonzero linear equation, evaluated in B. If the line section had length≥4, then dim eB≤3. Multiplication of the three-dimensional space of restricted linear forms R_1 into eB is injective: any nonzero linear kernel would give a nonzero plane quadratic vanishing on the horizon. Hence dim eB=3 and e R_1=eB. Now λ(g e R_1)=0 from λ(g R_2)=0, so λ(g e b)=0 for every b in B. Perfectness gives ge=0, whence eB⊆Ann(g), impossible because Ann(g) has dimension≤1. This upgrades the previous bound four and is valid also for nonreduced horizons and a possible exceptional distinguished point.

A simple additional consequence: for a scheme with exactly one double and otherwise simple points, if g is a unit at the double, no nonzero conic can vanish on every geometric support. In that event its evaluation can only be a multiple of the unique nilpotent on the double. If this multiple were nonzero, λ(g times the conic) would be nonzero by perfectness on the local socle; so the evaluation is zero, contradicting quadratic injectivity.

### 2. Two general active-chart lemmas in an Artin algebra

Call a fat point on Y active if either (i) g_Y is a unit normalized to 1 and the derivative of a_Y along its curvilinear tangent is nonzero, or (ii) it is the distinguished g_Y-zero and a_Y(p)≠0. In the first case reparameterize the curvilinear local algebra by t=a_Y−a_Y(p); in the second use t=g_Y. Let L(t,x)=L_0(x)+t L_1(x)+⋯ be the ordinary row at this point, with L_0 and L_1 independent linear forms on the opposite plane. On any subscheme B' of X where L_0 is a unit, the previously derived exact equations are
    (L/L_0)^5 = 1+b(x)t                                   (unit case),
    (L/L_0)^5 = 1+b(x)t+Σ_(j≥2)c_j t^j                    (nonunit case),             (2)
where c_j are scalars independent of x. In the first case b=g_X/L_0^5, in the second b=a'_Y(0)/a_Y(0)+a_X/(a_Y(0)g_X) on the unit locus of g_X. We only use the second case on that unit locus. All X variables here are restrictions in the actual finite algebra, not just support values. Ordinary jet coefficient j after dividing by L_0 is degree j in b with leading coefficient binom(1/5,j); this is nonzero for j=0,…,6 in F17. Quartic jet coefficients j=0,1,2 similarly have nonzero leading coefficients binom(4/5,j). For any other Y support row L_w, (1) says (L_w/L_0)^5 is affine in b on B', from the value and derivative equations in (2).

#### A. Third jets in eligible Artin algebras

Assume dim B'≥6 and its geometric support has at least five points. If L_0,L_1,L_2 are independent, then their two ratios to L_0, being polynomials in b, generate the whole affine coordinate algebra of B'. Thus B'=K[b] with minimal polynomial degree dim B' (possibly nonreduced). The first three quartic jets have rank three on B' and exhaust the quartic image there. For any Y support row its normalized ordinary and quartic evaluations have the forms P(b) and R(b), each degree≤2, because the first three ordinary linear forms are a basis and the quartic image is span(1,b,b²). Then P R is affine in b. Since dim B'≥6 this is an exact polynomial identity of degree≤4. Neither P nor R is zero: otherwise the ordinary row vanishes at every geometric support of B', at least five points on a forbidden line. Therefore deg P+deg R≤1. If P is nonconstant it is linear and R constant, but the polynomial P^4−R of degree four cannot vanish in B'. Thus P is constant and the support row equals p projectively (a nonzero ordinary linear form cannot vanish on B'). All Y supports would collapse to p, whose curvilinear local length is then seven. The seven ordinary jet coefficients from (2) have rank at least six on B', by their triangular polynomials in the generator b, contradicting rank(l)≤3. So a noncollinear third jet is impossible in this situation.

If the first three jet forms span dimension two, (2) instead forces b to satisfy a polynomial of degree≤2 on B'. The ratios show that this is exactly a quadratic equation in the two independent projective linear forms L_0,L_1. When B'=B_X this is already a prohibited conic. The case of the eligible algebra of length six after removing a simple P_* needs separate treatment in Section 4.

#### B. Two active fat points with globally unit value rows

Suppose there are two distinct active curvilinear fat points p,q on Y, and their value rows L,M are units on the whole X algebra. Write L',M' for their genuine tangent forms. They cannot both be distinguished zeros. With both g_Y units, (1) gives
    L^5−M^5=Δ g_X,       L^4L'=c g_X,       M^4M'=d g_X
with c,d nonzero. If Δ=0 then L'M=(c/d)M'L in the entire algebra by dividing by L and M. This is a forbidden nonzero conic on X: L,M independent and unique factorization would otherwise make L' proportional to L. If Δ≠0, put r=L/M. Then M'/M is a nonzero constant times r^5−1, and L'/M a nonzero constant times r−r^(−4). Hence the two values and the two derivative rows of the quartic image, after dividing by M^4, span 1,r⁴,r⁵,1/r. This statement does not require g_X to be a unit.

If one point is distinguished with g_Y-zero, let it correspond to L, and use its local parameter t=g_Y. Then for a nonzero scalar α we have L^5=αg_X, and ordinary differentiation at the two supports gives the same formulas as in [D2](D-HORIZONS.md#d2) §3:
    M'/M = (d/α) r^5,
    L'/M = (1/5) r^(−4) + C r,
with d nonzero. Again the span of quartic value and tangent rows is 1,r⁴,r⁵,1/r.

In both cases at least one of L',M' is outside span(L,M): otherwise both length-two tangent truncations on Y lie in the line through p,q, giving scheme length≥4 on that line, contrary to Section 1. In the chart M=1 on the whole opposite horizon, that independent derivative ratio and r give affine coordinates. Both are functions of r in K[r], because r is a unit and its inverse belongs to the subalgebra it generates. Consequently B_X=K[r] has dimension seven. Rank(l^4)≤3 gives a linear relation among 1,r⁴,r⁵,1/r. Multiplying by r yields a nonzero polynomial of degree≤6 in r (exponents 0,1,5,6), impossible for an algebra generator of degree seven. This proof works on the whole nonreduced scheme, not only when r distinguishes seven geometric points.

### 3. A single double with unit g at that double: active opposite points

Suppose X has unique double u and five simple support points, with g_X(u)≠0. If Y is reduced the mixed closure already excludes it; suppose Y is nonreduced. For any fat point p of Y with unit g_Y normalized to 1, the derivative of a_Y along **every nonzero tangent is nonzero**. Otherwise derivative of (1) gives L_0^4 L_1=0 in B_X for a genuine projective tangent, so the six distinct X supports lie on the union of two distinct lines L_0=0 and L_1=0. By line length≤3 these must be disjoint triples of geometric supports. The unique X double cannot lie on the L_1 group: there L_0 is a unit and the identity forces L_1 zero on the double scheme, making its line section length≥4. Thus the conic L_0 L_1 is zero at all supports, impossible by the unit-at-double consequence of Section 1. This excludes a derivative kernel tangent and also forces every such fat p on Y to be curvilinear (a two-dimensional tangent space always has a nonzero vector annihilated by the scalar derivative). If a fat p has g_Y=0, it is curvilinear by its distinguished-point property and the value equation (L_0^5=a_Y(p)g_X) has a_Y(p)≠0, since otherwise all six geometric X supports lie on a line. Hence every Y fat is active.

If g_X is globally a unit, L_0 is a unit on the whole X algebra in every one of these charts by the derivative or value equation. Then Section 2A excludes every Y third jet, and Section 2B allows at most one Y double. As Y is not reduced, it must now also be a double and five simple, with one active chart on each side. This last case is excluded by Section 5 below (and its proof also applies to the present unit-at-double case).

The remaining possibility in this section is that g_X vanishes at a simple q_0≠u. Then the eligible X algebra B' is the product over the other supports, length six, with one double and four simple points. Every fat Y chart has L_0 unit on B' by (1), and the noncollinear-third-jet part of Section 2A applies immediately.

### 4. The sole excluded-from-chart simple point cannot save a third jet

Continue with g_X vanishing at the simple q_0 and g_X unit at the X double. Suppose Y fat p has a collinear third jet. By Section 2A, b has minimal polynomial degree≤2 on B'. It cannot have a single scalar value on its five geometric supports, for then those supports would all lie on one line L_1/L_0=constant. Thus its minimal polynomial is squarefree of degree two. The two idempotent groups of B' lie scheme-theoretically on two different pencil lines, each of length≤3 by Section 1, and their lengths must be exactly 3+3. Since B' has just one double, at least one of its two groups consists of three distinct simple points. The union of the two lines is the unique conic containing B': any degree-two form containing a subscheme of length three on a line restricts identically to zero on that line, so each line is a factor. The point q_0 is not on this conic, by quadratic injectivity on X.

If g_Y is unit at p and L_0(q_0)≠0, the fifth power is constant there, so the first three normalized ordinary jets give the vector for b=0, a third distinct b-value (on B' b is a unit). This contradicts collinearity by the same 3×3 Vandermonde argument. If g_Y=0 at p, its value equation forces L_0(q_0)=0 directly. Thus L_0(q_0)=0 in either case, and L_1(q_0)≠0 because otherwise q_0 is on both lines of the conic. The value equation if g_Y unit, or the first coefficient in the parameter t=g_Y if g_Y zero, now implies a_X(q_0)=0. Consequently all Y geometric supports lie on q_0^⊥. Every third jet of Y has its linear pencil span equal to the space W of the two conic-line equations, by uniqueness above. Its base support is the unique projective point of W∩q_0^⊥ (L_1(q_0)≠0). Thus p is the only third-jet support. Further l(t,q_0)^5=0 and l(t,q_0) has order one at p, so its local length m is at most five.

Use the simple three-point group on one of the two eligible X lines. Choose another form U in q_0^⊥ independent from L_0; it is outside W. On the simple triple in the chart L_0=1, U/L_0 takes three distinct values because L_1/L_0 is fixed there. Any other Y support row w on q_0^⊥ is affine nonconstant in this coordinate; its fifth power is constant on the triple by (2), and nonzero because its three values are distinct. The exact μ5 affine rigidity in [D5](D-HORIZONS.md#d5) B says two such rows must be proportional there, hence represent the same projective support in q_0^⊥. For clarity the rigidity applies because division by respective fixed fifth roots makes triples of distinct fifth roots of unity, and an affine map between such triples has translation zero. Thus at most one support besides p exists. The total length seven and m≤5 leave only split (5,2).

At p of length five, the first two quartic jet rows, divided by L_0^4 on B', span the two group-constant functions because their normalized coefficients of degrees zero and one in b are independent. They both vanish at q_0. The quartic t^4 coefficient is nonzero at q_0 since L(t,q_0) has order one. Its restriction on B' is still a function of the same semisimple b, so subtract a linear combination of first two rows to produce a nonzero row supported only at q_0. These three rows exhaust rank(l^4)≤3. Thus all Y support rows on the simple triple have both fourth and fifth powers constant in the chart. Coprime powers 4 and 5 force ordinary values constant there as scalars, including zero. A form constant on this simple triple lies in W; it also annihilates q_0, hence equals p projectively. This contradicts split (5,2). Notice that no step needs fourth/fifth powers to identify nilpotent coordinates on the nonsimple group.

There are therefore no third jets in Y. Being of length seven, Y now has at least four geometric supports (all multiplicities≤2). In every fat Y chart L_0 is globally a unit on X: failure at q_0 when g_Y unit forces a_X(q_0)=0 and hence all Y supports on q_0^⊥, impossible with at least four; if g_Y zero at a fat point, its value at q_0 is zero and its first derivative similarly forces a_X(q_0)=0. Thus Section 2B applies, giving at most one Y double. The resulting paired single-double case is closed next.

### 5. Complete single-double exclusion

#### A. If Y is another single-double horizon and u is unit

Here both have six geometric supports. For X's own unit-g double u, the argument of Section 3 with sides rotated shows its derivative is active, **even if g_Y vanishes at Y's own double**. In the zero-derivative branch the double on Y must lie on the L_0 line in the two disjoint triples as before. If it has unit g_Y, the socle prevents the support conic. If it has nonunit g_Y, then L_0(q)=0 at that distinguished q and (1) at the normalized g_X=1 value point u gives a_Y(q)=0. Then all six X support points would lie on q^⊥, impossible. Also the value row L_0 is globally a unit: if g_Y has an exceptional q and L_0(q)=0, the same all-support contradiction applies, while on unit points the active derivative ensures L_0 unit.

Normalize the affine chart L_0=1 on Y and let x=L_1/L_0. For each support w of X, its normalized row P_w is affine linear in the plane chart and P_w^5 is affine in x. There exists an outside row y (outside W=span(1,x) in X) whose fifth power is nonconstant **as an element of B_Y**. In fact line cap3 and the double u in W imply at least four outside support rows. If all were constant-fifth, all their constants are nonzero since Y has six supports. Thus they all have zero derivative along the genuine tangent at the Y double. Their forms lie in the line annihilating that tangent vector in the affine chart, which contains the constant row u, so holds at most two outside rows. Contradiction.

Then y^5=A+B x with B≠0, so y generates the whole Artin algebra B_Y of length seven. The quartic image is span(1,y⁴,y⁵), using the value and derivative at u and the y row; those three functions are independent by the degree-seven minimal polynomial. If y vanishes at the unique Y double, its derivative in this chart is nonzero because it generates that local double. Then x' is zero there by differentiating y^5=A+B x. For every X support row, differentiating its affine-in-x fifth power identity gives (P_w(q)^4 P'_w(q)=0). Hence all X supports lie in the union of the lines of forms vanishing at q and annihilating its affine tangent. These are a nonzero conic through all supports, forbidden since g_X is unit at the sole X double (Section 1).

If y is nonzero at the Y double, use the determinant conic calculation of [D4](D-HORIZONS.md#d4) §2, now in the monogenic length-seven algebra rather than requiring distinct y values. Explicitly (P=a+b y+c y^5), (P^4=R=d+e y^4+f y^5) and (PR) affine in y⁵. Reduce modulo the degree-seven minimal polynomial of y; the degree-four and degree-six coefficient equations give
    (a−W_4 c)e−Z_4 c f=0,    (b−T_6 c)f−S_6 c e=0,
with W_4,Z_4,S_6,T_6 fixed negatives of the corresponding remainder coefficients of y⁹,y¹⁰. These equations apply unchanged even when the minimal polynomial has a repeated root. If (e,f)≠(0,0) the nonzero determinant conic ((a−W_4 c)(b−T_6 c)−Z_4 S_6 c²=0) holds. If e=f=0, then P⁴=d is constant, with d≠0 since P cannot vanish at all six Y supports. Comparing dP affine in y⁵ yields b=0 by minimal degree seven. If c≠0, the derivative of (P=a+c y^5) at the double is nonzero since y and its derivative are both nonzero there. Its fourth power could not be constant (P itself is a unit), contradiction. Hence c=0 and P corresponds to u, also on the conic. It follows that the conic vanishes on all X supports, impossible by its unit-g double again. This finishes the branch of X with unit g at its double.

#### B. If X's unique double u is the distinguished zero

Take its local parameter t=g_X. If Y had any distinguished zero q, then (1) on the t-jet against q gives (ell(t,q)^5=t a_Y(q)). The scalar on the left is zero at t=0, so its fifth power is zero modulo t² and a_Y(q)=0. Evaluation at q would put all six X supports on a line. Thus g_Y is globally a unit. Normalize it to 1. The value and first coefficient of (1) at the X double say
    E^5=a_X(u),        a_Y=5 E^4 L_1−a'_X(0),                  (4)
where E=l(u,·) and L_1 is the X first-jet linear form.

We first show a_X(u)≠0. Otherwise all Y supports lie on E=0 (hence at most three), and (4) gives that the derivative of a_Y on every tangent at every fat Y point is zero. At any such point p and any nonzero tangent, differentiating (1) makes the six X supports lie on the two disjoint triples of its value and tangent lines by line cap3. The X double u is on the value line because E(p)=0, and therefore the tangent linear form is nonzero at u. In particular the linear differential of E on the tangent space at each fat p has no nonzero kernel. Thus each fat p is curvilinear and E vanishes to exact order one there. Since E⁵=0, all fat lengths are at most five. There cannot be two distinct such fat points p,p': their value lines on X are distinct and meet at u, each containing two disjoint other simple X points. The tangent line of p must contain both simple points of the value line of p', so equals that value line, incorrectly containing u. Thus Y has only one fat point and at most two simple supports. Length seven and max fat length five force exactly (5,1,1). Then E⁴ is a nonzero socle element at the lone fat point and zero on the other supports. Choose some X support x with (l(x,p)
e0) (six supports cannot lie in its perpendicular line). The cubic row (l(x,·)^3) times E⁴ is a nonzero multiple of this socle; cubic–quartic orthogonality would make its λ_Y value zero, contradicting perfectness. This proves the claim.

The value row E is therefore a unit on the entire Y scheme. Normalize E=1 in Y's plane chart; then g_Y becomes a constant nonzero function (rescale λ accordingly). Let x=L_1/E and W=span(1,x). Fifth powers of all X support rows are affine in x. Again an outside support row y must have nonconstant fifth in B_Y. Suppose otherwise. For any fat Y point p take a nonzero tangent; the outside rows with nonzero constant fifth kill that tangent in the affine chart, so lie on one line through u and are at most two. If there are at least two distinct Y supports, a zero-constant-fifth row has to vanish on all those supports, yielding at most one projective row. If Y is punctual curvilinear of length seven, such a row must vanish to order≥2 (order one has nonzero fifth power), again at most one projective row, the tangent line form. If Y is punctual noncurvilinear, its two-dimensional tangent space fills its affine plane, so nonzero-constant-fifth rows would have to be affine constants and not outside, and zero-constant rows all lie on its perpendicular line, at most three. All cases contradict the at least four outside supports of X (its line W already contains the length-two u). Thus choose y with (y^5=A+B x) nonconstant; y generates the full length-seven Y algebra.

Since normalized g_Y is constant, λ_Y kills the quadratic coordinate space. In particular λ_Y kills (1,y,y²,y⁵,y⁶) using x affine in y⁵ and the quadratics x,xy. By the cubic–quartic orthogonality it also kills y³ and y⁴ (use y's cubic or quartic row, and the value row 1 in the other degree at u). These seven powers are a basis of the monogenic length-seven algebra, even if nonreduced. This contradicts λ_Y perfect, completing the exclusion.

## D7. Directional lower bound and the initial small-support exclusions

Use [C5](C-CURVES.md#c5), [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1) and [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6). Work with a hypothetical septic mate pair X,Y of size 34 satisfying all prior reductions. The geometric horizon schemes are length seven with injective plane quadratics and length≤3 per projective line section, and their seventh-moment functionals λ have perfect multiplication pairing. For a direction d, a homogeneous linear form (ℓ_d) on the actual X horizon normalized by a chart form, the actual seventh moment is
    (M_7^X(d)=λ_X(ℓ_d^7)).
This follows from the graded restriction in [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1), rather than assuming a standalone local algebra necessarily arises from actual points. The form vanishes on the opposite (D(Y)) by spectral uniformity and the F17 seventh power sum. On any component, a unit change of representative scales the input by its ordinary linear degree and λ by its inverse seventh power, so the polynomial remains the same. The product pairing on the finite algebra is perfect componentwise, and λ at any sole local socle is nonzero: annihilating its generator would annihilate its products with all functions.

### 1. A stronger universal directional count: |D|≥59

Let (n=|D(Y)|), (w(v)) the number of unordered actual secants of direction (v), and (k_L=|D(Y)∩L|) over projective lines L in its direction plane. The previous [C5](C-CURVES.md#c5) gives (w(v)≤10), the actual line cap (k_L≤6), (2≤k_L) for this septic, and every (k_L=2) line has actual weights summing to 17 (each of the seventeen parallel affine level planes contains exactly two marks). The total secant weight is (binom(34,2)=561), hence (n≥57). By standard projective point/pair incidence the previous proof gave
    (Σ_L (k_L−3)(k_L−6)=n²−145n+5526),
so the number T of 2-secants is at least (⌈(n²−145n+5526)/4⌉): each 2-line contributes four, and all contributions with k=3 through 6 are nonpositive.

Let the weight deficit at v mean (10−w(v)≥0). On each direction 2-secant the sum of the two deficits is exactly three. A direction is contained in 18 total projective lines, so
    (3T≤18Σ_v  (10−w(v))=18(10n−561)).
At n=57 this gives T≤54 versus T≥128; at n=58 it gives T≤114 versus T≥120. Both are impossible. Thus **n≥59** on either actual mate. This universal sharpening uses no new spectral hypothesis.

### 2. A directional pencil test for seventh-moment zeros

Choose rational independent linear forms (A,B,H) on the X plane horizon; write directions as ([a:b:c]) relative to their coefficients. On each affine fiber (a=1,b) fixed, the form (M_7^X(1,b,c)) is a polynomial in c. If degree≤3 with its cubic coefficient everywhere nonzero, it has at most three roots on each of the seventeen affine lines. The remaining projective line (a=0) contains at most six of the actual (D(Y)) by its line cap. So (|D(Y)|≤57), contradictory. Likewise, if degree≤2 with nonzero quadratic coefficient except at at most one b value, the count is at most (16·2+6+6=44) (or smaller), using the actual cap on the one exceptional line. Variants allow a coefficient with a unique zero pencil line on the projective quotient ([a:b]): among the 18 pencil lines through ([0:0:1]), on every nonexceptional line all points except the common vertex are parametrized by c and there are at most d roots if the leading c^d coefficient is nonzero. An exceptional pencil contains the common vertex and at most six of the direction set. Hence one exception gives (17d+6), and no exception at most (18d+1). These are counts of actual directions, not assumptions that all polynomial zeros are directions.

### 3. Exclude every punctual X

Its unique geometric support is rational by Frobenius. Choose a rational chart trivializing there, so A=1 locally; choose affine linear F,H vanishing at that support and giving rational projective basis (1,F,H). Its local algebra B has residue field F17.

**Curvilinear.** Choose H the rational tangent line form. It has order 2 or 3 by line cap3, and F can be any transverse form. If H has order two, H^4=0 and (H³) is nonzero socle. The top coefficient of (M_7^X(1,b,c)=λ_X((1+bF+cH)^7)) is the constant (binom(7,3)·λ_X(H³)≠0), since H³ kills all nilpotents. If H has order three, H³=0 and H² nonzero socle; likewise its quadratic top coefficient is the constant (binom(7,2)·λ_X(H²)≠0). Section 2 contradicts n≥59 in both cases.

**Noncurvilinear, nonstretched.** Write m for the maximal ideal. Its local Hilbert function is ((1,2,2,1,1)) or ((1,2,1,1,1,1)): h_2=3 would give ((1,2,3,1)) with the 3→2 multiplication radical adding a socle; if h_2=2 the alternative terminal h_3=2 is non-Gorenstein; if h_2=1 standard graded growth stays at most one and has no gaps. In the nonstretched case ((1,2,2,1,1)), multiplication into m⁴ from (m/m²) times (m³/m⁴) is a nonzero linear functional on (m/m²) up to scalar. It is defined over F17. Choose rational H from its one-dimensional kernel (the rational affine line coefficients give all of m/m² since the actual point has full plane tangent), and F another independent form. Then (H·m³=0) exactly, using m⁵=0. It follows (H⁴=0) and H³ is in the socle: multiplication by F gives (H·(H²F)=0) as H²F∈m³. If H³≠0 we have the same nonzero constant cubic top coefficient as in curvilinear contact2.

If H³=0, then H²≠0: otherwise the image of multiplication by H is contained in its kernel, whereas line cap3 forces kernel length≤3 and image dimension≥4. We have (H²F²=H·(HF²)=0). The element (H²F), if nonzero, lies in the socle because it is killed by H and F (which generate m). If H²F=0, H² itself is in the socle. Accordingly the quadratic top coefficient
    (21{λ_X(H²)+5b·λ_X(H²F)})
is a nonzero constant or a nonzero affine linear polynomial, so Section 2 applies. The terms involving F² and higher in the coefficient vanish as just noted. Perfectness makes each listed nonzero socle λ value nonzero, independently of g.

**Noncurvilinear, stretched.** For Hilbert sequence ((1,2,1,1,1,1)), choose a rational tangent linear F with (F⁵≠0). This exists by polarization since (m⁵≠0) and degree five is less than 17 (a nonzero two-variable degree-five form over F17 cannot vanish at every rational input). Choose a complementary rational tangent linear H with HF∈m³. The exact stretched normal form from the prior note gives (H=y+cF²+…, yF=0, y²=α F^5, α≠0). For completeness subtract a polynomial of degrees 2 through 4 in F to make yF=0 (F³,F⁴,F⁵ span m³), and then y² belongs to the kernel of multiplication by F on m², namely span F⁵. Its coefficient cannot be zero, or y is a second socle. Thus H³=0, (H²=c²F⁴+ρ F⁵), (H²F=c²F⁵), and H²F²=0. If c≠0, H²F is a nonzero socle giving nonzero slope in the same quadratic top coefficient. If c=0, H²=(α F^5) is already a nonzero socle giving nonzero constant. Section 2 again contradicts n≥59. This excludes every punctual horizon.

### 4. Two-support partitions

Two different local lengths make their support points u,v both rational. Let H be their rational joining line, and choose rational complementary forms A,B with values (1,0) and (0,1) at (u,v) respectively (choose rational vector representatives). Use A as local chart trivialization at u, B at v, adjusting each λ summand by its appropriate seventh unit power. Then the scalar parts of aA+bB+cH at u and v are a,b, and (M_7^X) is the sum of the λ contributions of these two components. The H-section of the two local schemes has total length≤3; the other support contributes at least one, so at either component it is at most two.

**Type (4,3), all local structures.** If the length-four component u is curvilinear and H is transverse there, H³ on that component is a nonzero socle; no other component contributes to c³ because the opposite length is three. The coefficient is constant nonzero on a=1, so Section 2 gives ≤57. Otherwise H cuts u to local length two: for a nontransverse curvilinear u this follows from total cap3; at an embedding-two Gorenstein length-four u, (m^3=0), (dim m²=1) and its symmetric binary tangent multiplication is nondegenerate by perfectness, so every nonzero line induces ideal of dimension two, hence section length two. In either case H³=0 on u, and H² there is zero or a socle. At v H must then cut length one, making its length-three algebra curvilinear and H a parameter; H² is nonzero socle. Thus the whole c-degree is≤2, and coefficient c² on a=1 is (21(λ_u(H²)+b^5λ_v(H²))), with second scalar nonzero. Fifth powers are a permutation of F17, so at most one b is exceptional. Section 2 gives contradiction. This proves the full (4,3) exclusion.

**Type (5,2).** If the length-five u is curvilinear and its tangent line contains v, the joining H has order two at u (by cap3), H² its nonzero socle, and the length-two side has H²=0. Thus a constant nonzero quadratic top coefficient excludes. If u is noncurvilinear, length five and Gorenstein force Hilbert sequence (1,2,1,1) and m⁴=0 (terminal h_2=2 would be a nonperfect socle). Then H³ is zero or a sole socle on u, and H³=0 on v, so any nonzero H³ gives the constant cubic case. Otherwise H² on u is nonzero, since a square-zero multiplication would require dim B_u≤2 dim(B_u/HB_u)≤4, contrary to length five. H²B (where B is the local transverse linear coordinate, not the algebra) is either zero or a socle because m⁴=0; if zero, H² itself is socle. Thus the same affine b-coefficient argument as in Section 3 gives degree two with at most one exception. This excludes noncurvilinear length five in (5,2). The curvilinear length-five case with v off its tangent remains.


### 5. Three collinear supports of certain partitions

When three different supports are collinear, their line H is rational by Frobenius. Line cap3 means it cuts length exactly one at each. Thus every fat component is curvilinear and H a parameter there. If the partition is (4,2,1), the length-four point is rational, and its H³ is nonzero socle while the other components have H³=0. The degree-three leading coefficient is a nonzero scalar times the fourth power of the value linear function of (a,b) at the length-four support, because the c³ coefficient uses four remaining factors. It vanishes at only the one pencil where that scalar vanishes. The degree-three pencil test excludes (4,2,1) with collinear support.

For (3,2,2), only the rational triple support contributes to c², giving a nonzero scalar times its value linear form to the fifth power, also with just one exceptional pencil. For (3,3,1), the two triple supports u,v contribute nonzero multiples of the fifth powers of their two value linear forms (ℓ_u(a,b),ℓ_v(a,b)); the simple support contributes no c². If both triples are rational, their ratio is an injective projective coordinate away from its pole, and fifth power is injective on F17*. Thus their sum has at most one exceptional pencil. If they are conjugate, choose conjugate projective representatives. For rational (a,b)≠(0,0) neither value vanishes (both would by conjugacy, impossible since u,v independent on their joining line). Their ratio belongs to the norm-one subgroup of F_(17²)*, of order 18, and is injective as a Möbius ratio of these independent linear forms. Fifth power is injective on that subgroup as well. Hence equation of the two fifth powers has at most one rational pencil solution, and again the degree-two pencil test excludes. This uses the actual Frobenius alternative, not an assumption that both triple supports are individually rational. The collinear (5,1,1) case is not included.

## D8. All longer components excluded

### 2. A new small-support line restriction

On the actual $X$ horizon, $M_7^X(d)=\lambda_X(\ell_d^7)$ vanishes at every actual opposite direction. Each local contribution vanishes completely when $\ell_d$ has scalar zero there: any length-$m\le7$ local algebra has $\mathfrak m^m=0$. The weight of a simple component is nonzero by perfectness. Thus on the rational direction line $u^\perp$ annihilating a rational support $u$ one may erase its whole local contribution, not just its scalar term.

* **$(6,1)$, all local structures:** the unique long support $u$ and simple support $v$ are rational. On $u^\perp$ only the nonzero multiple of $\ell_d(v)^7$ remains, with exactly one rational zero (the common annihilator). This contradicts the ≥2 actual directions on every rational line.
* **Noncollinear $(5,1,1)$:** the unique long $u$ is rational. On $u^\perp$ the two simple contributions are $\alpha s^7+\beta t^7$, with $\alpha,\beta\ne0$, and $s,t$ independent there by noncollinearity. If the two sites are rational, the projective ratio and invertibility of seventh power on F17* give at most one rational zero; the poles/zeros of the ratio are not additional roots. If conjugate, choose conjugate representatives and retain the rational initial chart, so $t=s^{17}$ on rational vectors. Neither is zero there, and $s/t$ is injective into the order-18 norm-one group. Seventh powers also permute that group. Again there is at most one rational zero, disallowed. In the collinear case the two forms on $u^\perp$ are instead proportional with nonzero factor; the necessary conclusion there is that their two contributions cancel identically on the line. The stronger final elimination of this case uses §3.

### 3. A new long-row lemma, including the distinguished-zero cases

**Lemma.** Let an actual $X$ horizon component $A$ be curvilinear of length four or five, or embedding dimension two of length four. Then the ordinary value form $E(y)=\ell(u,y)$ at its support $u$ vanishes at **every geometric support of $Y$**. This lemma does not require four or more supports on either side.

We use the exact identity $\ell^5=g_Xa_Y+a_Xg_Y$, ordinary pairing rank at most three, scheme line cap three, and the same cubic–quartic orthogonality as above. In particular $\lambda_Y(E^7)=0$ because $E^3$ and $E^4$ are ordinary value rows in the respective cubic and quartic images. If exactly one simple support were off $E=0$, its contribution to $\lambda_Y(E^7)$ would be nonzero and all other contributions would be zero, an impossibility.

**Curvilinear, unit $g_X$.** Normalize $g_X=1$ on $A=K[t]/t^m$, $m=4$ or $5$, and put $\widetilde a=a_X(t)-a_X(0)$. Let $B'$ be the product of whole opposite components on which $E$ is a unit. On $A\otimes B'$,
$$
(\ell/E)^5=1+\widetilde a\,b,\qquad b=g_Y/E^5.
$$
If $\dim B'\ge4$, the first genuine ordinary tangent form cannot vanish on $B'$, by scheme line cap. This identity forces $\widetilde a$ to be a parameter. In that parameter the first four normalized ordinary jets are $\binom{1/5}{j}b^j$, $j=0,1,2,3$, with nonzero diagonal coefficients. In fact all ordinary coefficient forms divided by $E$ are in $K[b]$. They generate $B'$ as an algebra: the ordinary forms on the full $X$ component span the three-dimensional projective linear space, since otherwise a line would contain the length-four truncation, and their ratios to $E$ therefore give the affine plane coordinates of $B'$. Consequently $B'=K[b]$ and $1,b,b^2,b^3$ are linearly independent if $\dim B'\ge4$, even for a nonreduced algebra (minimal polynomial degree equals its length). This would contradict $\operatorname{rank}\ell\le3$. Thus $\dim B'\le3$, and an $E$-zero support exists.

If some $E$-zero support $p$ has $g_Y(p)\ne0$, evaluate separation at $p$ in $A$. The fifth power of its scalar-zero row is zero as $m\le5$, so $\widetilde a=0$. Otherwise all $E$-zero supports are the one distinguished $p$, whose component then has length at least four because $\dim B'\le3$. Take its parameter $s=g_Y$. If $L(t)$ and $M(t)$ are the value and first jet forms there, comparing the coefficient of $s$ at variable $t$ and at $t=0$ gives $\widetilde a=5L(t)^4M(t)$ since $L(0)=0$. Thus $\widetilde a$ starts at order four (and is zero at $m=4$). In either case, on $B'$ the positive ordinary coefficients of orders 1 through 3 vanish: the unit-normalized root of $\ell/E$ modulo $t^4$ is the unique fifth root of 1 with constant term 1. This uniqueness follows from $(1+N)^5-1=N(5+\text{nilpotent})$.

At least two of these three coefficient forms are independent, because their span together with $E$ must be all of the projective linear form space (the length-four truncation is not on a line). Two independent linear forms have a common zero which is at most one reduced point, scheme-theoretically. Hence $B'$ is empty or a one-dimensional simple component. The latter is forbidden by $\lambda_Y(E^7)=0$. This establishes all supports on $E=0$.

**Curvilinear, distinguished $u$.** Use $t=g_X$, and write $A_0=a_X(0)$. If $A_0\ne0$, then $E^5=A_0 g_Y$, so on the full unit-$g_Y$ subalgebra, $E$ is a unit. Dividing by $E^5$ gives $1+t b+\sum_{j\ge2}c_jt^j$ with scalars $c_j$ and $b=a_X'(0)/A_0+a_Y/(A_0g_Y)$. The ordinary fifth root has triangular leading coefficients $\binom{1/5}{j}b^j$, and the same affine generation plus ordinary rank argument forces that subalgebra to have length at most three. Hence the opposite distinguished zero must exist and be fat, of length at least four. But at it $E^5=A_0 g_Y=A_0s$ is impossible: $E$ has scalar zero and its fifth power has zero first coefficient, while $s$ is a parameter. Therefore $A_0=0$ and the identity $E^5=0$ puts every support on $E=0$.

**Embedding dimension two, length four.** Here $g_X$ is unit because the distinguished point is smooth on the containing septic and hence its horizon component is curvilinear. Normalize $g_X=1$. Choose a nonzero tangent in the scalar derivative kernel of $a_X$; its genuine linear form $M$ has $E^4M=0$. Thus the whole off-$E$ scheme lies in $M=0$ and has length at most three. An $E$-zero support exists. With a unit such support, evaluating the fifth separation as above forces $a_X$ constant on $A$. Otherwise sole $E$-zero $p$ is again distinguished and fat. Taking its $s=g_Y$ coefficient gives $a_X-a_X(u)=5L^4M'$, with $L\in\mathfrak m_A$. This is zero because $\mathfrak m_A^4=0$. On the whole off-$E$ subalgebra every positive (not just tangent) coefficient of $\ell$ vanishes by the unit fifth-root identity. These coefficient forms contain at least two independent linear forms because the length-four component is not on a line. As before the off-$E$ subalgebra is empty or one simple, and the latter contradicts $\lambda_Y(E^7)=0$. This proves the lemma.

### 4. Finish the length-five and then length-four exclusions

After §2, the only surviving partitions with a length-five component are $(5,2)$ with a curvilinear long point and the other support off its tangent, and **collinear** $(5,1,1)$. In the latter, line cap makes the support line cut length one at the long point, so the local algebra is also curvilinear. Apply the long-row lemma at that point of $X$. All $Y$ supports lie on its value line, so $Y$ has at most three collinear supports. Punctual $(7)$, two-support $(6,1),(4,3)$, and collinear $(4,2,1),(3,3,1),(3,2,2)$ are already impossible. Therefore $Y$ also has one of the two surviving length-five types. Apply the long-row lemma to its long point as well.

At the two long points, the dual ordinary value lines now coincide on each side with their rational support-joining lines. Each is transverse at its long point, by the remaining $(5,2)$ condition or the scheme length cap for three collinear supports. The bilinear form in the two curvilinear parameters has scalar zero and nonzero linear part $\alpha t+\beta s$ with both coefficients nonzero. Its fifth power therefore has a nonzero coefficient $10\alpha^2\beta^3$ on $t^2s^3$ in $K[t,s]/(t^5,s^5)$. Higher-degree terms of the form cannot affect a total-degree-five coefficient. Normalize each $g$ to 1 if unit and otherwise choose it as its parameter. The exact separated right-hand side $g_X(t)a_Y(s)+a_X(t)g_Y(s)$ then has **zero** coefficient on $t^2s^3$ in all four marked/unmarked combinations. Contradiction. All length-five types are excluded.

A length-four component is impossible by the long-row lemma: its annihilator would contain the other support in one line, and the punctual, two-support, and collinear-three-support cases were excluded above or in D7. Thus every component has length at most three.

## D9. Triple with four simple sites

Use [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1), [D5](D-HORIZONS.md#d5) and [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6). All statements concern the actual geometric horizon schemes, over the algebraic closure K of F17. We use the strengthened universal line-section cap **three** in scheme length, exact fifth-power separation, quartic rank≤3, perfect λ and λ(g R_2)=0, plus cubic–quartic orthogonality on each side. Let X have a length-three local component at u and four simple geometric components. The length-three algebra is curvilinear: if its maximal ideal had embedding dimension two then its square would be zero and its socle would be two-dimensional, incompatible with the perfect Gorenstein pairing. Let Y have at least four geometric supports. If Y is reduced the audited mixed closure already excludes it. Thus suppose Y has a fat point.

### 1. Active charts are global units here

At a unit-g_Y fat point p, normalize g_Y=1 locally. A nonzero tangent derivative of a_Y is called active. Its value and tangent forms L_0,L_1 on X satisfy (5L_0^4 L_1=cg_X) with c≠0. Hence L_0 is a unit at every unit-g_X support. If L_0 were zero at the possible g_X-zero q, the value equation gives a_X(q)=0 and all geometric Y supports would lie on q^⊥, contrary to having at least four. Therefore L_0 is a unit globally. At a distinguished fat point p with g_Y=0, the value equation gives (L_0^5=a_Y(p)g_X), with a_Y(p)≠0 since X has five distinct geometric supports. If g_X had any zero q, the first coefficient in the parameter t=g_Y gives a_X(q)=0 as before (L_0(q)=0), again putting all Y supports on a line. Hence in this case g_X and L_0 are globally units, and this is the nonunit-g_Y active chart.

Consequently for an active **curvilinear** fat Y point, the third-jet monogenic argument of [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6) §2A applies on the entire X algebra, giving local length at most two. The collinear case directly contradicts quadratic injectivity, and the noncollinear case uses dim B_X=7 and five supports. Section 2B permits at most one active Y fat point. These uses are symmetric later: for an active curvilinear X triple with Y having at least four supports, its whole-algebra chart is again unit (each side has >3 supports), and the same §2A proof applies. The one place it needs enough geometric supports is that a nonzero linear form cannot vanish on all of them, here ensured by line cap3 even for four supports. Thus the X triple cannot be active either.

### 2. Inactive tangent forces one specific configuration

Let p be a unit-g_Y fat point, normalized to 1, with some genuine tangent along which the derivative of a_Y is zero. Its value/tangent linear forms L_0,L_1 on X are independent and satisfy (L_0^4L_1=0) on the entire X algebra. Every simple X support outside L_0 is therefore on L_1, and its full scheme portion where L_0 is a unit is annihilated by L_1.

First (L_0(u)=0). Otherwise L_1 vanishes on the full local length-three scheme at u, exhausting its line capacity; all four simple X supports must be on L_0, also impossible.

If (L_1(u)=0), the two lines both contain u and meet only there. The four simple X supports must lie on their union, hence exactly two on each. They both cut the local triple to order precisely one by line cap3. The product (L_0 L_1) is therefore a nonzero socle element at u and zero at all other supports. If g_X is unit at u this contradicts λ_X(g_X R_2)=0 and perfectness. If g_X vanishes at u, the value equation (l(p,u)=0) with g_Y(p)=1 instead yields a_X(u)=0, putting all Y supports on the line u^⊥, impossible. Thus (L_1(u)
e0).

If L_0 had order one on the triple at u, the first cubic jet (3L_0^2 L_1) on X would be a nonzero multiple of its sole u-socle and zero on every simple support (each such support has L_0 or L_1 zero). Choosing an actual Y support with nonzero pairing at u is possible by cap3. Its fourth-power row times the jet would have nonzero λ_X value, contrary to orthogonality. Hence L_0 must have order at least two at u. It cannot cut order three: then no simple X support lies on L_0 and four would lie on L_1. Therefore the exact order is two. The line L_0 contains u and one of the four simple X points, whereas L_1 omits u and contains the other three distinct simple X points. In particular L_0 is the unique tangent line at u, so the projective Y support p of any inactive tangent is unique.

This also verifies all Y fat points are curvilinear. A noncurvilinear point with g_Y=0 cannot occur by smoothness of P_*. At a unit-g_Y point with tangent dimension two, choose a nonzero tangent in the derivative kernel of a_Y and obtain the above L_0(u)=0, order two. If some tangent had nonzero derivative, its fifth derivative equation would give (5L_0^4L_1'=c g_X) with c≠0 even on the local triple at u. The left vanishes there while g_X is nonzero in that local algebra (unit or simple parameter), contradiction. Thus only curvilinear fat points remain.

### 3. An inactive Y point cannot have length three or larger

In the above configuration expand the local ordinary rows as (L(t)=L_0+tL_1+t²L_2+cdots) modulo its curvilinear local parameter. In a length≥3 local ring the second coefficient in fifth-power separation is a scalar (a_{Y,2}) times g_X on X (g_Y=1 locally). Evaluated on the triple at u it is zero: both terms (5L_0^4 L_2) and (10L_0^3L_1²) are zero since L_0 has order two. As g_X is nonzero there this forces (a_{Y,2}=0). At the three simple X points off L_0, where L_1 is zero and L_0 nonzero, this same identity forces L_2=0. The second cubic jet

is therefore zero on all four simple X supports; both terms vanish at the simple support on L_0 as well. On the X triple at u, its first term is zero and its second is an exact order-two nonzero socle element (L_1(u)≠0 and L_0 order two). Multiplication by a fourth-power row from a Y support not perpendicular to u has nonzero λ_X value, again contradicting cubic–quartic orthogonality. Such support exists because Y has at least four geometric supports. Thus all Y fat points are doubles.

### 4. The only possible two-double type also fails

The preceding results give at most one active and one inactive Y double. Y is nonreduced, and a single double with five simple sites is already excluded by [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6). The only candidate Y is therefore (2,2,1,1,1), with one active q and one inactive p. But (E_0(p)=0), giving four different Y supports on the E_0 line. Contradiction.

The proof uses exact local coefficient identities and dimension, not an enumerative subcase convention. No external action.

## D10. Two doubles with three simple sites

Use [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1) and [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6), including its strengthened **scheme** line cap three, perfect multiplication functional λ with λ(g R_2)=0, exact separated fifth power, quartic rank≤3 and cubic–quartic orthogonality on each side. All computations are in the actual finite algebra over an algebraic closure K of F17. Denote X's length-two support points by u,v and its three simple geometric points by a,b,c. Its doubles are curvilinear automatically (a local plane algebra of length two has one tangent direction). Suppose Y has at least four geometric supports. By the already audited mixed exclusion it is not reduced, so it has a fat point; we also assume the independently audited earlier single-double exclusion.

A fat point with unit g is called *active* if the derivative of a_Y after normalizing g_Y=1 is nonzero in some tangent direction. A distinguished g_Y-zero fat point will automatically count as active in this setting. If a genuine tangent has zero derivative of a_Y at a unit-g_Y point, call it an inactive tangent; the discussion below proves there is no noncurvilinear ambiguity left between these cases.

### 1. Global active charts

At a unit-g_Y fat point p with an active tangent, write L_0,L_1 for its ordinary value and tangent linear forms on X. Then (5L_0^4 L_1=cg_X) with c≠0, so L_0 is a unit at each eligible unit-g_X support. If its value is zero at an exceptional g_X-zero q, the value equation forces a_X(q)=0 and all Y supports would lie in q^⊥, impossible with at least four. Thus L_0 is globally a unit. At a distinguished g_Y-zero fat point, (L_0^5=a_Y(p)g_X), with a_Y(p)≠0 since X has five supports. If g_X vanished at q, the first coefficient in the local parameter t=g_Y evaluated at q would give a_X(q)=0 (the fifth power of a form with scalar zero has zero first coefficient), again putting all Y supports on a line. Thus in this case g_X and L_0 are globally units and the point is active, with curvilinear local parameter t=g_Y.

As soon as a fat Y point is known curvilinear, Sections 2A–B of [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6) apply to these whole-algebra active charts: active local lengths cannot exceed two (the first three jets would either force a conic or force monogenic rank >3), and there can be at most one active fat point. These sections apply here with dim B_X=7 and five geometric supports.

### 2. Every inactive value row is a specific transverse line through both X doubles

At a unit-g_Y fat point p with an inactive tangent, normalize g_Y=1 and write L=L_0 and M=L_1 for the ordinary value and tangent forms. They are independent, and
    L^4 M=0 in B_X.                                              (1)
Each support not on L=0 is contained scheme-theoretically on M=0 because L is a local unit there. Thus the union of whole local components supported on L=0 has total length at least four, since the M line has section length at most three. In particular L contains at least one X double.

If L contains only one X double u, it must also contain two simple points to have that total length. These three supports exhaust its line capacity, so L has local order one at u. The complement—one double v and one simple—has whole length three on M, so M does not contain any support on L. The conic L M is then supported only at the u component, an exact nonzero order-one socle there. If g_X(u) is a unit this contradicts λ_X(g_X R_2)=0 and perfectness. If g_X(u)=0, value separation at p,u gives a_X(u)=0 since g_Y(p)=1 and L(u)=0, putting every Y support on u^⊥. This too is impossible. Thus L contains both X doubles. A line through them is unique, so the projective point p of any inactive tangent is unique. Since L is zero at both doubles, the same distinguished argument now proves **both X doubles have unit g_X** whenever there is an inactive Y point.

There are two possibilities for simple points on L. If none, M contains all three simples and cannot contain either u or v (its length is full). The line L cannot be tangent to both doubles (length≥4); if tangent to just one, the conic L M is nonzero only at the other double's socle, contradicting λ_X(g_X R_2)=0 because both g_X values are units. Thus L is transverse at both. If L has one simple, it is automatically transverse at both doubles by line cap3; M cannot contain both doubles since L and M independent, and if M contains just one double the conic L M again has exactly one nonzero socle, forbidden. In either case **L vanishes to order one at both doubles, M nonzero at both, and at least two distinct simple X supports lie off L and on M**. A second simple on L would already violate cap3.

There are no noncurvilinear Y fat points. If g_Y=0 they are smooth by the distinguished-point property. Otherwise a two-dimensional tangent space has a nonzero tangent in the derivative kernel; the above geometry then gives (L(u)=L(v)=0). Any other tangent with nonzero derivative would yield the active equation (5 L^4 M'=c g_X), impossible even at u, with unit g_X(u). If all tangent derivatives are zero, choose a nonzero tangent vector whose associated form vanishes at u, since that evaluation is a scalar linear functional on a two-dimensional space. This contradicts the required M(u)≠0 for every inactive tangent. Thus every Y fat point is curvilinear, and active or inactive as specified. There is at most one of each type, so a Y with three doubles is already impossible.

### 3. Inactive length four or larger is impossible

At the unique possible inactive p normalize g_Y=1 near p and write the true local expansion
    L(t)=L+t M+t²N_2+t³N_3+⋯ .
The coefficients of t^j of (L(t)^5) at X's doubles vanish for j=1,2,3 (when defined): every summand has at least (5-j≥2) factors L, and L²=0 on those components. By (1)'s underlying fifth-power separation, these coefficients are precisely (a_{Y,j} g_X). Since g_X is unit at both doubles, (a_{Y,j}=0). At the at least two simple X points off L, where L is unit, the fifth power of the row is consequently constant through the same orders. Uniqueness of a fifth root of fixed scalar in a nilpotent algebra (or elementary coefficient induction using 5 invertible) gives (M=N_2=N_3=0) there. Thus all three tangent-order forms are proportional to the line equation M through these two distinct simple points. If the local length of p were at least four, its length-four truncation would lie in the projective line spanned by L and M on Y. Indeed each of its vector-valued coefficient vectors through order three is in that span, so the line equation annihilates its entire truncated algebra. This violates scheme line cap3. Active points already cannot have length≥3, so no Y component of length at least four occurs.

### 4. An inactive triple and an active double cannot coexist

Assume p is an inactive triple and q is the other fat point, an active double. Section 3, truncated through order two, gives (a_{Y,2}=0) and (N_2=0) at all simple X points off L, along with M=0 there. At a simple X point on L the quadratic-order cubic jet is zero too. Thus the cubic second jet
    J=3 L² N_2+3 L M²
is zero on the simple X supports and is a nonzero socle element at **both** doubles, since L is transverse and M nonzero there. Under the perfect λ_X each of these two socle contributions has nonzero coefficient. Multiplication by the fourth-power row as a function of the opposite Y point therefore gives, by cubic–quartic orthogonality,
    U^4=C V^4             in the whole B_Y,                       (2)
where U,V are the ordinary value forms of X's two double supports, normalized by the nonzero fifth roots of their own g_X values, and C≠0. The equation is an identity of the entire algebra, not just individual support values: apply λ_X to J times the fourth-power tensor, or test its pairing with every functional on B_Y. On the X double components, multiplication by the fourth-power tensor acts on each socle exactly by its ordinary value to the fourth power, which gives the displayed formula up to the nonzero constants.

In the same normalization, separated fifth power gives
    U^5−V^5=Δ g_Y                                                  (3)
for a scalar Δ. On the active Y double q the ordinary values U,V are units by its globally unit chart. Equation (2) says r=U/V has constant fourth power, hence r is itself constant on that local ring (fourth power has invertible derivative at a unit). If q has unit g_Y, normalize to 1 locally. The derivatives of U^5 and V^5 there are the same nonzero derivative of a_Y. Since (U^5=r^5 V^5) with constant r, this gives (r^5=1), and (3) then gives Δ=0. If q is its distinguished zero, use t=g_Y. At its support (3) gives r⁵=1; local constancy again gives r⁵=1 on the double, so (3) says Δt=0, whence Δ=0. Thus (U^5=V^5) on all Y. For every geometric Y support except p both ordinary values are nonzero, since by (2) vanishing of either forces both and two distinct value lines meet only at p. Equations (2) and (3) force a single ratio (U/V) by the coprime powers 4,5. Hence all those supports, and p as well (both values zero), lie on one projective line of Y. This contradicts at least four supports. Therefore the inactive triple plus active double type (3,2,1,1) is impossible. Triple with four simples, if needed, was already excluded by [D9](D-HORIZONS.md#d9) with sides reversed.

### 5. Identical two-double type

We have excluded Y's single double, three doubles, any component of length≥4, and the triple+double type. The only remaining nonreduced Y with at least four supports is (2,2,1,1,1), since the triple with four simple points was handled in the earlier note. It has exactly one active and one inactive point. Apply the same analysis with X and Y exchanged: X also has one active and one inactive double. The value of X's inactive point is perpendicular to both doubles of Y by Section 2 on the reversed pair, including Y's active double. But the active Y value row was proved a unit on the entire X horizon in Section 1, so it cannot vanish at that inactive X support. This contradiction completes the claimed exclusion.

All steps are analytical, with exact small coefficients in characteristic 17 and the previously verified underlying moment identities. No standalone artificial subset census or publication.

## D11. Three-double pairings

Use [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1) and [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6) for the actual perfect horizon pairings, separated fifth-power equation, quartic rank≤3, cubic–quartic orthogonality and scheme line cap3. Work over the algebraic closure. In type (2,2,2,1) all fat points are curvilinear doubles. A fat point with unit g is active when its derivative of a after local normalization g=1 is nonzero; a fat distinguished g-zero is also active. The active charts in Section 1 of [D10](D-HORIZONS.md#d10) are globally unit on the opposite horizon as soon as both have at least four support points: a zero at any exceptional g-zero point would force all current supports onto its perpendicular line. The distinguished active chart uses its actual parameter g. Hence the two-active obstruction ([D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6) §2B) applies unchanged, and each horizon has at most one active double.

Let p be an inactive double of Y, with value and genuine tangent forms L,M on X. Its derivative identity is L⁴M=0. All whole X components with support off L=0 lie on the tangent line M=0 scheme-theoretically, of total length at most three. Thus the total lengths of the local components supported on L=0 are at least four. Since X has three doubles and only one simple, L=0 contains at least two of its double support points (one double and the only simple have mass at most three).

Each horizon has at least two inactive doubles. If X had an active double, no inactive Y value could vanish at it: that active X value form is globally a unit on Y, and the pairing itself is symmetric as a dual pairing. Therefore each inactive Y value line would have to go through the same two other X doubles. Its projective value would then be unique, impossible for two different Y double support points. Thus X has no active double, and by symmetry neither does Y. All six double points are inactive.

The three X double supports are not collinear: if they were, all three pair lines would coincide, again giving a unique possible Y inactive point. Similarly on Y. The three distinct Y double support values therefore correspond to the three distinct pair lines of the X double supports, necessarily each used once. Choose those X supports as a vector basis, and label the Y double rows such that they are nonzero scalar multiples of the respective coordinate linear forms x_1,x_2,x_3 on X.

For distinct i,j, the value row x_i³ belongs to the cubic image on X, and x_j⁴ to its quartic image (scalar row factors do not matter). Orthogonality gives λ_X(x_i³ x_j⁴)=0. At any of the three X doubles at least one of x_i or x_j has scalar value zero; a scalar-zero linear form has its square zero on a length-two local algebra, so the product contributes zero there. Thus all the contribution comes from the simple support point a:
    λ_X at a times a_i³ a_j⁴ = 0.
The simple weight is nonzero by perfectness. It follows there is no pair of nonzero coordinates a_i,a_j with i≠j, so the simple projective support is on one of the coordinate axes, exactly one of its three double supports. This contradicts the partition.



### The mixed four-support pair (2,2,2,1) versus (3,2,1,1)

Let X be the orientation with three doubles, and Y have its length-three component at p and its double at q. The component of length three is curvilinear: a local length-three algebra of embedding dimension two has two-dimensional socle, incompatible with perfectness. It cannot be active by [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6) §2A applied to its globally unit chart on X. The monogenic proof there needs an opposite support not on a line to ensure row functions nonzero; four supports suffice by cap3. Thus p is a unit-g_Y inactive support, and its value line L=0 contains at least two X doubles by the weight-four argument above.

**Collinear X doubles.** In this case L contains all three, and is the only possible inactive Y value line. The distinct Y double q must be active. Every X double is perpendicular to p and hence inactive (otherwise its own active chart would have unit value everywhere on Y). Each of these three distinct inactive X value lines on Y must avoid q because q is active, and each must contain supports carrying whole local mass at least four (off-line components fit in the tangent line cap3). Therefore each line must contain p of length three and at least one of Y's two simple points. There are only two such lines, contradiction.

**Noncollinear X doubles.** Now p's line L contains exactly two X doubles u,v. Both have unit g_X: if either distinguished g_X vanished there, the value separation at the normalized inactive p gives a_X=0 at that double, putting all four Y supports on its perpendicular line. If the X simple point is not on L, the other X double and the simple fill length three on the tangent line M. Thus M does not contain either u or v. At most one tangent to L is possible by cap3, but then the conic L M would have exactly one nonzero double socle on X, contrary to λ_X(g_X R_2)=0. If instead the simple point is on L, both doubles are automatically transverse to L by cap3. The tangent line M cannot contain both doubles because it differs from L, and if it contained one the product L M would again have only the other double's nonzero socle. Thus in either subcase L is transverse at both u,v and M nonzero at both.

Expand the unit-normalized ordinary p rows through t² as L+t M+t²N. Since L²=0 on both on-line doubles, the second coefficient of ((L+t M+t²N)^5) vanishes there; this is (a_{Y,2}g_X), so that scalar is zero. Off L, M=0 by the first coefficient and L a unit, so the second coefficient forces N=0 on the whole off-line algebra. The cubic second jet (3L²N+3LM²) is then zero everywhere except the two nonzero socles at u,v. Exactly as in [D10](D-HORIZONS.md#d10) §4, cubic–quartic orthogonality yields an identity (U^4=C V^4) in B_Y, with C≠0 and U,V the g_X-normalized value forms of u,v. Also (U^5−V^5=Δ g_Y).

If Y double q is inactive, its value line on X contains two of the three noncollinear doubles, and it is a different line from p's. It therefore contains exactly one of u,v. The scalar equation (U(q)^4=C V(q)^4) is impossible. If q is active, U,V are units on its local double, so their ratio, of constant fourth power, is locally constant. If g_Y is unit, the two derivatives of the normalized fifth powers at this active double are equal and nonzero, forcing that constant ratio to have fifth power 1. If q is the distinguished g-zero, its scalar already has fifth power 1, and the local identity then forces Δ=0 because t=g_Y is a nonzero first-order parameter. The derivative argument also gives Δ=0 in the unit case. Thus (U^5=V^5) globally. As also (U^4=C V^4), any geometric point with nonzero ordinary values has one fixed ratio; at a point with either zero, both vanish. All Y supports are consequently on a line, contrary to four distinct support points. This closes the mixed four-support pair.

The remaining exactly-four-support pairings are (2,2,2,1) versus (4,1,1,1), the two types (3,2,1,1) and (4,1,1,1) paired with each other, and either of those two paired with itself. No finite search, outside action, or publication.

## D12. Two triple-double horizons

Use [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1), [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6) and the active-chart arguments in [D10](D-HORIZONS.md#d10). Work over the algebraic closure. The actual horizon algebras have scheme line cap3, injective quadratics, perfect λ with λ(g R_2)=0, the separated fifth identity, and cubic–quartic orthogonality. Every local triple is curvilinear: an embedding-dimension-two local length-three algebra would have two-dimensional square-zero maximal ideal and nonperfect socle pairing. Doubles are curvilinear as well.

Both horizons have four geometric supports. An active fat value row is therefore globally unit on the opposite scheme, including the distinguished-zero case, by the same argument as [D10](D-HORIZONS.md#d10) §1: at a hypothetical exceptional zero, a fifth value or first coefficient would put all four current supports on the corresponding perpendicular line. Any active triple is excluded by the monogenic third-jet argument of [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6) §2A. In that argument the only use of the number of geometric supports is that a nonzero linear form cannot vanish on them all, so the strengthened cap3 suffices with four supports. A distinguished zero triple would be active (its a value is nonzero, else all four opposite supports are on a line). Thus both actual triple points are inactive unit-g points.

### 1. The value lines of inactive points

Write X's partition as triple u, double v, simple a,b. At any opposite inactive fat point p of Y (normalized to unit g_Y=1), denote its value and genuine tangent forms by L,M. They are independent, and the derivative of the fifth identity says L⁴M=0 on X. Thus all whole local components supported off L are scheme-theoretically on M, with length at most three, and the local components supported on L have total length at least four.

Every such L contains u. Otherwise, to carry this mass, it contains v,a,b (three distinct geometric points), so its vanishing order at v is one by line cap3. The full triple at u lies on M, exhausting its cap and making M(v)≠0. Therefore L M evaluates to exactly one nonzero socle element at v and zero elsewhere. If g_X(v) is unit this contradicts λ_X(g_X R_2)=0 and perfectness; if g_X(v)=0, the fifth separation at p,v with g_Y(p)=1 and L(v)=0 gives a_X(v)=0, putting all Y supports on v^⊥, also impossible.

Suppose L contains u but not v. It must then contain at least one of a,b. If it contains both, it is transverse at u by cap3. If M(u)≠0, the cubic first jet (3L²M) is a sole nonzero socle at u (all other components are on L or lie scheme-theoretically in M). A fourth-power row with nonzero value at u exists because four Y supports cannot lie in u^⊥. This contradicts cubic–quartic orthogonality and perfectness. If M(u)=0 instead, M contains both the full double at v and the support u, so it is transverse at u by cap3. Now L M is a sole nonzero socle at u, impossible by λ_X(g_X R_2)=0 for unit g_X(u) or by the same distinguished-zero perpendicular-line argument. Consequently L contains exactly one simple. Its complement (v double plus the other simple) fills length three on M, so M(u)≠0. The cubic first jet again forbids L having order one at u. Since there is one simple on L, cap3 forces exact order two at u, the unique tangent line there.

Moreover this last configuration (omitting v) is impossible if p has length at least three. Write its ordinary jet through order two as L+t M+t²N. The second coefficient of its fifth power is zero on the triple at u since L has order two; by separation it is (a_{Y,2}g_X), and g_X is nonzero on that local component even at its possible simple-parameter zero. Thus (a_{Y,2}=0). Off L, the linear form M vanishes throughout by the first coefficient and L is unit, so the second coefficient forces N=0 there as well. The second cubic jet (3L²N+3LM²) is then a sole nonzero socle at u, giving the same orthogonality contradiction.

We have proved: every inactive value line contains the X triple; an inactive triple value line contains **both** X fat supports; any inactive value line omitting the X double is specifically the tangent line at the X triple.

### 2. Apply symmetrically

Let Y's triple and double be p,q. Its inactive triple p has a value line through both u and v on X. Symmetrically X's inactive triple u has value line through both p and q on Y. The perpendicularity of u with q prevents q from being active (a globally unit active value row on X cannot vanish at u). Likewise v is inactive. Since q's value line cannot equal p's distinct line through u,v, it must omit v. It is therefore the unique tangent line at the X triple u. Symmetrically v's value line is the tangent line at the Y triple p.

Now look at p's ordinary value and first tangent forms L,M on X. The latter evaluates zero at v because v's value line on Y is tangent to p. Since L contains both u and v and M independent of L, this forces M(u)≠0. If L were transverse at u, the first cubic jet (3L² M) would again be the sole u-socle: at v the scalar-zero L has square zero in a double, and at either simple support one of L,M vanishes. Orthogonality forbids it as before. Therefore L too is tangent along u. This is the same unique line already occupied by q's value, contradiction to p and q being different geometric support points.

## D13. Completion of the both-nonreduced branch

### 1. Setting, exact hypotheses, and residual partitions

Use [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1) for the exact horizon identities and [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6) for the scheme line length bound. Work throughout over K, the algebraic closure of F17. Each horizon is a length-seven scheme in its projective plane, with Artin coordinate algebra B after any trivialization of O(1) nonvanishing at every support. The two planes are paired by the nondegenerate ordinary linear pairing l, and K_j denotes the one-side image of its j-th power tensor. We use only:

* No nonzero homogeneous plane conic restricts to zero on either horizon; the three-dimensional space of linear forms is also injected.
* Each line meets the horizon in scheme length at most three.
* rank(l^4) ≤ 3 and the exact identity l^5 = g_X a_Y + a_X g_Y, where g and a are restrictions of degree-five forms/sections. The only possible zero of g is a distinguished support. On a fat component there, g is a first-order parameter since the distinguished point is smooth on the septic curve.
* By [D8](D-HORIZONS.md#d8), all actual local lengths are at most three; any horizon with exactly three geometric supports is noncollinear. With total length seven, each horizon has at least three supports, so in all cases here its geometric supports span the plane.
* By the partitions covered in [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6) and [D9–D12](D-HORIZONS.md#d9), two nonreduced mates cannot both have at least four geometric supports. Thus one must have exactly three, with partition (3,3,1) or (3,2,2).

At lengths two and three in this proof, the local algebras are curvilinear. Indeed a length-three local algebra of embedding dimension two would have square-zero maximal ideal of dimension two, contradicting one-dimensional Gorenstein socle ([D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1) gives a perfect multiplication functional). We may write a length-m component as K[t]/(t^m). Rescaling a local projective representative by an arbitrary unit rescales the restrictions of j-th degree forms by the j-th power of that unit, preserving both the rank identities (multiplication by a unit on the whole product algebra is invertible) and the separated identity. Thus g can be normalized to 1 on any component where it is a unit: use a scalar fifth root and the finite nilpotent binomial expansion. At a distinguished fat point, use parameter t=g instead. The first ordinary jet row at a fat point is a linear form independent of its scalar value row because the plane tangent is a genuine immersion of its length-two subscheme.

We call a fat point **inactive** if g is unit and the normalized a coefficient of t is zero. It is **active** if g is unit and that coefficient is nonzero, or if g is zero at that point. For the latter type, a(p) cannot be zero: evaluating l^5 at p would otherwise put the entire opposite geometric support on a line. The normalized derivative criterion is independent of local parameter because its change at first order is nonzero.

### 2. Every active fat point has a globally unit value row

For an active point of X, write l(t,y)=E(y)+tF(y)+.... Let g,a in the following paragraph on the opposite side mean g_Y,a_Y.

If g_X=1, the first coefficient in l^5 is a nonzero multiple of g_Y, so E^4 F = c g_Y with c nonzero; therefore E is unit at every opposite support where g_Y is nonzero. If E vanished at an opposite distinguished support v, the value equation E^5=a_Y+a_X(p)g_Y there would force a_Y(v)=0. Evaluating the separated identity at (x,v) would then annihilate all X geometric supports against v, contradicting their spanning the plane.

If g_X=t, the value equation is E^5=αg_Y with α=a_X(p) nonzero. If g_Y vanished at an opposite support v, E(v)=0, and the first coefficient equation 5E^4F=a_Y+a_X'(p)g_Y forces a_Y(v)=0. This same spanning contradiction rules out any opposite distinguished zero in this case. Thus E is again globally a unit. These arguments do not require four geometric support points or reducedness of the opposite scheme.

### 3. No active triple, including when the opposite has only three supports

Assume an X active point has local length three. We use B=B_Y, trivialized in the global affine chart E=1, legitimate by §2. In the first active case, change t to a_X-a_X(p); in the second keep t=g_X. The separated identity becomes

    (l/E)^5 = 1 + b t + c t²

in B[t]/(t³), where c is a scalar (zero in the first case). In the first case b=g_Y/E^5 and a_Y/E^5=1-a_X(p)b. In the second E^5=αg_Y, so b=a_Y/E^5+a_X'(p)/α and g_Y/E^5 is constant. Thus for every ordinary support row W of X the function (W/E)^5 is affine in b.

The normalized ordinary rows of orders zero, one, two are 1, b/5, and a polynomial of degree two in b with nonzero leading coefficient binom(1/5,2)=4 in F17. If the three underlying linear forms are dependent, their relation gives a quadratic relation with nonzero quadratic coefficient in b (the first two forms are independent). Homogenizing it in E and the genuine first jet form gives a nonzero homogeneous plane conic vanishing on all of Y, impossible. Hence the three forms are independent. The ratios of the second and third to the first are a full pair of affine coordinate functions in the E-chart; they generate B as a K-algebra because Y is a finite closed subscheme of this affine plane. Both ratios belong to K[b], so B=K[b] and the minimal polynomial of b has degree seven.

The first three normalized quartic rows, pulled from (l/E)^4, have triangular degrees 0,1,2 in b and nonzero leading coefficients (4/5=11 and binom(4/5,2)=4 in F17). They therefore exhaust the quartic image by rank(l^4) ≤ 3. Given any support row W of X, choose polynomial representatives P=W/E and R=(W/E)^4 of degrees at most two in b. For P use the ordinary basis from the triple, and for R use the three quartic rows. Both are nonzero, since Y spans and W is a nonzero linear form, so W/E is nonzero at some geometric support. As PR is affine in b and the degree of the difference is at most four, degree-seven minimality makes this a polynomial identity. If P is nonconstant it must be linear and R constant. But then P^4-R is a nonzero polynomial of degree four vanishing in B, again impossible. Thus P is constant, so the linear form W is genuinely proportional to E because their appropriate difference is zero on the spanning geometric Y support. Every X support would coincide projectively with the active support, contradicting its spanning the plane. This proves no active triples.

### 4. At most one inactive fat point on a side

At an inactive X point p normalize g_X=1. As its a derivative is zero, the first coefficient of the fifth identity gives E_p^4 F_p=0 in B_Y, with E_p the value row and F_p its genuine first jet form. On every whole opposite local component where E_p is a unit, F_p vanishes scheme-theoretically. The total length of these whole components is at most three, by the line length bound on F_p=0. Consequently the geometric supports on E_p=0 carry at least four in local lengths. This is a statement about the sum of whole local lengths at those supports, not the scheme intersection length with E_p=0.

Suppose there are two distinct inactive points p,q. Their normalized value equations give

    E_p^5 - E_q^5 = Δ g_Y

for a scalar Δ. If Δ=0, their scalar-zero support groups coincide. But distinct projective p,q give independent linear forms; their common annihilator contains only one geometric projective point, and that component has length at most three. This contradicts the mass at least four. If Δ≠0, the two groups of mass at least four in total length seven have some common geometric support v. At it g_Y(v)=0 by the difference equation, and a_Y(v)=0 by E_p^5=a_Y+a_X(p)g_Y. The separated identity at v now annihilates all X support points against v, again contradicting the spanning X support. Hence there can be at most one inactive fat point. In particular this proof works whether the overlapping support, hypothetically, was fat, simple, rational, or not.

### 5. At most one active fat point on a side

For completeness this is the ordinary two-active whole-algebra argument from [D6](D-HORIZONS.md#d6), with the global unit scope justified explicitly by §2. Suppose two active X points have value rows L,M and genuine tangent rows L',M', respectively. Both L,M are globally units on B_Y. Put r=L/M. All divisions here are in the entire opposite Artin algebra, so no local components are omitted.

If both active points have g_X unit, write L^5-M^5=Δg_Y, L^4L'=c g_Y, M^4M'=d g_Y with c,d nonzero after normalizing local g_X=1. If Δ=0, division gives L'M=(c/d)M'L. This is a forbidden nonzero conic relation: if the formal polynomials were equal, unique factorization and L independent of M would force L proportional to L', impossible. If Δ≠0, exact division gives

    M'/M = (d/Δ)(r^5-1),       L'/M = (c/Δ)(r-r^(-4)).

If instead the point with row L is distinguished, L^5=αg_Y with α nonzero. Differentiating at it and using the value equation at M gives 5L^4L'=M^5+Cg_Y for a scalar C, while M^4M'=d g_Y with d nonzero. Thus

    M'/M = (d/α) r^5,        L'/M = (1/5)r^(-4) + (C/(5α))r.

There cannot be two distinguished points. In either allowable case, the ordinary value and first tangent rows of the quartic tensor, divided by M^4, span 1, r^4, r^5, r^(-1). (The tangent rows are 4L^3 L', 4M^3 M'; the factor four is invertible.) At least one of L',M' lies outside span(L,M), since otherwise the line through the two active X supports contains both full length-two tangent truncations, total length at least four. That independent tangent and r are affine coordinate functions in the whole M-chart. As both belong to K[r] by the displayed formulas (r invertible implies its inverse is a polynomial in r in this finite algebra), B_Y=K[r] has dimension seven. Rank(l^4) ≤ 3 forces a nonzero linear relation on 1,r^4,r^5,r^(-1). Multiplying by r gives a nonzero formal polynomial of degree at most six (distinct exponents 0,1,5,6), impossible in a length-seven algebra generated by r. Thus at most one active fat point exists.

### 6. Application and remaining scope

In (3,3,1), the two triple points must both be inactive by §3, contrary to §4. In (3,2,2), the triple is inactive, so both doubles must be active by §4, contrary to §5. This completes the both-nonreduced case. Together with the mixed exclusion, any remaining pair would be geometrically reduced on both sides.

## D14. Both reduced horizons must be marked

### 2. New reduced gate: every actual horizon must contain its distinguished point

Start with both algebraically reduced horizons and the original rational nonvanishing chart forms. The exact separated identity $\ell^5=g_Xa_Y+a_Xg_Y$, the perfect multiplication functionals, and $\lambda(g R_2)=0$ hold over $\mathbb F_{17}$. We may use rational sections in that identity because it is a rational linear system (or directly the rational construction in [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1)). Here $R_2$ is the full six-dimensional image of quadratic restrictions, whose annihilator under the perfect multiplication pairing is exactly span $g$.

For each actual ordinary $X$ support row $E_x(y)=\ell(x,y)$, its own cubic and fourth rows are mutually orthogonal, so $\lambda_Y(E_x^7)=0$. Define the homogeneous conic on the $X$ plane
$$
Q_X(x)=\lambda_Y\big(a_Y E_x^2\big).
$$
Multiplying the fifth identity by $E_x^2$ and applying $\lambda_Y$ gives $g_X(x)Q_X(x)=0$ on every geometric $X$ support (the $a_X$-term vanishes by $\lambda_Y(g_YR_2)=0$). If $g_X$ is nonzero at every support, $Q_X$ is then identically zero as a formal conic: reducedness upgrades support vanishing to zero restriction, and quadratic restriction is injective. Since the squares of ordinary linear forms span the quadratics, by polarization and nondegeneracy of $\ell$, this says $\lambda_Y(a_YR_2)=0$. Consequently $a_Y$ is proportional to $g_Y$, and the fifth identity factors as $\ell^5=(a_X+c g_X)g_Y$.

If $g_Y$ vanished at any support $y_*$, this factorization would put all $X$ support on its perpendicular. This is impossible since $X$ spans. Thus $g_Y$ is likewise everywhere nonzero, and reversing the argument shows also $a_X$ proportional to $g_X$. We obtain
$$
\ell^5=Cg_Xg_Y,\qquad C\in\mathbb F_{17}^{\!*}.
$$
The proportionality constants are rational by the rational underlying sections; $C\ne0$ because the support planes span and $\ell$ is nondegenerate. This already excludes configurations in which exactly one of the distinguished points is on the horizon.

It remains to exclude simultaneous zero-free $g$. Normalize each support representative by a fifth root to make all pairwise ordinary dot values lie in $\mu_5$, absorbing $C$ by a rational fifth root. Call a support *short* when its Frobenius orbit degree is not four. In a reduced length-seven scheme this means exactly the degrees not divisible by four. Every horizon has at least three short sites. At a short point the required fifth root of its $g$ is unique in its own residue field and normalization can be made there. Any pair of short points lies in the compositum of two fields of degrees not divisible by four, hence the compositum does not contain any nontrivial element of $\mu_5$ (the order of 17 in $ (\mathbb Z/5)^\times $ is four). **All short-by-short ordinary dot values are therefore 1.** This conclusion uses field-specific representatives, not arbitrary chosen roots.

If one horizon is entirely short, two distinct short opposite row vectors have equal evaluations on its seven spanning points, so coincide, impossible. Thus each horizon has one four-orbit and exactly three short sites. Subtracting any two different short $Y$ rows (or columns) shows the short $X$ sites are collinear. In fact their normalized $X$ representatives are $x_0+t_i x_1$ for three distinct scalars $t_i$ and independent vectors $x_0,x_1$: their projective span is two-dimensional in vector coordinates, and evaluation at a fixed short $Y$ site puts all of them in the affine slice with value 1.

For any $Y$ site $y$, its three evaluations are $\alpha(y)+t_i\beta(y)\in\mu_5$, with $\alpha=x_0\cdot y$, $\beta=x_1\cdot y$. The sites with $\beta=0$ are on one line. Among the others, the three evaluation values are distinct. Any two such triples are related by an affine map $z\mapsto Az+B$, $A\ne0$, taking three distinct fifth roots to fifth roots. The affine root lemma below forces $B=0$. Hence all sites with $\beta\ne0$ share one ratio $\alpha/\beta$, giving one further line. Their union would contain the entire reduced $Y$ scheme and is a forbidden nonzero conic. Therefore both $g_X$ and $g_Y$ must each vanish at their own distinguished rational support.

**Affine fifth-root lemma over characteristic 17 (coefficient comparison).** An affine map taking three distinct elements of $\mu_5$ to $\mu_5$ and having nonzero slope must have zero intercept. Multiply source and target by fifth roots so the first input and its image are 1. Choose a primitive root $\zeta$. The two remaining normalized exponents $i,j$ are distinct and nonzero mod 5, as are their images $i',j'$. Equality of differences gives
$$
\zeta^{j+i'}-\zeta^j-\zeta^{i'}=\zeta^{j'+i}-\zeta^{j'}-\zeta^i.
$$
For exponents read modulo 5, equality of these sums is equality of the coefficient vectors: the only possible dependency is a multiple of the all-ones coefficient vector because $1+z+\cdots+z^4$ is irreducible over F17, and the difference of the coefficient sums is zero, whereas 5 is invertible. Each positive index differs from either negative index on its own side, since $i,i',j,j'\ne0$. Thus the positive indices agree and the negative multisets match. The swapped match would give $j=i$, so $j=j',i=i'$. The normalized affine map fixes two distinct points, hence is the identity. Undo the normalizations to get zero intercept as claimed.

## D15. The final two-cubic contradiction

### 1. Abstract marked hypotheses

Work over the algebraic closure K of F17 (indeed the short lemma below works in characteristic not 2, 3, or 5). The two horizon planes come from dual three-dimensional vector spaces, with ordinary nondegenerate bilinear pairing l. Each horizon consists of seven distinct geometric points; no line contains four. Each has one distinguished point x_* or y_* at which its section g vanishes and six eligible points elsewhere, at which g is nonzero.

A projective representative is chosen at each point, so restrictions of homogeneous forms are ordinary evaluation vectors. Write λ_X, λ_Y for the degree-seven functionals. The assumptions from [D1](D-HORIZONS.md#d1) are:

* Cubic restriction on either horizon is surjective to all functions on its seven points.
* λ has perfect multiplication pairing, so at every simple point its coefficient is nonzero.
* λ(g Q)=0 for every homogeneous quadratic Q on the corresponding plane.
* On each side the images of l³ and l⁴ from the opposite side are mutually orthogonal under the λ multiplication pairing. In particular, for any two opposite geometric point representatives u,v, λ(l(u,·)³ l(v,·)⁴)=0.
* On the pair of horizons, l⁵ = g_X a_Y + a_X g_Y, for the degree-five vectors a and g.

These facts remain exactly true after independently rescaling any support representative by s: the value of any degree-j form or vector scales by s^j, and the corresponding λ coefficient scales by s^(-7). This also preserves surjectivity of cubic restriction. Passing to K preserves perfectness and surjectivity; no constraint concerning whether the eligible points are rational is required.

### 2. Dual affine gauges from the two marks

The separated fifth identity gives l(x_*,y_*)=0. It also gives

    l(x_*,y)^5 = a_X(x_*) g_Y(y),
    l(x,y_*)^5 = g_X(x) a_Y(y_*).

Both marked a values are nonzero: if for example a_X(x_*)=0, the whole opposite horizon would be on the line annihilating x_*, impossible by its line cap and seven distinct sites. Scale the two marked representatives to make their a values 1. Scale each eligible representative by the inverse of its ordinary dot with the opposite marked representative. The displayed equations show this is permitted and that, after this scaling, g=1 on all eligible sites and the two ordinary marked rows are 1 on all eligible opposite sites, 0 at the opposite mark.

Choose a basis e_0,e_1,e_2 of the X vector space with e_2=x_*, y_*(e_0)=1, and e_1 in ker y_* independent of e_2. In dual coordinates y_*=e_0^*. Thus the six eligible representatives on each side have the form

    X: (1,c_i,a_i),     Y: (b_j,d_j,1),       1 ≤ i,j ≤ 6,

and their ordinary dot is a_i+b_j+c_i d_j. In these formulas a_i and b_j are just affine coordinates, not the fifth-power sections a_X and a_Y.

For a given c, all X sites with c_i=c lie on the projective line x_1=c x_0, which also contains the mark x_*. By the line cap there can be at most two such eligible sites. The same argument for d_j on Y uses the line y_1=d_j y_2 through y_*. There are therefore at least three distinct c values and at least three distinct d values.

### 3. Two cubic root counts and a Vandermonde contradiction

Let L_Y be λ_Y restricted to the six eligible Y sites, equivalently extend functions on those sites by zero at y_*. This is a nonzero functional because λ_Y has nonzero coefficient at every point. It kills evaluations of all affine polynomials in (b,d) of degree at most two: homogenize in the coordinate y_2 (which is 1 at eligible sites), then λ_Y(g_Y Q)=0 is exactly that identity because g_Y=1 on those sites and g_Y=0 at the mark. Cubic affine evaluations, on the other hand, span all functions on the six sites. Indeed every homogeneous cubic is an affine polynomial of degree ≤3 in this chart, and cubic restriction on the full horizon is surjective, hence remains so on the six eligible sites.

For each eligible x_i, the ordinary quartic row of x_* takes value 1 at the six eligible Y sites and 0 at y_*. Pair it against the cubic row of x_i. Orthogonality says

    0 = L_Y((a_i+b+c_i d)^3) = L_Y((b+c_i d)^3),

since terms containing a_i have degree at most two in (b,d). Define the one-variable polynomial

    T_Y(C)=L_Y((b+C d)^3).

It is nonzero: if all four coefficients vanished, since 3 is invertible, L_Y would kill each of b³, b²d, bd² and d³, in addition to all quadratics and lower degrees. It would kill all affine cubics, which span the six-site function space, contradicting L_Y nonzero. It has degree at most three and every c_i is a root. Thus there are exactly three distinct c values, necessarily with two eligible sites at each, since no fiber has more than two.

Repeat exactly this argument with X and Y interchanged, using the quartic row of the Y mark, the affine polynomials in the two X coordinates, and the six-site X cubic restriction. It follows that there are exactly three distinct d values, also with two eligible sites per value.

Write them as d^(1),d^(2),d^(3), and let w_k be the total coefficient of L_Y on the two sites over d^(k). The identities L_Y(1)=L_Y(d)=L_Y(d²)=0 read

    Σ_k w_k = Σ_k w_k d^(k) = Σ_k w_k (d^(k))² = 0.

The square Vandermonde determinant is the product of the three nonzero pair differences, so all w_k vanish. In particular L_Y(d³)=0. But this is the coefficient of C³ in the nonzero polynomial T_Y. Its degree is now at most two, yet the three distinct c values are roots, contradiction.

This proof is algebraic over K, with no appeal to ordering, rational points, nonsplit orbit classification, or the power-matrix ranks. The quadratic/cubic divided coefficients and the Vandermonde proof do not require the conic through the six eligible sites to be smooth.
