# Independent audit: three-setting paired reflection count

**Result.** The zero-energy determinant identity and its fixed-input three-setting sufficiency hold, including for a leaky nonunitary reflection block. The two fixed analyzer setting obstruction and the separate all-entry single-particle intensity collision both hold. None of these are claims about a bulk phase, an experimental platform, or necessity of pair preparation relative to all possible coherent single-particle tomography.

## Algebra and observables

Let the device preserve quasiparticle number at positive excitation energy (not the physical electronic charge) under coherent elastic quadratic BdG evolution. For two occupied incident electron orbitals, stack the electron- and hole-reflection amplitudes into \(C=\binom A B\). At the continuous zero-energy limit, particle-hole symmetry gives \(r=[C,\tau_x\overline C]\). The incoming state is a normalized Slater determinant if the orbital inputs are orthogonal and share the same spectral envelope. After global unitary scattering each pair of distinct outgoing modes \(i,j\) has amplitude equal to the minor of the corresponding rows of \(C_{\rm full}\). Restricting the output rows to the measured lead leaves the **absolute** pair probabilities, since the events with an escaped excitation are still part of the sample.

Let \(P_{ee}=|\det A|^2\), \(P_{hh}=|\det B|^2\), and \(M_{ij}\) the mixed minor of rows \(e_i,h_j\), in that order. Expanding along the first two columns of \(r\), the two pure-charge terms have plus sign, a mixed complement toggles charge and must be reordered, and the net mixed terms are
\[
-|M_{12}|^2-|M_{21}|^2+2\Re(M_{11}\overline M_{22}).
\]
It follows that
\[
\det r=P_{ee}+P_{hh}-\|M\|_F^2+|\mathrm{tr}M|^2.
\]
There is no unitarity assumption on \(r\). The coherent normal rotation \(V\oplus\bar V\) sends \(M\) to \(VMV^\dagger\), not \(VMV^T\). Decompose \(M=tI+\sum_jv_j\sigma_j\). In analyzer axis \(a\), the mixed/different-orbital probability is \(2(\sum_j|v_j|^2-|v_a|^2)\). Its mean over \(x,y,z\) is \(2(\|M\|_F^2-\frac12|\mathrm{tr}M|^2)/3\). The score \(P_{\rm 2returns}-3\overline P_{\rm mixed,diff}\) is therefore exactly \(\det r\). Equivalently the three axes extract the usual conjugate-basis Bell overlap in the \(eh\) sector. The new statement is the link of that standard measurement to the reflection determinant and the two-case identifiability separation. In either ideal unitary or leaky case, \(|\det r|\le P_{\rm 2returns}\) by the six-dimensional exterior-square inner-product Cauchy–Schwarz bound. A singular \(r\) requires no artificial binary classification.

Pure gauge changes of normal mode phases change \(r\) by input/output blocks of determinant one. Nor is coherent rotation between charge types necessary. On the other hand, coherent **orbital** preparation and measurement sufficient to reconstruct each of the two linear blocks separately up to phase would also suffice without simultaneous particles: phases \(A'=e^{i\alpha}A,\ B'=e^{i\beta}B\) correspond to left and right uniform Nambu phase matrices with exponents \((\alpha-\beta)/2\) and \((\alpha+\beta)/2\). Both have determinant one. A claim of intrinsically necessary two-particle detection over arbitrary input/output tomography would be false.

## Opposite-sign examples and strict realizability

**Full one-particle intensity equality.** Write \(T=\begin{psmallmatrix}c&s\\-s&c\end{psmallmatrix}\), \(c=4/5,s=3/5\), \(P=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}\), \(Q=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1)P\). The two exact real matrices
\[
S_-=\mathrm{diag}(T\otimes I,T\otimes P),\qquad
S_+=\mathrm{diag}(T\otimes I,T\otimes Q)
\]
are in \(SO(8)\), with the first/second index of \(T\) denoting physical lead, and with two quadrature sectors. This is a common bottom-lead physical output sign change relative to the original involutive block construction: it preserves every intensity and the left block. In electron/hole coordinates the blocks are half the sums and differences of the two real sectors. The identity orbital support is disjoint from the off-diagonal supports of \(P,Q\), and those off-diagonal magnitudes agree. Every \(|S_{ij}|^2\) matches in these fixed calibrated Nambu bases. Reflection determinants are \(\mp c^4\); transmission per incident mode is \(s^2=9/25\). This does not say that arbitrary rotated-basis intensities, finite energy profiles, or charge-noise statistics are identical.

**Any two fixed local normal output bases.** A common perpendicular orbital Bloch axis has eigenstates \(u_\pm\) with balanced amplitudes in both given analyzer bases. Set \(A=aI\), \(M_\pm=ab\,u_+u_\pm^\dagger\), \(B_\pm^T=-JM_\pm/a\), where \(J_{12}=1\), \(J_{21}=-1\). Indeed mixed minors are \(aJB_\pm^T\). All four mixed-pair event probabilities in either observed basis are \(a^2b^2/4\), with \(P_{ee}=a^4\) and \(P_{hh}=0\). Single-occupancy marginals are \(a^2\) in each electron output and \(b^2/2\) in each hole output. An exactly-one probability for row \(i\) is its marginal minus the pair probabilities through that row; by subtraction the zero-return probability also agrees. Arbitrary separate single-incident electron or hole coherent preparations still give the same local intensities in those two bases: the input covector of \(B_\pm\) is unchanged, and the output vector has balanced projections in both, with the analogous conjugated relation for a hole input. Nothing is asserted for a third analyzer, other lead counts, or a different joint incident state. A deterministic adaptive second basis based on exact first-law data also fails: the first law is independent of the direction within its perpendicular plane, so pick the common perpendicular once the rule's second choice is known.

The original \(a=2/5,b=3/5\) pair is admissible but one matrix has norm one (in the common-normal frame \(B_-=bE_{22}\)). For a strict pair use \(a=2/5,b=7/12\): \(\|r_\pm\|\le a+b=59/60<1\) and their determinants are \(a^4=16/625\) and \(a^4-a^2b^2=-649/22500\). All equal-data claims continue to hold.

**Normal two-lead finite-dot realization.** A real strict \(4\times4\) contraction \(R\) has the rotation-sign Julia completion
\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}R & \sqrt{I-RR^T}\\ -\sqrt{I-R^TR} & R^T\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is orthogonal by SVD, with determinant \(+1\) by the path \(tR\) to zero. It has no \(+1\) eigenvalue: an eigenvector \((x,y)\) would give \(y^T(I-R)x=y^T\sqrt{I-RR^T}y\) and \(x^T(I-R^T)y=-x^T\sqrt{I-R^TR}x\), so both vanish; the roots are positive definite. The explicit \(T\)-sector intensity examples likewise have no \(+1\) (the eigenvalues of \(P,Q\) are \(\pm1,\pm i\), whereas those of \(T\) have real part \(4/5\) and are not such roots). Then \(D=\gamma(S-I)^{-1}(S+I)\) is real skew, and \(H=iD,W=\sqrt{\gamma}\,I\) in the finite wide-band Mahaux–Weidenmüller convention gives \(S(E)=I-2iW^T(E-H+iWW^T)^{-1}W\) with \(S(0)=S\) exactly. Up to the common, physically irrelevant phase of \(W\), the full-rank identity contact is compatible with ordinary charge-preserving tunneling in the physical electron/hole matched bases (take one-body Nambu hopping \(i\sqrt{\gamma}\,I\), which equals \(iW\) and gives the same scattering formula); the internal generic BdG Hamiltonian provides pairing. No extra open lead or anomalous lead coupling is needed for these examples. This is an effective finite-Hamiltonian realization, not a specified local device or experiment.

## Finite energy and measurements

- The literal Fermi energy is not treated as two independent copies of an electron operator and its adjoint. The readout measures positive-energy electron/hole quasiparticle excitations and uses the one-sided continuous on-shell limit. The positive-energy incoming one-body state must contain the two specified orbitals sharing the same indistinguishable wavepacket, an otherwise empty quasiparticle background, and coherent elastic evolution.
- A constant ideal reflectionless normal mixer can also rotate inward-going orbitals without affecting the two-electron input: the filled two-orbital Slater determinant changes by only an overall unitary determinant. It need not be chiral. Reflections/loading that change \(r\) are not covered; finite-energy dependence on either propagation direction must be bounded separately when it changes the prepared input state. Exact charge conjugate calibration holds at zero, whereas a real finite bandwidth demands accuracy for both electron and hole transfer matrices.
- The score systematic bound is verified: a calibrated uniform one-body composition error \(\delta\) changes the exterior-square operator by at most \(2\delta\); return-law total variation is bounded by trace distance, with range 3. Hence scalar bias at most \(6\delta+3\eta+3\zeta\) when \(\eta\) is source trace distance and \(\zeta\) detection total variation. The independent bounded trial Hoeffding radius is \(\sqrt{9\log(2/\alpha)/(2n)}\). No assumed instrument feasibility, noise levels or usable repetition rate.
- Ordinary signed DC currents do not measure all needed probabilities. If an ideal apparatus resolves net charges in individual output orbitals but cannot distinguish a simultaneous same-orbital \(e+h\) from no counts, optional extra single-excitation calibration suffices: the sum \(\mu\) of absolute reflection probabilities in separate \(e_1,e_2\) trials equals the paired trial's mean local quasiparticle number. For paired trials, \(\Pr(|Q_L|=1)=P_1\) and \(P_{\rm mixed,diff}\) is the opposite-nonzero-charge event in distinct orbitals, so \(P_{\rm 2returns}=(\mu-P_1)/2\). This modified protocol requires matched calibration and per-trial counting, not just net-charge averages.

## Primary-source boundaries

- [Akhmerov et al., PRL 106 057001](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.5542) and [Fulga et al., PRB 83 155429](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.1749) give the well-known scattering invariant. [Das Sarma–Nag–Sau](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.00041) already call the continuous leaky value a topological visibility. [Krasnok, Nov. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.17001), Eq. (5), shows conductance directly reads the determinant **if** the monitored lead couples through a single Majorana mode (\(r-I\) rank one). Multiple lead modes there do not mean a general two-orbital reflection block. The broader interpretation of finite reflection zeros in that paper does not give this measurement without its rank-one hypothesis.
- [Strübi–Belzig–Schmidt–Bruder](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.08774) Eq. (2), §§2 and 4, already derives the antisymmetrized two-particle probabilities, treats the two-electron filled setting, and in §4.3 discusses general **global** \(SO(4)\) scattering for their chiral interferometer. They also explicitly warn that charge statistics alone conflate local electron+hole pairs with no net charge. [Chirolli–Baltanás–Frustaglia](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01736) uses two-particle current correlations for electron/hole entanglement, allowing coherent charge rotations; [Jamotte et al., Apr. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09463) uses superconducting beam splitter current cross correlations to characterize Andreev processes, not a general reflection determinant. [Spengler et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.5058) is an antecedent for conjugate-basis Pauli coincidence measurement itself. These are material prior building blocks, not same-claim matches.
- For the class-D Mahaux–Weidenmüller equation one can cite [Vuik et al., SciPost Phys. 7, 061 (2019)](https://pure.tudelft.nl/ws/files/110858445/SciPostPhys_7_5_061.pdf), Eq. (17), or Krasnok Eq. (1). The first draft DOI ending in 94.035145 was an unrelated multiferroic x-ray article.

Not a general diagnostic of Majorana protection or characterization of all possible protocols. No identical prior for this combined assertion surfaced in the bounded search.
