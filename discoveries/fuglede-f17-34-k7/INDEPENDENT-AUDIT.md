# Independent audit: no seven-uniform pencil at size 34 over F17

**Status.** The proof and frozen author replay pass. Under the previous size-34 line cap 2 and uniform-pencil cap 7, both plane cap and maximum number of uniform normals in a pencil are at most 6. The elementary projective point count is 91; the published multiple-blocking theorem sharpens this application to at most 86 projective uniform normals, hence at most 86 difference directions, per member. This does **not** exclude size 34.

## Geometry, independent replay and primary prior

Seven points of either member in an affine plane would be an affine arc. Each direction pairs at most three disjoint pairs so the 21 pairs force at least seven directions; membership of these directions in the other member's uniform pencil forces exactly seven. This affine planar fact is governed by **existing** work: [Beutelspacher–Wettl, Theorem 2.3(d), p.129](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01388411) uses a projective arc and a disjoint exterior-line subset J avoided by every arc secant, with combined size q+1. Take J to be all eleven missing directions on the line at infinity; q=17 and theorem implies 7 divides one of 16,17,18, impossible. Thus the affine plane cap is already six before eliminating K=7.

I independently checked the same fact without a PGL mask catalogue. Normalize any noncollinear triple to (0,0),(1,0),(0,1), so 0, infinity, -1 must be allowed slopes. Try all 1365 choices of the remaining four slopes and all other allowed point sequences in increasing point order. Mark lines containing two already selected points and remove any future point on them. Exact replay `independent-arc.cjs` enumerates 6720 initial points across masks, 4068 recursion visits, and no complete branch. A separate allowed-eight positive control finds a seven-point arc with exactly eight directions. This is an independent numerical check of known geometry, not a newly proved seven-arc theorem.

For the pencil computation specifically, my independent PGL orbit queue starts from all 31824 masks of eleven bad labels and uses direction maps s+1, 3s, and 1/s, which generate every projectivity. It chooses a minimum numerical mask with 0 and 1 bad and infinity uniform in each orbit; the ten representatives cover all 5005 such normalized masks. This differs from the frozen author's lex-first slope ordering and explains changes in any intermediate constant-only filters as origin centering can differ.

## Formal equations and centred residue catalogues

For a putative seven-uniform pencil, let \(g_i(t)\) be all eleven nonuniform population profiles. They have integer values in \([0,6]\), mass 34, and the Radon identity \(\sum_i g_i(y-s_i x)=20+17f(x,y)\). The quotient multiplicity lies in \(\{0,1,2\}\) and need not be Boolean. Reducing modulo 17 yields the necessary formal identities \(\sum_i c_{ij}s_i^h=0\) for \(0\le h\le j\), with constant sum 3 modulo 17. As in [Ferguson–Iosevich §8.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1), write \(c_{ij}=w_i R_j(s_i)\), \(\deg R_j\le9-j\), for \(w_i=(\prod_{k\ne i}(s_i-s_k))^{-1}\). Larger degrees vanish by Vandermonde. These are conditions needed by an actual pencil, not claims of realizability of an arbitrary formal tuple.

No bad profile has degree 0 because its values lie strictly below 17 and its mass is 34, nor degrees 1 through 5 because independent exact least residue sums are \(136,102,85,51,51\). A common translation changes the profile argument by an affine function of slope. In exact degree \(d\), the degree-\((d-1)\) coefficient shifts by \(d\) times its leading coefficient times that function. Each individual centered translate is unique. Common coordinate dilation multiplies argument coefficients by powers of a single nonzero scalar while preserving slopes. Input transformations preserve all occupancy bounds and integer mass.

Independent catalogues include precisely 16 centered sextics (one per lead, all even/cap6), five centered monic septics of residue mass 34 (three under cap6), 272 centered octics under cap6 across all 16 leads, and 188 centered monic nonics under cap6. For the latter two numbers my alternate code uses values at zero plus three squared nonzero anchors, deviating from the author's four-nonzero-anchor enumeration. \[
 Q(s_i)R_{d-2}(s_i)=A_i+\frac{\binom d2}{d^2}R(s_i)^2.
\]
For \(d=6,7,8\) the correction factors modulo 17 are respectively \(-1,-2,10\), and \(A=Q R_{d-2}-(\binom d2/d^2)R^2\) must have degree at most \(8,6,4\) respectively. ## Degree nine and conclusion

If any profile is nonic then all are: \(R_9\) is a single nonzero constant, normalized to 1 since ninth powers permute all nonzero field scalars. \(R_8\) is affine, so a common translation centers all profiles simultaneously. The one-label actual leading coefficients are then \(w_i\) and the verified catalogue gives the complete 188 centered shapes for each after argument scaling by the unique appropriate constant. In the author's source the profile arrays are divided by \(w_i\) for interpolation and multiplied back for all genuine coefficient checks. For each of the \(17^3\) quadratics \(R_7\), enumerate cubic \(R_6\) by four legal anchor values, then quartic \(R_5\) by five legal anchor values, preserving all tied shape runs. The polynomial degree constraints and distinct labels guarantee these anchor captures are exhaustive. I reran the exact frozen source stage 9 in 58.4 seconds: 49,130 quadratic choices, 132,509,280 cubic anchors, 2,807,552 complete cubic choices, 2,828,549 fifth anchors, ten full fifth-compatible tuples, zero quartic passes.

Thus all four possible highest degrees \(6,7,8,9\) fail. The same reasoning applies to each member of the hypothetical spectral pair. At most 6 points of any \(U\) on every projective line implies the elementary 91 bound by lines through one point of \(U\). A stronger immediate **imported** consequence is \(|U|\le86\): the complement is a 12-fold blocking set in \(\mathrm{PG}(2,17)\), so [Ball (1996), Theorem 4.2(ii)](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/54.3.581) gives size at least \(17(12+1)=221\); subtract from \(307\). Both difference sets inherit this bound from the opposite uniform sets. Even the stronger result still only guarantees at least seven parallel secants, not a contradiction. No K6 or global size-34 exclusion is asserted. Nearby primary Fourier-profile method is Ferguson–Iosevich at p11 and is explicitly inherited.
